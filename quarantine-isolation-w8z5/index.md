# Quarantine vs Isolation Explained

> **Understanding the Difference** - These terms are often confused but serve different purposes. Knowing the difference helps you respond appropriately during disease outbreaks.

## Key Definitions

### Isolation

**Who:** People who are SICK
**Purpose:** Separate sick people from healthy people
**Duration:** Until no longer contagious

### Quarantine

**Who:** People who were EXPOSED but may not be sick
**Purpose:** Separate exposed people to see if they develop illness
**Duration:** Typically the incubation period of the disease

| | Isolation | Quarantine |
|---|----------|-----------|
| **Subject** | Sick people | Exposed people |
| **Confirmed illness** | Yes | No (but possible) |
| **Can spread disease** | Yes | Unknown |
| **Duration** | Until recovered | Incubation period |

## When Each is Used

### Isolation is Used When

- Person has confirmed diagnosis
- Person has symptoms consistent with disease
- Need to prevent spread to others
- Medical care may be needed

### Quarantine is Used When

- Person had contact with sick person
- Person traveled from affected area
- Exposure to pathogen occurred
- Monitoring for symptoms needed

## Home Isolation Guidelines

### Setting Up

- Designated sick room (ideally with own bathroom)
- Separate from other family members
- Dedicated caregiver (one person)
- Supplies in room (water, medications, phone)

### Practices

- Sick person stays in room
- Caregiver wears appropriate PPE
- Good ventilation when safe
- Frequent hand washing
- Clean high-touch surfaces
- Separate laundry handling
- Separate dishes (or disposable)

### When to End Isolation

Based on disease and official guidance, typically:
- Fever-free for specified period
- Symptoms improved
- Specified number of days passed
- Or negative tests (if applicable)

## Home Quarantine Guidelines

### What to Do

- Stay home (don't go to work, school, public places)
- Monitor for symptoms daily
- Maintain distance from household members
- Don't share items
- Know what symptoms to watch for
- Know when to seek medical care

### Monitoring

Check daily for:
- Fever (temperature check)
- Respiratory symptoms (cough, breathing difficulty)
- Other disease-specific symptoms
- Report symptoms to health authorities if directed

### Duration

- Typically equals incubation period of disease
- May vary by health authority guidance
- Complete full period even if feeling well
- Disease can still develop late in quarantine

## Supplies Needed

### For Sick Room

- Thermometer
- Medications (fever/pain relief)
- Fluids (water, electrolyte drinks)
- Tissues
- Waste basket with lid and liner
- Phone and charger
- Disinfectant
- Clean towels
- Entertainment (books, devices)

### For Caregiver

- Masks (N95 if available)
- Gloves
- Eye protection (if needed)
- Gown or designated clothing
- Hand sanitizer
- Disinfectant supplies

## Mental Health Considerations

Isolation and quarantine are hard:
- Loneliness and boredom
- Anxiety about illness
- Cabin fever
- Disruption of routine
- Financial stress

### Coping Strategies

- Video calls with family/friends
- Entertainment (books, movies, games)
- Exercise (if able)
- Maintain routines
- Limit news consumption
- Reach out for support

## Quick Reference

```
ISOLATION = SICK
- Confirmed illness
- Stay separated until recovered
- Caregiver uses PPE
- Dedicated room/bathroom

QUARANTINE = EXPOSED
- Was exposed but may not be sick
- Monitor for symptoms
- Stay home during incubation period
- May still develop illness

BOTH REQUIRE:
- Staying home
- Separation from others
- Monitoring health
- Following official guidance
```
