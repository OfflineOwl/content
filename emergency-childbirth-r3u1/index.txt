EMERGENCY CHILDBIRTH BASICS
===========================

If you must assist with childbirth when medical help is unavailable,
this guide provides basic information. Birth is a natural process—
your role is mainly to support and catch.

IMPORTANT DISCLAIMER
--------------------
This guide is for TRUE emergencies only. Childbirth should occur
with trained medical professionals whenever possible. Complications
can be life-threatening for mother and baby.

WHEN BIRTH IS IMMINENT
----------------------

Signs that delivery is about to happen:
- Strong, regular contractions (every 2-3 minutes)
- Mother feels urge to push
- Visible bulging at vaginal opening
- Mother says "the baby is coming"
- Crowning (baby's head visible)

If these signs present and help is more than 20 minutes away,
prepare for emergency delivery.

PREPARING FOR DELIVERY
----------------------

GATHER SUPPLIES:
- Clean towels or sheets (several)
- Clean blankets for baby
- Clean string/shoelaces (to tie cord)
- Clean scissors (sterilized if possible)
- Bulb syringe (if available)
- Gloves (if available)
- Plastic sheet or clean surface
- Warm water
- Container for placenta

PREPARE THE AREA:
1. Find clean, warm location
2. Ensure privacy
3. Lay plastic sheet, then clean sheets
4. Have towels ready
5. Warm blankets for baby
6. Wash your hands thoroughly

POSITION THE MOTHER:
- On back with knees bent and apart
- Or on side with top leg raised
- Or squatting (gravity assists)
- Whatever is most comfortable for her

THE DELIVERY PROCESS
--------------------

STAGE 1: CROWNING
When baby's head becomes visible at vaginal opening:

1. DO NOT pull on baby
2. Support head gently as it emerges
3. Tell mother to pant or blow (slows delivery)
4. Check for cord around neck
   - If cord around neck: gently slip over head
   - If too tight: leave alone, deliver quickly

STAGE 2: HEAD DELIVERY
1. Head will rotate naturally
2. Support head, don't pull
3. Mucus will come out - wipe from nose/mouth
4. Wait for next contraction

STAGE 3: BODY DELIVERY
1. Guide shoulders out (usually one at a time)
2. Top shoulder first, then bottom
3. Support body as it emerges
4. Baby is slippery - be ready
5. Body slides out quickly after shoulders

IMMEDIATELY AFTER BIRTH
-----------------------

FOR THE BABY:

1. Keep baby at level of mother or slightly below
2. Note the time of birth
3. Clear airway:
   - Wipe face
   - Use bulb syringe if available
   - Suction mouth first, then nose
4. Stimulate if not crying:
   - Rub back
   - Flick soles of feet
5. Keep warm:
   - Dry thoroughly
   - Place skin-to-skin on mother's chest
   - Cover with warm blanket
   - Cover head (babies lose heat through head)
6. Baby should cry and turn pink
7. Do NOT wash baby (vernix protects skin)

FOR THE UMBILICAL CORD:

1. No need to cut immediately
2. Wait until cord stops pulsing (usually 1-3 minutes)
3. If you must cut:
   - Tie string tightly at two points
   - 6 inches from baby
   - 8 inches from baby
   - Cut between ties with clean scissors
4. If you cannot cut safely, leave attached
   - Wrap placenta with baby
   - Medical personnel can cut later

FOR THE MOTHER:

1. Placenta will deliver (usually 5-30 minutes)
2. DO NOT pull on cord
3. Watch for placenta delivery
4. Massage lower abdomen firmly after placenta
5. This helps uterus contract and reduces bleeding
6. Save placenta for medical personnel to examine

NEWBORN ASSESSMENT: APGAR
-------------------------

Assess at 1 minute and 5 minutes after birth:

A - APPEARANCE (skin color)
  2 = Pink all over
  1 = Pink body, blue hands/feet
  0 = Blue/pale all over

P - PULSE (heart rate)
  2 = Over 100 beats/min
  1 = Under 100 beats/min
  0 = Absent

G - GRIMACE (reflex response)
  2 = Cries, coughs, sneezes
  1 = Grimaces
  0 = No response

A - ACTIVITY (muscle tone)
  2 = Active movement
  1 = Some flexion
  0 = Limp

R - RESPIRATION (breathing)
  2 = Strong cry
  1 = Weak cry, irregular breathing
  0 = Not breathing

Total score:
7-10 = Normal
4-6  = Needs some assistance
0-3  = Emergency intervention needed

IF BABY NOT BREATHING
---------------------

1. Clear airway (suction, wipe)
2. Stimulate (rub back, flick feet)
3. Position head in neutral position
4. If still not breathing:
   - Cover baby's mouth AND nose with your mouth
   - Give very gentle puffs (cheek breaths, not lung)
   - Just enough to see chest rise
   - 30 breaths per minute
5. Check for pulse (inside upper arm)
6. If no pulse, begin infant CPR

WARNING SIGNS - MOTHER
----------------------

SEEK IMMEDIATE HELP IF:
- Heavy bleeding (soaking more than 2 pads/hour)
- Prolapsed cord (cord comes out before baby)
- Baby in abnormal position (breech, transverse)
- Labor not progressing for hours
- Mother unconscious or severely unwell
- Green or brown amniotic fluid (meconium)
- Multiple babies suspected

WARNING SIGNS - BABY
--------------------

SEEK IMMEDIATE HELP IF:
- Baby not breathing after stimulation
- Baby blue or very pale
- Baby limp and unresponsive
- Chest retracting with breathing
- Unusual sounds when breathing
- Visible birth defects
- Baby very small

POST-DELIVERY CARE
------------------

FOR MOTHER:
- Monitor bleeding (some is normal)
- Encourage breastfeeding (helps uterus contract)
- Keep warm and comfortable
- Provide food and drink
- Watch for signs of infection
- Watch for excessive bleeding

FOR BABY:
- Keep warm (skin-to-skin is best)
- Encourage breastfeeding as soon as possible
- Monitor breathing and color
- Keep cord stump clean and dry
- Watch for signs of infection

WHAT NOT TO DO
--------------

DO NOT:
- Pull on baby during delivery
- Pull on umbilical cord
- Cut cord with unclean instrument
- Wash off vernix (white coating)
- Separate mother and baby unnecessarily
- Leave baby uncovered
- Give baby water or other liquids
- Delay skin-to-skin contact
- Put anything in vagina

COMFORT AND SUPPORT
-------------------

Your most important roles:
- Stay calm (your calm helps mother)
- Provide encouragement
- Hold her hand
- Help her breathe through contractions
- Keep her informed of progress
- Protect privacy and dignity
- Keep environment warm and quiet

Remember: Women have been giving birth for thousands of years.
Your role is support and catching—the mother's body knows what to do.
