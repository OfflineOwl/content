INDOOR AIR QUALITY IN EMERGENCIES
==================================

During emergencies, indoor air quality can become a serious concern.
This guide covers threats and how to manage them.

COMMON THREATS
--------------

CARBON MONOXIDE (CO):
Source: Fuel-burning devices (generators, heaters, stoves)
Risk: Colorless, odorless, deadly
Prevention: Never use fuel-burning devices indoors
Detection: CO detectors essential

SMOKE:
Source: Wildfires, structural fires
Risk: Respiratory damage, long-term health effects
Prevention: Air filtration, sealing home
Detection: Visible, smell

CHEMICAL CONTAMINATION:
Source: Industrial accidents, spills
Risk: Varies by chemical
Prevention: Shelter-in-place, air sealing
Detection: May have odor or may not

MOLD:
Source: Water damage, flooding
Risk: Respiratory problems, allergies
Prevention: Dry areas quickly, ventilation
Detection: Visible growth, musty smell

DUST AND PARTICULATES:
Source: Building damage, dust storms
Risk: Respiratory irritation
Prevention: Air filtration, masks
Detection: Visible, breathing difficulty

CARBON MONOXIDE SAFETY
----------------------

CO SOURCES DURING EMERGENCIES:
- Generators (NEVER indoors)
- Portable heaters (some types)
- Gas stoves used for heat
- Vehicles running in garage
- Charcoal/gas grills
- Camp stoves

CO DETECTOR REQUIREMENTS:
- Battery backup essential
- On every level of home
- Near sleeping areas
- Test monthly
- Replace batteries annually
- Replace unit per manufacturer guidelines

CO POISONING SYMPTOMS:
Early:
- Headache
- Dizziness
- Weakness
- Nausea
- Confusion

Severe:
- Loss of consciousness
- Death

IF CO ALARM SOUNDS:
1. Get everyone outside immediately
2. Call 911
3. Account for all people
4. Don't re-enter until cleared
5. Seek medical evaluation

SMOKE AND WILDFIRE
------------------

INDOOR PROTECTION:
1. Close all windows and doors
2. Run air conditioner (recirculate mode)
3. Use air purifiers with HEPA filters
4. Seal gaps with damp towels
5. Create clean air room

AIR PURIFIERS:
- HEPA filters most effective
- DIY: Box fan + MERV 13 filter
- Run continuously during smoke events
- Replace filters as needed

SIGNS TO EVACUATE:
- Visible flames approaching
- Air quality unbearable
- Official evacuation orders
- Breathing difficulty despite measures

MASK PROTECTION:
- N95 or P100 respirators effective
- Surgical masks provide limited protection
- Cloth masks provide minimal protection
- Ensure proper fit (seal to face)

SHELTER-IN-PLACE (CHEMICAL)
---------------------------

IF CHEMICAL RELEASE SUSPECTED:

1. GET INDOORS
   - Nearest building
   - Close all doors and windows
   - Turn off HVAC systems

2. SEAL THE ROOM
   - Choose interior room (above ground for most chemicals)
   - Close door
   - Seal gaps with wet towels
   - Cover windows with plastic and tape
   - Seal vents, outlets, and gaps

3. REDUCE AIR EXCHANGE
   - Fireplace dampers closed
   - Bathroom/kitchen vents sealed
   - Minimize door opening

4. MONITOR INFORMATION
   - Emergency radio
   - Phone alerts
   - Don't leave until all-clear

IMPORTANT:
- Sealed rooms have limited air
- Don't seal for more than 2-3 hours
- Be ready to ventilate when safe
- Listen for official guidance

WATER DAMAGE AND MOLD
---------------------

MOLD GROWTH CONDITIONS:
- Moisture
- Warmth
- Organic material
- Begins within 24-48 hours of water exposure

PREVENTION:
- Dry wet areas within 24 hours
- Remove wet carpeting, padding
- Ventilate damp areas
- Use fans to circulate air
- Dehumidify if possible
- Discard porous items that can't dry

CLEANING MOLD:
Small areas (less than 10 sq ft):
- Mix 1 cup bleach per gallon water
- Or use commercial mold cleaner
- Wear N95 mask, gloves, eye protection
- Scrub affected area
- Allow to dry completely

Large areas:
- Professional remediation recommended
- Significant health risk
- May require demolition

IMPROVING AIR QUALITY
---------------------

VENTILATION:
- Open windows when outdoor air is better
- Cross-ventilation when possible
- Use fans to circulate
- Exhaust stale air

AIR FILTRATION:
- HEPA air purifiers
- Furnace filters (MERV 13+)
- DIY box fan filters
- Replace filters regularly

REDUCING POLLUTANTS:
- Don't burn candles unnecessarily
- Avoid aerosol products
- Clean up mold and moisture
- Remove damaged materials
- Minimize dust-producing activities

AIR QUALITY EQUIPMENT
---------------------

ESSENTIAL:
- CO detectors (battery backup)
- Smoke detectors (battery backup)
- N95 masks
- Wet towels (for sealing)

RECOMMENDED:
- HEPA air purifier
- Plastic sheeting and tape
- Extra HVAC filters
- P100 respirators
- Air quality monitor

DIY BOX FAN FILTER:
Materials:
- 20" box fan
- 20" MERV 13 filter
- Tape or bungee cord

Assembly:
1. Tape filter to intake side of fan
2. Arrow on filter points toward fan
3. Run continuously as needed
4. Replace filter when visibly dirty

QUICK REFERENCE
---------------

CO SAFETY:
- Never run fuel-burning devices indoors
- CO detector on every level
- Symptoms: Headache, dizziness, confusion
- If alarm sounds: Get out, call 911

SMOKE PROTECTION:
- Close windows/doors
- Run AC on recirculate
- Use HEPA air purifier
- Wear N95 mask

SHELTER-IN-PLACE:
- Interior room, above ground
- Seal gaps with wet towels
- Cover windows with plastic
- Turn off HVAC
- Monitor for all-clear

MOLD PREVENTION:
- Dry wet areas within 24 hours
- Ventilate
- Remove damaged materials
