Home Security During Civil Unrest

Your Home is Your Sanctuary - During civil unrest or breakdown of order, protecting your home and family becomes paramount. This guide covers practical, non-violent approaches to home security.

Understanding the Threat

Levels of Unrest

Level Characteristics Response
Peaceful protests Localized, organized Awareness
Civil disturbance Property damage, looting Enhanced security
Widespread unrest Violence, fire, no police response Full security measures

Risk Assessment

Higher risk:

  • Near commercial areas
  • On main roads/routes
  • Ground floor access
  • Weak entry points
  • Isolated location

Lower risk:

  • Residential neighborhood
  • Limited access
  • Strong community
  • Reinforced entry points

Immediate Security Measures

Entry Point Hardening

Doors:

  • Reinforce strike plates (3" screws into studs)
  • Add door security bar
  • Consider reinforcing hinges
  • Lock all entry points
  • Don't open for strangers

Windows:

  • Lock all windows
  • Consider window security film
  • Plywood available for boarding (pre-cut)
  • Close blinds/curtains (visibility denial)

Visibility Denial

  • Close curtains and blinds
  • Minimize visible light at night
  • Don't advertise resources
  • Keep valuable items out of sight
  • Make home look "not worth the trouble"

Deterrence Layers

Visual Deterrents

  • Motion-activated lights
  • Security cameras (visible)
  • Security system signs
  • Dog (or "Beware of Dog" sign)
  • Well-maintained appearance
  • No hiding spots near entry points

Sound Deterrents

  • Dogs barking
  • Alarm systems
  • Motion-activated alarms

Barriers

  • Fences with locked gates
  • Thorny bushes under windows
  • Gravel paths (noise when walked on)
  • Vehicles blocking driveways

Situational Awareness

Monitor Developments

  • Local news
  • Emergency radio
  • Social media (with skepticism)
  • Police scanner (if legal)
  • Community contacts

Know When to Worry

  • Unrest moving toward your area
  • Police withdrawing from area
  • Sounds of breaking glass, fire
  • Large groups gathering nearby
  • No emergency response to calls

If Unrest Approaches

Stay or Go Decision

Stay if:

  • Home is defensible
  • You have supplies
  • No immediate threat
  • Evacuation routes blocked

Leave if:

  • Direct threat to home
  • Fire approaching
  • Overwhelming numbers
  • Safe evacuation possible

If Staying

  1. Gather family in interior room
  2. All entry points secured
  3. Communication devices ready
  4. Emergency supplies accessible
  5. Escape route identified
  6. Stay away from windows
  7. Lights minimal

Communication Strategy

With Family

  • Designated safe room
  • Rally points if separated
  • Code words for emergencies
  • Roles assigned

With Neighbors

  • Mutual aid agreements
  • Signal systems
  • Shared watch schedule
  • Communication methods

With Authorities

  • Know emergency numbers
  • Document incidents
  • Keep phone charged
  • Don't rely on 911 response during widespread unrest

Rules of Engagement

Legal Considerations

  • Self-defense laws vary by location
  • Know your local laws
  • Proportional response
  • Duty to retreat (some jurisdictions)
  • Castle doctrine (some jurisdictions)

Principles

  1. Avoid confrontation when possible
  2. De-escalate if confronted
  3. Property is not worth your life
  4. Defend life as last resort
  5. Document everything possible

Security Checklist

Immediate Actions

  • Lock all doors and windows
  • Close blinds/curtains
  • Turn off unnecessary lights
  • Gather family together
  • Phone charged and ready
  • Emergency supplies accessible
  • Fire extinguisher ready
  • Monitor news/radio

Ongoing Security

  • Entry points reinforced
  • Security plan discussed
  • Rally points established
  • Neighbors coordinated
  • Documentation ready
