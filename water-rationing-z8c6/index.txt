WATER RATIONING GUIDELINES
==========================

When water supply is limited, careful rationing extends your 
survival time. This guide helps you use water most efficiently.

SURVIVAL WATER NEEDS
--------------------

ABSOLUTE MINIMUM (survival only):
- 1/2 liter (2 cups) per day in cool conditions
- This maintains basic survival but leads to dehydration
- Not sustainable beyond several days

MINIMUM FOR FUNCTION:
- 1 liter (4 cups) per day
- Allows basic function
- Still leads to mild dehydration

SUSTAINABLE MINIMUM:
- 2 liters (8 cups) per day for drinking
- Prevents significant dehydration
- More in heat or with activity

FACTORS THAT INCREASE NEED:
- Hot weather
- Physical activity
- Illness (fever, diarrhea, vomiting)
- Breastfeeding
- Children (relative to body weight)

RATIONING PRINCIPLES
--------------------

1. KNOW YOUR SUPPLY
   - Calculate total water available
   - Estimate how long until resupply
   - Plan daily allocation

2. PRIORITIZE USE
   Priority order:
   1. Drinking water
   2. Medical needs
   3. Oral hygiene (minimal)
   4. Food preparation
   5. Sanitation
   6. All other uses

3. MINIMIZE LOSS
   - No water for washing (use wipes)
   - No water for flushing (alternative methods)
   - Capture all usable water

RATIONING LEVELS
----------------

LEVEL 1: NORMAL CONSERVATION
(Supply adequate but uncertain)
- Reduce non-essential use
- No lawn/garden watering
- Shorter showers
- Full loads only for laundry
- Fix any leaks

LEVEL 2: MODERATE RATIONING
(Supply limited, resupply expected)
- 1 gallon per person per day total
- Drinking: 1/2 gallon
- Cooking: 1/4 gallon
- Hygiene: 1/4 gallon (sponge bath, minimal)
- No laundry
- No dishwashing (disposable/wipes)

LEVEL 3: SEVERE RATIONING
(Supply critical, resupply uncertain)
- 1/2 gallon per person per day
- All water for drinking
- Wipes for all hygiene
- No cooking requiring water
- No-cook food only

LEVEL 4: SURVIVAL RATIONING
(Supply nearly exhausted)
- Minimum survival water only
- Sip frequently, don't gulp
- Stay inactive to reduce need
- Stay cool to reduce sweating
- All other uses suspended

WATER-SAVING TECHNIQUES
-----------------------

DRINKING EFFICIENCY:
- Sip throughout day (more efficient)
- Don't gulp large amounts
- Keep mouth moist with small sips
- Suck on hard candy (stimulates saliva)
- Avoid salty foods (increases thirst)

REDUCE WATER LOSS:
- Stay in shade
- Avoid activity during hot hours
- Rest more, move less
- Breathe through nose (less water loss)
- Wear light, loose clothing

NO-WATER HYGIENE:
- Baby wipes or wet wipes
- Hand sanitizer
- Dry shampoo
- Baking soda for teeth (minimal water rinse)
- Damp cloth wipe-down (using minimal water)

NO-WATER SANITATION:
- Bucket toilet with cat litter
- Double-bag waste
- No flushing

NO-WATER FOOD:
- Ready-to-eat foods
- Canned goods with liquid
- Foods that don't require water to prepare
- Avoid dehydrated foods (require water)

SPECIAL SITUATIONS
------------------

CHILDREN:
- Require more water relative to body weight
- More vulnerable to dehydration
- Monitor for signs of dehydration
- Priority for available water

ELDERLY:
- May not feel thirst as strongly
- More vulnerable to dehydration
- May have medication needs
- Monitor closely, encourage drinking

MEDICAL NEEDS:
- Some medications require water
- Illness increases water needs
- Fever, vomiting, diarrhea require extra water
- Priority allocation for sick individuals

BREASTFEEDING:
- Mother needs additional water
- Approximately 1 additional liter per day
- Priority to maintain milk supply

HOT WEATHER:
- Needs can double or triple
- Stay inactive during heat
- Find shade and coolest areas
- Recognize heat illness signs

CALCULATING RATION DURATION
---------------------------

FORMULA:
Available water ÷ Daily allocation per person ÷ Number of people = Days

EXAMPLE:
50 gallons ÷ 1 gallon/person/day ÷ 4 people = 12.5 days

ADJUST FOR:
- Expected resupply date
- Weather conditions
- Activity level
- Health needs
- Safety margin

SIGNS OF DEHYDRATION
--------------------

MILD (still manageable):
- Thirst
- Dry mouth
- Dark yellow urine
- Decreased urination
- Headache

MODERATE (concerning):
- Very dry mouth
- Little or no urination
- Sunken eyes
- Rapid heartbeat
- Irritability

SEVERE (emergency):
- No urination
- Very sunken eyes
- Sunken fontanel (infants)
- Rapid breathing
- Cold, mottled hands/feet
- Confusion
- Unconsciousness

RESPONSE TO DEHYDRATION:
Mild/Moderate: Increase water allocation if possible
Severe: Medical emergency, need significant hydration

PRIORITIZING WATER ALLOCATION
-----------------------------

IF WATER MUST BE DISTRIBUTED UNEQUALLY:

Higher priority:
- Those showing dehydration
- Children
- Sick individuals
- Elderly
- Pregnant/breastfeeding

Lower priority (can tolerate mild dehydration):
- Healthy adults at rest
- Those with body reserves
- Those doing less activity

DIFFICULT DECISIONS:
In extreme rationing, healthy adults may receive 
less to ensure children and vulnerable survive.

FINDING MORE WATER
------------------

WHILE RATIONING, ACTIVELY SEEK MORE:
- Rainwater collection
- Water heater (home)
- Pipe drainage (home)
- Toilet tanks (not bowls)
- Natural sources (with treatment)
- Ice from freezer (melted)
- Food liquids (canned goods, fruits)

NEVER USE FOR DRINKING:
- Pool water (chemicals) - external use only
- Radiator fluid
- Toilet bowl water
- Unknown liquids

---------------

- Staying cool
- Staying still
- Staying in shade
- Eating moist foods
- Avoiding salt
