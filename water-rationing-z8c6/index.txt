WATER RATIONING GUIDELINES
==========================

When water is limited, cut every use except drinking. Official
guidance is clear: don't ration drinking water unless authorities
tell you to. Drink what you need today and work on finding more for
tomorrow. Holding back drinking water causes dehydration, which
leads to poor judgment, illness, and heat injury.

DO THIS NOW
-----------

1. Drink what you need. Don't cut drinking water to stretch supply.
2. Stop all non-essential water use: no flushing with drinking
   water, no laundry, no outdoor watering.
3. Reduce how much water your body needs: stay cool, stay in the
   shade, rest, and avoid hard work in the heat.
4. Avoid salty foods and alcohol. Both increase your need for water.
5. Count your supply and start finding more now (see FINDING MORE
   WATER below). Listen for official water distribution points.
6. Watch everyone, especially children, older adults, and sick
   people, for signs of dehydration (see below).

HOW MUCH WATER PEOPLE NEED
--------------------------

- A normally active person needs about 3/4 gallon (about 3 liters)
  of fluid a day, from water and other drinks.
- Children, nursing mothers, and people who are sick may need more.
- Needs can double in very hot weather.
- Fever, vomiting, and diarrhea all increase water needs.
- For planning, count 1 gallon per person per day for drinking plus
  basic sanitation.

There is no safe "survival minimum" to aim for. Less water than
your body needs means dehydration, and in heat that can become an
emergency within a day.

RATIONING PRINCIPLES
--------------------

1. KNOW YOUR SUPPLY
   - Add up all stored water, including hidden water at home
     (water heater, pipes, ice, toilet tank).
   - Estimate how long until service returns or you can resupply.

2. PROTECT DRINKING WATER FIRST
   Priority order for safe water:
   1. Drinking
   2. Medicines and medical needs (including mixing infant formula)
   3. Food preparation
   4. Handwashing before eating and after using the toilet
   5. Brushing teeth
   6. Other hygiene and cleaning

3. USE THE RIGHT WATER FOR THE JOB
   - Safe (treated or stored) water: drinking, food, medicine,
     brushing teeth.
   - Pool, rain, or untreated water: flushing toilets, cleaning,
     and washing (not drinking unless treated).

4. WASTE NOTHING
   - Reuse washing water for flushing.
   - Catch rainwater for cleaning and flushing.

CONSERVATION LEVELS
-------------------

These levels cut non-drinking uses. Drinking water stays at what
each person needs at every level.

LEVEL 1: CONSERVE
(Supply adequate but uncertain)
- No lawn or garden watering
- Short showers, or sponge baths
- Full loads only for laundry and dishes
- Fix leaks
- Flush only when needed

LEVEL 2: CUT BACK
(Supply limited, resupply expected)
- Drinking: what each person needs (about 3/4 gallon for an active
  adult, more in heat)
- Cooking: choose foods that need little or no water
- Hygiene: sponge baths and handwashing only
- No laundry
- Disposable plates and utensils, or wipe dishes before a minimal
  wash
- Flush with non-drinking water only

LEVEL 3: ESSENTIALS ONLY
(Supply critical, resupply uncertain)
- Drinking water first
- Water for medicine and infant formula
- No-cook foods that don't need water
- Hand sanitizer instead of handwashing when hands aren't visibly
  dirty
- Bucket toilet instead of flushing
- Seek official water distribution or evacuate if you can

LEVEL 4: OUT OF WATER SOON
(Supply nearly exhausted)
- Drink what you need now; don't save it for later
- Get help: call your local emergency management office or 911 if
  someone is in danger, go to a distribution point or shelter, or
  ask neighbors
- Stay still, cool, and in shade to reduce your body's needs
- Treat any available source (see the Water Purification guide)

WATER-SAVING TECHNIQUES
-----------------------

REDUCE YOUR BODY'S NEED:
- Stay in the shade or the coolest part of the building
- Do essential work in the cooler early morning or evening
- Rest; avoid heavy exertion
- Wear light, loose clothing
- Avoid alcohol

EAT TO SAVE WATER:
- Avoid salty foods; they make you thirsty
- Choose salt-free crackers, whole-grain cereals, and canned foods
  with high liquid content (canned fruit, vegetables, soups)
- Avoid dehydrated and freeze-dried foods that need water
- Unlike water, food can be rationed safely for a while by healthy
  adults (not by children or pregnant women)

LOW-WATER HYGIENE:
- Hand sanitizer with at least 60% alcohol when soap and water are
  scarce (it doesn't work well on visibly dirty or greasy hands)
- Still wash hands with soap and water before preparing food and
  after using the toilet when you can; diarrhea costs far more
  water than handwashing
- Baby wipes or a damp cloth for a body wipe-down
- Brush teeth with a small cup of safe water

LOW-WATER SANITATION:
- Bucket toilet lined with a heavy-duty bag, with cat litter or
  sawdust to absorb liquid and odor
- Double-bag waste and keep it sealed and away from living areas
  and water sources
- Flush with non-drinking water only

SPECIAL SITUATIONS
------------------

INFANTS:
- Breastfeeding needs no water and protects the baby. Mothers need
  extra fluids.
- Ready-to-feed formula needs no water. If you must mix formula,
  use bottled water, or water that was boiled and cooled.
- Never dilute formula more than the label says to stretch supply.

CHILDREN:
- Dehydrate faster than adults
- May not ask for water; offer it regularly
- Watch for dehydration signs closely

OLDER ADULTS:
- May not feel thirst
- Often take medicines that increase water loss
- Offer water regularly

PEOPLE WHO ARE SICK:
- Fever, vomiting, and diarrhea raise water needs sharply
- Use oral rehydration solution if available (see the Foodborne
  Illness guide)

PREGNANT AND NURSING WOMEN:
- Need more water than other adults

PETS:
- Need water too; count it in your supply
- Don't let pets drink floodwater or scummy water

HOT WEATHER:
- Needs can double
- Rest during the hottest hours
- Watch for heat exhaustion and heat stroke (see the heat guides)

CALCULATING HOW LONG WATER LASTS
--------------------------------

FORMULA:
Total water ÷ (gallons per person per day × number of people) = days

EXAMPLE:
50 gallons ÷ (1 gallon × 4 people) = 12.5 days

ADJUST FOR:
- Hot weather (needs can double)
- Babies, sick people, nursing mothers, and pets
- When you expect service or resupply
- A safety margin: start looking for more water long before you
  run out

SIGNS OF DEHYDRATION
--------------------

MILD TO MODERATE:
- Thirst
- Dry or sticky mouth
- Peeing less than usual; dark yellow urine
- Headache, tiredness, muscle cramps
- Lightheadedness

INFANTS AND YOUNG CHILDREN:
- No wet diaper for 3 hours or more
- Crying without tears
- Dry mouth and tongue
- Sunken eyes
- Unusual sleepiness or irritability

SEVERE (MEDICAL EMERGENCY):
- No urine, or very dark urine
- Confusion, irritability, or extreme sleepiness
- Rapid heartbeat and rapid breathing
- Sunken eyes, dry shriveled skin
- Fainting or unconsciousness

WHAT TO DO:
- Mild to moderate: give water or oral rehydration solution in
  small, frequent sips, rest in the shade, and keep giving fluids.
- Call 911 or your local emergency number if the person is
  confused, has a seizure, loses consciousness, has a fever over
  102°F (38.9°C), shows signs of heat stroke, or gets worse
  despite fluids.

IF THERE ISN'T ENOUGH FOR EVERYONE
----------------------------------

This means you are in an emergency. Get help now: official
distribution points, shelters, neighbors, or 911 if anyone is in
danger. While you do, give water first to:
- Anyone showing signs of dehydration
- Infants and children
- People who are sick, elderly, pregnant, or nursing

FINDING MORE WATER
------------------

WHILE CONSERVING, LOOK FOR MORE:
- Official water distribution points (listen to local radio and
  emergency alerts)
- Water heater tank and household pipes
- Toilet tank (not the bowl) if no cleaners have been added
- Ice cubes and liquid from canned fruit and vegetables
- Rainwater (treat before drinking)
- Streams, lakes, and springs (always treat)
See the Finding Safe Water guide.

NEVER DRINK:
- Floodwater
- Pool or hot tub water (washing and cleaning only)
- Water from radiators, heating boilers, or waterbeds
- Toilet bowl water
- Water that smells of fuel or chemicals
