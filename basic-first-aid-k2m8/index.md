# Basic First Aid Without Power

> **Life-Saving Skills for Grid-Down Situations** - When hospitals are overwhelmed and power is out, knowing basic first aid can mean the difference between life and death. This guide covers essential techniques that require no electricity.

## The First Aid Mindset

In an emergency without power:
- You may be the only help available
- Professional medical care may be delayed hours or days
- Basic interventions save lives
- Stay calm—panic helps no one

**Remember:** You don't need to be a doctor. You need to know enough to stabilize someone until help arrives—or keep them alive if it doesn't.

---

## Scene Safety First

Before helping anyone:

1. **Stop and assess** - Is the scene safe?
2. **Identify hazards** - Downed power lines, fire, unstable structures, violence
3. **Protect yourself** - Use gloves, don't become a victim
4. **Only then** - Approach and help

**BSI (Body Substance Isolation):**
- Always assume blood/fluids are infectious
- Wear gloves if available
- Use barrier between you and patient's fluids
- Wash hands thoroughly after

## Primary Assessment: The ABCs

Check in this order—each step is more important than the next:

### A - Airway

**Is the airway open?**

Check:
- Is the person responsive?
- Can they speak?
- Are they choking?

If unconscious:
- Head-tilt, chin-lift to open airway
- Look, listen, feel for breathing
- Clear visible obstructions

### B - Breathing

**Are they breathing normally?**

Check:
- Watch chest rise and fall
- Listen for breath sounds
- Feel for air on your cheek

If not breathing:
- Begin rescue breathing (see CPR guide)
- Check for obstructions

### C - Circulation

**Is blood circulating? Is there severe bleeding?**

Check:
- Feel for pulse (neck or wrist)
- Check skin color and temperature
- Look for severe bleeding

If severe bleeding:
- Control bleeding immediately (see Bleeding Control guide)
- This takes priority over almost everything else

## Common Emergency Situations

### Unconscious but Breathing

**Recovery Position:**

1. Kneel beside person
2. Place arm nearest you at right angle
3. Bring far arm across chest, hold hand against cheek
4. Pull far knee up, keeping foot flat
5. Roll toward you onto their side
6. Tilt head back to keep airway open
7. Monitor breathing continuously

**Why:**
- Prevents choking on vomit or fluids
- Keeps airway open
- Allows monitoring

### Shock

**Recognition:**
- Pale, cold, clammy skin
- Rapid, weak pulse
- Rapid, shallow breathing
- Confusion or anxiety
- Weakness or dizziness

**Treatment:**
1. Lay person down
2. Elevate legs 8-12 inches (if no spinal injury suspected)
3. Keep warm with blankets
4. Do NOT give food or drink
5. Treat underlying cause if possible
6. Monitor and reassure

### Choking (Conscious Adult)

**Heimlich Maneuver:**

1. Stand behind person
2. Make a fist with one hand
3. Place fist just above navel
4. Grasp fist with other hand
5. Give quick, upward thrusts
6. Repeat until object expelled or person loses consciousness

**If they become unconscious:**
- Lower to ground
- Begin CPR (compressions may expel object)
- Check mouth for visible object before breaths

### Diabetic Emergency

**Low blood sugar (hypoglycemia):**
- Symptoms: Confusion, shakiness, sweating, irritability
- If conscious: Give sugar (juice, candy, glucose tablets)
- If unconscious: Do NOT give anything by mouth, call for help

**High blood sugar (hyperglycemia):**
- Symptoms: Thirst, frequent urination, confusion, fruity breath
- Requires professional medical attention
- Keep hydrated with water if conscious

### Seizure

**During seizure:**
1. Clear area of hazards
2. Protect head (place something soft underneath)
3. Do NOT restrain
4. Do NOT put anything in mouth
5. Time the seizure

**After seizure:**
1. Place in recovery position
2. Stay with them as they recover
3. Be calm and reassuring
4. Note how long seizure lasted

**Seek emergency help if:**
- Seizure lasts more than 5 minutes
- Person doesn't regain consciousness
- Another seizure follows quickly
- Person is injured
- First known seizure
- Person is pregnant

### Allergic Reaction / Anaphylaxis

**Mild reaction:**
- Hives, itching, minor swelling
- Give antihistamine (Benadryl) if available
- Monitor for worsening

**Severe reaction (anaphylaxis):**
- Difficulty breathing
- Swelling of throat/tongue
- Rapid pulse
- Dizziness

**Treatment:**
1. Use epinephrine auto-injector (EpiPen) if available
   - Remove cap
   - Press firmly against outer thigh (through clothing okay)
   - Hold 10 seconds
2. Call for emergency help
3. Have person lie down, elevate legs
4. Be prepared for second reaction
5. Give second EpiPen after 5-15 minutes if needed

## Wound Care Without Modern Supplies

### Basic Wound Cleaning

**Supplies needed:**
- Clean water (boiled and cooled if possible)
- Clean cloth
- Mild soap if available

**Procedure:**
1. Wash your hands
2. Stop bleeding with direct pressure
3. Rinse wound with clean water
4. Remove visible debris gently
5. Wash around wound with soap
6. Do NOT put soap directly in wound
7. Pat dry with clean cloth
8. Apply bandage

### Improvised Bandages

**If you don't have sterile gauze:**
- Clean cotton t-shirt (boiled if possible)
- Clean towels
- Sanitary pads (excellent for wounds)
- Coffee filters
- Clean pillowcase

**Securing bandages without tape:**
- Strips of cloth tied
- Belt or rope (for limbs)
- Safety pins
- Bandana tied around wound

### Wound Infection Signs

Watch for (usually appear 2-3 days after injury):
- Increasing pain
- Redness spreading from wound
- Swelling increasing
- Warmth around wound
- Pus or discharge
- Red streaks extending from wound
- Fever

**If infection develops:**
- Clean wound more frequently
- Keep elevated if possible
- Use any available antibiotics appropriately
- Seek professional medical help when available

## Pain Management Without Medication

### Non-Drug Pain Relief

**Cold therapy:**
- Reduces swelling and numbs area
- Use for first 48 hours after injury
- 20 minutes on, 20 minutes off
- Wrap ice in cloth (don't apply directly)

**Heat therapy:**
- Relaxes muscles, increases blood flow
- Use after 48 hours, or for muscle pain
- Warm water bottle wrapped in cloth
- Heated rice sock

**Elevation:**
- Raise injured area above heart
- Reduces swelling and pain
- Especially effective for limb injuries

**Immobilization:**
- Splinting prevents movement
- Reduces pain from fractures/sprains
- See Treating Burns, Cuts, and Fractures guide

**Distraction:**
- Talking, music, mental activities
- Especially helpful for children
- Reduces perception of pain

## Documentation

Keep records during extended emergencies:

**Track for each patient:**
- Name and age
- Time of injury/illness
- What happened
- Treatments given and times
- Vital signs if possible
- Response to treatment
- Any allergies or medications

**Why:**
- Helps when professional help arrives
- Tracks changes over time
- Documents care provided
- Aids in ongoing treatment decisions

## When You Cannot Get Professional Help

If professional medical care is truly unavailable:

**Prioritize:**
1. Life-threatening conditions first
2. Infections (can become life-threatening)
3. Pain management
4. Preventing complications

**Accept limitations:**
- You cannot treat everything
- Some conditions require surgery/advanced care
- Focus on what you CAN do
- Comfort care matters

**Seek help when possible:**
- Medical professionals in community
- Veterinarians (human physiology knowledge)
- Pharmacists
- Nurses, EMTs, medics
- Telemedicine when communications restored

---

## Key Takeaways

1. **Scene safety first** - Don't become another victim
2. **ABCs** - Airway, Breathing, Circulation
3. **Stop severe bleeding** - It kills quickly
4. **Keep wounds clean** - Infection is a major threat
5. **Monitor and reassess** - Conditions change
6. **Stay calm** - Your composure helps the patient
7. **Know your limits** - Seek help when available
