# Household Water Shutoff & Safety

> **Know Before You Need It** - Knowing how to shut off your home's water supply can prevent flood damage, allow repairs, and protect your family during emergencies.

## Why Water Shutoff Knowledge Matters

You may need to shut off water when:
- Pipe bursts or major leak
- Earthquake damages pipes
- Leaving home for extended period
- Freezing weather threatens pipes
- Water main break in neighborhood
- Contamination warning
- Before certain repairs

**Finding shutoffs in an emergency wastes precious time and water. Locate them now.**

---

## Types of Shutoffs

### Main Water Shutoff

**Location options:**
- Near water meter (often at street)
- Where water line enters house
- Basement or crawl space
- Utility closet
- Near water heater

### Individual Fixture Shutoffs

Most fixtures have dedicated shutoffs:
- Under sinks (look in cabinet)
- Behind toilets
- Under dishwasher
- Behind washing machine
- Near water heater

### Street Shutoff (Meter Box)

- Located at property line
- Usually requires special tool
- Used by utility company
- May require permission to operate

## Locating Your Main Shutoff

### Common Locations

**Single-family home:**
1. Follow pipe from water meter toward house
2. First valve after meter is usually main shutoff
3. Often in basement, crawl space, or near water heater

**Apartment/Condo:**
1. Under kitchen sink
2. In utility closet
3. In hallway access panel
4. Building may have main only (contact management)

**Mobile home:**
1. Near hose bib on exterior
2. Inside utility compartment
3. May be where supply line connects

## Types of Shutoff Valves

### Gate Valve

**Appearance:** Round wheel handle
**Operation:** Turn clockwise to close (many turns)
**Common issues:** Can seize if not used regularly

### Ball Valve

**Appearance:** Lever handle
**Operation:** Turn 90 degrees to close
**Best type:** Reliable, quick operation

### Globe Valve

**Appearance:** Round handle, bulbous body
**Operation:** Turn clockwise to close
**Use:** Flow regulation, can restrict without fully closing

### Curb Stop (Street)

**Appearance:** Pentagon or square nut
**Operation:** Requires curb key tool
**Location:** In meter box at street

## How to Shut Off Water

### Main Valve Shutdown

**Gate valve:**
1. Locate valve
2. Turn wheel clockwise
3. Keep turning until it stops
4. May take many turns (5-10+)
5. Don't over-tighten

**Ball valve:**
1. Locate valve
2. Grip lever handle
3. Turn 90 degrees
4. Perpendicular to pipe = closed
5. Parallel to pipe = open

### After Shutting Off

1. Turn on a faucet to release pressure
2. Start with highest faucet in house
3. Open lowest faucet to drain lines
4. This prevents pressure buildup
5. Now safe to make repairs

### Turning Water Back On

1. Close all faucets first
2. Slowly open main valve
3. Open valve completely (gate valves)
4. Turn on faucets one at a time
5. Flush until water runs clear
6. Check for leaks at repair site

## Emergency Water Shutoff Procedures

### Burst Pipe

**Immediately:**
1. Turn off main water shutoff
2. Turn off water heater (prevent damage)
3. Open faucets to drain pipes
4. Collect water in containers if possible
5. Locate break
6. Clean up water to prevent damage

### Flooding from Unknown Source

1. Shut off main water
2. If water continues, it's not supply side
3. Check drains/sewer backup
4. May need to check neighbor's property
5. Call utility if can't find source

### Before Extended Absence

**In cold weather:**
1. Shut off main water
2. Drain pipes (open faucets until dry)
3. Drain water heater (if extended absence)
4. Add antifreeze to drains/toilets
5. Leave heat on low (55°F minimum)

**In normal weather:**
1. Shut off main valve
2. Consider draining for very long absences
3. Leave a small drip if pipes are vulnerable

## Water Heater Shutoff

### Locating Controls

**Electric water heater:**
- Circuit breaker in electrical panel
- Sometimes dedicated shutoff near unit
- Cold water inlet valve on top

**Gas water heater:**
- Gas valve on gas supply line
- Cold water inlet valve on top
- Temperature dial (turn to "pilot" or "vacation")

### Emergency Shutdown

**Electric:**
1. Turn off breaker first
2. Then close water inlet valve

**Gas:**
1. Turn gas valve to OFF (perpendicular to pipe)
2. Close water inlet valve
3. If gas smell, leave immediately, call utility

### Using Water Heater as Emergency Supply

Your water heater holds 30-80 gallons:
1. Turn off power/gas
2. Close cold water inlet
3. Turn on hot water faucet to break vacuum
4. Connect hose to drain at bottom
5. Open drain valve
6. Collect in clean containers

## Preventing Water Damage

### High-Risk Areas

Monitor these locations:
- Under sinks
- Behind toilets
- At washing machine connections
- Around water heater
- Refrigerator ice maker line
- Dishwasher connections
- In basement/crawl space

### Preventive Measures

- Check supply lines annually
- Replace rubber hoses with braided steel
- Install water leak detectors
- Know shutoff locations
- Have basic repair supplies
- Insulate vulnerable pipes

### Emergency Kit

Keep near main shutoff:
- Flashlight
- Shutoff wrench (for main valve)
- Curb key (if applicable)
- Plumber's tape
- Pipe clamps
- Towels/rags
- Bucket
- Emergency repair patches

## Shutoff for Different Fixtures

### Toilet

**Location:** Behind toilet, near floor, usually chrome or plastic valve

**To shut off:**
1. Turn valve clockwise (righty-tighty)
2. Flush toilet to empty tank
3. Tank should not refill

### Sink

**Location:** Under sink in cabinet, two valves (hot and cold)

**To shut off:**
1. Turn both valves clockwise
2. Run faucet briefly to confirm
3. Water should stop flowing

### Washing Machine

**Location:** Behind or above machine, usually two valves with hoses attached

**To shut off:**
1. Turn both valves clockwise
2. Good practice: shut off when not in use
3. Prevents burst hose flooding

### Dishwasher

**Location:** Under sink, connected to hot water supply

**To shut off:**
1. Find dedicated valve or use sink hot water shutoff
2. May be at dishwasher connection point

## Valve Maintenance

### Annual Maintenance

**Exercise your valves:**
1. Turn main shutoff off and on
2. Do same for fixture shutoffs
3. Prevents seizing
4. Identifies stuck valves before emergency

### Signs of Valve Problems

- Handle won't turn
- Leaks around valve stem
- Won't fully close (water still flows)
- Corrosion visible

### If Valve is Stuck

**Do NOT force it - can break:**
1. Apply penetrating oil
2. Wait 15-30 minutes
3. Try gently again
4. May need professional replacement
