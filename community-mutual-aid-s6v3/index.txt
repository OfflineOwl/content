COMMUNITY MUTUAL AID BASICS
===========================

When professional help is overwhelmed or unavailable, neighbors 
helping neighbors can mean the difference between survival and tragedy.

WHAT IS MUTUAL AID?
-------------------

Mutual aid is:
- Neighbors helping neighbors
- Sharing resources and skills
- Coordinated community response
- Voluntary cooperation
- Based on solidarity, not charity

Why it matters:
- First responders may be overwhelmed
- Help may take days to arrive
- Community knows local needs
- Distributed resources are resilient
- Human connection aids recovery

BEFORE EMERGENCY
----------------

BUILD RELATIONSHIPS NOW:

Get to know neighbors:
- Introduce yourself
- Exchange contact information
- Learn about their situation
- Identify vulnerabilities
- Build trust before crisis

Identify resources:
- Who has medical training?
- Who has tools/equipment?
- Who has generators?
- Who has space for shelter?
- Who has specific skills?

Identify vulnerabilities:
- Elderly living alone
- Disabled individuals
- Families with infants
- People with medical needs
- Non-English speakers

Create contact list:
- Name and address
- Phone numbers
- Known vulnerabilities
- Skills/resources offered
- Special needs

ORGANIZING MUTUAL AID
---------------------

INFORMAL MUTUAL AID:
- Just neighbors helping
- No formal structure
- Spontaneous cooperation
- Works for small groups

ORGANIZED MUTUAL AID:
For larger areas or ongoing needs:

1. Communication coordinator
   - Collects and shares information
   - Maintains contact list
   - Organizes check-ins

2. Resource coordinator
   - Tracks available resources
   - Matches needs with resources
   - Manages sharing

3. Wellness checkers
   - Check on vulnerable neighbors
   - Report needs to coordinators
   - Provide companionship

4. Security/safety
   - Watch for hazards
   - Report problems
   - Organize patrols if needed

DURING EMERGENCY
----------------

IMMEDIATE ACTIONS:
1. Ensure your household is safe
2. Check on immediate neighbors
3. Report urgent needs
4. Share information
5. Offer what you can

CHECK-IN SYSTEM:
Establish regular check-ins:
- Visual signal = we're okay
- No signal = check on us
- Flag/ribbon system
- Timed rounds

RESOURCE SHARING:
Share what you can:
- Water
- Food
- Shelter
- Tools
- Transportation
- Communication
- Skills

COORDINATING HELP:
Match needs with resources:
- Who needs water?
- Who has extra?
- Who can transport?
- Who needs help with debris?
- Who has chainsaw skills?

PRACTICAL MUTUAL AID ACTIVITIES
-------------------------------

SHARING RESOURCES:
- Pool fuel for vehicles/generators
- Share cooking facilities
- Distribute water fairly
- Pool food resources
- Share tools and equipment

PRACTICAL HELP:
- Clear debris together
- Secure damaged homes
- Share transportation
- Provide childcare
- Cook communal meals

INFORMATION SHARING:
- Share emergency broadcasts
- Report road conditions
- Share official updates
- Coordinate with authorities
- Track missing persons

CARING FOR VULNERABLE:
- Regular wellness checks
- Deliver supplies to homebound
- Assist with medications
- Provide companionship
- Help with special needs

MAINTAINING TRUST
-----------------

PRINCIPLES:
- Everyone contributes what they can
- No one is left behind
- Fair distribution
- Respect for all
- Transparency

AVOID:
- Hoarding
- Exploitation
- Exclusion
- Rumor spreading
- Taking advantage

ADDRESS CONFLICTS:
- Listen to all sides
- Focus on needs, not personalities
- Find compromises
- Involve neutral party if needed
- Remember the shared goal

CONNECTING WITH FORMAL RESPONSE
-------------------------------

AS HELP ARRIVES:
- Report community status
- Share information about needs
- Coordinate with official response
- Don't duplicate efforts
- Direct resources to gaps

INFORMATION TO SHARE:
- Number of affected households
- Immediate needs
- Resources available
- Vulnerable individuals
- Access issues

AFTER EMERGENCY
---------------

CONTINUE SUPPORT:
Recovery takes time:
- Check on neighbors regularly
- Help with cleanup
- Share rebuilding resources
- Provide emotional support
- Celebrate milestones

LESSONS LEARNED:
- What worked well?
- What needs improvement?
- Who stepped up?
- What resources were lacking?
- How can we prepare better?

MAINTAIN CONNECTIONS:
- Keep contact lists current
- Have periodic gatherings
- Continue building relationships
- Update plans based on experience
