SHELTER-IN-PLACE PROCEDURES
============================

A quick-reference guide for sheltering safely when evacuation is not possible or recommended.

WHAT IS SHELTER-IN-PLACE?
-------------------------
Shelter-in-place means staying indoors and taking protective measures when
going outside would expose you to danger. This could be for:

- Severe weather (tornado, hurricane)
- Chemical or industrial accidents
- Active threat situations
- Airborne hazards
- Civil unrest

IMMEDIATE ACTIONS
-----------------
1. GET INSIDE
   - Move indoors immediately
   - Call family members to come home if safe to do so
   - Bring pets inside

2. SECURE THE BUILDING
   - Lock all doors and windows
   - Close all blinds and curtains
   - Move away from windows

3. ACCOUNT FOR EVERYONE
   - Verify all family members are present
   - Contact anyone who is not home
   - Establish their location and status

WEATHER-RELATED SHELTER
-----------------------
TORNADO:
- Go to basement or lowest floor
- If no basement: interior room, closet, or bathroom
- Stay away from windows
- Get under sturdy furniture
- Cover yourself with mattress or blankets
- Mobile homes: evacuate to sturdy building

HURRICANE:
- Stay indoors throughout the storm
- Move to interior room during worst winds
- Stay away from windows and glass doors
- Do NOT go outside during the eye
- Wait for official all-clear

SEVERE THUNDERSTORM:
- Stay indoors
- Avoid water and plumbing
- Stay off corded phones
- Unplug sensitive electronics
- Stay away from windows

CHEMICAL/HAZMAT SHELTER
-----------------------
If authorities warn of chemical, biological, or radiological threat:

SEAL THE ROOM:
1. Choose interior room with few windows
2. Bring supplies: water, radio, phone, first aid
3. Shut off HVAC systems (if possible)
4. Close fireplace dampers
5. Seal door gaps with wet towels
6. Cover windows with plastic sheeting and tape
7. Seal electrical outlets and vents with tape
8. Turn off fans and air conditioners

ROOM PRIORITIES:
- Interior room preferred
- Above ground (chemicals sink)
- Access to bathroom if possible
- Enough space for all occupants

IMPORTANT: 
- A sealed room has limited air
- Do not seal for more than 2-3 hours
- Listen to radio for all-clear
- Be ready to ventilate or evacuate when safe

ACTIVE THREAT SHELTER
---------------------
RUN-HIDE-FIGHT protocol (as last resort):

IF YOU CANNOT RUN:
1. Find a room that locks
2. Lock and barricade the door
3. Turn off lights
4. Silence phones (not off - you may need to text)
5. Hide behind large objects
6. Stay away from doors and windows
7. Remain silent
8. Call 911 when safe to do so
9. Do not open door until you verify it's law enforcement

COMMUNICATION DURING SHELTER
----------------------------
- Designate one person to monitor radio/news
- Text rather than call (preserves battery, reduces noise)
- Check in with out-of-area contact
- Post status on social media check-in features (if safe)
- Keep phone charged and on silent

SUPPLY NEEDS FOR SHELTER-IN-PLACE
---------------------------------
Per person, per day:
- Water: 1 gallon
- Food: non-perishable, no cooking required
- Medications: any daily prescriptions
- First aid kit
- Flashlight and batteries
- Battery or hand-crank radio
- Phone charger
- Sanitation supplies
- Entertainment for children

SHELTER-IN-PLACE DURATION
-------------------------
Short-term (hours):
- Severe weather
- Most chemical incidents
- Active threats

Medium-term (days):
- Extended power outages
- Civil unrest
- Pandemic lockdowns

Long-term (weeks):
- Major disasters
- Quarantine situations
- Infrastructure collapse

WHEN TO END SHELTER-IN-PLACE
----------------------------
End shelter only when:
- Official all-clear is announced
- Immediate danger has clearly passed
- Authorities advise it is safe
- You must evacuate due to building damage

DO NOT end shelter because:
- It has been quiet for a while
- You assume the danger is over
- You are bored or uncomfortable

MISTAKES TO AVOID
-----------------
- Leaving to check on things
- Opening doors/windows prematurely
- Running out of water (dehydration risk)
- Panic and poor decisions
- Using candles near sealed plastic
- Sealing room too tightly for too long
- Ignoring official communications

SPECIAL CONSIDERATIONS
----------------------
CHILDREN:
- Keep them calm with activities
- Explain situation age-appropriately
- Maintain routines where possible
- Stay close for reassurance

ELDERLY:
- Ensure medications are accessible
- Check on regularly
- Help with mobility needs
- Monitor for stress/confusion

PETS:
- Keep contained for their safety
- Have food and water available
- Walk on leash only (if necessary)
- Have carrier ready if evacuation needed

MEDICAL NEEDS:
- Ensure power for medical devices
- Extra batteries for hearing aids
- Accessible medications
- Know when to seek help despite shelter
