SHELTER-IN-PLACE PROCEDURES
============================

Shelter-in-place means staying inside, or getting inside, because
going out would be more dangerous. WHERE you shelter depends on
the danger. The wrong room can make things worse.

============================================================
DO THIS NOW
============================================================

1. GET INSIDE. Bring everyone and pets in. Take your emergency
   kit, phone, and charger if they're within reach.
2. CLOSE UP. Shut and lock doors and windows. Close fireplace
   dampers. Turn off fans, air conditioning, and forced-air
   heating.
3. PICK THE RIGHT ROOM FOR THE DANGER:

   +------------------+---------------------------+-----------+
   | DANGER           | WHERE TO GO               | SEAL IT?  |
   +------------------+---------------------------+-----------+
   | CHEMICAL release | Interior room ABOVE       | YES       |
   | (gas cloud,      | ground, few or no         | plastic + |
   | hazmat, strong   | windows. NOT the          | duct tape |
   | chemical smell)  | basement.                 |           |
   +------------------+---------------------------+-----------+
   | RADIATION        | BASEMENT or CENTER of the | NO need.  |
   | (nuclear blast,  | building, away from roof  | Just      |
   | fallout, dirty   | and outer walls.          | close up. |
   | bomb, nuclear    |                           |           |
   | plant release)   |                           |           |
   +------------------+---------------------------+-----------+
   | TORNADO /        | Basement, safe room, or   | NO        |
   | high wind        | small interior room on    |           |
   |                  | the LOWEST floor          |           |
   +------------------+---------------------------+-----------+
   | ACTIVE THREAT    | Room that locks, out of   | NO        |
   | (attacker)       | sight (RUN first if safe) |           |
   +------------------+---------------------------+-----------+

   NOT SURE what the danger is? Go to an interior room, close up,
   turn on the radio, and move once officials say what it is.

4. ACCOUNT FOR EVERYONE. Text family members who aren't home.
   Don't go out to get them; schools and workplaces have their
   own shelter plans.
5. STAY TUNED. Battery or hand-crank radio, phone alerts, TV.
6. STAY INSIDE until officials give the all-clear or tell you to
   evacuate.

CALL 911 (or your local emergency number) if someone has trouble
breathing, chest pain, seizures, burns, or serious injuries.

============================================================
WHY CHEMICAL AND RADIATION ARE OPPOSITE
============================================================

CHEMICAL RELEASE: go UP and SEAL.
Many toxic gases, including chlorine and sulfur mustard, are
heavier than air. They sink and collect in low areas, so they can
seep into a basement even with the windows closed. A sealed room
above ground keeps the most contaminated air away from you.

RADIATION: go DOWN or to the CENTER. No sealing needed.
Fallout is radioactive dust that settles on the roof and ground.
The danger is the radiation it gives off, which passes through
walls. Concrete, brick, and earth block it. A basement or the
middle of a large building puts the most mass and distance
between you and the fallout. Buildings don't need to be airtight
to protect you, so don't waste time taping. Close up and move.

============================================================
CHEMICAL RELEASE: SEALED ROOM
============================================================

Use when officials say to shelter from a chemical release, or
you smell a strong chemical or see a cloud and can't get away.
Some toxic chemicals have no smell, so follow official
instructions even if you smell nothing.

IF YOU'RE OUTSIDE: move away, upwind of the source, and get into
the nearest building. In a car, close the windows and vents and
turn off the air conditioner and heater, then get inside.

CHOOSE THE ROOM:
- Interior room with as few windows and openings as possible
- Above ground level (the higher, the better). NOT the basement.
- Large enough for everyone: about 10 square feet of floor space
  per person gives enough air for up to about 5 hours at rest
- A bathroom or water source is a plus

SEAL IT:
1. Bring supplies in: water, radio, phone and charger, first aid
   kit, medications, flashlight.
2. Close vents, fireplace dampers, and as many interior doors
   as possible. HVAC off (or set to recirculate if you can't turn
   it off).
3. Tape plastic sheeting over windows, vents, and the door. Tape
   the corners first, then all edges.
4. Stuff wet towels in the gap under the door.
5. Tape over outlets, switch plates, and other gaps.
6. No plastic? Use towels, sheets, clothing, wax paper, or foil.
7. If gas or vapor may have gotten inside, take shallow breaths
   through a cloth or towel.
8. Don't eat or drink anything that may be contaminated.

HOW LONG:
- Sealed rooms are meant for short chemical releases: usually a
  few hours.
- Outside air slowly leaks in, so protection drops with time.
- Officials will tell you when to unseal, or to evacuate if the
  release goes on.
- If someone has trouble breathing, call 911.

AFTER THE ALL-CLEAR:
- Open windows and doors, turn ventilation back on, and air out
  the building. Go outside if officials say to.
- If you were exposed, take off your clothes, bag them, and wash
  with soap and water (see the Decontamination Basics guide).
  Call Poison Control (1-800-222-1222 in the US) or 911 if you
  have symptoms.

============================================================
RADIATION: BASEMENT OR CENTER
============================================================

1. Get inside a sturdy building. Brick or concrete is best. A car
   is not a shelter.
2. Go to the basement, or the center of the building away from
   windows, outer walls, and the roof. In a tall building, go to
   the center of a middle floor.
3. If you were outside, take off your outer layer of clothing
   before joining others (up to 90% of the contamination comes
   off with it). Bag it. Shower with soap and shampoo, no
   conditioner.
4. Close windows, doors, and dampers. Turn off ventilation that
   pulls in outside air. Taping is not needed.
5. Stay inside at least 24 hours unless officials say otherwise.
6. Take potassium iodide (KI) ONLY if officials tell you to.

See the Nuclear Fallout Protection guide for details.

============================================================
WEATHER
============================================================

TORNADO:
- Go to a basement, safe room, or storm shelter. If none, a small
  interior room (closet, bathroom, hallway) on the lowest floor.
- Stay away from windows, doors, and outside walls.
- Cover your head and neck with your arms. Put blankets,
  cushions, or a mattress around or over you.
- Mobile homes, sheds, tents, and vehicles are NOT safe. Go to a
  sturdy building before the tornado arrives.

HURRICANE:
- Stay indoors through the storm. Follow evacuation orders.
- During the worst winds, go to an interior room away from
  windows and glass doors.
- Do NOT go outside during the eye. Winds return suddenly from
  the other direction.
- Wait for the official all-clear.

SEVERE THUNDERSTORM:
- Stay indoors until the storm has passed.
- Avoid showers, baths, and other plumbing.
- Stay off corded phones.
- Stay away from windows.

============================================================
ACTIVE THREAT: RUN, HIDE, FIGHT
============================================================

1. RUN if you can do it safely. Leave belongings behind. Keep
   your hands visible and empty.
2. HIDE if you can't get away:
   - Get out of the attacker's view, behind something solid.
   - Lock and block the door. Close blinds. Turn off lights.
   - Silence your phone (don't turn it off; you may need to
     text) and stay quiet.
3. FIGHT only as a last resort, when your life is in immediate
   danger.
- Call or text 911 when it's safe.
- When police arrive: stay calm, keep hands visible and empty,
  follow their instructions.

============================================================
COMMUNICATION DURING SHELTER
============================================================

- Text instead of calling. Texts often get through when calls
  don't, and they keep lines free for emergencies.
- Check in with your out-of-town contact.
- One person monitors the radio or alerts.
- Keep phones charged. Don't use airplane mode while you're
  waiting for alerts; phones in airplane mode don't get Wireless
  Emergency Alerts.
- Never charge a phone in a car in a closed garage (carbon
  monoxide).

============================================================
SUPPLIES
============================================================

Keep these where you'd shelter:
- Water: 1 gallon per person per day
- Food that needs no cooking, and a manual can opener
- Medications and medical supplies
- First aid kit
- Flashlight and extra batteries
- Battery or hand-crank radio (NOAA Weather Radio if possible)
- Phone charger and backup battery
- Plastic sheeting, duct tape, and scissors (chemical sealing)
- Dust masks
- Sanitation supplies: moist towelettes, garbage bags, plastic
  ties
- Pet food and water
- Things to keep children busy

============================================================
HOW LONG SHELTERING LASTS
============================================================

- Sealed room (chemical): usually a few hours
- Radiation: at least 24 hours, sometimes longer
- Tornado or storm: until the warning ends
- Other shelter-in-place orders: a few hours, possibly days

Officials decide. Plan for longer than you expect.

============================================================
WHEN TO END SHELTER
============================================================

End it when:
- Officials announce the all-clear, or
- Officials tell you to evacuate, or
- Staying is more dangerous: fire, gas leak, or the building is
  collapsing

DON'T end it because:
- It's been quiet for a while
- You think the danger has passed
- You're bored or uncomfortable

============================================================
MISTAKES TO AVOID
============================================================

- Going to the BASEMENT for a CHEMICAL release (gases sink)
- Going UPSTAIRS or near the roof for RADIATION
- Wasting time taping windows during a nuclear emergency instead
  of getting to the basement or center
- Leaving to check on things or collect family
- Opening doors or windows before the all-clear
- Using candles near plastic sheeting
- Running generators, grills, or camp stoves indoors (carbon
  monoxide)
- Ignoring official instructions

============================================================
SPECIAL CONSIDERATIONS
============================================================

CHILDREN:
- Keep them close and busy
- Explain what's happening simply and calmly
- Keep routines where you can

OLDER ADULTS AND PEOPLE WITH DISABILITIES:
- Move them to the shelter room first
- Keep medications, glasses, hearing aids, and mobility aids in
  reach
- Check on them often and watch for confusion or distress

PETS:
- Bring them inside and keep them contained
- Have food, water, and a carrier ready in case you evacuate

MEDICAL NEEDS:
- Spare batteries or a power plan for medical devices
- If a medical emergency happens, call 911 even during shelter
