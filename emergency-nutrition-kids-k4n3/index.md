# Emergency Nutrition for Children

> **Growing Bodies Need Proper Fuel** - Children have unique nutritional needs, especially during the stress of emergencies. This guide helps ensure kids get what they need when normal food access is disrupted.

## Why Children's Nutrition is Different

Children are more vulnerable because:
- Higher metabolic rates (need more calories per pound)
- Bodies still developing (need specific nutrients)
- Less body reserves than adults
- More susceptible to dehydration
- May refuse unfamiliar foods
- Emotional stress affects eating

## Calorie Needs by Age

| Age | Daily Calories | Notes |
|-----|---------------|-------|
| Infants (0-6 mo) | 550-650 | Breast milk/formula only |
| Infants (6-12 mo) | 700-850 | Plus solid foods |
| Toddlers (1-3) | 1,000-1,400 | Active period |
| Children (4-8) | 1,200-2,000 | Varies by activity |
| Children (9-13) | 1,600-2,600 | Growth spurt years |
| Teens (14-18) | 1,800-3,200 | Highest needs |

**During emergencies:** Stress and activity may increase needs.

## Infant Feeding

### Breastfeeding

**Best option during emergencies:**
- Always available
- No preparation needed
- No contamination risk
- Provides immune support
- Continue if possible

**Mother's needs:**
- Extra water (1+ additional liter/day)
- Extra calories (500+)
- Stress management
- Rest when possible

### Formula Feeding

**Priority storage items:**
- Ready-to-feed formula (safest—no water needed)
- Powdered formula (requires clean water)
- Clean bottles and nipples
- Bottle brush

**Safety concerns:**
- Water must be safe
- Preparation must be clean
- Don't dilute formula
- Use within time limits once prepared
- Ready-to-feed is safest option

### Introducing Solids (6+ months)

If emergency occurs during weaning:
- Continue breast milk/formula as primary
- Single-ingredient baby foods
- Soft, mashed table foods
- Avoid choking hazards

## Toddler and Preschool Nutrition

### Key Nutrients

| Nutrient | Why Important | Sources |
|----------|--------------|---------|
| Protein | Growth, immune function | Peanut butter, canned meat, beans |
| Calcium | Bone development | Powdered milk, shelf-stable milk |
| Iron | Brain development | Fortified cereals, beans |
| Vitamin C | Immunity, iron absorption | Canned fruits, juice |
| Vitamin A | Vision, immunity | Canned carrots, sweet potatoes |
| Healthy fats | Brain development | Nuts, peanut butter |

### Kid-Friendly Emergency Foods

**Stock these:**
- Peanut butter (high calorie, protein)
- Crackers (familiar, energy)
- Canned fruits (vitamins, familiar taste)
- Applesauce cups (easy to eat)
- Cereal (fortified, familiar)
- Shelf-stable milk
- Pudding cups
- Cheese and crackers
- Graham crackers
- Goldfish crackers
- Granola bars (watch sugar)
- Fruit snacks
- Juice boxes
- Mac and cheese cups

### Picky Eaters

During emergencies, food pickiness often worsens:
- Offer familiar foods when possible
- Don't force unfamiliar foods
- Make foods as normal-looking as possible
- Let children help with preparation
- Eat together as family
- Keep mealtimes calm
- Small portions, offer seconds

## School-Age Children

### Balancing Nutrition

Aim for:
- Protein at each meal
- Fruits or vegetables daily
- Whole grains when possible
- Adequate fluids
- Limited sugar (affects behavior)

### Involving Children

- Let them choose from options
- Teach them about nutrition
- Have them help with meal prep
- Give age-appropriate tasks
- Make it a game or learning experience

### School-Age Portions

| Food | Portion Guide |
|------|--------------|
| Meat/protein | Palm-sized |
| Grains | Fist-sized |
| Vegetables | Fist-sized |
| Fruit | Fist-sized |
| Dairy | 1 cup |

## Teenagers

### Unique Needs

- Highest calorie needs of any age
- Rapid growth requires nutrients
- Often hungry frequently
- May be picky or restrictive
- Body image concerns
- Need autonomy

### Keeping Teens Fed

- Stock larger quantities
- Include substantial snacks
- High-calorie options important
- Protein for muscle development
- Don't restrict portions

## Hydration for Children

### Water Needs

| Age | Daily Water Need |
|-----|-----------------|
| 0-6 months | From breast milk/formula |
| 6-12 months | 4-8 oz (plus breast milk/formula) |
| 1-3 years | 4 cups |
| 4-8 years | 5 cups |
| 9-13 years | 7-8 cups |
| 14-18 years | 8-11 cups |

### Signs of Dehydration in Children

**Mild:**
- Thirst
- Dry lips and mouth
- Darker urine
- Less urination

**Moderate:**
- Sunken eyes
- Few or no tears
- Very dry mouth
- Irritability or lethargy
- Little urine output

**Severe (Emergency):**
- Very sunken eyes
- No urine for 6-8 hours
- Cold, mottled extremities
- Difficult to wake
- No tears when crying

### Encouraging Fluid Intake

- Offer water frequently
- Make it a game
- Use fun cups or straws
- Flavor with small amount of juice
- Popsicles count
- Fruit with high water content
- Soup and broth

## Special Dietary Needs

### Food Allergies

**Stock allergen-free versions:**
- Know your child's allergens
- Read all labels carefully
- Have safe alternatives
- EpiPen if prescribed (check expiration)
- Medical information readily available

### Diabetes

- Consistent carbohydrate intake
- Regular meal times when possible
- Glucose tablets or gel
- Blood sugar monitoring
- Extra supplies of insulin/medications

### Other Medical Conditions

- Stock appropriate foods
- Have medical information written
- Extra medications
- Know emergency protocols

---

## Emotional Eating in Emergencies

### Normal Responses

Children may:
- Refuse to eat
- Overeat (comfort)
- Want only certain foods
- Regress in eating behaviors
- Have stomach aches from stress

### Helpful Approaches

- Keep mealtimes calm
- Maintain routines when possible
- Don't force eating
- Offer favorite comfort foods
- Eat together
- Avoid using food as reward/punishment
- Be patient with regression

## Sample Emergency Menu for Children

### Day 1

**Breakfast:** Cereal with shelf-stable milk, juice
**Snack:** Crackers with peanut butter
**Lunch:** Peanut butter and jelly, fruit cup, milk
**Snack:** Granola bar
**Dinner:** Canned ravioli, canned green beans, applesauce

### Day 2

**Breakfast:** Oatmeal (instant) with dried fruit
**Snack:** Cheese and crackers
**Lunch:** Canned chicken on crackers, fruit cup
**Snack:** Trail mix
**Dinner:** Mac and cheese, canned carrots, pudding cup

### Day 3

**Breakfast:** Granola with milk, juice
**Snack:** Apple slices with peanut butter
**Lunch:** Tuna salad on bread, pretzels, fruit
**Snack:** Yogurt (shelf-stable)
**Dinner:** Rice and canned beans, canned corn, fruit
