MANAGING CHRONIC CONDITIONS IN EMERGENCIES
==========================================

When regular medical care is unavailable, managing chronic health 
conditions becomes critical. This guide covers preparedness and
emergency management for common conditions.

GENERAL PREPARATION
-------------------

FOR ALL CHRONIC CONDITIONS:

1. MEDICATION SUPPLY
   - Maintain 30-day minimum supply
   - Ideally 90-day supply
   - Know generic names of all medications
   - Store properly (temperature, humidity)
   - Check expiration dates quarterly
   - Rotate stock (use oldest first)

2. DOCUMENTATION
   - List of all medications with doses
   - Prescribing physician information
   - Pharmacy information
   - Diagnosis list
   - Recent lab values
   - Allergies
   - Emergency contacts

3. EQUIPMENT
   - Backup supplies for monitoring devices
   - Extra batteries
   - Backup power options
   - Manual alternatives when possible

4. KNOWLEDGE
   - Understand your condition
   - Know warning signs of complications
   - Know when to seek emergency care
   - Have a contingency plan

DIABETES
--------

PREPARATION:
- 30-90 day supply of medication
- Extra glucose meter and test strips
- Backup insulin storage (if applicable)
- Glucose tablets/gel
- Glucagon kit
- Medical ID bracelet
- Sharps container
- Alcohol wipes

EMERGENCY MANAGEMENT:

Blood Sugar Monitoring:
- Monitor more frequently during stress
- Illness and stress raise blood sugar
- Document readings

If Running Low on Insulin:
- Ration carefully
- Focus on preventing ketoacidosis
- Reduce carbohydrate intake
- Stay hydrated
- Monitor closely for DKA symptoms
- Seek medical help ASAP

Low Blood Sugar (Hypoglycemia):
Symptoms: Shakiness, sweating, confusion, irritability, hunger
Treatment:
1. Give 15-20g fast-acting sugar
   - Glucose tablets (3-4)
   - Juice (4 oz)
   - Regular soda (4 oz)
   - Hard candy
2. Wait 15 minutes
3. Recheck blood sugar
4. Repeat if still low
5. Follow with protein/complex carb

High Blood Sugar (Hyperglycemia):
Symptoms: Thirst, frequent urination, fatigue, blurred vision
Treatment:
1. Take prescribed medication
2. Stay hydrated (water)
3. Monitor for DKA
4. Seek medical care if very high

Diabetic Ketoacidosis (DKA) - EMERGENCY:
Symptoms: Fruity breath, nausea/vomiting, abdominal pain,
         rapid breathing, confusion
Action: Seek emergency medical care immediately

HEART DISEASE / HYPERTENSION
----------------------------

PREPARATION:
- Medication supply (blood pressure, cholesterol, blood thinners)
- Blood pressure monitor with batteries
- Aspirin (unless contraindicated)
- Nitroglycerin if prescribed
- Low-sodium food options
- Medical ID

EMERGENCY MANAGEMENT:

Daily Management:
- Monitor blood pressure if possible
- Take medications consistently
- Limit sodium intake
- Stay as active as possible
- Manage stress

If Running Low on Medication:
- Prioritize blood pressure medications
- Monitor BP closely
- Reduce sodium strictly
- Reduce physical exertion
- Seek medication access urgently

Warning Signs - Heart Attack:
- Chest pain/pressure
- Pain in arm, jaw, neck, back
- Shortness of breath
- Cold sweats
- Nausea

Action:
1. Chew aspirin (325mg) if not allergic
2. Nitroglycerin if prescribed
3. Sit or lie down
4. Seek emergency care immediately

Warning Signs - Stroke (FAST):
F - Face drooping
A - Arm weakness
S - Speech difficulty
T - Time to call emergency

Action: Seek emergency care immediately

ASTHMA / COPD
-------------

PREPARATION:
- Rescue inhaler (albuterol) - multiple
- Maintenance inhalers
- Peak flow meter
- Nebulizer with backup power option
- Spacer
- Prednisone course (if prescribed for emergencies)
- N95 masks
- Air purifier if power available

EMERGENCY MANAGEMENT:

Asthma Attack:
1. Sit upright
2. Stay calm
3. Use rescue inhaler (2 puffs)
4. Wait 1 minute
5. Repeat up to 3 times if needed
6. Seek emergency care if not improving

Severe Attack Signs - EMERGENCY:
- Rescue inhaler not helping
- Can't speak in full sentences
- Lips turning blue
- Using accessory muscles to breathe
- Getting worse despite treatment

Environmental Management:
- Avoid smoke/air pollution
- Stay indoors during poor air quality
- Use N95 mask if must go out
- Identify and avoid triggers

EPILEPSY
--------

PREPARATION:
- Anti-seizure medication supply
- Diastat (rectal diazepam) if prescribed
- Soft padding/pillow
- Medical ID
- Seizure diary/documentation

EMERGENCY MANAGEMENT:

During a Seizure (for caregivers):
1. Clear area of hazards
2. Protect head (pillow underneath)
3. Do NOT restrain
4. Do NOT put anything in mouth
5. Turn on side after convulsions stop
6. Time the seizure
7. Stay until fully recovered

Seek Emergency Care If:
- Seizure lasts more than 5 minutes
- Second seizure follows quickly
- Person doesn't regain consciousness
- Person is injured
- Person is pregnant
- First known seizure

If Running Low on Medication:
- Do NOT stop suddenly (can cause severe seizures)
- Taper very slowly if must reduce
- Seek medication access urgently
- Avoid triggers (lack of sleep, alcohol)

KIDNEY DISEASE
--------------

PREPARATION:
- Medication supply
- Dialysis supplies if applicable
- Low sodium/potassium/phosphorus foods
- Emergency dialysis plan
- Fluid restriction supplies

EMERGENCY MANAGEMENT:

Dietary Restrictions:
- Strictly limit potassium if kidney failure
  (bananas, oranges, potatoes, tomatoes)
- Limit sodium
- Limit phosphorus
- Monitor fluid intake

Warning Signs - EMERGENCY:
- Severe shortness of breath
- Severe swelling
- Chest pain
- Confusion
- Dangerous blood pressure

Dialysis Patients:
- Know your dialysis center's emergency plan
- Have backup facility information
- Know emergency dialysis access points
- Have emergency dialysis kit if peritoneal

MENTAL HEALTH CONDITIONS
------------------------

PREPARATION:
- Medication supply (critical)
- Written crisis plan
- Support contacts list
- Calming items/activities
- Documentation of medications/doses
- Therapist/psychiatrist contact info

EMERGENCY MANAGEMENT:

Medication Continuity:
- Do NOT stop psychiatric medications suddenly
- Withdrawal can be dangerous
- If rationing needed, consult professional
- Some medications need slow tapering

Managing Increased Stress:
- Maintain routines as much as possible
- Connect with support system
- Use coping strategies you've learned
- Physical activity helps
- Limit news/stress exposure
- Get adequate sleep

Crisis Warning Signs:
- Thoughts of self-harm
- Severe anxiety/panic
- Hearing/seeing things
- Unable to function
- Feeling out of control

Crisis Resources:
- 988 Suicide & Crisis Lifeline
- Crisis text line: Text HOME to 741741
- Local emergency services

GENERAL EMERGENCY TIPS
----------------------

MEDICATION RATIONING:
- Consult medical professional if possible
- Prioritize life-sustaining medications
- Understand which medications are most critical
- Know which can be stretched vs. which cannot
- Document any changes in dosing

SEEKING CARE:
- Community health centers
- Hospital emergency departments
- Evacuation shelters with medical services
- Veterinarians (human physiology knowledge)
- Pharmacists (medication advice)
- Telehealth when communications available
