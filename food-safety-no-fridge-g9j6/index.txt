FOOD SAFETY WITHOUT REFRIGERATION
==================================

When the power goes out, your refrigerator becomes a race against time.
This guide covers how to keep food safe and when to throw it out.

THE TEMPERATURE DANGER ZONE
---------------------------

DANGER ZONE: 40°F - 140°F (4°C - 60°C)

In this range, bacteria grow rapidly.
Food in the danger zone for more than 2 hours should be discarded.
(1 hour if ambient temperature is above 90°F)

REFRIGERATOR TIMELINE
---------------------

POWER OUT - REFRIGERATOR:
- Keep door CLOSED
- Maintains cold 4 hours (if full)
- Maintains cold 2 hours (if half-full)
- After this, food enters danger zone

POWER OUT - FREEZER:
- Keep door CLOSED
- Full freezer: 48 hours
- Half-full freezer: 24 hours
- After this, thawing begins

EXTENDING REFRIGERATOR LIFE:
- Fill empty space with water containers
- Add ice if available
- Open door only when necessary
- Group items you need together
- Use coolers for frequently accessed items

WHEN TO DISCARD REFRIGERATED FOOD
---------------------------------

DISCARD AFTER 4+ HOURS ABOVE 40°F:

Meat and Poultry:
- Raw or cooked meat
- Raw or cooked poultry
- Deli meats/hot dogs
- Bacon, sausage
- Gravy, stuffing

Fish and Seafood:
- All fresh fish
- Shellfish
- Cooked seafood

Dairy:
- Milk
- Cream
- Soft cheeses
- Shredded cheese
- Yogurt
- Sour cream

Eggs:
- Fresh eggs (if not in shell)
- Egg dishes
- Egg substitutes (opened)

Other:
- Cut fruits/vegetables
- Opened baby food
- Leftover cooked foods
- Cream-based dishes
- Pizza with meat toppings
- Casseroles, soups, stews

FOODS THAT CAN BE KEPT (if no signs of spoilage):

Condiments:
- Ketchup, mustard
- Mayonnaise (evaluate)
- Pickles, relish
- Soy sauce
- Vinegar-based dressings
- Worcestershire sauce
- Hot sauce

Produce:
- Fresh fruits (whole)
- Fresh vegetables (whole)
- Dried fruits

Other:
- Hard cheeses (Parmesan, cheddar)
- Processed cheese
- Butter, margarine
- Fresh bread, rolls
- Peanut butter
- Jams, jellies
- Uncut raw vegetables

FREEZER FOOD RULES
------------------

FOOD STILL CONTAINS ICE CRYSTALS OR IS 40°F OR BELOW:
- Can be refrozen
- Quality may decrease
- Safe to cook and eat

FOOD THAWED AND HELD ABOVE 40°F FOR OVER 2 HOURS:
- Do not refreeze
- Cook immediately or discard
- When in doubt, throw it out

CAN REFREEZE (if ice crystals present):
- Fruits
- Vegetables
- Breads
- Nuts

COOK OR DISCARD (do not refreeze):
- Meats, poultry, seafood
- Ice cream
- Casseroles
- Pies with filling
- Prepared meals

ASSESSING FOOD SAFETY
---------------------

DO NOT RELY ON:
- Smell alone (some bacteria don't cause odor)
- Appearance alone (contamination can be invisible)
- Taste (dangerous - don't taste questionable food)

USE:
- Temperature (food thermometer is best)
- Time at temperature
- Visual signs of spoilage
- The 2-hour rule

WHEN IN DOUBT, THROW IT OUT
This is the golden rule of food safety.

COOLER STRATEGIES
-----------------

IF POWER WILL BE OUT FOR EXTENDED PERIOD:

1. Move critical items to cooler
2. Add ice (block ice lasts longer than cubes)
3. Fill empty space with frozen water bottles
4. Keep cooler in coolest location
5. Keep cooler closed as much as possible
6. Drain water as ice melts (unless it's keeping items cold)
7. Keep thermometer in cooler

ICE MANAGEMENT:
- Block ice outlasts cubed ice
- Freeze water bottles ahead of time
- Dry ice extends cold significantly
  - Handle with gloves (burns skin)
  - Ensure ventilation (releases CO2)

PRIORITY FOR COOLING:
1. Medications that need refrigeration
2. Breast milk/formula
3. Meats and dairy
4. Leftovers and prepared foods
5. Condiments last (usually safer)

SHELF-STABLE ALTERNATIVES
-------------------------

INSTEAD OF REFRIGERATED, STOCK:

Instead of:          Stock this:
Fresh milk           Shelf-stable milk, powdered milk
Fresh eggs           Powdered eggs (for cooking)
Fresh meat           Canned meat, jerky
Lunch meat           Canned tuna/chicken
Fresh vegetables     Canned/dried vegetables
Cheese               Shelf-stable cheese, Parmesan
Butter               Shelf-stable butter, oil

FOOD PRESERVATION WITHOUT ELECTRICITY
-------------------------------------

SHORT-TERM (hours to days):
- Keep refrigerator/freezer closed
- Use coolers with ice
- Store in coolest area of home
- Cook and eat perishables first

MEDIUM-TERM (days):
- Salt-cure meats
- Smoke meats (if possible)
- Make jerky
- Pickle vegetables
- Rely on shelf-stable foods

LONG-TERM (if extended outage):
- See food preservation guide
- Focus on shelf-stable supplies
- Consider root cellar/cold storage

FOOD TEMPERATURE GUIDE
----------------------

REFRIGERATED (below 40°F):
Perishables, dairy, meats, eggs

FREEZER (0°F or below):
Frozen foods, ice cream, meats for long-term

ROOM TEMPERATURE (50-70°F acceptable):
Canned goods, dry goods, most produce

DANGER ZONE (40-140°F):
Bacteria multiply rapidly
Maximum 2 hours for food safety

COOKING TEMPERATURES:
Ground meat: 160°F
Poultry: 165°F
Pork: 145°F
Fish: 145°F
Eggs: 160°F (if not immediately served)

QUICK REFERENCE
---------------

FRIDGE WITHOUT POWER:
- Full: Safe 4 hours
- Half-full: Safe 2 hours
- Keep closed!

FREEZER WITHOUT POWER:
- Full: Safe 48 hours
- Half-full: Safe 24 hours
- Keep closed!

2-HOUR RULE:
Food above 40°F for more than 2 hours = discard

WHEN IN DOUBT:
Throw it out!
