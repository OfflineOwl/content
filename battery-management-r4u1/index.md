# Battery Management & Rotation

> **Power When You Need It** - Batteries are the backbone of emergency preparedness. Proper storage, rotation, and management ensures they work when you need them most.

## Battery Types Overview

### Disposable (Primary) Batteries

| Type | Voltage | Shelf Life | Best For |
|------|---------|-----------|----------|
| Alkaline | 1.5V | 5-10 years | General use |
| Lithium | 1.5V | 15-20 years | Long-term storage, extreme temps |
| Zinc-carbon | 1.5V | 1-3 years | Low-drain devices |

### Rechargeable (Secondary) Batteries

| Type | Voltage | Cycles | Notes |
|------|---------|--------|-------|
| NiMH | 1.2V | 500-1000 | Good all-around, self-discharge |
| Low self-discharge NiMH | 1.2V | 500-1000 | Better storage, higher cost |
| Lithium-ion | 3.7V | 300-500 | High energy density |
| Li-ion (18650) | 3.7V | 300-500 | Common in flashlights |

## Standardizing Your Batteries

### Why Standardize?

- Easier inventory management
- Interchangeable across devices
- Bulk purchasing savings
- Simpler rotation

### Recommended Standard Sizes

**Primary (stock heavily):**
- AA - Most common, most devices
- AAA - Smaller devices, remotes

**Secondary (as needed):**
- D cells - Large lanterns
- C cells - Some devices
- 9V - Smoke detectors
- CR123A - Tactical lights
- 18650 - Many modern LED lights

## Proper Battery Storage

### Storage Conditions

| Factor | Ideal | Avoid |
|--------|-------|-------|
| Temperature | 50-70°F | Extreme heat (accelerates discharge) |
| Humidity | Low | High humidity (corrosion) |
| Light | Dark | Direct sunlight |

### Storage Best Practices

- Keep in original packaging
- Or use dedicated battery storage case
- Organize by type and expiration
- Store away from metal objects
- Don't store loose in drawers (shorting risk)
- Keep away from conductive materials

### Temperature Effects

- **Heat:** Accelerates self-discharge, reduces life
- **Cold:** Temporarily reduces capacity
- **Freezing:** Can damage some battery types
- **Extreme heat:** Can cause leakage or explosion

## Rotation System

### The FIFO Method

**First In, First Out:**
1. Date all batteries when purchased
2. Store new batteries in back
3. Use oldest batteries first
4. Maintain organized system

### Practical Rotation

**Option 1: Regular use**
- Use stored batteries in everyday devices
- Replace with fresh when buying
- Keeps stock rotating naturally

**Option 2: Scheduled rotation**
- Annual battery check (same date yearly)
- Replace batteries in all emergency devices
- Used batteries go to everyday devices

### Rotation Schedule by Type

| Battery Type | Replace Every | Check Every |
|--------------|---------------|-------------|
| Alkaline | 5 years | Annually |
| Lithium primary | 10 years | Annually |
| NiMH | When degraded | 6 months |
| Li-ion | 2-3 years | 6 months |

## Rechargeable Battery Management

### Initial Charge

- Charge fully before first use
- Follow manufacturer recommendations
- Some batteries need conditioning cycles

### Maintaining Capacity

**NiMH batteries:**
- Use regularly
- Occasional full discharge helps
- Don't leave in charger indefinitely
- Low self-discharge types better for emergency storage

**Li-ion batteries:**
- Store at 40-60% charge for long-term
- Avoid full discharge
- Avoid storing fully charged
- Temperature sensitive

### Charger Options

**Grid-dependent:**
- Wall chargers (fastest)
- USB chargers

**Off-grid:**
- Solar panel + charger
- Hand-crank chargers
- 12V car chargers
- Goal Zero or similar systems

## How Much to Store?

### Estimation Method

1. List all battery-dependent emergency devices
2. Note battery type and quantity for each
3. Estimate runtime per set
4. Calculate needs for target duration
5. Add 50% buffer

### Example Calculation

**Device: LED flashlight (2 AA)**
- Runtime: 50 hours per set
- Target: 100 hours of use
- Need: 4 AA batteries
- With buffer: 6 AA batteries

**Multiply across all devices**

### General Guidelines

| Household Size | AA/AAA Minimum |
|----------------|----------------|
| 1-2 people | 48+ each |
| 3-4 people | 72+ each |
| 5+ people | 100+ each |

## Emergency Battery Tips

### Extend Battery Life

- Use lowest brightness setting
- Turn off devices when not needed
- Remove batteries from unused devices
- Keep batteries warm in cold weather
- Avoid mixing old and new batteries

### Signs of Bad Batteries

- Leaking or corrosion
- Bulging
- Discoloration
- Won't hold charge (rechargeable)
- Significantly reduced runtime

### Reviving Cold Batteries

Cold batteries appear dead but may have life:
1. Warm them (body heat, warm pocket)
2. Don't heat artificially
3. Try device again when warm

## Safe Disposal

### Do Not Throw in Trash

- Rechargeable batteries (all types)
- Lithium batteries
- Button cell batteries

### Safe Disposal Methods

- Battery recycling centers
- Many stores accept batteries
- Household hazardous waste collection
- Tape terminals before disposing
