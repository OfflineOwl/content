# Protective Measures & Basics for CBR Events

> **Four levers: time, distance, shielding, and keeping it off you.** Every official instruction after a chemical, biological or radiological (CBR) release is a way of pulling one of them. Learn the levers and the right move is clear even when the instructions are late, and you will know why a dust mask is no answer to a gas cloud.

This guide is the overview: what each protective action is for, how to choose when nobody has told you yet, and what masks and medicines can and cannot do. The step-by-step details live in the Shelter in Place, Decontamination Basics, Recognizing CBR Warning Signs and Nuclear Fallout Protection guides.

## Do this now

You get a warning, or you suspect a release, and you do not yet know the details:

1. **Get away from the source.** Outdoors, move away from it and upwind. If the release is inside your building, get out to fresh air.
2. **Get inside a building.** A car is not shelter. Bring children and pets in.
3. **Close up.** Shut and lock windows and doors. Turn off fans, air conditioning and forced-air heating. Close the fireplace damper.
4. **Tune in.** Check phone alerts and a battery or hand-crank radio. Do what officials say, whether that is to stay or to leave.
5. **If anything got on you,** take off your outer clothing, bag it, and wash with soap and water.
6. **Stay put until officials say otherwise.** Do not go out to look or to collect family.

## Get emergency help if

Call 911 (or your local emergency number) for:

- Trouble breathing, chest tightness or choking
- Seizures, collapse or confusion
- Burns, or a chemical in the eyes
- Many people around you falling ill at the same moment

For questions about a chemical exposure in the US, call Poison Help at 1-800-222-1222. Do not call 911 just for information. Use the radio and official alerts.

## The four levers

Radiation safety is built on three ideas: **time, distance and shielding.** They apply almost as well to chemical and biological hazards, with one addition.

| Lever | What it means | What it looks like |
|-------|---------------|--------------------|
| **Time** | Less time exposed means a smaller dose | Move fast, don't linger to watch or film, wash promptly |
| **Distance** | Dose falls off sharply as you move away | Get upwind and away, or deep inside a building |
| **Shielding** | Put something between you and the hazard | Walls, closed windows, a sealed room, concrete and earth for radiation |
| **Keep it off and out** | Stop it getting onto skin, into lungs or into your mouth | Clothes off and bagged, soap and water, a fitted respirator for particles, clean food and water |

When an instruction seems strange, ask which lever it pulls. "Stay inside for 24 hours" is shielding and time. "Take off your outer layer" is keeping it off you. "Evacuate now" is distance.

## What the official instructions mean

| Instruction | What to do | Lever |
|-------------|-----------|-------|
| **Evacuate** | Leave now by the route given. Take your go bag, medications and pets. No shortcuts. | Distance |
| **Shelter in place** (chemical) | Go to an interior room above ground, seal it, and wait. Usually hours. | Shielding |
| **Get inside, stay inside, stay tuned** (radiation) | Basement or the middle of a sturdy building, at least 24 hours unless told otherwise | Shielding, time |
| **Decontaminate** | Outer clothes off and bagged, wash with soap and water | Keep it off |
| **Take potassium iodide (KI)** | Take the stated dose, and only when told | Keep it out |
| **Stay away from others, or quarantine** (biological) | Stay home, avoid crowds, and follow health officials | Distance |
| **Don't eat or drink certain things** | Use sealed food and bottled water until cleared | Keep it out |
| **All clear** | Leave shelter only once officials say it is safe, and follow any instructions on airing out and cleaning up | |

Officials choose between evacuating and sheltering for you, because the right answer depends on the substance, the wind and the time available. Sometimes staying is safer, and sometimes leaving is.

In the US, alerts reach you through Wireless Emergency Alerts on your phone (no sign-up needed), the Emergency Alert System on radio and TV, and NOAA Weather Radio, which also carries non-weather emergencies.

### If nobody has told you yet

- **Can you get to clean air quickly without passing through the contaminated area?** Go, upwind, and keep going until you are well clear.
- **You can't?** Move as far from it as you can, get inside, and close up.
- **In a car:** close the windows and vents, turn off the air conditioner and heater, and get to the nearest building. Cars are not airtight.
- **To find a public shelter in the US,** text SHELTER and your ZIP code to 43362.

Then keep listening. The first move buys time for the official one.

## What changes with each hazard

| | Chemical | Biological | Radiological |
|---|----------|------------|--------------|
| **How it harms** | Breathing gas or vapor, or contact with skin and eyes | Breathing or touching germs or toxins | Radiation from dust settling outside, and dust on or in you |
| **Signs** | Often fast. Some toxic chemicals have no smell. | Often none for days | None you can sense |
| **Best place** | Interior room above ground, sealed | Indoors, away from the substance and from crowds | Basement or center of a brick or concrete building |
| **Why there** | Many toxic gases are heavier than air and sink | Distance from the source and from sick people | Mass between you and the dust. Sealing is not needed. |
| **Do particle masks help?** | No, not against gases and vapors | Yes, against airborne particles | Yes, against breathing in dust. They do not stop the radiation itself. |
| **Clean off** | Fast. Undress and wash, ideally within 10 minutes. | Bag clothes, wash, call authorities | Outer layer off removes up to 90 percent of it, then wash |
| **How long** | Usually hours | As long as health officials advise | At least 24 hours inside |
| **Read next** | Shelter in Place | Recognizing CBR Warning Signs | Nuclear Fallout Protection |

Chemical and radiation call for opposite rooms: up and sealed for one, down or center for the other. If you do not know which you face, start in an interior room, close up, turn on the radio, and move when you find out.

## Breathing protection: what works and what doesn't

A mask is the last lever, not the first. Getting away or getting inside does more than anything you can put on your face. It still helps to know what each option does.

| What you have | Particles (dust, smoke, germs) | Gases and vapors |
|---------------|--------------------------------|------------------|
| **2 to 3 layers of cotton** (T-shirt, handkerchief, towel) held over nose and mouth | Some. An official stopgap when you have nothing better. | No |
| **Cloth or disposable mask** | Loose fit. May not stop small particles. | No |
| **KN95** | Filters particles, but may not seal as well as a NIOSH Approved respirator | No |
| **N95** (NIOSH Approved) | At least 95 percent of airborne particles when it seals to your face | No |
| **P100** (NIOSH Approved) | At least 99.97 percent | No |
| **Reusable half-mask respirator with cartridges** | Yes, with particle filters fitted | Only with the right cartridge for that chemical |

What follows from the table:

- **No particle filter stops a gas.** An N95 does nothing against chlorine, ammonia or any other gas or vapor. In a chemical release your protection is distance and a closed building.
- **Cartridge respirators are not a household plan.** They protect only when the cartridge matches the chemical, and in a real release you may not know what the chemical is.
- **Fit decides everything.** A respirator must seal against the skin all the way around. Choose one marked "NIOSH" and "N95" or "P100" with two straps that go around the head, not ear loops. Facial hair where the edge sits breaks the seal.
- **Respirators are not sized for children.** For children the protection is the building, not a mask.
- **They make breathing harder.** If you have heart or lung disease, ask your doctor now whether you should use one. If you feel dizzy or short of breath in one, get to cleaner air and take it off.
- **A mask does nothing for skin or eyes.**

When to use the cloth stopgap: Ready.gov advises layers of fabric over the nose and mouth if you are near a suspicious powder or spray. The CDC advises a mask, cloth or towel over the nose and mouth if you cannot get indoors right away in a radiation emergency. Ready.gov says to take shallow breaths through a cloth or towel if gas or vapor may have got into your sealed room. In each case you keep moving toward real protection.

## Keep it off your skin and out of your mouth

- **Outer clothing off, bagged, and away from people and pets.** Cut off anything you would have to pull over your head.
- **Wash** with soap and water. For a chemical, do it within the first 10 minutes if you can.
- **Don't eat, drink, smoke or touch your face** until you have washed.
- **After a radiological release:** food and water that were inside a building are safe. Sealed containers that were outside are safe once wiped with a damp cloth before opening. Bag the cloth. Do not eat garden produce or uncovered food until officials clear it.
- **After a chemical release:** do not eat or drink anything that may have been contaminated.
- **Pets:** bring them in. After radioactive dust, brush the coat gently, then wash them with soap and water.

The Decontamination Basics guide has every step, including eyes, wounds and children.

## Medicines: only when told

- **Potassium iodide (KI)** protects one organ, the thyroid, from one substance, radioactive iodine. It works best taken within 24 hours before or 4 hours after exposure, and one dose lasts about 24 hours. It does nothing against radiation from fallout outside your body, against other radioactive materials, or against chemicals and germs. Taking more than directed can cause serious harm. Take it only when public health or emergency officials, or your doctor, tell you to. Table salt and iodine supplements are not substitutes.
- **Antibiotics and vaccines** for a biological event are given out by public health officials, who will say who needs them and where to go. Do not treat yourself from an old prescription.
- **Chemical antidotes** are given by medical teams. Your part is to get clean and get to care.

## People who need extra thought

- **Children:** no respirator fits them, so get them indoors first and wash them first. Explain in one sentence what you are doing.
- **Pregnant or breastfeeding:** KI guidance depends on age, pregnancy and breastfeeding. Follow what officials say for your group.
- **Older adults and people with heart or lung disease:** shelter early. A respirator may be too hard to breathe through.
- **People who use medical equipment:** closing up and waiting may last a day. Keep spare batteries and medication in the shelter room.
- **Outside the US:** warning systems, emergency numbers and KI plans differ. Follow your national civil protection agency.

## Kit checklist

- [ ] Battery or hand-crank radio, with NOAA Weather Radio in the US
- [ ] Phone charger and power bank
- [ ] NIOSH Approved N95 or P100 respirators that fit each adult, tried on once
- [ ] Plastic sheeting, duct tape and scissors
- [ ] Heavy trash bags for clothing, soap, and moist wipes
- [ ] A sealed bag of clean clothes for each person
- [ ] Bottled water and sealed food
- [ ] Flashlight, first aid kit and essential medications
- [ ] A note on how to switch off your heating, cooling and fans

## Practice

**10-minute drill:** Run a close-up drill and time it. On "go," bring everyone and the pets inside, shut and lock every window and outside door, switch off the heating, cooling and every fan, close the fireplace damper, and meet with the radio. Then answer two questions out loud: which room would we use for a chemical release, and which for radiation? Finish by having each adult put on an N95 and check its seal. Most households find one window that will not latch and one fan nobody knew how to turn off.

**What would you do?**

1. *A freight train has derailed a mile away and a phone alert says to shelter in place. Your neighbor is loading his car to drive out through town. You have a box of N95 masks and wonder if you should follow him.*

   **Answer:** Stay, and follow the order. Officials chose sheltering because of what was released and where the wind is taking it. A car is not airtight, the road may lead through the cloud, and an N95 does not stop gases or vapors. Go to an interior room above ground, seal it, and keep the radio on. If the order changes to evacuate, leave by the route they give.

2. *You are driving home when the radio reports an explosion downtown that has spread radioactive material. You are a couple of miles away, and your children are at school across town.*

   **Answer:** Get into the nearest sturdy building, not the car, and go to the basement or the center of it. Do not drive to the school. It has its own plan, and going out could expose you and the children. Take off your outer layer, wash exposed skin, text your family, and stay tuned. Expect to stay inside for at least 24 hours unless officials say otherwise.

3. *News breaks of a leak at a chemical plant in the next county. A relative has a bottle of potassium iodide tablets and urges everyone to take one "just in case."*

   **Answer:** Don't. KI only protects the thyroid from radioactive iodine. It does nothing in a chemical release, and taken without need it can do harm. What helps here is the alert on your phone: stay in or leave as officials direct, and close up the house if the plume could reach you.
