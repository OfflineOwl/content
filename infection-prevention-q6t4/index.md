# Infection Prevention Without Modern Healthcare

> **Your Best Defense** - When antibiotics and hospitals aren't available, preventing infection becomes critical. It's far easier to prevent infection than to treat it without modern medicine.

## Understanding Infection Risk

### How Infections Spread

| Route | Examples | Prevention |
|-------|----------|------------|
| **Contact** | Wound infection, skin-to-skin | Hand hygiene, wound care |
| **Droplet** | Flu, cold, COVID | Masks, distance, ventilation |
| **Airborne** | TB, measles | Masks, ventilation, isolation |
| **Fecal-oral** | Cholera, hepatitis A | Hand hygiene, safe water/food |
| **Vector** | Malaria, Lyme disease | Insect protection |
| **Blood** | HIV, hepatitis B | Avoid blood contact, PPE |

### Infection Risk Factors

Higher risk situations:
- Open wounds
- Compromised immune systems
- Poor nutrition
- Dehydration
- Crowded conditions
- Poor sanitation
- Lack of clean water
- Stress and lack of sleep

## Hand Hygiene: The #1 Prevention

### When to Wash Hands

**Critical times:**
- Before and after treating wounds
- Before and after preparing food
- Before eating
- After using the bathroom
- After handling waste
- After contact with sick person
- After touching contaminated surfaces
- After handling animals

### Proper Handwashing Technique

**With soap and water (preferred):**

1. Wet hands with clean water
2. Apply soap
3. Lather thoroughly—cover all surfaces
4. Scrub for at least 20 seconds (sing "Happy Birthday" twice)
5. Don't forget:
   - Between fingers
   - Under nails
   - Back of hands
   - Wrists
6. Rinse under running water
7. Dry with clean towel or air dry

**Without running water:**
- Pour water over hands (have someone assist)
- Or use wet cloth to scrub with soap
- Rinse with clean water
- Any washing is better than none

### Hand Sanitizer Use

**When soap and water unavailable:**

Requirements:
- Must be 60%+ alcohol
- Hands must be visibly clean (sanitizer doesn't remove dirt)

Technique:
1. Apply enough to cover all surfaces
2. Rub all surfaces of hands
3. Continue until completely dry (15-30 seconds)
4. Don't wipe off

**Limitations:**
- Doesn't work on dirty hands
- Less effective against some pathogens
- Can dry out skin

### Making Emergency Hand Sanitizer

If commercial unavailable:

**Rubbing alcohol solution:**
- 2 parts isopropyl alcohol (91%+)
- 1 part aloe vera gel
- Mix thoroughly
- Store in clean container

*Note: Homemade is less reliable than commercial*

## Wound Care for Infection Prevention

### Clean Wound Immediately

**Timeline matters:**
- Infection risk increases dramatically after 6-8 hours
- Clean wounds as soon as possible
- Even imperfect cleaning helps

### Wound Cleaning Procedure

1. **Wash your hands** (with gloves if available)

2. **Stop bleeding first** (pressure, elevation)

3. **Irrigate wound**
   - Use cleanest water available
   - Boiled and cooled water is best
   - Flush debris out
   - High pressure helps (syringe, squeeze bottle)

4. **Remove debris**
   - Use clean tweezers
   - Remove visible dirt, splinters, etc.
   - Don't dig deeply

5. **Wash around wound**
   - Use mild soap
   - Clean skin around wound
   - Don't put soap IN wound

6. **Apply thin layer of antibiotic ointment** (if available)

7. **Cover with clean bandage**
   - Change daily minimum
   - Change if wet or dirty

### Signs of Wound Infection

Watch for (usually 2-3 days after injury):
- Increasing redness (especially spreading)
- Increasing swelling
- Increasing pain (should be decreasing)
- Warmth around wound
- Pus or discharge
- Red streaks extending from wound
- Fever
- Swollen lymph nodes near wound
- Foul smell

### If Infection Develops

Without antibiotics:
1. Keep wound open (don't cover tightly)
2. Clean more frequently (3-4 times daily)
3. Apply warm compresses
4. Elevate if possible
5. Rest
6. Stay hydrated
7. Watch for spreading infection (red streaks = emergency)
8. Seek medical care when possible

## Respiratory Infection Prevention

### Reduce Transmission

**When sick people are present:**
- Separate sick individuals if possible
- Improve ventilation (open windows)
- Sick person wears mask
- Maintain distance (6 feet minimum)
- Cover coughs/sneezes
- Frequent hand washing

### Mask Use

**When to use masks:**
- When caring for sick person
- When you are sick
- In crowded conditions during outbreaks
- When air quality is poor

**Mask effectiveness hierarchy:**
1. N95/KN95 (best)
2. KF94
3. Surgical masks
4. Multi-layer cloth masks
5. Single-layer cloth (minimal protection)

**Proper mask use:**
- Cover nose AND mouth
- Fit snugly to face
- Don't touch front while wearing
- Change when damp
- Wash hands after removing

### Respiratory Hygiene

**When no mask:**
- Cough/sneeze into elbow
- Use tissues and dispose immediately
- Wash hands after coughing/sneezing
- Avoid touching face

## Food Safety Without Refrigeration

### Preventing Foodborne Illness

**Safe food handling:**
1. Wash hands before food prep
2. Wash fruits and vegetables
3. Keep raw and cooked foods separate
4. Cook foods thoroughly
5. Don't let food sit in danger zone (40-140°F)

**Danger zone rules:**
- Hot foods: Keep above 140°F
- Cold foods: Keep below 40°F
- 2-hour rule: Don't eat food left out more than 2 hours (1 hour if >90°F outside)

### Foods That Keep Without Refrigeration

**Safe options:**
- Canned goods (until opened)
- Dried foods (pasta, rice, beans)
- Hard cheeses (short term)
- Peanut butter (unopened or short term after)
- Honey
- Dried fruits
- Nuts
- Crackers
- Hard bread

**High risk without refrigeration:**
- Dairy products
- Eggs (unless preserved)
- Meat and fish
- Cut fruits/vegetables
- Cooked rice (surprising risk)
- Soft cheeses

## Sanitation Without Plumbing

### Human Waste Management

**Critical for disease prevention:**

**Emergency toilet options:**
1. **Bucket system**
   - 5-gallon bucket with lid
   - Toilet seat attachment
   - Line with garbage bag
   - Add cat litter, sawdust, or dirt after each use
   - Seal bag when full
   - Dispose away from water sources

2. **Trench latrine**
   - Dig 6-8 inches deep
   - At least 200 feet from water sources
   - Cover waste each time
   - Fill when 1 foot from top

**Hand washing after toilet is essential**

### Garbage Management

- Keep garbage contained and covered
- Dispose away from living areas
- Burn if possible and safe
- Bury if burning not possible
- Keep away from water sources

## Isolation Procedures

### When to Isolate

- Suspected contagious illness
- Confirmed infection
- Unknown illness with fever
- Diarrheal illness

### Home Isolation Setup

**Create sick room:**
- Separate room if possible
- Keep door closed
- Designate one caregiver
- Separate bathroom if possible

**In sick room:**
- Waste basket with lid (lined with bag)
- Tissues
- Water and cup
- Mask for sick person
- Thermometer
- Medications
- Towel (not shared)

**Caregiver precautions:**
- Wear mask when in room
- Wash hands before and after
- Wear gloves for body fluid contact
- Don't touch face
- Minimize time in room

### Cleaning Sick Room

- Clean frequently touched surfaces daily
- Use bleach solution (1/3 cup per gallon water)
- Or soap and water if no bleach
- Wear gloves when cleaning
- Wash hands after removing gloves

## Personal Protective Equipment (PPE)

### Basic PPE Kit

- Nitrile or latex gloves
- Surgical or N95 masks
- Eye protection (goggles or face shield)
- Gowns or large garbage bags with holes
- Shoe covers or dedicated shoes

### When to Use PPE

| Situation | Gloves | Mask | Eye Protection |
|-----------|--------|------|----------------|
| Wound care | Yes | If splatter risk | If splatter risk |
| Respiratory illness care | Yes | Yes | Yes if coughing |
| Cleaning up body fluids | Yes | Yes | Yes |
| Handling waste | Yes | Optional | No |
| General sick care | Yes | Yes | Recommended |

### Proper PPE Removal

**Order matters (outside is contaminated):**
1. Remove gloves (don't touch outside)
2. Wash hands or sanitize
3. Remove eye protection (by strap/arms)
4. Remove gown (from inside)
5. Remove mask last (by straps only)
6. Wash hands thoroughly

## Natural Infection Fighters

*Note: These support but don't replace proper hygiene and wound care*

**Some evidence-based options:**

- **Honey** (on wounds): Antimicrobial properties, especially Manuka
- **Garlic**: Some antimicrobial properties (internal use)
- **Coconut oil**: Mild antimicrobial (topical)
- **Echinacea**: May support immune function
- **Vitamin C**: Supports immune function
- **Zinc**: Supports immune function
- **Elderberry**: May reduce duration of colds/flu

**Caution:** Natural remedies are not substitutes for antibiotics in serious infections.
