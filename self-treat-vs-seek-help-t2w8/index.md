# When to Self-Treat vs Seek Help

> **Know Your Limits** - In emergencies, you may need to handle medical issues yourself. But knowing when a condition exceeds your capabilities could save a life.

## The Decision Framework

Ask these questions:
1. Is this life-threatening?
2. Is it getting worse?
3. Do I have the skills and supplies to help?
4. What are the risks of waiting?
5. What are the risks of attempting treatment?

**When in doubt, seek help.**

---

## Life-Threatening Emergencies

### Always Seek Immediate Help For:

| Emergency | Why |
|-----------|-----|
| Not breathing | Minutes to brain damage |
| Severe bleeding not stopping | Death in minutes |
| Chest pain with sweating, arm pain | Heart attack |
| Sudden severe headache | Possible stroke |
| One-sided weakness/numbness | Stroke |
| Difficulty breathing | Many serious causes |
| Severe allergic reaction | Anaphylaxis can kill |
| Loss of consciousness | Many serious causes |
| Poisoning/overdose | Can be rapidly fatal |
| Severe trauma | Internal injuries |
| Seizures (prolonged/first time) | Brain emergency |
| High fever with stiff neck | Meningitis |

**These conditions cannot be safely managed without professional care.**

## Conditions You Can Manage

### Minor Wounds

**Can self-treat if:**
- Bleeding stops with pressure
- Wound is superficial (doesn't expose fat/muscle)
- Clean cut with smooth edges
- Not on face, joints, hands, feet, genitals
- No embedded objects
- Tetanus vaccination current

**How to treat:**
- Clean thoroughly
- Apply antibiotic ointment
- Cover with clean bandage
- Change dressing daily
- Watch for infection

**Seek help if:**
- Won't stop bleeding
- Deep or gaping
- Signs of infection develop
- On face or critical area
- Caused by bite
- Contains embedded material

### Minor Burns

**Can self-treat if:**
- First-degree (red, no blisters)
- Second-degree smaller than 3 inches
- Not on face, hands, feet, genitals, joints
- Not electrical or chemical
- No inhalation injury

**How to treat:**
- Cool with running water (10-20 min)
- Apply burn gel or aloe
- Cover loosely
- Pain management
- Watch for infection

**Seek help if:**
- Third-degree (white/charred)
- Large area affected
- On face, hands, feet, joints, genitals
- Blisters cover large area
- Signs of infection
- Electrical or chemical burn

### Sprains and Strains

**Can self-treat if:**
- Can bear some weight
- No obvious deformity
- Pain is manageable
- Joint can move (even if painful)
- No numbness/tingling

**How to treat:**
- RICE: Rest, Ice, Compression, Elevation
- Pain medication
- Gentle movement as tolerated
- Gradually return to activity

**Seek help if:**
- Unable to bear weight at all
- Visible deformity
- Severe swelling immediately
- Numbness or tingling
- Pain not improving after 48-72 hours
- Possible fracture

### Dehydration (Mild to Moderate)

**Can self-treat if:**
- Person is conscious and alert
- Can swallow
- Mild symptoms (thirst, dark urine, dry mouth)

**How to treat:**
- Oral rehydration solution
- Small, frequent sips
- Avoid caffeine and alcohol
- Rest in cool area

**Seek help if:**
- Cannot keep fluids down
- Severe symptoms (confusion, no urination)
- Signs of shock
- In infants or elderly

### Fever

**Can self-treat if:**
- Adult with fever under 103°F
- Child over 3 months with fever under 102°F
- No other concerning symptoms
- Alert and responsive
- Drinking fluids

**How to treat:**
- Fever reducers (acetaminophen, ibuprofen)
- Fluids
- Rest
- Light clothing
- Cool compresses

**Seek help if:**
- Very high fever (103°F+ adult, 102°F+ child)
- Infant under 3 months with any fever
- Fever with rash
- Fever with stiff neck
- Fever with severe headache
- Fever lasting more than 3 days
- Person very ill-appearing

### Diarrhea and Vomiting

**Can self-treat if:**
- Mild symptoms
- Can keep some fluids down
- No blood in vomit/stool
- Alert and responsive
- No severe abdominal pain

**How to treat:**
- Clear fluids initially
- Oral rehydration solutions
- Gradually reintroduce bland foods
- Rest
- Hygiene to prevent spread

**Seek help if:**
- Blood in vomit or stool
- Cannot keep any fluids down
- Signs of dehydration
- Severe abdominal pain
- Fever with diarrhea
- Symptoms lasting more than 48 hours
- In infants, elderly, or immunocompromised

### Common Cold/Flu

**Can self-treat if:**
- Typical symptoms (congestion, sore throat, mild fever)
- Symptoms manageable
- Able to eat and drink
- No difficulty breathing

**How to treat:**
- Rest
- Fluids
- Pain/fever reducers
- Decongestants (if appropriate)
- Honey for cough (over age 1)
- Time (most resolve in 7-10 days)

**Seek help if:**
- High fever
- Difficulty breathing
- Severe headache
- Symptoms worsening after 7 days
- Chest pain
- Confusion
- Persistent vomiting
- High-risk individual (elderly, pregnant, immunocompromised)

## Gray Zone Conditions

These require careful judgment:

### Fractures

**May be able to manage temporarily if:**
- Closed fracture (no bone visible)
- Circulation intact below injury
- You can effectively immobilize
- Pain manageable

**Temporary management:**
- Immobilize above and below injury
- Ice and elevate
- Pain management
- Monitor circulation

**Seek help:**
- As soon as possible
- Any open fracture immediately
- If circulation compromised
- If significant deformity

### Wound Infections

**May be able to manage if:**
- Localized redness/swelling
- No red streaks
- No fever
- Draining but not spreading

**How to treat:**
- Clean frequently
- Keep open/draining
- Warm compresses
- Watch closely

**Seek help if:**
- Red streaks spreading
- Fever develops
- Infection spreading despite treatment
- Person becoming ill

### Asthma Attack

**May be able to manage if:**
- Rescue inhaler available
- Responds to inhaler
- Can speak in sentences
- Not turning blue

**How to treat:**
- Sit upright
- 2-4 puffs rescue inhaler
- Wait 1 minute between puffs
- Calm breathing
- Remove from triggers

**Seek help if:**
- Not responding to inhaler
- Can't speak in sentences
- Lips turning blue
- Using accessory muscles
- Getting worse

### Allergic Reactions

**May be able to manage if:**
- Mild symptoms (hives, itching only)
- No breathing difficulty
- No throat swelling
- Antihistamine available

**How to treat:**
- Remove allergen
- Antihistamine (Benadryl)
- Cool compress for hives
- Monitor closely

**Seek help immediately if:**
- ANY difficulty breathing
- Throat tightness/swelling
- Dizziness or confusion
- Rapid pulse
- Use EpiPen if available and call for help

## Resource Limitations Decision Tree

When professional help is unavailable:

```
Is this immediately life-threatening?
├── YES → Do everything possible, keep trying for help
└── NO → Continue ↓

Is the condition getting worse?
├── YES → Higher priority for seeking help
└── NO → Continue ↓

Can I effectively treat this with available resources?
├── YES → Treat and monitor closely
└── NO → Continue ↓

What happens if I wait?
├── Gets much worse → Prioritize seeking help
└── Stays same or slowly improves → Monitor and treat symptoms
```

## Skills Assessment

Be honest about your capabilities:

### Basic Skills (Most People Can Learn)
- Wound cleaning and bandaging
- Splinting
- CPR
- Bleeding control with pressure
- Fever/pain management
- Oral rehydration
- Basic assessment

### Intermediate Skills (Requires Training)
- Tourniquet application
- Wound closure
- Fracture reduction
- Injectable medications
- IV fluids
- Advanced airway

### Advanced Skills (Medical Training Required)
- Surgery
- Complex fracture management
- Medication dosing
- Diagnosis of internal conditions
- Treatment of organ system failures

**Know where your skills end.**

---

## When All Else Fails

If professional help is truly unavailable:

1. **Do what you can** with available resources
2. **Document everything** (time, treatments, responses)
3. **Monitor continuously** for changes
4. **Keep trying** to reach help (radio, runners, signals)
5. **Provide comfort** if treatment isn't possible
6. **Don't blame yourself** for limitations
