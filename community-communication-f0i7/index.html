Community Communication During Emergencies

Stronger Together - When official communication fails, community networks fill the gap. Building these connections before emergencies strengthens everyone.

Why Community Communication Matters

When Official Systems Fail

  • Cell towers damaged
  • Internet down
  • Emergency services overwhelmed
  • Information delays
  • Local knowledge essential

Benefits of Community Networks

  • Local, real-time information
  • Resource sharing
  • Mutual assistance
  • Emotional support
  • Faster response

Building Your Network

Know Your Neighbors

Start simple:

  • Introduce yourself
  • Exchange phone numbers
  • Identify vulnerable neighbors
  • Learn about skills/resources

Create Contact List

Gather for trusted neighbors:

  • Names and addresses
  • Phone numbers
  • Special needs
  • Skills or resources
  • Out-of-area contacts

Establish Communication Methods

  • Primary: Text messages (work better than calls)
  • Backup: Two-way radios
  • Emergency: Physical check-ins

Communication Methods

Text/Phone Tree

How it works:

  1. Designated person sends to 5 people
  2. Each of those sends to 5 more
  3. Message spreads quickly
  4. Confirmations flow back

Two-Way Radios (FRS/GMRS)

  • Agree on channel in advance
  • Set check-in times
  • Keep transmissions brief
  • No license needed for FRS

Physical Methods

  • Designated message board location
  • Check-in schedule
  • Visual signals (flags, items)
  • Runner system if needed

Message Protocols

What to Communicate

  • Safety status
  • Resource needs
  • Hazard locations
  • Official updates
  • Meeting times

Keep Messages Clear

Good message:

"Smith family all safe. Power out. Have water for 3 days. Need baby formula. Will check board at noon."

Bad message:

"Everything's crazy here, not sure what's happening, think we're okay maybe."

Confirmation System

Ensure messages received:

  • Acknowledge all messages
  • Repeat key information
  • Confirm understanding
  • Report delivery failures

Neighborhood Check-In System

Visual Signals

Pre-agreed signals:

  • Green = all okay
  • Red = need assistance
  • No signal = check on us

Physical Rounds

  • Designated times
  • Specific route
  • Check on vulnerable first
  • Report back to coordinator

Community Meeting Point

Establish Location

Choose spot that's:

  • Central to neighborhood
  • Open and visible
  • Safe from hazards
  • Known to everyone

Meeting Schedule

Example:

  • 8am and 6pm daily during emergency
  • Or as conditions allow
  • Designate someone to always attend

Quick Reference

BEFORE EMERGENCY

Know neighbors:
- Names and numbers
- Special needs
- Skills/resources

Establish:
- Communication methods
- Check-in signals
- Meeting point

DURING EMERGENCY

Share:
- Status
- Needs
- Resources
- Information

Check on:
- Vulnerable neighbors
- Those with no signal

Meet:
- At designated times
- At designated place
