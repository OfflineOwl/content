# Verifying Information During Emergencies

> **Truth in Crisis** - During emergencies, misinformation spreads rapidly. Knowing how to verify information before acting can prevent panic and bad decisions.

## Why Verification Matters

### During Emergencies

- Rumors spread faster than facts
- Wrong information causes panic
- Bad data leads to bad decisions
- Lives depend on accuracy
- Resources wasted on false threats

### Common Misinformation

- Exaggerated damage reports
- False evacuation orders
- Inaccurate weather predictions
- Made-up safety advice
- Manipulated images/videos

## Trusted Sources Hierarchy

### Most Reliable

1. **Official government sources**
   - FEMA
   - National Weather Service
   - CDC
   - State/local emergency management

2. **Emergency services**
   - Local police/fire
   - Emergency management office
   - 911 dispatchers

3. **Established news organizations**
   - Local TV/radio stations
   - Wire services (AP, Reuters)
   - Major newspapers

### Less Reliable

- Social media posts
- Forwarded messages
- Unknown websites
- "Friend of a friend" information
- Unverified videos

## Verification Techniques

### Check the Source

| Question | Action |
|----------|--------|
| Who is sharing this? | Check their credibility |
| What's their source? | Trace back to origin |
| When was it created? | Check date/time |
| Why might they share this? | Consider motives |

### Cross-Reference

- Check multiple independent sources
- Official sources should confirm
- If only one source, be skeptical
- Breaking news takes time to verify

### Image/Video Verification

- Reverse image search
- Check metadata if possible
- Look for context clues
- Old images often recycled
- AI-generated content increasing

## Red Flags

### Suspicious Information

- Only one source
- Emotional language
- "Share this before deleted"
- Anonymous sources
- Too convenient/dramatic
- Conflicts with official reports

### Social Media Warning Signs

- New accounts posting
- Bot-like behavior
- Unverified accounts
- Copy-paste text
- Appeal to panic

## Verification Checklist

### Before Acting on Information

- [ ] Identified original source
- [ ] Source is credible
- [ ] Information is recent
- [ ] Cross-referenced with other sources
- [ ] Official sources confirm
- [ ] Details are specific, not vague
- [ ] No obvious red flags

## What to Do With Unverified Info

### Don't

- Share immediately
- Panic
- Take major action
- Assume it's true

### Do

- Wait for verification
- Check official sources
- Ask questions
- Report obvious misinformation

## Reliable Information Sources

### Set Up Before Emergency

- Local emergency management social media
- Local news alerts
- NOAA Weather Radio
- Wireless Emergency Alerts
- Community notification systems

## Quick Reference

```
4. Everything else - verify first
```
