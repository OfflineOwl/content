POST-EVENT CLEANUP SAFETY
=========================

After a disaster, the cleanup can be just as dangerous as the event
itself. This guide covers safe practices for returning and cleaning up.

BEFORE ENTERING YOUR HOME
-------------------------

WAIT FOR ALL-CLEAR:
- Authorities must declare area safe
- Structural safety must be confirmed
- Utilities must be assessed

DANGER SIGNS (DO NOT ENTER):
- Visible structural damage
- Gas odor
- Standing water with electrical hazards
- Unstable debris
- Condemned notice

INITIAL INSPECTION:
From outside, check for:
- Foundation cracks or shifting
- Roof damage
- Wall damage or leaning
- Downed power lines nearby
- Broken gas lines

PERSONAL PROTECTIVE EQUIPMENT
-----------------------------

ESSENTIAL GEAR:
- Hard hat (falling debris)
- Safety glasses/goggles
- Dust mask or N95 respirator
- Work gloves (heavy duty)
- Sturdy boots (steel toe ideal)
- Long pants and sleeves

FOR SPECIFIC HAZARDS:
Flood cleanup:
- Rubber boots and gloves
- N95 or better mask

Mold exposure:
- N95 or P100 respirator
- Goggles (no vents)

Chemical hazards:
- Chemical-resistant gloves
- Respirator rated for chemicals

UTILITY SAFETY
--------------

ELECTRICITY:
- Don't touch electrical equipment if wet
- Turn off main breaker before entering wet building
- Don't use electrical items until checked
- Watch for downed power lines

GAS:
- If you smell gas, leave immediately
- Don't turn on lights or create sparks
- Call gas company from outside
- Don't return until cleared

WATER:
- Turn off main if pipes are damaged
- Don't use contaminated water
- Be cautious of sewage backup

COMMON CLEANUP HAZARDS
----------------------

STRUCTURAL:
- Weakened floors
- Damaged roofs
- Unstable walls
- Hidden voids

DEBRIS:
- Nails and sharp objects
- Broken glass
- Unstable piles
- Heavy objects

BIOLOGICAL:
- Mold (starts within 24-48 hours)
- Sewage contamination
- Dead animals
- Contaminated water

CHEMICAL:
- Cleaning chemical mixing
- Fuel/oil contamination
- Unknown substances
- Carbon monoxide from generators

ENVIRONMENTAL:
- Heat exposure
- Cold exposure
- Physical exhaustion
- Dehydration

SAFE WORK PRACTICES
-------------------

PACE YOURSELF:
- Take frequent breaks
- Stay hydrated
- Don't overexert
- Work in teams
- Know your limits

LIFTING:
- Lift with legs, not back
- Don't lift alone if too heavy
- Clear path before carrying
- Ask for help

LADDER SAFETY:
- Check stability before climbing
- Three points of contact
- Don't overreach
- Have spotter if possible

TOOL SAFETY:
- Right tool for job
- Inspect before use
- Use safely, not rushed
- Store properly

MOLD PREVENTION
---------------

TIMING IS CRITICAL:
Mold begins growing within 24-48 hours

PREVENTION:
- Remove standing water immediately
- Remove wet materials
- Open windows for ventilation
- Use fans and dehumidifiers
- Dry thoroughly within 24-48 hours

MATERIALS THAT MUST BE DISCARDED:
- Wet insulation
- Wet carpet and padding
- Wet upholstered furniture
- Moldy drywall

CLEANING MOLD:
- Small areas: Detergent solution or bleach dilution
- Wear PPE (N95, goggles, gloves)
- Don't mix cleaning chemicals
- Large areas: Professional remediation

FLOOD CLEANUP SPECIFICS
-----------------------

CONTAMINATED EVERYTHING:
Flood water is contaminated with:
- Sewage
- Chemicals
- Bacteria
- Debris

CLEANING PROTOCOL:
1. Pump out standing water
2. Remove debris and mud
3. Remove damaged materials
4. Clean hard surfaces with disinfectant
5. Dry completely
6. Monitor for mold

FOOD AND WATER:
- Discard all food touched by flood water
- Discard medications in flood water
- Don't drink tap water until cleared
- Disinfect dishes and cookware

DOCUMENTATION
-------------

BEFORE CLEANUP:
- Photograph all damage
- Make detailed lists
- Don't throw away anything until documented
- Contact insurance company

DURING CLEANUP:
- Keep receipts for all expenses
- Document work done
- Note materials used
- Record time spent (if filing claims)

EMOTIONAL WELL-BEING
--------------------

CLEANUP IS HARD:
- Physically exhausting
- Emotionally draining
- Can trigger grief
- May cause anxiety

SELF-CARE:
- Take breaks
- Accept help
- Talk about feelings
- Maintain routines when possible
- Seek professional help if needed
