Emergency Defensive Driving

Drive to Survive - Emergency situations require different driving skills. Defensive driving techniques can help you navigate dangerous conditions safely.

Emergency Driving Principles

Core Concepts

1. Awareness

Know what's happening around you

2. Space

Maintain distance from hazards

3. Escape Routes

Always have an out

4. Control

Smooth inputs, no panic

Evacuation Driving

Before Departure

  • Full tank of fuel
  • Vehicle inspected
  • Route planned (plus alternates)
  • Supplies loaded
  • Communication plan set

During Evacuation

Hazard awareness:

  • Other panicked drivers
  • Emergency vehicles
  • Road debris
  • Damaged infrastructure
  • Flooded roads

Traffic Management

  • Leave early (before crowds)
  • Use alternate routes
  • Be patient (panic causes accidents)
  • Don't block intersections
  • Let emergency vehicles pass

Driving Through Danger Zones

Civil Unrest Areas

  • Keep windows up, doors locked
  • Don't stop if avoidable
  • Stay on main roads
  • Maintain awareness
  • Don't engage with crowds
  • Have alternate route ready

Suspicious Situations

If approached:

  • Keep moving if safe
  • Don't roll down windows
  • Drive to police station if followed
  • Call 911 if threatened

Hazardous Conditions

Flooded Roads

TURN AROUND, DON'T DROWN

6 inches
can sweep you off your feet
12 inches
can float a car
2 feet
will carry away most vehicles

Don't attempt to cross

Debris on Roads

  • Slow down well in advance
  • Identify clear path
  • Watch for hidden hazards
  • Be prepared to stop

Damaged Roads

  • Bridge damage may not be visible
  • Sinkholes can appear
  • Road surface may be compromised
  • When in doubt, don't proceed

Vehicle as Shelter

When to Stay in Vehicle

  • Extreme weather (not tornado)
  • Stuck in safe location
  • Waiting for help
  • No better shelter available

When to Leave Vehicle

  • Fire hazard
  • Flood water rising
  • Vehicle becoming unstable
  • Better shelter available

Emergency Maneuvers

Threshold Braking (No ABS)

  • Apply firm, steady pressure
  • Just before lockup
  • If wheels lock, release slightly
  • Reapply

ABS Braking

  • Firm, continuous pressure
  • Don't pump
  • Steer while braking
  • Pulsing is normal

Evasive Steering

  • Look where you want to go
  • Smooth, rapid steering input
  • Don't overcorrect
  • Hands at 9 and 3

Vehicle Emergency Kit

In Car at All Times

  • Jumper cables
  • Flashlight
  • First aid kit
  • Water
  • Blanket
  • Phone charger
  • Basic tools
  • Reflective triangles

For Emergency Situations

  • Extra fuel container
  • Tow strap
  • Fire extinguisher
  • Maps (paper)
