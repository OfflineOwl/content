# Preserving Food Without Electricity

> **Ancient Methods, Modern Value** - Humans preserved food for thousands of years before refrigeration. These methods can extend food availability during extended emergencies.

## Why Preservation Matters

When power is out long-term:
- Refrigerated food spoils
- Fresh food is unavailable
- Preserved food provides nutrition
- Extends limited supplies
- Creates variety in diet

## Preservation Methods Overview

| Method | Best For | Difficulty | Equipment Needed |
|--------|----------|------------|-----------------|
| Drying | Fruits, meats, herbs | Easy | Racks, airflow |

| Salt curing | Meats, fish | Easy | Salt |

| Fermenting | Vegetables | Medium | Salt, containers |

## Drying/Dehydrating

### How It Works

Removing moisture prevents bacterial growth. Dried foods can last months to years.

### Sun Drying

**Requirements:**
- Hot, dry weather (85°F+)
- Low humidity
- Good airflow
- Protection from insects/debris

**Method:**
1. Slice food thin (1/4 inch)
2. Place on drying racks
3. Cover with cheesecloth (keeps insects off)
4. Bring indoors at night (prevents moisture)
5. Dry until leathery (fruits) or brittle (vegetables)
6. Test: No moisture when squeezed or broken

**Time:** 2-4 days depending on conditions

### Indoor Air Drying

**For herbs and some vegetables:**
1. Bundle herbs, hang upside down
2. Or spread on racks in warm, dry room
3. Ensure good airflow
4. Takes longer than sun drying

### Fire/Heat Drying

**Using campfire or wood stove:**
1. Create drying rack above heat source
2. Maintain low, steady heat
3. Turn food regularly
4. Avoid cooking—just drying
5. Watch carefully for burning

### What to Dry

**Fruits:** Apples, berries, grapes, bananas, peaches
**Vegetables:** Tomatoes, peppers, onions, beans, corn
**Herbs:** Any culinary herb
**Meat:** Lean cuts only (see jerky below)

### Making Jerky

1. Use lean meat only (fat goes rancid)
2. Slice thin, against the grain
3. Remove all visible fat
4. Season with salt (essential for preservation)
5. Dry until bendable but not breaking
6. Should crack but not snap

## Salt Curing

### How It Works

Salt draws out moisture and creates an environment hostile to bacteria.

### Dry Curing Method

1. **Prepare meat** - Trim excess fat
2. **Apply cure** - Coat thoroughly with salt mixture
3. **Pack** - Layer in container with salt
4. **Wait** - 2-3 days per pound at cool temps
5. **Rinse** - Remove excess salt
6. **Dry** - Air dry or smoke

### Basic Cure Mix

- 1 lb salt
- 1/2 cup sugar
- Optional: spices, curing salt (pink salt #1)

### Salt Curing Tips

- Use non-iodized salt
- Cool temperatures essential (38-40°F ideal)
- Proper salt ratio critical
- Test by cooking sample before serving
- May be very salty—soak before cooking

## Smoking

### How It Works

Smoke contains antimicrobial compounds and dries the food surface.

### Types of Smoking

**Cold smoking (under 90°F):**
- Preserves but doesn't cook
- Requires salt curing first
- Days of smoking needed
- Higher skill required

**Hot smoking (140-225°F):**
- Cooks and flavors
- Shorter time (hours)
- Less preservation effect
- Easier for beginners

### Improvised Smoker

**Simple smoker from barrel/box:**
1. Heat source at bottom
2. Wood chips for smoke
3. Racks for food in upper section
4. Ventilation at top
5. Temperature monitoring important

### Woods for Smoking

**Good choices:**
- Hickory (strong)
- Apple (mild, sweet)
- Oak (medium)
- Cherry (mild)
- Maple (mild)

**NEVER use:**
- Pine or softwoods (toxic)
- Treated wood
- Painted wood

## Pickling

### How It Works

Acid environment (vinegar) prevents bacterial growth.

### Basic Quick Pickle

**Ingredients:**
- 2 cups vinegar (5% acidity)
- 2 cups water
- 2 tbsp salt
- Optional: sugar, spices

**Method:**
1. Heat brine to dissolve salt
2. Pack vegetables in clean jars
3. Pour hot brine over vegetables
4. Cool and store

**Storage:** Refrigerate if possible, or keep very cool

### Best Vegetables for Pickling

- Cucumbers
- Onions
- Peppers
- Green beans
- Carrots
- Cabbage (sauerkraut)

## Fermentation

### How It Works

Beneficial bacteria convert sugars to acid, preserving the food and adding nutrition.

### Basic Sauerkraut

**Ingredients:**
- Cabbage
- Salt (2% of cabbage weight)

**Method:**
1. Shred cabbage finely
2. Mix with salt in bowl
3. Pound or squeeze until liquid releases
4. Pack into jar, pressing down
5. Liquid should cover cabbage
6. Weight to keep submerged
7. Cover loosely (needs to vent)
8. Ferment 3-4 weeks at room temperature

### Fermentation Tips

- Cleanliness is essential
- Keep vegetables submerged
- Room temperature 60-75°F ideal
- Taste test to check progress
- Mold on surface can be skimmed (if just starting)
- Slimy texture = discard

## Root Cellaring

### How It Works

Cool, humid, dark conditions slow spoilage of root vegetables.

### Ideal Conditions

- Temperature: 32-40°F
- Humidity: 85-95%
- Dark
- Good ventilation

### Storage Life

| Vegetable | Conditions | Duration |
|-----------|------------|----------|
| Potatoes | Cool, dark, humid | 4-6 months |
| Carrots | Cold, very humid | 4-6 months |
| Beets | Cold, humid | 3-5 months |
| Turnips | Cold, humid | 4-5 months |
| Cabbage | Cold, humid | 3-4 months |
| Onions | Cool, dry | 6-8 months |
| Winter squash | Cool, dry | 3-6 months |
| Apples | Cold, humid | 2-6 months |

### Improvised Root Cellar

**Options:**
- Buried trash can or barrel
- Basement corner (if cool enough)
- Unheated garage
- Crawl space
- Hay bale insulation outdoors

## Canning (Heat Processing)

### Warning

Improper canning can cause botulism poisoning, which is potentially fatal.

### Two Methods

**Water bath canning (high-acid foods):**
- Fruits
- Tomatoes (with added acid)
- Pickles
- Jams and jellies
- Salsa (high acid recipes)

**Pressure canning (low-acid foods):**
- Vegetables
- Meats
- Beans
- Soups

### Without Power

**If you have:**
- Canning supplies (jars, lids, equipment)
- Outdoor cooking capability
- Pressure canner (for low-acid)
- Knowledge and experience

**You can:**
- Can using outdoor fire
- Must maintain proper temperatures
- Must follow tested recipes exactly
- More difficult than with electric/gas stove

---

## Food Safety Warning

### Critical Points

- Follow established methods exactly
- Don't improvise with low-acid foods
- When in doubt, throw it out
- Botulism has no smell or taste
- Some preservation requires specific conditions

### Signs of Spoilage

- Off smells
- Mold (except as designed)
- Slimy texture
- Unusual colors
- Bulging containers (canned goods)
- Bubbling (when not fermenting)
