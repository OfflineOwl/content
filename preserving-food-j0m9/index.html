Preserving Food Without Electricity

Ancient Methods, Modern Value - Humans preserved food for thousands of years before refrigeration. These methods can extend food availability during extended emergencies.

Why Preservation Matters

When power is out long-term:

  • Refrigerated food spoils
  • Fresh food is unavailable
  • Preserved food provides nutrition
  • Extends limited supplies
  • Creates variety in diet

Drying/Dehydrating

How It Works

Removing moisture prevents bacterial growth. Dried foods can last months to years.

Sun Drying Method

Requirements:

  • Hot, dry weather (85°F+)
  • Low humidity
  • Good airflow
  • Protection from insects/debris

Method:

  1. Slice food thin (1/4 inch)
  2. Place on drying racks
  3. Cover with cheesecloth (keeps insects off)
  4. Bring indoors at night (prevents moisture)
  5. Dry until leathery (fruits) or brittle (vegetables)
  6. Test: No moisture when squeezed or broken

Time: 2-4 days depending on conditions

What to Dry

  • Fruits: Apples, berries, grapes, bananas, peaches
  • Vegetables: Tomatoes, peppers, onions, beans, corn
  • Herbs: Any culinary herb
  • Meat: Lean cuts only (see jerky below)

Making Jerky

  1. Use lean meat only (fat goes rancid)
  2. Slice thin, against the grain
  3. Remove all visible fat
  4. Season with salt (essential for preservation)
  5. Dry until bendable but not breaking
  6. Should crack but not snap

Salt Curing

How It Works

Salt draws out moisture and creates an environment hostile to bacteria.

Dry Curing Method

  1. Prepare meat - Trim excess fat
  2. Apply cure - Coat thoroughly with salt mixture
  3. Pack - Layer in container with salt
  4. Wait - 2-3 days per pound at cool temps
  5. Rinse - Remove excess salt
  6. Dry - Air dry or smoke

Basic Cure Mix

  • 1 lb salt
  • 1/2 cup sugar
  • Optional: spices, curing salt (pink salt #1)

Pickling

How It Works

Acid environment (vinegar) prevents bacterial growth.

Basic Quick Pickle

Ingredients:

  • 2 cups vinegar (5% acidity)
  • 2 cups water
  • 2 tbsp salt
  • Optional: sugar, spices

Method:

  1. Heat brine to dissolve salt
  2. Pack vegetables in clean jars
  3. Pour hot brine over vegetables
  4. Cool and store

Best Vegetables for Pickling

  • Cucumbers
  • Onions
  • Peppers
  • Green beans
  • Carrots
  • Cabbage (sauerkraut)

Fermentation

How It Works

Beneficial bacteria convert sugars to acid, preserving the food and adding nutrition.

Basic Sauerkraut

Ingredients:

  • Cabbage
  • Salt (2% of cabbage weight)

Method:

  1. Shred cabbage finely
  2. Mix with salt in bowl
  3. Pound or squeeze until liquid releases
  4. Pack into jar, pressing down
  5. Liquid should cover cabbage
  6. Weight to keep submerged
  7. Cover loosely (needs to vent)
  8. Ferment 3-4 weeks at room temperature

Root Cellaring

How It Works

Cool, humid, dark conditions slow spoilage of root vegetables.

Ideal Conditions

  • Temperature: 32-40°F
  • Humidity: 85-95%
  • Dark
  • Good ventilation

Food Safety Warning

Critical Points:

  • Follow established methods exactly
  • Don't improvise with low-acid foods
  • When in doubt, throw it out
  • Botulism has no smell or taste
  • Some preservation requires specific conditions

Signs of Spoilage:

  • Off smells
  • Mold (except as designed)
  • Slimy texture
  • Unusual colors
  • Bulging containers (canned goods)
  • Bubbling (when not fermenting)
