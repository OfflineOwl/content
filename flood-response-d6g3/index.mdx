# Flood Response & Water Safety

> **Underestimated Killer** - Floods cause more deaths than any other natural disaster. Most flood deaths occur when people attempt to walk or drive through flood water.

## Flood Types

| Type | Warning Time | Characteristics |
|------|-------------|-----------------|
| Flash flood | Minutes to hours | Rapid onset, very dangerous |
| River flood | Hours to days | Rising water, prolonged |
| Coastal flood | Hours (storm surge) | Wind-driven, saltwater |
| Urban flood | Hours | Poor drainage, fast |

## Before Flooding

### Know Your Risk

- Are you in a flood zone?
- What's your elevation?
- Does your area flood historically?
- Where will water come from?

### Preparation

- Have evacuation route planned
- Know how to shut off utilities
- Move valuables to higher floors
- Have sandbags or flood barriers ready
- Keep emergency supplies upstairs

## During Flood Warning

### Immediate Actions

1. Monitor emergency broadcasts
2. Prepare to evacuate
3. Move important items up
4. Charge devices
5. Fill bathtub with clean water
6. Don't wait until last minute to leave

## During Flooding

### The Golden Rule

**Turn Around, Don't Drown**

- 6 inches of water can knock you down
- 12 inches can carry away a vehicle
- 2 feet can float most vehicles

### If Ordered to Evacuate

- Leave immediately
- Follow designated routes
- Don't drive through flooded roads
- Don't walk through flowing water

### If Trapped by Rising Water

**In Building:**
- Go to highest floor
- Don't go into attic (may trap you)
- Go to roof if necessary
- Signal for help
- Call 911 with location

**In Vehicle:**
- If water is rising, get out
- Move to higher ground
- Abandon vehicle if necessary
- Don't try to drive through flood

### Driving Through Water

**DON'T.**

If you must:
- Turn around if depth is unknown
- 6 inches = loss of control
- 12 inches = vehicle floats
- Roadbed may be washed out
- Power lines may be down

## After Flooding

### Safety Hazards

- Contaminated water everywhere
- Electrical hazards
- Structural damage
- Gas leaks
- Debris and sharp objects
- Displaced animals (snakes, etc.)

### Returning Home

**Only when cleared by authorities:**
- Wear protective gear
- Don't touch electrical equipment if wet
- Document damage before cleanup
- Check for structural damage
- Beware of gas leaks

### Water Safety

- Assume all flood water is contaminated
- Don't drink tap water until cleared
- Don't use well water until tested
- Boil water if unsure
- Discard food touched by flood water

### Cleanup Safety

- Wear boots, gloves, mask
- Open windows for ventilation
- Discard items that can't be cleaned
- Clean and disinfect everything
- Watch for mold (starts within 24-48 hours)
