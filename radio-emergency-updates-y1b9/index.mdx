# Radio Use for Emergency Updates

> **Your Lifeline to Information** - When power is out and internet is down, radio remains the most reliable source of emergency information. Knowing how to use it effectively can save your life.

## Why Radio Matters

### Advantages

- Works without internet
- Works without cell service
- Battery/hand-crank powered options
- Wide coverage area
- Official emergency broadcasts
- Real-time information

### When Radio is Essential

- Power outages
- Natural disasters
- Civil emergencies
- Evacuation orders
- All-hazard emergencies

---

## Types of Emergency Radio

### NOAA Weather Radio

**Dedicated weather/emergency broadcasts:**
- National Weather Service operated
- 24/7 broadcasts
- Automatic alerts for your county
- Seven frequencies (162.400-162.550 MHz)

**Best for:** Weather alerts, official emergency information

### AM/FM Radio

**Commercial broadcast stations:**
- Wide availability
- Local news and updates
- May have emergency broadcasting agreements
- AM carries farther at night

**Best for:** Local news, community information

### Shortwave Radio

**International broadcasts:**
- Long-range reception
- Multiple countries/perspectives
- Requires larger antenna
- More complex operation

**Best for:** Large-scale events, international news

### Scanner/Public Safety Radio

**Police, fire, EMS communications:**
- Real-time emergency response
- Local activity awareness
- May require programming
- Legal to listen (in most areas)

**Best for:** Situational awareness

## Operating Your Radio

### Finding Emergency Stations

**NOAA Weather Radio:**
1. Turn to WX band
2. Find clearest frequency for your area
3. Program SAME code for your county

**AM/FM Emergency:**
- Local news stations often have emergency agreements
- Note emergency frequencies in advance
- Try AM at night (better range)

### SAME (Specific Area Message Encoding)

- Six-digit code for your county
- Programs radio to alert only for your area
- Find your code at weather.gov
- Program multiple counties if near borders

### Battery Conservation

- Turn off when not actively listening
- Use scheduled check-in times
- Lower volume saves power
- Hand-crank to extend battery
- Have spare batteries

## Understanding Emergency Alerts

### Alert Types

| Alert | Meaning |
|-------|---------|
| **Watch** | Conditions favorable, prepare |
| **Warning** | Event occurring or imminent, take action |
| **Emergency** | Immediate threat to life |
| **Advisory** | Significant inconvenience |

### Alert Tones

- **Attention tone:** Steady 8-second tone before alerts
- **EAS tones:** Three-tone attention signal
- Learn to recognize these sounds

### Alert Sources

- National Weather Service
- Emergency Management
- FEMA
- Local authorities
- Amber Alert system

---

## Radio Check-In Schedule

During emergencies, establish listening schedule:

**Suggested schedule:**
- Top of each hour for 10 minutes
- Full hour at 6am, 12pm, 6pm
- Immediately after any alert tone
- Before making major decisions

## Two-Way Radio Communication

### FRS/GMRS Radios

**Family Radio Service:**
- No license required
- 1-2 mile range typical
- 22 channels available

**General Mobile Radio Service:**
- License required ($35, no test)
- Greater range (5-25 miles)
- More power allowed

### Using Two-Way Radios

- Establish primary/backup channels
- Set check-in times
- Keep transmissions brief
- Use standard phrases
- Monitor before transmitting
