# Decontamination Basics

> **Remove the Threat** - If you've been exposed to hazardous materials, proper decontamination reduces harm and prevents spreading contamination to others.

## When Decontamination is Needed

### Situations Requiring Decon

- Exposure to chemical release
- Contact with biological hazard
- Radiological fallout exposure
- Unknown substance contact
- Industrial accident exposure

### Signs You Need Decon

- You were outdoors during event
- Visible contamination on clothing
- Smell chemicals on yourself
- Officials advise decontamination
- Symptoms of exposure

## Basic Decontamination Steps

### The Process

**1. Remove Clothing**
- Remove outer clothing first
- Cut off if necessary (don't pull over head)
- Place in plastic bag
- Seal bag
- This removes ~90% of contamination

**2. Rinse**
- Rinse body with water
- Lukewarm, not hot
- Start from head, work down
- Avoid spreading contamination

**3. Wash**
- Use soap or shampoo
- Wash entire body
- Pay attention to hair
- **Don't use conditioner** (binds particles)
- Clean under nails

**4. Dry and Dress**
- Use clean towel
- Put on clean clothes
- Bag used towel for disposal

## Specific Contamination Types

### Chemical

- Remove all contaminated clothing
- Rinse immediately
- Wash with soap and water
- Flush eyes if affected
- Seek medical attention

### Biological

- Remove contaminated clothing
- Shower with soap
- Wash hands thoroughly
- Monitor for symptoms
- Follow medical guidance

### Radiological

- Remove outer clothing
- Shower thoroughly
- Wash hair with shampoo only
- Blow nose, wipe ears, clean eyelids
- Put on clean clothes

## Special Considerations

### Eyes

If eyes contaminated:
- Flush with clean water
- 15-20 minutes minimum
- Flush from inner to outer corner
- Don't rub
- Seek medical care

### Wounds

If skin is broken:
- Cover wound before washing
- Avoid contaminating wound
- Wash around wound carefully
- Seek medical attention

## Setting Up Decontamination Area

### At Home

**Ideal setup:**
- Entry point outside or in garage
- Plastic sheeting on floor
- Bags for clothing
- Water source
- Clean clothes ready
- Disposal plan for contaminated items

### Improvised Setup

- Garden hose (lukewarm water)
- Plastic bags
- Soap or dish detergent
- Clean towels and clothes

## Don'ts of Decontamination

- **Don't** use hot water (opens pores)
- **Don't** scrub vigorously (irritates skin)
- **Don't** use conditioner (binds particles)
- **Don't** contaminate others
- **Don't** re-enter contaminated area
- **Don't** eat or drink until clean

## Helping Others Decontaminate

### Protect Yourself First

- Wear PPE (gloves, mask, eye protection)
- Minimize your exposure
- Decontaminate yourself after helping
- Don't become a victim

### Assisting Someone

1. Guide them to decon area
2. Help remove clothing if needed
3. Direct water flow
4. Provide clean materials
5. Monitor their condition
