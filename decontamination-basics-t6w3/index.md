# Decontamination Basics

> If a chemical, radioactive dust, or a biological agent got on you, getting it off fast lowers the harm and keeps you from spreading it. Taking off your clothes removes most of it. Washing with soap and water removes most of the rest.

## Do This Now

1. **Get away from the source** and into fresh air or a safe building (for radiation, get inside).
2. **Take off your clothes**, starting with the outer layer, ideally within 10 minutes for a chemical. This removes about 80-90% of the contamination. **Cut off** anything you'd have to pull over your head; if you can't, hold your breath and close your eyes while you pull it off.
3. **Bag it.** Put clothes, jewelry, and contact lenses in a plastic bag, close it, and put that in a second bag. Keep it away from people and pets, and don't put it in the regular trash.
4. **Wash** with lukewarm water and mild soap, head to toe, for at least 2-3 minutes. **Don't scrub**, scald, or scratch your skin. Keep water out of your eyes, nose, and mouth. Keep cuts covered.
5. **No shower?** Blot (don't rub) your skin, face, hands, and hair with a moist wipe, wet cloth, or damp paper towel.
6. **Eyes burning or vision blurry?** Flush them with clean water for 10-15 minutes. Don't use eye drops.
7. **Put on clean clothes** that were stored away from the contamination.
8. **Get help:** call **911** or **Poison Control (1-800-222-1222, US)** if you have symptoms, can't get the chemical off, or swallowed it.

**Call 911 right away** for trouble breathing, chest tightness, seizures, collapse, or burns.

**If a chemical was swallowed:** don't make yourself throw up and don't drink anything else. Call Poison Control or 911.

## When Decontamination Is Needed

### Situations

- A chemical release, spill, or industrial accident
- Contact with a suspicious powder or spray (possible biological agent)
- Being outside when radioactive fallout or dust was falling
- Contact with an unknown substance

### Signs You Need It

- You were outdoors during the event
- You can see or smell something on your skin or clothes
- Your skin, eyes, nose, or throat burn or itch
- Officials tell you to decontaminate

Don't wait to find out what the chemical is. Start right away.

## Basic Decontamination Steps

**1. Remove clothing**
- Take off your outer clothing first, then the rest if a chemical soaked through
- Cut off anything that goes over your head
- Remove jewelry, watches, and contact lenses
- This removes about 80-90% of the contamination

**2. Bag it**
- Plastic bag, closed, inside a second plastic bag
- Keep it away from people and pets
- Don't put it in the regular trash; tell local officials where it is

**3. Wash**
- Lukewarm water and mild soap
- Start at your head and work down
- At least 2-3 minutes
- Don't scrub, scald, or scratch
- Keep water out of your eyes, nose, and mouth
- Keep cuts and scrapes covered

**4. Dry and dress**
- Dry off with a clean towel or anything absorbent
- Put on clean clothes stored away from the contamination
- Bag the used towel
- Stay warm; washing in cold weather can chill you quickly

**Tap water is fine for washing** even if officials say not to drink it.

## By Type of Contamination

### Chemical

- Get to fresh air: outdoors, move upwind; indoors, leave the building if the chemical is inside
- Undress and wash within 10 minutes if you can
- Cut off clothing that goes over your head
- Wash 2-3 minutes with lukewarm water and mild soap; don't scrub
- Flush eyes 10-15 minutes if they burn or your vision is blurry
- Glasses: soak them in household bleach, then rinse and dry before wearing
- Call Poison Control or 911 if you have symptoms; you may need a medical check even if you feel OK

### Biological

- Get away from the suspicious substance
- Cover your mouth and nose with layers of fabric or a mask
- Take off and bag your clothes and personal items
- Wash with soap and water and put on clean clothes
- Contact authorities and get medical advice
- Follow public health instructions about medications, vaccines, or staying away from others

### Radiological

- Take off your outer layer of clothing carefully, without shaking it (removes up to 90%)
- Shower with warm water and lots of soap
- Wash hair with shampoo or soap. **Don't use conditioner**; it makes radioactive material stick to hair
- Gently blow your nose, and wipe your eyelids, eyelashes, and ears
- Put on clean clothes
- Don't rely on hand sanitizer, and don't use household cleaning wipes on skin

## Special Considerations

### Eyes

If your eyes burn or you can't see normally:
- Take out contact lenses and bag them
- Flush with clean, lukewarm water for 10-15 minutes
- Hold your eyelids open and let water run from the inner corner (by the nose) to the outer corner
- Don't rub your eyes and don't use eye drops
- Get medical care after flushing

### Wounds

- Keep cuts and scrapes covered while you wash
- Avoid washing contamination into the wound
- Get medical care for wounds that may be contaminated

### Children

- Put on a mask and gloves if you have them, then help them through every step
- Keep them warm afterward
- Cut off clothing rather than pulling it over their heads

### Pets

- Wear gloves if you can
- For radioactive dust: gently brush the coat, then wash with shampoo or soap and water and rinse well
- Wash your own hands and face afterward
- Ask a veterinarian if your pet may have been exposed to a chemical

## Setting Up a Decontamination Area

### At Home

**Ideal setup:**
- Just inside an entrance, garage, or porch, away from air intakes
- Plastic sheeting on the floor
- Plastic bags for clothing (double-bag)
- Soap and water, or moist wipes
- Clean towels and clothes ready
- A plan for where the bags will go until officials collect them

### Improvised

- A garden hose works outdoors in warm weather; use lukewarm water if you can
- Dish soap or any mild soap
- Trash bags
- Clean towels and clothes

---

## Don'ts of Decontamination

- **Don't** scald, scrub, or scratch your skin (broken skin lets contamination in)
- **Don't** pull contaminated clothing over your head
- **Don't** use conditioner after radioactive contamination
- **Don't** use eye drops after a chemical splash
- **Don't** make yourself vomit if you swallowed a chemical
- **Don't** contaminate others or clean areas
- **Don't** eat, drink, or touch your face until you're clean
- **Don't** put contaminated items in the regular trash

---

## Helping Others Decontaminate

### Protect Yourself First

- Wear waterproof gloves, a mask, and eye protection if you have them
- Avoid touching the contamination
- Wash your own hands, face, and exposed skin afterward
- Don't become a second victim

### Assisting Someone

1. Guide them to the decontamination area
2. Help remove clothing if needed (cut it off rather than pulling it over the head)
3. Help them wash, gently, head to toe
4. Give them clean towels and clothes
5. Watch for trouble breathing or worsening symptoms, and call 911 if they appear
