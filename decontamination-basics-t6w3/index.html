Decontamination Basics

⚠️ CRITICAL SAFETY

If a chemical, radioactive dust, or a biological agent got on you, get it off fast. Taking off your clothes removes most of it; washing with soap and water removes most of the rest. Call 911 for trouble breathing, seizures, collapse, or burns. Poison Control (US): 1-800-222-1222.

Do This Now

  1. Get away from the source and into fresh air or a safe building (for radiation, get inside).
  2. Take off your clothes, starting with the outer layer, ideally within 10 minutes for a chemical. This removes about 80-90% of the contamination. Cut off anything you'd have to pull over your head; if you can't, hold your breath and close your eyes while you pull it off.
  3. Bag it. Put clothes, jewelry, and contact lenses in a plastic bag, close it, and put that in a second bag. Keep it away from people and pets, and out of the regular trash.
  4. Wash with lukewarm water and mild soap, head to toe, for at least 2-3 minutes. Don't scrub, scald, or scratch your skin. Keep water out of your eyes, nose, and mouth. Keep cuts covered.
  5. No shower? Blot (don't rub) your skin, face, hands, and hair with a moist wipe, wet cloth, or damp paper towel.
  6. Eyes burning or vision blurry? Flush with clean water for 10-15 minutes. No eye drops.
  7. Put on clean clothes that were stored away from the contamination.
  8. Get help: call 911 or Poison Control if you have symptoms, can't get the chemical off, or swallowed it.

If a chemical was swallowed: don't make yourself throw up and don't drink anything else. Call Poison Control or 911.

Tap water is fine for washing even if officials say not to drink it.

Identify Your Contamination Type

Different contamination types need slightly different steps:

Industrial spills, household chemicals, unknown substances

Steps for Chemical Decon:
1Get to fresh air. Outdoors, move upwind. Indoors, leave the building if the chemical is inside.
2Take off your clothes within 10 minutes if you can. Cut off anything that goes over your head.
3Double-bag clothing, jewelry, and contact lenses. Keep the bag out of the regular trash.
4Wash at least 2-3 minutes with lukewarm water and mild soap, head to toe
5Eyes burning? Flush with clean water for 10-15 minutes. No eye drops.
6Call Poison Control (1-800-222-1222) or 911 if you have symptoms or cannot get it off
⚠️ Warning: Do NOT scrub your skin. If the chemical was swallowed, do NOT make yourself vomit.

Infectious disease, bodily fluids, biological agents

Steps for Biological Decon:
1Get away from the suspicious substance. Do not touch or clean it up.
2Cover your mouth and nose with layers of fabric or a mask
3Take off and bag your clothes and personal items
4Wash with soap and water, then put on clean clothes
5Report it to authorities and follow public health instructions
⚠️ Warning: Do not touch your face until you have washed

Nuclear fallout, radiation exposure, dirty bomb

Steps for Radiological Decon:
1Take off your outer layer of clothing without shaking it (removes up to 90%)
2Seal it in a plastic bag, away from people and pets
3Shower with warm water and lots of soap. Do not scald, scrub, or scratch.
4Wash hair with shampoo or soap. NO conditioner.
5Gently blow your nose; wipe eyelids, eyelashes, and ears
6Put on clean clothes that were stored inside
⚠️ Warning: NEVER use conditioner: it makes radioactive material stick to hair. Hand sanitizer does not remove it.

Do's and Don'ts

✅ DO

✓Use lukewarm water
✓Work from head to toe
✓Cut off clothing that goes over your head
✓Double-bag contaminated items
✓Wear gloves and a mask when helping others

❌ DON'T

✗Scald, scrub, or scratch your skin
✗Pull contaminated clothes over your head
✗Use conditioner after radiation exposure
✗Use eye drops after a chemical splash
✗Eat/drink until fully clean

Also: don't make yourself vomit if you swallowed a chemical, don't rely on hand sanitizer or household cleaning wipes for radioactive contamination, and don't put contaminated items in the regular trash.

Special Situations

  • Wounds: keep cuts and scrapes covered while you wash, and get medical care for wounds that may be contaminated.
  • Glasses: after a chemical, soak them in household bleach, then rinse and dry before wearing.
  • Children: put on a mask and gloves if you have them, then help them through every step. Cut clothing off rather than pulling it over their heads. Keep them warm afterward.
  • Pets: wear gloves. For radioactive dust, gently brush the coat, then wash with shampoo or soap and water. Wash your own hands and face afterward. Ask a veterinarian about chemical exposure.
  • Cold weather: dry off and dress quickly; washing can chill you fast.

Setting Up a Decon Area

Location

  • Just inside an entrance, garage, or porch
  • Away from HVAC air intakes
  • Near a water source
  • Easy to clean

Supplies Ready

  • Plastic sheeting for the floor
  • Trash bags (to double-bag clothing)
  • Mild soap, water, moist wipes
  • Clean towels and clothes

Helping Others Decontaminate

⚠️ Protect Yourself First

  1. Wear waterproof gloves, a mask, and eye protection if you have them
  2. Avoid touching the contamination
  3. Help them undress (cut off over-the-head clothing) and wash gently
  4. Watch for trouble breathing or worse symptoms; call 911 if they appear
  5. Wash your own hands, face, and exposed skin afterward
