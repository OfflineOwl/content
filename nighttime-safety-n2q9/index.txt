NIGHTTIME SAFETY DURING EMERGENCIES
===================================

Darkness adds significant risk during emergencies. This guide
covers staying safe when the lights are out.

CHALLENGES OF DARKNESS
----------------------

REDUCED VISIBILITY:
- Can't see hazards
- Can't see threats
- Navigation difficult
- Can't assess situations

PSYCHOLOGICAL EFFECTS:
- Increased anxiety
- Disorientation
- Heightened fear
- Impaired judgment

PRACTICAL PROBLEMS:
- Tasks take longer
- Injuries more likely
- Communication harder
- Help harder to find

LIGHT DISCIPLINE
----------------

WHEN TO USE LIGHT:
- Necessary tasks
- Identifying hazards
- Emergency signaling
- Medical needs

WHEN TO AVOID LIGHT:
- Hiding from threats
- Moving undetected
- Security concerns
- Conserving batteries

TYPES OF LIGHT:
White: Most visible, best for work
Red: Preserves night vision, less visible
Blue: Less visible than white
No light: Best for concealment

RED LIGHT BENEFITS:
- Preserves night vision
- Less visible to others
- Still allows task completion
- Good for security situations

NIGHT NAVIGATION
----------------

MOVING IN DARKNESS:

Before moving:
- Let eyes adjust (20+ minutes ideal)
- Plan your route
- Identify landmarks
- Clear path of obstacles

While moving:
- Go slowly
- Use peripheral vision
- Feel with hands/feet
- Low profile
- Regular pauses to listen

AIDS:
- Glow sticks (mark paths)
- Reflective tape
- Familiar routes
- Handrails and walls

HOME SAFETY AT NIGHT
--------------------

WITHOUT POWER:

LIGHTING:
- Place lights strategically
- Battery-powered preferred
- Candles = fire risk
- Don't carry open flames

HAZARD PREVENTION:
- Clear pathways
- Mark obstacles
- Glow sticks at hazards
- Keep flashlight at bedside

SECURITY:
- Light discipline if threatened
- Secure all entry points
- Awareness of surroundings
- Communication plan

FIRE SAFETY:
- Avoid candles if possible
- Never leave flames unattended
- Keep extinguisher accessible
- Clear combustibles

SECURITY AT NIGHT
-----------------

DEFENSIVE CONSIDERATIONS:
- Darkness helps AND hurts
- You can't see, they can't see
- Familiar territory advantage
- Sound more important

IF SHELTERING:
- Minimize light showing outside
- Cover windows
- Stay quiet
- Listen for threats
- Have escape route

IF MOVING:
- Avoid lit areas (you're visible)
- Use shadows
- Move quietly
- Stop and listen frequently
- Travel with others if possible

CHILDREN AT NIGHT
-----------------

MANAGING FEAR:
- Comfort items nearby
- Gentle night lights (red)
- Stay close together
- Reassure safety
- Routine helps

SAFETY:
- Keep near adults
- Glow sticks for identification
- Don't let wander
- Designated sleep area

COMMUNICATION AT NIGHT
----------------------

CHALLENGES:
- Can't see hand signals
- Identifying people harder
- Sounds travel differently
- Mistakes more likely

SOLUTIONS:
- Pre-arranged signals
- Verbal challenges/passwords
- Touch signals
- Glow sticks for identification

CHALLENGE/PASSWORD:
If someone approaches in dark:
You say: Challenge word
They respond: Password
Confirms identity

SLEEP CONSIDERATIONS
--------------------

SECURITY WHILE SLEEPING:
- Someone stays awake (watch)
- Rotation schedule
- Early warning system
- Secure entry points
- Emergency plan if awakened

SLEEP AIDS:
- Familiar items
- Comfortable as possible
- Eye mask (for light changes)
- Earplugs (selective use)

WAKE-UP PROTOCOL:
- How to wake others quickly
- Without panic
- Ready to respond

EMERGENCY TASKS AT NIGHT
------------------------

MEDICAL:
- Have light source
- Know first aid location
- Pre-positioned supplies
- Headlamp keeps hands free

EVACUATION:
- Know route by feel
- Glow sticks mark path
- Rally point visible
- Account for everyone

QUICK REFERENCE
---------------

Red light = preserves night vision

No light = best for concealment
