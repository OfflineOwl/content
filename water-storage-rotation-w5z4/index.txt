WATER STORAGE & ROTATION
========================

Stored water is your first line of defense when the taps stop or the
water becomes unsafe. This guide covers how much to store, which
containers to use, and how to keep stored water safe.

DO THIS NOW
-----------

1. Count people and pets. Plan at least 1 gallon (about 4 liters)
   per person per day.
2. Start with a 3-day supply. Build toward 2 weeks if you have space.
3. Easiest and safest: buy commercially bottled water and keep it
   sealed until you need it.
4. Filling your own containers? Use food-grade containers, wash and
   sanitize them, fill with chlorinated tap water, and label them
   "drinking water" with the date.
5. Store in a cool, dark place, away from gasoline, pesticides, and
   other chemicals.
6. Replace water you bottled yourself every 6 months. Use store-bought
   water by its expiration or "use by" date.
7. Storm or outage coming? Fill extra containers and the bathtub
   (see EMERGENCY WATER EXPANSION below).

HOW MUCH TO STORE
-----------------

MINIMUM: 1 gallon per person per day, for drinking and sanitation.

- A normally active person needs about 3/4 gallon of fluid a day
  for drinking alone.
- The rest covers basic hygiene and food preparation.

STORE MORE FOR:
- Hot climates (drinking needs can double in very hot weather)
- Pregnant or nursing women
- Children
- People who are sick
- Pets

STORAGE TARGETS:
- 3 days: minimum for every household (CDC, Ready.gov)
- 2 weeks: recommended if you have space (CDC, FEMA)
- Longer: add a way to collect and treat rainwater or other sources

FAMILY EXAMPLE (4 people, 1 gallon each per day):
- 3 days: 12 gallons
- 2 weeks: 56 gallons
- 1 month (30 days): 120 gallons

Add more for pets and hot weather.

CONTAINER OPTIONS
-----------------

BEST: COMMERCIALLY BOTTLED WATER
- Safest and most reliable option
- Keep it sealed in the original container until you need it
- Observe the expiration or "use by" date

FOOD-GRADE WATER STORAGE CONTAINERS:

Food-Grade Jugs (1-7 gallons)
- Pros: Portable, affordable, stackable
- Cons: Many containers needed
- Best for: Portable supply, go-bag, evacuation

Stackable Bricks (about 3-5 gallons)
- Pros: Stackable, modular, portable
- Cons: Cost more per gallon
- Best for: Organized storage in small spaces

55-Gallon Drums
- Pros: Large capacity, lower cost per gallon
- Cons: Over 450 lb when full, need a pump to get water out,
  can't be moved
- Best for: Primary home storage

IBC Totes (about 275 gallons)
- Pros: Very large capacity
- Cons: Very heavy, need space and a sturdy base; buy food-grade
  totes that held only food or water
- Best for: Large-scale home storage

Water Storage Tanks (100+ gallons)
- Pros: Built for purpose, various sizes
- Cons: Expensive, fixed location
- Best for: Permanent installation

REUSING CONTAINERS:

OK to reuse (after washing and sanitizing):
- 2-liter plastic soda bottles
- Food-grade buckets or jugs with tight lids that held only food
  or water

DON'T USE:
- Milk jugs or cardboard containers that held milk or fruit juice
  (milk protein and fruit sugar can't be fully washed out and feed
  bacteria; cardboard leaks)
- Any container that held toxic chemicals, including bleach,
  pesticides, or petroleum products
- Non-food-grade plastic
- Glass (heavy and breaks)
- Containers that can't be sealed tightly

Pick containers with tight-closing caps and, if possible, a narrow
opening so hands and scoops stay out.

PREPARING CONTAINERS
--------------------

NEW FOOD-GRADE CONTAINERS:
Wash and rinse, or follow the manufacturer's instructions.

REUSED CONTAINERS (CDC method):
1. Wash with dish soap and water. Rinse well.
2. Mix 1 teaspoon of unscented liquid household chlorine bleach
   (5-9% sodium hypochlorite) into 1 quart (4 cups) of water.
3. Pour the solution into the container, cap it, and shake so it
   touches every inside surface.
4. Wait at least 30 seconds, then pour it out.
5. Let the container air-dry, or rinse it with clean, safe water.

FILLING CONTAINERS
------------------

CHLORINATED TAP WATER (most city water):
- Fill straight from the tap. Nothing else needs to be added.
- Don't touch the inside of the cap or container with your fingers.

WELL WATER OR OTHER UNCHLORINATED WATER:
- Have the well tested if you can.
- Disinfect before storing. With unscented bleach, EPA's dose is
  8 drops of 6% bleach or 6 drops of 8.25% bleach per gallon
  (2 drops per quart or liter). Double it if the water is cloudy,
  colored, or very cold. Stir, wait 30 minutes, then seal.
- See the Water Purification guide for other methods.

COMMERCIAL WATER PRESERVERS:
- Follow the product's instructions and replacement interval.

LABEL EVERY CONTAINER:
"DRINKING WATER" plus the date you filled it.

STORAGE CONDITIONS
------------------

KEEP IT:
- Cool: about 50-70°F (10-21°C)
- Dark: out of direct sunlight
- Away from gasoline, kerosene, paint, pesticides, and other
  chemicals (plastic can absorb their vapors)
- Where it won't freeze (freezing can crack containers)
- Off the floor on a shelf or pallet, where you can inspect it and
  where it is safer from floods

AVOID:
- Direct sunlight (algae growth, plastic breakdown)
- Hot attics, cars, and sheds
- Garages with fuel, chemicals, or freezing temperatures

GOOD LOCATIONS:
- Interior closets
- Under beds
- Cool basements that don't flood
- Several places in the house, so one damaged area doesn't take
  all your water

ROTATION SCHEDULE
-----------------

- Water you bottled yourself: replace every 6 months.
- Commercially bottled water: use by its expiration or
  "use by" date; keep it sealed.
- Commercial water preservers: follow the product label.

ROTATION PROCESS:
1. Label every container with the fill date.
2. Use the oldest water first.
3. Use rotated-out water for cooking, cleaning, or plants.
4. Wash, sanitize, and refill containers right away.
5. Set a reminder (for example, when clocks change in spring
   and fall).

WHY ROTATION MATTERS:
- Chlorine in tap water fades over time
- Containers can crack, leak, or lose their seal
- Old containers you never check can fail when you need them

USING STORED WATER SAFELY
-------------------------

- Pour water out, or use a clean scoop each time. Don't dip hands
  or cups into the container.
- Keep the container covered between uses.
- Open one container at a time.

55-GALLON DRUMS:
- Siphon or drum pump (manual or battery) and a bung wrench
- Fill smaller containers for daily use
- Practice getting water out before an emergency

IBC TOTES:
- Valve at the bottom
- Raise the tote on a sturdy platform for gravity flow
- Fill smaller containers for daily use

LARGE TANKS:
- Gravity feed if elevated, pump if not
- Know how to get water out without power

CHECK BEFORE DRINKING
---------------------

LOOK FOR:
- Unusual color or cloudiness
- Strange smell or taste
- Anything floating in it
- Cracked containers or broken seals

IF WATER SEEMS OFF:
- Don't drink it as-is
- Use it for flushing or cleaning, or
- Treat it (boil or disinfect) before drinking, or
- Discard it and use another container

EMERGENCY WATER EXPANSION
-------------------------

When a storm, outage, or water shutoff is forecast:

1. Fill every clean, food-grade container you have with tap water
2. Fill pitchers, pots, and bottles
3. Fill the bathtub (a tub liner keeps it cleaner). Use tub water
   for flushing and washing, or treat it before drinking
4. Freeze containers of water (leave room for expansion). They help
   keep food cold and become drinking water as they melt
5. Know how to drain your water heater and pipes (see the Finding
   Safe Water guide)

DON'T RATION DRINKING WATER
---------------------------

If supplies run low, drink what you need today and look for more
for tomorrow. Don't ration drinking water unless officials tell you
to. Cut back on other uses instead, and reduce your need by staying
cool and resting. See the Water Rationing guide.

QUICK REFERENCE
---------------

AMOUNT:
1 gallon per person per day
Minimum 3 days; goal 2 weeks

CONTAINERS:
Bottled water, food-grade containers, or clean soda bottles
Never milk or juice jugs, or chemical containers

SANITIZE CONTAINERS:
1 tsp unscented bleach per quart of water, shake, wait 30 seconds

CONDITIONS:
Cool (50-70°F), dark, away from chemicals

REPLACE:
Self-filled water every 6 months
Store-bought water by its date
