WATER STORAGE & ROTATION
========================

Stored water is your first line of defense against water emergencies.
This guide covers proper storage methods to keep your water safe.

HOW MUCH TO STORE
-----------------

MINIMUM RECOMMENDATION:
1 gallon per person per day

This covers:
- Drinking: 1/2 gallon
- Hygiene/cooking: 1/2 gallon

STORAGE TARGETS:
- 3 days (72 hours): FEMA minimum
- 2 weeks: Recommended for most households
- 1 month: Better preparedness
- Longer: Consider rainwater collection

FAMILY EXAMPLE (4 people):
- 3 days: 12 gallons
- 2 weeks: 56 gallons
- 1 month: 120 gallons

CONTAINER OPTIONS
-----------------

COMMERCIAL WATER CONTAINERS:

Food-Grade Plastic Jugs (1-7 gallons)
- Pros: Portable, affordable, stackable
- Cons: Limited capacity, many containers
- Best for: Portable supply, bug-out bag

WaterBricks (3.5 gallons each)
- Pros: Stackable, modular, portable
- Cons: More expensive, smaller capacity
- Best for: Organized storage, mobility

55-Gallon Drums
- Pros: Large capacity, affordable per gallon
- Cons: Heavy, need pump to access, hard to move
- Best for: Primary home storage

IBC Totes (275 gallons)
- Pros: Very large capacity, pallet-ready
- Cons: Heavy, need space, hard to move
- Best for: Large-scale home storage

Water Storage Tanks (100+ gallons)
- Pros: Built for purpose, various sizes
- Cons: Expensive, fixed location
- Best for: Permanent installation

CONTAINERS TO AVOID:
- Milk jugs (hard to sanitize, degrade quickly)
- Non-food-grade plastic (chemical leaching)
- Containers that held chemicals
- Cardboard juice containers
- Thin disposable bottles (long-term)

REPURPOSING CONTAINERS:

Safe to reuse (after cleaning):
- 2-liter soda bottles
- Juice containers (not cardboard)
- Food-grade buckets (with lids)
- Commercial water containers

NEVER reuse containers that held:
- Non-food substances
- Chemicals
- Bleach (unless for non-drinking)
- Petroleum products

PREPARING CONTAINERS
--------------------

FOR NEW FOOD-GRADE CONTAINERS:
1. Rinse with clean water
2. Ready to fill

FOR REUSED CONTAINERS:
1. Wash with dish soap
2. Rinse thoroughly (no soap residue)
3. Sanitize with dilute bleach solution:
   - 1 teaspoon bleach per quart of water
   - Swish to coat all surfaces
   - Let sit 30 seconds
   - Rinse with clean water
4. Air dry completely

WATER SOURCES FOR STORAGE
-------------------------

BEST OPTIONS (in order):
1. Municipal tap water (already treated)
2. Well water (test first, may need treatment)
3. Commercial bottled water
4. Filtered water

MUNICIPAL WATER:
- Already chlorinated
- Generally safe to store as-is
- May benefit from small bleach addition for long-term

WELL WATER:
- Test before storing long-term
- Treat with bleach if untreated
- Consider filtration

TREATING WATER FOR STORAGE
--------------------------

IF USING MUNICIPAL WATER:
- Usually no treatment needed
- For extra security: 1/8 teaspoon bleach per gallon
- Unscented household bleach (5.25-8.25% sodium hypochlorite)

IF USING UNTREATED WATER:
- 1/4 teaspoon bleach per gallon
- Mix thoroughly
- Wait 30 minutes before sealing

COMMERCIAL TREATMENT:
- Water preserver drops (5+ year shelf life)
- Follow manufacturer instructions
- More expensive but convenient

STORAGE CONDITIONS
------------------

IDEAL CONDITIONS:
- Cool: 50-70°F
- Dark: Away from light
- Stable temperature: Avoid freeze/thaw
- Off concrete: On wood or pallets
- Away from chemicals: No fumes to absorb

AVOID:
- Direct sunlight (algae growth, plastic degradation)
- Near gasoline, pesticides, chemicals (odor absorption)
- Extreme heat (accelerates degradation)
- Freezing (can crack containers)
- On bare concrete (absorbs chemicals)

LOCATIONS:
Good:
- Basement (if not damp)
- Interior closets
- Under beds
- Climate-controlled garage

Less ideal:
- Attic (temperature extremes)
- Outdoor (exposure)
- Near windows (light)

ROTATION SCHEDULE
-----------------

GENERAL GUIDELINES:

Store-bought sealed water: Check manufacturer date (usually 1-2 years)
Home-stored tap water: 6-12 months (or use preserver)
Home-stored with preserver: 5+ years

ROTATION PROCESS:
1. Mark containers with fill date
2. Use oldest water first (FIFO)
3. When using, refill immediately
4. Use stored water for regular needs before replacement

EASY ROTATION METHODS:
- Use stored water for cooking/drinking, refill
- Water plants with old stored water
- Use for cleaning, refill
- Cycle through before expiration

WHY ROTATION MATTERS:
- Chlorine dissipates over time
- Plastic can degrade
- Containers can develop leaks
- Ensures freshness when needed

ACCESSING STORED WATER
----------------------

55-GALLON DRUMS:
- Siphon pump (manual or battery)
- Bung wrench to open
- Small containers for daily use
- Practice accessing before emergency

IBC TOTES:
- Built-in valve at bottom
- May need raised platform for gravity flow
- Use smaller containers for daily needs

LARGE TANKS:
- Gravity feed if elevated
- Pump if not elevated
- Know how to access manually

SIGNS OF COMPROMISED WATER
--------------------------

CHECK BEFORE DRINKING:
- Unusual color
- Cloudiness
- Strange smell
- Visible contamination
- Container damage
- Compromised seal

IF WATER SEEMS OFF:
- Use for non-drinking purposes
- Or re-treat before drinking
- Or dispose safely

EMERGENCY WATER EXPANSION
-------------------------

When emergency warning received:

1. Fill bathtub (with WaterBOB if available)
2. Fill all available containers
3. Fill washing machine
4. Fill pots and pans
5. Fill bottles and pitchers
6. Fill sinks with stoppers

Can significantly increase available water with advance warning.
