# Signs of Contaminated Water

> **Know what to look for.** Contaminated water can cause serious illness or death. Many contaminants are invisible and odorless, so warning signs can only rule water out, never prove it safe. Treat any doubtful water, and refuse water that may contain chemicals.

## Do This Now

1. **Follow official notices first.** A **boil-water advisory** means boil before use. A **do-not-drink** advisory means boiling will not help; use bottled or stored water. A **do-not-use** advisory means don't use tap water for anything.
2. **Never drink floodwater**, and don't use a well that was flooded until it has been tested or disinfected.
3. **Refuse water with signs of chemicals:** fuel or chemical smell, oily rainbow sheen, unusual bright colors, dead fish or animals, or green "spilled paint" scum. Boiling and disinfecting will not make it safe, and boiling can concentrate some chemicals.
4. **Treat everything else of unknown quality:** rolling boil for 1 minute (3 minutes above 5,000 ft), or disinfect it. See the Water Purification guide.
5. **Keep children and pets away** from scummy or discolored water. Toxic algae can kill a dog within hours.
6. **If someone gets sick** after drinking suspect water, see "If Someone Gets Sick" below.

## Types of Contamination

### Germs

| Type | Usual source | Illnesses |
|------|--------------|-----------|
| **Bacteria** | Sewage, human and animal waste | E. coli infection, cholera, typhoid |
| **Viruses** | Sewage, human waste | Hepatitis A, norovirus |
| **Parasites** | Human and animal waste | Giardia, Cryptosporidium |

**Germs are usually invisible.** Clear, cold, fast-flowing water can still make you sick.

### Chemicals

| Source | Examples |
|--------|----------|
| Industrial sites and spills | Fuel, solvents, heavy metals |
| Farms | Pesticides, fertilizers, nitrate |
| Natural geology | Arsenic, fluoride, radon |
| Old plumbing | Lead, copper |
| Algal blooms | Cyanobacteria (blue-green algae) toxins |
| Nuclear or radiological incidents | Radioactive material (invisible; follow official instructions) |

### Sediment

Mud, sand, and debris make water cloudy. Cloudiness also shields germs from bleach and UV light, so clear the water before treating it.

## Visual Warning Signs

### The water itself

| What you see | Possible cause | What to do |
|--------------|----------------|------------|
| Cloudy or muddy | Sediment, sewage, runoff | Settle, filter, then treat; double the bleach dose |
| Green tint | Algae | Treat; don't use if there's scum |
| Green or blue-green scum, "spilled paint," or streaks on the surface | Toxic algae (cyanobacteria) | **Don't drink, even boiled.** Keep pets out |
| Brown or rust color | Iron, sediment | Settle and treat; find a better source if you can |
| Yellow or tea color | Tannins from plants, or chemicals | Treat; avoid if near industry |
| Blue-green stains in plumbing | Copper from pipes | Don't drink regularly; have tap water tested |
| Oily film or rainbow sheen | Fuel or oil | **Don't drink** |
| Foam that doesn't break up | Detergents or pollution | Avoid |
| Floating debris, dead insects or animals | Contamination, possible decay | Avoid |

### The surroundings

**Red flags:**
- Dead vegetation at the water's edge
- Dead fish or animals in or near the water
- Discolored soil or unusual deposits
- Pipes, drains, or outflows nearby
- Factories, mines, landfills, or sewage plants upstream
- Fields, feedlots, or grazing animals upstream
- Roads, towns, or campsites upstream
- Recent flooding

**Wildlife is not a safety sign.** Animals drink contaminated water, and their waste spreads Giardia and Cryptosporidium. Animal tracks leading to water don't mean it's safe.

## Smell Warning Signs

| Smell | Possible cause | What to do |
|-------|----------------|------------|
| Fuel, gasoline, solvent | Fuel or industrial chemicals | **Don't drink.** Find another source |
| Sewage | Fecal contamination | Don't drink. If there is truly nothing else, settle, filter, and boil |
| Rotten eggs | Hydrogen sulfide, sewage, or bacteria | Avoid; if it's your well, have it tested |
| Musty, earthy, or fishy | Algae or decaying plants | Treat; avoid if scum is present |
| Sweet chemical smell | Antifreeze or other chemicals | **Don't drink** |
| Strong bleach | Too much chlorine after treatment | Pour between clean containers and let stand a few hours |

**No smell does not mean safe.** Many dangerous germs and chemicals have no odor.

---

## Taste Warning Signs

**Don't taste-test water from an unknown source.** If your tap or treated water suddenly tastes wrong, stop drinking it and use another supply:

- **Metallic:** possible lead, copper, or iron
- **Salty:** possible salt-water intrusion (for example, after coastal flooding)
- **Bitter, soapy, or chemical:** possible chemical contamination
- **Earthy or musty:** algae or organic matter

## After Disasters

### Floods

**Assume all floodwater is contaminated** with sewage, fuel, farm chemicals, and industrial waste. Never drink it, and don't use it for washing dishes, brushing teeth, preparing food, or making ice.

- **Wells:** if floodwater covered the wellhead or entered the well, don't use it until it has been disinfected and tested.
- **Cisterns and rain barrels:** a cistern that took in floodwater or storm debris must be cleaned before use. Adding disinfectant to it does not work well.
- **Food:** throw out food that touched floodwater (see the Food Safety Without Refrigeration guide).

### Earthquakes

- Broken water and sewer pipes can mix.
- Close your home's main water valve to keep contaminated water out of your plumbing.
- Follow boil-water notices.

### Hurricanes and storms

- Power loss can shut down water treatment and pumping.
- Coastal flooding can push salt water into wells and water supplies.
- Follow local advisories.

### Chemical spills, fires, and industrial accidents

- Boiling, bleach, and filters will not make chemically contaminated water safe.
- Use stored or bottled water.
- Follow official guidance, which may last weeks.

### Radiological emergencies

Radioactive contamination can't be seen, smelled, or tasted, and boiling won't remove it. Water in sealed bottles, your water heater, and your toilet tank (not the bowl) is protected. Follow official instructions.

## Water Advisories: What They Mean

### Boil-water advisory

Issued for germs, for example after a main break, pressure loss, or treatment failure.

**Use bottled water, or boil tap water** (rolling boil for 1 minute, 3 minutes above 5,000 ft, then cool) for:
- Drinking and cooking
- Brushing teeth
- Washing fruit and vegetables
- Mixing baby formula (ready-to-feed formula is best)
- Pets' drinking water

Boil even if your tap or pitcher has a filter.

**Also:**
- **Ice:** don't use ice or water from your refrigerator dispenser.
- **Dishes by hand:** wash and rinse in hot water, then soak at least 1 minute in a basin with 1 teaspoon of unscented bleach per gallon of warm water, and air-dry. A dishwasher is generally fine if it has a sanitizing cycle or a final rinse of at least 150°F (66°C).
- **Bathing:** don't swallow the water. Give babies and young children sponge baths.
- **Handwashing:** tap water and soap are usually fine unless officials say otherwise.
- **Laundry:** wash as usual.

### Do-not-drink advisory

Issued for chemicals or toxins. **Boiling does not help.** Use bottled water for drinking, cooking, brushing teeth, preparing food and formula, making ice, and pets.

### Do-not-use advisory

Rare. Don't use tap water for anything, including bathing, because skin, eye, or lung contact may be harmful.

### When the advisory ends

Follow your water utility's instructions for flushing pipes, emptying ice makers, and replacing filters.

## Testing Water

### Home test kits

Kits can check for coliform bacteria, pH, chlorine, hardness, lead, nitrate, and some pesticides. They are a rough guide: basic kits miss many contaminants. Certified lab testing is more reliable. Your local health department can tell you which tests to run and where.

### When to test a private well

- Once a year as routine
- After flooding or nearby construction
- If someone in the household gets sick
- If the taste, smell, or appearance changes

### When to test tap water

- In older homes with possible lead pipes or fittings
- If the taste, smell, or appearance changes
- After water-main breaks nearby

## Matching Treatment to the Problem

| Problem | What works | What doesn't |
|---------|------------|--------------|
| Germs | Boiling (best); filter then disinfect; chemical disinfection; UV in clear water | An improvised sand or charcoal filter alone |
| Sediment | Settle, then filter through cloth or a coffee filter; then disinfect | Disinfecting cloudy water without clearing it |
| Chemicals | Another water source. Reverse osmosis or distillation remove some chemicals | Boiling, bleach, iodine, UV, most portable filters |
| Algae toxins | Another water source | Boiling (can concentrate toxins) |
| Nitrate or lead | Another water source, or a filter certified for that contaminant | Boiling (concentrates them), activated carbon pitchers for nitrate |

## If Someone Gets Sick

Watch for diarrhea, vomiting, stomach cramps, and fever after drinking suspect water. Keep the person drinking safe fluids.

**Get medical help, or call 911 or your local emergency number, for:**
- Bloody diarrhea
- Fever over 102°F (38.9°C)
- Vomiting so often they can't keep fluids down
- Diarrhea lasting more than 3 days
- Signs of dehydration: little or no urine, very dry mouth, dizziness when standing
- Confusion, trouble breathing, or seizures (possible chemical or toxin exposure; call Poison Control at 1-800-222-1222 in the US)

A pet that drank scummy water and is vomiting, drooling, weak, or seizing needs a veterinarian right away.

## Quick Checklist

Before using a source, check:

- [ ] **Advisories:** any boil-water, do-not-drink, or do-not-use notice?
- [ ] **Recent events:** flooding, spills, or fires nearby?
- [ ] **Location:** away from industry, farms, sewage, and roads?
- [ ] **Appearance:** no oil, scum, unusual color, or foam?
- [ ] **Smell:** no fuel, chemical, or sewage odor?
- [ ] **Surroundings:** no dead fish, animals, or vegetation?
- [ ] **Flow:** moving water rather than stagnant?

Even if every box is checked, **treat the water** before drinking.

## Summary

### Don't use water if:

- It may contain fuel, chemicals, or radioactive material
- It's floodwater
- It has an oily sheen, a fuel or chemical smell, or green or blue-green scum
- Dead fish or animals are nearby
- A do-not-drink or do-not-use advisory is in effect

### Treat thoroughly before use if:

- The source is unknown
- It's cloudy, stagnant, or slow-moving
- It's downstream of farms, towns, or camps
- A boil-water advisory is in effect
- There has been any disaster

### Lowest risk (still treat in an emergency):

- Your stored water
- Water already in your home's pipes and water heater
- Springs and properly built wells that weren't flooded
- Fast-flowing streams upstream of people
- Freshly collected rainwater from a clean surface
