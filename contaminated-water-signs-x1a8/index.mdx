# Signs of Contaminated Water

> **Know What to Look For** - Contaminated water can cause serious illness or death. While some contaminants are invisible, many warning signs can help you avoid dangerous water sources.

## Understanding Contamination Types

### Biological Contamination

| Organism | Source | Diseases |
|----------|--------|----------|
| **Bacteria** | Fecal matter, sewage | E. coli, Cholera, Typhoid |
| **Viruses** | Fecal matter, sewage | Hepatitis A, Norovirus |
| **Protozoa** | Animal/human waste | Giardia, Cryptosporidium |
| **Parasites** | Contaminated sources | Various worm infections |

**Biological contamination is usually invisible.**

### Chemical Contamination

| Source | Chemicals |
|--------|-----------|
| Industrial | Heavy metals, solvents |
| Agricultural | Pesticides, fertilizers, nitrates |
| Natural | Arsenic, fluoride, radon |
| Infrastructure | Lead, copper from pipes |

### Physical Contamination

- Sediment
- Debris
- Radioactive particles

## Visual Warning Signs

### Water Appearance

| Observation | Possible Cause | Concern Level |
|-------------|---------------|----------------|
| **Cloudy/turbid** | Sediment, bacteria | Medium-High |
| **Green tint** | Algae growth | Medium |
| **Brown/rust color** | Iron, sediment | Low-Medium |
| **Yellow tint** | Tannins, chromium | Low-Medium |
| **Blue-green tint** | Copper | Medium |
| **Oily film** | Petroleum products | High |
| **Foam/suds** | Detergents, organic matter | Medium-High |
| **Floating particles** | Various debris | Medium |

### Water Surface

**Warning signs:**
- Rainbow sheen (petroleum)
- Thick foam that doesn't dissipate
- Scum layer
- Dead insects/animals floating
- Visible debris or waste

### Surrounding Area

**Red flags:**
- Dead vegetation at water's edge
- Dead animals near water
- Absence of wildlife
- Discolored soil
- Nearby pipes or drainage
- Industrial facilities upstream
- Agricultural runoff visible

## Smell Warning Signs

### Dangerous Odors

| Smell | Possible Cause | Action |
|-------|---------------|--------|
| **Chlorine (strong)** | Over-chlorination | Aerate or use other source |
| **Rotten eggs** | Hydrogen sulfide, bacteria | Do not drink, treat heavily |
| **Sewage** | Fecal contamination | Do not drink |
| **Fishy/musty** | Algae, organic decay | Heavy treatment needed |
| **Chemical/gasoline** | Industrial/petroleum | Do not drink |
| **Metallic** | Heavy metals | Do not drink |
| **Sweet** | Antifreeze or chemicals | Do not drink |

**Important:** Absence of smell does NOT guarantee safety. Many dangerous contaminants are odorless.

## Taste Warning Signs

**Never taste test water from unknown sources.**

If water tastes:
- **Metallic** - May indicate lead, copper, iron
- **Salty** - Possible chloride contamination
- **Bitter** - Possible chemical contamination
- **Soapy** - Possible detergent contamination
- **Earthy/musty** - Possible organic contamination

**If water tastes wrong, stop drinking immediately.**

## Environmental Clues

### Upstream Assessment

Before collecting water, check upstream for:
- Industrial facilities
- Agricultural operations
- Sewage treatment plants
- Mining operations
- Roads/highways (runoff)
- Urban areas
- Camping areas
- Animal grazing areas
- Dead animals

### Natural Warning Signs

**Poor signs:**
- No animal tracks leading to water
- Birds and wildlife avoiding area
- Unusual silence near water
- Sick or dead vegetation
- Algae blooms (bright green scum)

**Better signs:**
- Animal tracks leading to water
- Healthy vegetation
- Active wildlife nearby
- Clear flowing water

## After Disasters

### Flood Contamination

**All flood water should be assumed contaminated with:**
- Sewage
- Agricultural chemicals
- Industrial waste
- Debris
- Fuel
- Deceased animals

**Never drink flood water.** Even after flooding, water sources may be contaminated for extended periods.

### After Earthquakes

- Municipal water may be compromised
- Pipes may be broken
- Sewage may mix with water supply
- Follow boil water advisories
- Well water may be affected

### After Hurricanes/Storms

- Power outages affect treatment plants
- Flooding contaminates sources
- Saltwater intrusion possible (coastal)
- Debris in water sources
- Follow local advisories

### After Industrial Accidents

- Chemical contamination possible
- Follow official guidance
- May need to avoid sources for extended time
- Boiling doesn't remove chemicals

## Boil Water Advisories

### When Issued

- Pressure loss in water system
- Main breaks
- Treatment plant problems
- Detected contamination
- After natural disasters

### What to Do

**Boil water advisory in effect:**
1. Bring water to rolling boil
2. Boil for 1 minute (3 minutes above 6,500 ft)
3. Let cool before use
4. Use boiled water for: Drinking, cooking, brushing teeth, making ice, pet water

**Until advisory lifted:**
- Use bottled water if available
- Don't use tap water for drinking
- Wash dishes in boiled water
- Laundry is usually okay

## Testing Water Quality

### Home Test Kits

**Available tests:**
- Bacteria presence (coliform)
- pH level
- Chlorine levels
- Hardness
- Lead
- Nitrates
- Pesticides

**Limitations:**
- Basic kits may not detect all contaminants
- Results are general indicators
- Professional testing more accurate

### When to Test

**Test well water:**
- Annually as routine
- After flooding
- After nearby construction
- If illness in household
- If taste/smell/appearance changes

**Test tap water:**
- If concerns about pipes (older homes)
- If taste/appearance changes
- After water main breaks nearby

## Treatment Based on Contamination Type

### Biological Contamination

**Effective treatments:**
- Boiling (most effective)
- Chemical disinfection (chlorine, iodine)
- UV treatment
- Filtration (depends on filter quality)

### Chemical Contamination

**Boiling does NOT remove chemicals.**

**May help:**
- Activated carbon filtration
- Reverse osmosis
- Distillation
- Specific chemical treatments

**Best option:** Find alternative water source.

### Sediment/Turbidity

**Treatments:**
- Let settle, decant clear water
- Pre-filter through cloth
- Filtration systems
- Then disinfect

## Quick Assessment Checklist

Before using a water source, check:

- [ ] **Location** - Away from contamination sources?
- [ ] **Appearance** - Clear? No unusual color?
- [ ] **Surface** - No oil, foam, or debris?
- [ ] **Smell** - No unusual odor?
- [ ] **Surroundings** - Healthy vegetation? Wildlife present?
- [ ] **Upstream** - No obvious pollution sources?
- [ ] **Flow** - Moving water (better) or stagnant?
- [ ] **Recent events** - Flooding, disasters, contamination notices?

**When in doubt:**
- Treat thoroughly
- Or find alternative source

## Warning Signs Summary

### Do Not Use Water If:

- Strong chemical odor
- Sewage smell
- Oil/gasoline sheen
- Bright colors (especially blue-green algae)
- Dead animals nearby
- Near known contamination
- Obviously polluted
- During boil water advisory (without boiling)

### Treat Thoroughly Before Use If:

- From unknown source
- Cloudy or turbid
- Stagnant or slow-moving
- Near agricultural areas
- Downstream from population
- Mild unusual smell
- After any disaster

### Generally Safe (with standard treatment):

- Clear, flowing streams (upstream from people)
- Springs
- Rainwater (freshly collected)
- Deep wells (properly constructed)
- Municipal water (normally)
