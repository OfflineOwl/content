# When to Seal a Room

> Sealing a room with plastic sheeting and duct tape is for **chemical** releases. It uses an **above-ground** room, because many toxic gases are heavier than air and sink into basements. For **radiation** (nuclear fallout, a dirty bomb, a nuclear plant release) do the opposite: go to the **basement or center** of the building. Sealing isn't needed.

## Do This Now (Chemical Release)

1. **Get inside.** If you're outside, move away and upwind of the source first. In a car, close windows and vents and turn off the air conditioner and heater.
2. **Close and lock** all outside doors and windows. Close fireplace dampers.
3. **Turn off** heating, air conditioning, and fans (or set the system to recirculate if you can't turn it off).
4. **Go to an interior room above ground level**, with as few windows and openings as possible. **Not the basement.** Bring water, a radio, phones and chargers, medications, and a first aid kit.
5. **Seal the room:** tape plastic sheeting over windows, vents, and the door; put wet towels under the door; tape over outlets and other gaps.
6. **Listen to the radio or alerts** and stay until officials say it's safe or tell you to evacuate.

**Call 911** (or your local emergency number) if anyone has trouble breathing, chest tightness, seizures, or burns to the skin or eyes. For questions about a chemical exposure, call **Poison Control: 1-800-222-1222** (US).

---

## Chemical vs. Radiation: Opposite Rooms

| | Chemical release | Radiation (fallout, dirty bomb, nuclear plant) |
|---|---|---|
| Where | Interior room **above ground**, as high as practical | **Basement**, or the **center** of the building away from the roof and outer walls |
| Why | Many toxic gases (chlorine, sulfur mustard) are heavier than air and collect in low areas | Fallout settles on the roof and ground; mass (concrete, brick, earth) and distance block its radiation |
| Seal with plastic? | **Yes** | **No need.** Close windows, doors, and vents; buildings don't need to be airtight to protect you |
| How long | Usually a few hours | At least 24 hours unless officials say otherwise |

**Not sure which it is?** Go to an interior room, close up, turn off ventilation, and get official information right away. Move upstairs and seal for a chemical; move down or to the center for radiation.

## When to Seal a Room

### Appropriate For

- Officials tell you to shelter in place because of a chemical release
- An industrial or transportation accident releases chemicals nearby and you can't get away
- A suspected chemical attack nearby
- An airborne hazard, when officials specifically say to seal a room

### NOT Appropriate For

- **Radiation emergencies:** go to the basement or center instead; taping wastes time
- **Fire:** get out
- **Flooding:** move to higher ground or evacuate
- **Carbon monoxide or a gas leak inside your home:** get out and call 911 from outside
- **Tornadoes:** go to the lowest floor; there's no time or need to seal
- Long periods without officials telling you to stay sealed

---

## Room Selection

### Ideal Characteristics

- Interior room with few or no windows
- **Above ground level**, as high as you can reasonably get
- Big enough for everyone: about **10 square feet of floor space per person** provides enough air for up to about 5 hours when everyone is resting
- Access to a bathroom or water if possible
- A phone, radio, or way to get alerts

### Avoid

- **Basements** (heavier-than-air chemicals collect there)
- Rooms with many windows or several outside walls
- Rooms with exhaust fans or vents you can't seal
- Very small rooms for a large group

## Sealing Materials

### Keep in Your Kit

- [ ] Plastic sheeting (CDC suggests 2-4 mil or thicker; heavier than food wrap)
- [ ] Duct tape
- [ ] Scissors
- [ ] Towels (to wet and stuff under the door)
- [ ] Battery or hand-crank radio
- [ ] Water, medications, and a first aid kit

### Pre-Cut Option

Measure and cut plastic ahead of time, several inches larger than each opening:
- Windows
- Doors
- Vents (including bathroom and kitchen exhaust fans)

Label each sheet and store them with the tape in the room you'll use.

---

## Sealing Procedure

### Step 1: Reduce Air Exchange

1. Close and lock all outside windows and doors
2. Turn off heating, air conditioning, and fans (or set to recirculate)
3. Turn off kitchen and bathroom exhaust fans and window AC units
4. Close fireplace dampers
5. Close as many interior doors as possible

### Step 2: Seal the Room

**Door:**
1. Put a wet towel along the bottom gap
2. Tape plastic over the door, corners first, then all edges

**Windows:**
1. Cover with plastic sheeting
2. Tape the corners first, then all edges

**Vents:**
1. Close them
2. Cover with plastic and tape all edges

**Other gaps:**
- Electrical outlets and switch plates
- Cable and phone jacks
- Any visible gaps to the outside

**No plastic?** Stuff towels, sheets, or clothing into vents and under the door. Wax paper or aluminum foil can cover small gaps.

### Step 3: Stay Informed

- Listen to the radio or watch for alerts
- Keep phones charged
- If gas or vapor may have gotten in, take shallow breaths through a cloth or towel
- Don't eat or drink anything that may be contaminated

---

## How Long a Sealed Room Works

Sealed rooms are meant for **short chemical releases**, usually a few hours, while a gas cloud passes.

- Outside air slowly leaks in, so protection drops over time.
- Air supply depends on room size and number of people. About 10 square feet of floor space per person gives enough air for up to about 5 hours at rest.
- More people or more activity means the air lasts less time.
- Officials will tell you when to unseal and ventilate, or to evacuate if the release continues.

### Signs of Trouble

- Anyone has difficulty breathing, chest pain, or confusion: **call 911**
- Eyes, nose, or throat start to burn: contaminated air may be getting in; cover your nose and mouth with a cloth and call 911 for advice
- The room gets dangerously hot

---

## After the All-Clear

1. Wait for officials to say it's safe
2. Open windows and doors and turn ventilation back on to air out the building
3. Go outside if officials tell you to
4. If you were exposed, take off your clothes, bag them, and wash with soap and water (see Decontamination Basics)
5. Call Poison Control or 911 if anyone has symptoms

### Afterward

- Bag the used plastic and tape; ask local officials how to dispose of it if the release was hazardous
- Restock your sealing kit
- Note what worked and what to improve

---

## Special Considerations

- **Children:** keep them calm and close; explain the plan simply.
- **Older adults and people with disabilities:** move them to the room first, with medications, glasses, hearing aids, and mobility aids.
- **Pets:** bring them into the sealed room with food and water.
- **Medical devices:** have spare batteries or a power plan.
- **No candles** near plastic sheeting.
