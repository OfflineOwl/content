# When to Seal a Room

> **Shelter-in-Place Protection** - During certain emergencies, sealing a room can protect you from airborne hazards. Know when this is appropriate and how to do it properly.

## When to Seal a Room

### Appropriate For

- Chemical release nearby
- Industrial accident
- Airborne biological threat
- Radiological fallout (in addition to other measures)
- Official shelter-in-place order

### NOT Appropriate For

- Fire (need to evacuate)
- Flooding (need to move up or evacuate)
- Long-term shelter without ventilation
- General power outages

## Room Selection

### Ideal Characteristics

- Interior room (fewer outside walls)
- Above ground floor (for most chemicals)
- Fewest windows/vents
- Large enough for occupants
- Access to bathroom if possible
- Phone/radio access

### Avoid

- Basement (many chemicals sink)
- Rooms with many windows
- Rooms with exterior walls on multiple sides
- Very small spaces for long duration

## Sealing Materials

### Keep on Hand

- [ ] Plastic sheeting (6 mil)
- [ ] Duct tape
- [ ] Scissors
- [ ] Wet towels
- [ ] Tape measure

### Pre-Cut Option

Pre-cut plastic to fit:
- Windows
- Doors
- Vents
Label and store for quick deployment.

## Sealing Procedure

### Step 1: Reduce Air Exchange

1. Close all windows and doors
2. Turn off HVAC systems
3. Turn off exhaust fans
4. Close fireplace damper
5. Turn off window AC units

### Step 2: Seal Gaps

**Door:**
1. Place wet towel at bottom
2. Tape edges with duct tape
3. Cover entire door with plastic if time permits

**Windows:**
1. Cover with plastic sheeting
2. Tape all edges completely
3. Overlap tape for good seal

**Vents:**
1. Cover with plastic
2. Tape all edges
3. Don't forget bathroom vents

**Other Gaps:**
- Electrical outlets (tape over)
- Phone jacks
- Any visible gaps to exterior

### Step 3: Monitor

- Listen to emergency radio
- Wait for all-clear
- Monitor air quality if possible

## Time Limitations

### Important Warning

A sealed room has LIMITED AIR.

**Duration guidelines:**
- Up to 2-3 hours is generally safe for healthy adults
- Larger room = more air = longer safe time
- More people = less time
- Monitor for breathing difficulty

### Signs to Unseal

- Difficulty breathing
- Excessive heat
- Official all-clear given
- Danger has clearly passed

## After All-Clear

### Unsealing

1. Wait for official all-clear
2. Open room carefully
3. Ventilate building (open windows if safe)
4. Check air quality outside
5. Monitor for symptoms

### Post-Event

- Remove sealing materials carefully
- Dispose of plastic properly
- Restock supplies
- Note improvements for next time
