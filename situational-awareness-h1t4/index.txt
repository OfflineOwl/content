SITUATIONAL AWARENESS FUNDAMENTALS
==================================

The art of knowing what's happening around you—and what might happen next.

WHAT IS SITUATIONAL AWARENESS?
------------------------------
Situational awareness (SA) is the ability to:
- Perceive what's happening around you
- Understand what it means
- Predict what might happen next
- Take appropriate action

It's not paranoia. It's paying attention.

WHY IT MATTERS
--------------
Most victims of crime, accidents, and emergencies report:
"I didn't see it coming"
"It happened so fast"
"I wasn't paying attention"

Situational awareness gives you:
- More time to react
- Better decisions under pressure
- Ability to avoid threats entirely
- Confidence in unfamiliar situations

THE COOPER COLOR CODE
---------------------
A mental readiness system developed by Lt. Col. Jeff Cooper.

WHITE - UNAWARE
  Status: Relaxed, unaware of surroundings
  When appropriate: At home, in secure environment
  Risk: Vulnerable to surprise
  
YELLOW - RELAXED ALERT
  Status: Aware of surroundings, no specific threat
  When appropriate: Public places, daily activities
  Mindset: "I'm paying attention"
  This should be your DEFAULT in public

ORANGE - SPECIFIC ALERT
  Status: Something has caught your attention
  Response: Evaluate the potential threat
  Mindset: "That person/situation needs watching"
  Action: Prepare response options

RED - ACTION REQUIRED
  Status: Threat confirmed, response needed
  Response: Execute your plan
  Mindset: "I'm acting NOW"
  Action: Fight, flight, or specific response

BLACK - OVERWHELMED (avoid this)
  Status: Panic, freezing, unable to function
  Prevention: Training, mental preparation
  
Goal: Move smoothly between Yellow, Orange, Red as needed.
Never be caught in White in public.

PRACTICAL OBSERVATION SKILLS
----------------------------

BASELINE AWARENESS
Every environment has a "normal" pattern. Learn to recognize it.

Questions to ask:
- What's normal behavior here?
- What's the typical noise level?
- What's the usual flow of people?
- What's out of place?

SCANNING TECHNIQUE
1. Enter any space: Pause and scan
2. Identify exits (at least two)
3. Note potential threats
4. Note potential cover
5. Observe people and activity
6. Check periodically while present

WHAT TO OBSERVE

PEOPLE:
- Clothing appropriate for weather/setting?
- Hands visible?
- Making eye contact or avoiding it?
- Body language (tense, nervous, angry?)
- Moving with purpose or loitering?
- Paying unusual attention to you or others?
- Carrying unusual items?

VEHICLES:
- Parked with engine running?
- Occupants waiting?
- Circling the area?
- Blocking exits?
- Out of place for the area?

ENVIRONMENT:
- Unusual sounds?
- Unexpected smells (gas, smoke)?
- Objects out of place?
- Doors/windows that should be secure?
- Areas with limited visibility?

PRE-ATTACK INDICATORS
---------------------
Most attacks have warning signs. Learn to recognize them.

INTERVIEW/APPROACH
- Stranger approaching with questions
- Getting uncomfortably close
- Invading personal space
- Asking for time, directions, money
Purpose: Assess you as a target

POSITIONING
- Someone moving to cut off your exit
- Person trying to get behind you
- Group spreading out around you
- Someone matching your pace

CONCEALMENT
- Hands hidden (pockets, bag, behind back)
- Clothing inappropriate for weather (hiding weapon?)
- Nervous glances around
- Looking for witnesses

FINAL APPROACH
- Sudden change in speed or direction
- Target fixation (staring at you)
- Scanning for witnesses
- Hand moving toward pocket/waist

ENVIRONMENTAL AWARENESS
-----------------------

KNOW YOUR EXITS
Always identify at least two exits from any space:
- Main entrance (often congested in emergency)
- Emergency exits
- Windows
- Service doors
- Alternative routes

IDENTIFY COVER VS CONCEALMENT
Cover: Stops bullets/projectiles (concrete, engine block)
Concealment: Hides you but won't stop anything (curtains, bushes)
Know the difference.

TRUST YOUR INSTINCTS
--------------------
Your subconscious processes information faster than your 
conscious mind. If something feels wrong, it probably is.

THE GIFT OF FEAR
- Don't rationalize away bad feelings
- Don't worry about being "rude"
- Don't let social pressure override instincts
- Act on discomfort, explain later

COMMON INSTINCT OVERRIDES TO AVOID:
"I'm probably being paranoid"
"I don't want to seem rude"
"That person probably needs help"
"It's probably nothing"
"I'm almost there, I'll be fine"

DAILY PRACTICE
--------------

IN YOUR CAR:
- Check mirrors regularly
- Note vehicles that stay with you
- Park in well-lit areas
- Back into spaces when possible
- Check around car before entering
- Lock doors immediately

WALKING:
- Stay aware, not on phone
- Walk with purpose and confidence
- Vary your routes
- Note people ahead and behind
- Cross street to avoid concerning people
- Stay in well-traveled areas

IN STORES/RESTAURANTS:
- Sit facing entrances when possible
- Note exit locations
- Be aware of people entering
- Know where you'd go if trouble started

AT HOME:
- Notice unfamiliar vehicles
- Be aware of people watching your routine
- Don't advertise absence (packages piling up)
- Secure entry points

AT ATMs:
- Use during daylight when possible
- Be aware of people nearby
- Shield PIN entry
- Get cash and leave quickly
- Be alert returning to car

OBSERVATION EXERCISE
--------------------
Practice regularly to build skills:

EXERCISE 1: Description Practice
After leaving a store, describe:
- How many people were there?
- What was the cashier wearing?
- Was anyone standing near the exit?

EXERCISE 2: Vehicle Memory
At a stoplight, note:
- What vehicle is behind you?
- What color?
- How many occupants?

EXERCISE 3: Exit Mapping
When entering any new space:
- Count the exits within 30 seconds
- Plan primary and backup escape routes

EXERCISE 4: Baseline Testing
Sit in a public place and note:
- What's the normal activity pattern?
- After 10 minutes, what's changed?
- Can you identify anything unusual?

WITH FAMILY
-----------

TEACHING CHILDREN (age-appropriate):
- Recognize trusted adults
- What to do if separated
- Basic stranger awareness
- How to get help
- Code words for pickup

FAMILY CODE WORDS:
- Duress word (everything's NOT fine)
- Pickup authorization word
- Family emergency signal

MENTAL PREPARATION
------------------
Awareness without preparation is incomplete.

VISUALIZATION:
Regularly think through scenarios:
- "What would I do if..."
- "If that happened, I would..."
- "My exits here are..."

This mental rehearsal builds reaction speed.

AVOIDING COMPLACENCY
- Routine breeds inattention
- Familiar places can be dangerous
- Stay in Yellow even in "safe" areas
- Criminals choose comfortable targets

SUMMARY: THE THREE RULES
------------------------
1. LOOK AROUND
   Actively observe, don't just be present

2. TRUST YOUR GUT
   If something feels wrong, act on it

3. HAVE A PLAN
   Know your exits, know your actions
