# Cooling Strategies in Extreme Heat

> **Heat Kills More Than Cold** - Extreme heat causes more deaths than any other weather event. When air conditioning isn't available, knowing how to stay cool can save lives.

## Understanding Heat Risk

### Heat-Related Illness Progression

| Condition | Signs | Severity |
|-----------|-------|----------|
| **Heat cramps** | Muscle cramps, sweating | Mild |
| **Heat exhaustion** | Heavy sweating, weakness, nausea | Moderate |
| **Heat stroke** | High temp, confusion, no sweating | **Emergency** |

### Who's Most at Risk?

- Elderly (over 65)
- Infants and young children
- Those with chronic illness
- People on certain medications
- Those without access to cooling
- Outdoor workers
- Athletes

## Passive Cooling Strategies

### Block the Heat

**Windows:**
- Close curtains/blinds on sun-facing windows
- Use reflective window coverings
- Hang white or reflective material
- Close windows when outside temp exceeds inside temp
- Open windows at night when it cools

**Shade:**
- Create shade with tarps/awnings
- Park vehicles to shade windows
- Use trees if available
- Close doors to hot rooms

### Ventilation

**Cross-ventilation:**
- Open windows on opposite sides
- Creates airflow through space
- Best when outside is cooler than inside
- Most effective morning and evening

**Strategic airflow:**
- Open high windows (hot air exits)
- Open low windows (cool air enters)
- Use fans to assist airflow

### Reduce Internal Heat

- Don't cook (or cook outside)
- Turn off unnecessary electronics
- Use no lights if possible (LEDs produce less heat)
- Don't use oven or stove

## Active Cooling Without AC

### Fans

**Battery-powered fans:**
- Essential emergency item
- Multiple sizes available
- Some include misting feature
- Rechargeable or battery-operated

**Effective fan use:**
- Point at skin, not just room
- Place bowl of ice in front
- Position to create airflow
- Use in evening with open windows

### Evaporative Cooling

**Wet cloth method:**
1. Wet cloth with cool water
2. Place on pulse points:
   - Neck
   - Wrists
   - Temples
   - Ankles
3. Replace when it warms

**DIY evaporative cooler:**
1. Hang wet sheet in doorway or window
2. Air passing through cools as water evaporates
3. Works best in low humidity

**Misting:**
- Spray bottle with cool water
- Mist skin lightly
- Allow to evaporate
- Repeat frequently

### Cold Water Immersion

**Most effective cooling method:**
- Cool (not cold) bath or shower
- Wading in pool or stream
- Wet clothing
- Cool foot bath

## Body Cooling Strategies

### Hydration

**Critical in heat:**
- Drink before you're thirsty
- Water is best
- Avoid alcohol and caffeine
- Include electrolytes if sweating heavily
- Watch urine color (pale = hydrated)

### Clothing

**Wear:**
- Light-colored, loose-fitting clothing
- Natural fibers (cotton, linen)
- Wet bandana around neck
- Wide-brimmed hat outdoors

**Avoid:**
- Dark colors (absorb heat)
- Tight clothing
- Synthetic materials

### Activity

- Minimize physical exertion
- Rest during hottest hours (10am-4pm)
- Do necessary tasks early morning or evening
- Take frequent breaks

### Food

- Eat lighter meals
- Cold foods when possible
- Fruits with high water content
- Avoid heavy, hot foods

## Sleeping in Heat

### Location

- Sleep in lowest level of home
- Heat rises—avoid upper floors
- Sleep outdoors if safe and cooler
- Hammocks allow air circulation below

### Bedding

- Use lightweight sheets only
- Cotton or linen (breathable)
- No blankets
- Consider sleeping bag liner only

### Techniques

- Cool shower before bed
- Damp sheet over body
- Wet hair
- Fan directed at sleeping area
- Frozen water bottles in bed

## Protecting Vulnerable People

### Children

- Watch for heat illness signs
- Ensure adequate fluids
- Keep in shade
- Never leave in vehicles
- Limit outdoor play during heat

### Elderly

- Check on frequently
- Ensure drinking fluids
- Watch for confusion
- May not feel thirst
- Medications can increase risk

### Pets

- Provide shade and water
- Bring indoors if possible
- Watch for panting, drooling
- Never leave in vehicles

## Heat Emergency Response

### Heat Exhaustion

**Signs:**
- Heavy sweating
- Cold, pale, clammy skin
- Fast, weak pulse
- Nausea or vomiting
- Muscle cramps
- Tiredness, weakness

**Treatment:**
1. Move to cool place
2. Lie down, elevate legs
3. Apply cool, wet cloths
4. Sip water
5. Seek help if worsening

### Heat Stroke (EMERGENCY)

**Signs:**
- High body temperature (103°F+)
- Hot, red, dry or damp skin
- Fast, strong pulse
- Confusion
- Possible unconsciousness

**Treatment:**
1. Call for emergency help
2. Move to cool place
3. Cool body rapidly
4. Use cold water immersion if possible
5. Do NOT give fluids if unconscious

## When to Seek Cooling Centers

- Indoor temp exceeds 95°F
- No relief from cooling strategies
- Vulnerable persons in home
- Extended heat event expected
- Signs of heat illness developing

**Options:**
- Public cooling centers
- Libraries, malls (if operating)
- Friends/family with AC
- Vehicles with AC (temporarily)

## Quick Reference

```
HEAT SAFETY PRIORITIES

1. HYDRATE
   - Drink before thirsty
   - Water + electrolytes

2. COOL THE BODY
   - Wet cloths on pulse points
   - Cool water on skin
   - Fans

3. REDUCE HEAT EXPOSURE
   - Stay in shade
   - Cover windows
   - Minimize activity

4. WATCH FOR HEAT ILLNESS
   - Heavy sweating → Heat exhaustion
   - No sweating + confusion → Heat stroke (EMERGENCY)

HEAT STROKE = EMERGENCY
- Cool body immediately
- Seek emergency help
```
