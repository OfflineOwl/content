FAMILY SIGNALING & CHECK-IN METHODS
====================================

When phones don't work, how do you let family know you're safe?
This guide covers non-electronic communication methods.

PRE-ESTABLISHED SIGNALS
-----------------------

VISUAL SIGNALS AT HOME:
Agree on signals that family will recognize:

Examples:
- Colored item in window = we're home/safe
- Specific item on porch = we've evacuated
- Flag or marker at rally point = we've been here
- Chalk mark on mailbox = check hidden message

Create your own system:
- Simple, easy to spot
- Unique to your family
- Placed in agreed locations
- Changed periodically for security

CHECK-IN SCHEDULE:
If separated without communication:
- Agree to check specific locations at specific times
- Example: Check home every 6 hours
- Check rally point at noon daily
- Leave messages at agreed spot

RALLY POINT SIGNALS:
At your meeting points, establish:
- Where to leave messages
- How to indicate you've been there
- Direction of travel if moved on
- Time to wait before moving

AUDIO SIGNALS
-------------

WHISTLE CODES:
Universal: 3 short blasts = distress/help

Family codes (examples):
- 2 short = I'm here/acknowledgment
- 3 short = need help/come to me
- 1 long = all clear
- Pattern (dot-dot-dash) = recognition

OTHER AUDIO:
- Car horn patterns
- Air horn
- Banging on metal
- Voice calls (specific phrases)

WRITTEN MESSAGES
----------------

MESSAGE SYSTEM:
Establish where to leave notes:
- Under specific rock/object at rally point
- Inside mailbox
- Taped inside shed door
- In hidden family spot

MESSAGE CONTENTS:
Include:
- Date and time
- Who is writing
- Current status
- Where going next
- When returning or next check
- Code words if security needed

PROTECT MESSAGES:
- Waterproof container
- Zip-lock bags
- Hidden from casual observation
- Changed locations if compromised

VEHICLE SIGNALS
---------------

IF DRIVING BY HOME:
Pre-arranged signals from car:
- Horn pattern
- Specific parking position
- Item on dashboard

IF LEAVING VEHICLE:
- Note on dashboard with destination
- Message in agreed hidden spot
- Direction indicator

OUT-OF-AREA CONTACT
-------------------

MOST IMPORTANT TOOL:
Designate someone 100+ miles away who:
- All family members can contact
- Relays status between family
- Knows your plan and rally points
- Has your emergency information

HOW IT WORKS:
1. Each family member contacts OAC
2. OAC records status and location
3. OAC relays information to others
4. Central hub for communication

WHY OUT OF AREA:
- Long distance often works when local doesn't
- Different infrastructure may be intact
- Not affected by same emergency
- Can coordinate from safe location

RADIO COMMUNICATION
-------------------

IF YOU HAVE RADIOS:
- Agree on channel/frequency
- Set check-in times
- Keep transmissions brief
- Use code words for locations

FRS/GMRS RADIOS:
- Decide on primary and backup channel
- Privacy codes (don't provide security)
- Check-in schedule
- Call signs for each family member

HAM RADIO:
- Can relay messages long distances
- Requires license
- Can contact emergency networks
- Family members don't need license to receive

COMMUNITY SIGNALS
-----------------

NEIGHBORHOOD COMMUNICATION:
Establish with trusted neighbors:
- All-clear signals
- Help needed signals
- Check-on-me system
- Mutual watch schedules

VISUAL COMMUNITY SIGNALS:
Examples used historically:
- Ribbon on mailbox = need assistance
- Flag in window = all okay
- Specific pattern = specific message

CREATING YOUR FAMILY SYSTEM
---------------------------

STEP 1: CHOOSE SIGNALS
- Visual signals for home
- Audio signals for close range
- Message locations

STEP 2: DOCUMENT
- Write down all signals/codes
- Give copy to each family member
- Give copy to OAC

STEP 3: PRACTICE
- Do a communication drill
- Identify problems
- Refine system

STEP 4: MAINTAIN
- Update as needed
- Practice periodically
- Everyone must remember
