# 72-Hour Survival Checklist (Urban & Rural)

> After a disaster, help may not reach you right away. This checklist follows the Ready.gov basic disaster supplies kit so your household can manage on its own for at least three days. For staying at home, aim for two weeks.

## Start Here: The Core Kit

If you only do one thing, gather these (Ready.gov basic kit):

1. **Water:** 1 gallon (about 3.8 liters) per person per day, for drinking and sanitation, for several days. For 72 hours that's 3 gallons per person.
2. **Food:** at least a several-day supply of non-perishable food, plus a manual can opener.
3. **Medications:** prescriptions (at least a 7-day supply), over-the-counter medicines, glasses and contact lens solution.
4. **Radio:** battery-powered or hand-crank, plus a NOAA Weather Radio with tone alert.
5. **Light and power:** flashlight, extra batteries, phone with chargers and a backup battery.
6. **First aid kit** and a **whistle** to signal for help.
7. **Documents:** copies of IDs, insurance policies, and bank records, saved electronically or in a waterproof, portable container. Cash.
8. **Shelter-in-place supplies:** dust masks, plastic sheeting, duct tape, scissors.
9. **Sanitation:** moist towelettes, garbage bags, plastic ties.
10. **Tools:** wrench or pliers to turn off utilities, local paper maps.
11. **Family needs:** supplies for infants and kids, pets, and anyone with medical or disability-related needs.

Keep the kit where everyone can find it, ready to grab if you have to leave quickly. Keep smaller kits at work (enough to shelter there for at least 24 hours) and in the car.

---

## Why 72 Hours?

- First responders handle life-threatening calls first
- Damaged roads and bridges can block access
- Stores, utilities, and supply chains take time to recover

**Reality check:** After major hurricanes and earthquakes, many households need to manage for longer. The American Red Cross recommends a 3-day supply for evacuation and a 2-week supply for home. Treat 72 hours as the minimum.

## Water Checklist

**1 gallon per person per day.** That's 3 gallons per person for 72 hours, and about 14 gallons per person for two weeks.

- Children, nursing mothers, and people who are sick may need more
- In very hot weather, water needs can double
- Add water for pets
- Water weighs about 8.3 pounds per gallon, so store it where you can load it into a car
- **Never ration drinking water** unless authorities tell you to. Drink what you need today and look for more tomorrow.

### Essential

- [ ] Store-bought bottled water, kept sealed in a cool, dark place (follow the date on the package)
- [ ] If you bottle your own: food-grade containers, cleaned and sanitized; replace every 6 months
- [ ] Water purification tablets or unscented household bleach, and a way to boil water
- [ ] Portable water filter

### Urban Additions

- [ ] Know where your water heater is (its tank holds usable water)
- [ ] Know how to shut off the main water valve
- [ ] Bathtub liner or extra containers to fill when a storm is forecast

### Rural Additions

- [ ] Map of nearby water sources (streams, ponds, wells); treat all of it before drinking
- [ ] Extra purification supplies
- [ ] Buckets for hauling water

## Food Checklist

**At least a several-day supply** of food that doesn't need refrigeration and needs little or no cooking or water. Plan roughly 2,000 calories per adult per day; active adults need more, small children less.

### Essential (No-Cook Options)

- [ ] Canned goods: beans, tuna, chicken, fruit, vegetables
- [ ] Manual can opener (essential)
- [ ] Peanut butter or other nut butter
- [ ] Crackers and whole-grain cereal
- [ ] Dried fruit and nuts
- [ ] Energy or granola bars
- [ ] Comfort foods
- [ ] Infant formula and baby food (if needed)
- [ ] Pet food (if needed)
- [ ] Foods for special diets and allergies

### Extended Cooking Capability

- [ ] Camp stove and fuel (**outdoors only**: carbon monoxide)
- [ ] Freeze-dried meals
- [ ] Instant rice and oatmeal
- [ ] Powdered milk
- [ ] Mess kit or disposable plates, cups, and utensils

### Urban Considerations

- [ ] Ready-to-eat options first (cooking may not be possible)
- [ ] Amounts you could carry or load quickly if you evacuate

### Rural Considerations

- [ ] Larger stockpile is practical
- [ ] Outdoor cooking equipment
- [ ] Cool, dry storage

Keep canned food in a cool, dry place and boxed food in tightly closed plastic or metal containers. Replace expired items.

## Shelter & Warmth Checklist

### Essential

- [ ] Sleeping bag or warm blanket for each person
- [ ] Emergency (mylar) blankets
- [ ] Change of clothes for the climate, and sturdy shoes, for each person
- [ ] Rain gear
- [ ] Plastic sheeting, duct tape, and scissors (for sealing a room in a chemical emergency)
- [ ] Tarp

### Urban Additions

- [ ] Window coverings and draft blockers for insulation
- [ ] Extra blankets instead of an indoor heater. **Never** use a grill, camp stove, or generator indoors.

### Rural Additions

- [ ] Tent or other shelter
- [ ] Seasoned firewood (for a working fireplace or wood stove)
- [ ] Axe or hatchet
- [ ] Fire starters (several types)
- [ ] Extra tarps

---

## Light & Power Checklist

### Essential

- [ ] Flashlights (LED), at least 2
- [ ] Headlamp (hands-free)
- [ ] Extra batteries for every device
- [ ] Battery-powered or hand-crank radio (AM/FM + NOAA Weather Radio)
- [ ] Battery-powered lanterns (safer than candles, which cause home fires)
- [ ] Waterproof matches and lighters

### Power Options

- [ ] Cell phone chargers and a fully charged backup battery
- [ ] Solar charger
- [ ] Car phone charger (charge only with the car outside, never in a closed garage)

### Urban Considerations

- [ ] Know where your breaker box is
- [ ] Surge protectors for when power returns

### Rural Considerations

- [ ] Generator and fuel, if you have one: run it **outdoors only, at least 20 feet from the house**, with exhaust pointed away from windows, doors, and vents. Never in a garage, basement, or porch.
- [ ] Carbon monoxide alarms on every level of the home
- [ ] Safe fuel storage
- [ ] Solar panels (if established)

## First Aid & Medical Checklist

### Essential First Aid Kit

- [ ] Adhesive bandages (various sizes)
- [ ] Sterile gauze pads and rolls
- [ ] Medical tape
- [ ] Elastic bandages
- [ ] Triangular bandage (sling)
- [ ] Scissors or trauma shears
- [ ] Tweezers
- [ ] Disposable gloves (nitrile)
- [ ] Antiseptic wipes
- [ ] Antibiotic ointment
- [ ] Burn gel
- [ ] Instant cold packs
- [ ] Thermometer
- [ ] CPR face shield

### Medications

- [ ] Prescription medications: at least a 7-day supply (ask your doctor or pharmacist how to build an emergency supply)
- [ ] Written list of medications, doses, and allergies
- [ ] Pain relievers
- [ ] Anti-diarrhea medicine, antacids, laxatives
- [ ] Antihistamine
- [ ] Oral rehydration salts
- [ ] Prescription glasses (backup pair) and contact lens solution

### Advanced Items (with training)

- [ ] Tourniquet
- [ ] Hemostatic gauze
- [ ] SAM splint
- [ ] Chest seals

## Communication & Documentation Checklist

### Essential

- [ ] Cell phone with chargers and a backup battery
- [ ] Emergency contact list on paper
- [ ] Out-of-town contact chosen and known to everyone
- [ ] Battery-powered or hand-crank radio
- [ ] Whistle
- [ ] Paper and pencil

### Documentation Kit

Copies, saved electronically or in a waterproof, portable container:
- [ ] IDs (driver's licenses, passports, birth certificates)
- [ ] Insurance policies
- [ ] Bank records and account information
- [ ] Medical information and medication lists
- [ ] Property deed or lease
- [ ] Photos of your home and valuables (for insurance)
- [ ] Pet records
- [ ] Cash in small bills (card readers and ATMs may not work)

### Urban Considerations

- [ ] Your building's emergency plan
- [ ] Neighbors' contact information

### Rural Considerations

- [ ] Paper maps of the area
- [ ] Two-way radios (see Emergency Communications Plan)

## Tools & Equipment Checklist

### Essential

- [ ] Wrench or pliers to turn off utilities (turn off gas only if you smell gas or hear hissing, or officials say to; only the gas company should turn it back on)
- [ ] Multi-tool
- [ ] Work gloves
- [ ] Dust masks (N95)
- [ ] Duct tape
- [ ] Rope or paracord
- [ ] Fire extinguisher

### Useful Additions

- [ ] Folding shovel
- [ ] Pry bar
- [ ] Hammer and screwdrivers
- [ ] Zip ties and bungee cords

## Sanitation & Hygiene Checklist

### Essential

- [ ] Moist towelettes
- [ ] Garbage bags and plastic ties
- [ ] Toilet paper
- [ ] Soap and hand sanitizer
- [ ] Disinfecting wipes
- [ ] Toothbrushes and toothpaste
- [ ] Feminine and personal hygiene supplies
- [ ] Bucket with a tight lid (emergency toilet)

### Extended Sanitation

- [ ] Kitty litter or toilet chemicals for the bucket
- [ ] Unscented household bleach (for disinfecting and water treatment)
- [ ] Towels and washcloths

---

## Family Needs

### Infants and Young Children

- [ ] Formula and bottles (ready-to-feed formula needs no water)
- [ ] Diapers, wipes, and diaper rash cream
- [ ] Children's medications
- [ ] Books, games, puzzles, and a comfort item

### Older Adults and People with Disabilities

- [ ] Medications (at least 7 days) and a written list
- [ ] Spare batteries for power wheelchairs, hearing aids, and other devices
- [ ] A list of medical devices with model and serial numbers
- [ ] A plan for power-dependent medical equipment (ask your provider; ask your utility about priority restoration)
- [ ] Mobility aids, glasses, and denture supplies
- [ ] A support network of people who know where your supplies are

### Pets

- [ ] Several days' food, in an airtight, waterproof container
- [ ] Water and a bowl
- [ ] Medications and copies of records
- [ ] Collar with ID tag, leash or harness (plus backups)
- [ ] Carrier or crate
- [ ] Litter and box, or waste bags
- [ ] A photo of you with your pet
- [ ] Many public shelters don't allow pets; know where you could take them

## Go-Bag Quick Pack List

The core kit above is your household supply. Each person should also have a go-bag they can carry (see Go-Bag Essentials by Family Member):

1. [ ] Water: as much as that person can carry (at least 1-2 liters), plus purification tablets or a filter. Load the rest of the household's 3-gallon-per-person supply into the car if you're driving.
2. [ ] Food: 3 days, no-cook
3. [ ] Medications (at least 7 days) and a medication list
4. [ ] First aid kit
5. [ ] Flashlight and batteries
6. [ ] Battery or hand-crank radio
7. [ ] Phone charger and backup battery
8. [ ] Cash in small bills
9. [ ] Copies of documents and an emergency contact card
10. [ ] Change of clothes and sturdy shoes
11. [ ] Emergency blanket
12. [ ] Dust mask and whistle
13. [ ] Hygiene basics
14. [ ] Glasses, hearing aid batteries, and other personal needs
15. [ ] Pet and child supplies, if needed

---

## Maintenance

**Every 6 months:**
- [ ] Replace water you bottled yourself; check dates on store-bought water
- [ ] Check food dates and rotate
- [ ] Test flashlights and radios; replace batteries
- [ ] Recharge backup batteries
- [ ] Refresh medications

**Every year:**
- [ ] Reassess the kit as your family changes (new baby, new pet, new medications)
- [ ] Update documents and contact lists
- [ ] Review and practice your family emergency plan
