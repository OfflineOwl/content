# Go-Bag Essentials by Family Member

> A go-bag is what each person grabs when you have to leave fast. It works alongside your household kit (see the 72-Hour Survival Checklist), which follows the Ready.gov basic kit. Each family member has different needs and can carry different amounts, so build a bag for each person.

## Start Here

Every go-bag, for every person, needs these:

1. **Water** they can carry (at least 1-2 liters for an adult) and a way to purify more
2. **Medications** (at least a 7-day supply) and a written medication list
3. **Copies of documents** and an emergency contact card
4. **Phone charger** and backup battery
5. **Flashlight**, **whistle**, and **dust mask**
6. **Change of clothes** and sturdy shoes
7. **Food** for 3 days that needs no cooking
8. **Personal needs:** glasses, hearing aid batteries, comfort items, and supplies for babies and pets

### About Water

The standard is **1 gallon (about 3.8 liters) per person per day**, so a 3-day supply is 3 gallons per person. That weighs about 25 pounds, which is more than most people can carry on foot along with everything else. So:

- **Driving out?** Load the household's full 3-gallon-per-person supply into the car next to the go-bags.
- **On foot?** Each person carries what they can, and the bag includes purification tablets or a filter to treat more.
- Never ration drinking water unless authorities tell you to.

---

## Go-Bag Philosophy

A go-bag should:
- Get you through about 72 hours away from home
- Be light enough for its owner to carry
- Hold things you actually know how to use
- Be packed and ready, stored near an exit

**Critical rule:** a bag that's too heavy won't get carried. Test-walk with it, and cut weight until you can.

---

## Weight Guidelines

There's no official weight limit for adult go-bags. Use these rules of thumb:

| Person | Guideline |
|--------|-----------|
| Adults | Test-walk for at least 30 minutes. If you can't carry it comfortably, lighten it. |
| Older adults | Essentials only; consider a rolling bag or have others carry heavy items |
| Teens | Their own essentials plus a few shared items |
| Children (school age) | No more than 10-20% of body weight (American Academy of Pediatrics backpack guideline) |
| Young children | Comfort items only; adults carry the rest |

## Primary Adult Go-Bag

This person carries the most and the most critical shared items.

### Water
- [ ] Water bottles (at least 2 liters)
- [ ] Water purification tablets
- [ ] Portable water filter
- [ ] Collapsible water container

### Food (3 days, no cooking needed)
- [ ] Energy bars
- [ ] Ready-to-eat pouches or pull-top cans (and a manual can opener if needed)
- [ ] Trail mix or nuts
- [ ] Electrolyte packets
- [ ] Hard candy

### Shelter & Warmth
- [ ] Emergency bivvy or space blankets (2)
- [ ] Lightweight tarp
- [ ] Paracord
- [ ] Change of clothes for the weather, and sturdy shoes
- [ ] Rain poncho
- [ ] Wool socks (2 pairs)
- [ ] Warm hat and gloves

### Light & Fire
- [ ] Flashlight (LED) and batteries
- [ ] Headlamp and batteries
- [ ] Lighters (2)
- [ ] Waterproof matches
- [ ] Fire starters (for outdoor use)

### First Aid & Medical
- [ ] First aid kit
- [ ] Prescription medications (at least 7 days) and a medication list
- [ ] Over-the-counter medicines (pain reliever, antihistamine, anti-diarrhea)
- [ ] Backup glasses and contact lens solution
- [ ] Hygiene items and hand sanitizer
- [ ] Sunscreen and insect repellent

### Tools & Protection
- [ ] Multi-tool
- [ ] Duct tape (small roll)
- [ ] Zip ties
- [ ] Work gloves
- [ ] N95 masks
- [ ] Safety glasses

### Navigation & Communication
- [ ] Local paper maps (with evacuation routes marked)
- [ ] Compass
- [ ] Battery or hand-crank radio with NOAA Weather Radio
- [ ] Phone charger and backup battery
- [ ] Whistle
- [ ] Emergency contact card (paper)

### Documents & Money
Copies, in a waterproof bag or saved electronically:
- [ ] IDs
- [ ] Insurance policies
- [ ] Bank records
- [ ] Medical information
- [ ] Cash in small bills (card readers and ATMs may not work)

## Secondary Adult Go-Bag

Shares the load and carries complementary items.

### Water
- [ ] Water bottles (at least 2 liters)
- [ ] Backup purification tablets

### Food
- [ ] 3 days of no-cook food
- [ ] Lightweight pot and utensils
- [ ] Extra snacks for children

### Shelter Support
- [ ] Ground cloth or second tarp
- [ ] Extra emergency blankets
- [ ] Sleeping bag liner

### Light
- [ ] Backup flashlight and batteries
- [ ] Glow sticks (safe in shelters, unlike candles)

### First Aid & Medical
- [ ] Their own medications (at least 7 days) and list
- [ ] Extra first aid supplies
- [ ] Feminine hygiene products

### Tools
- [ ] Extra cordage
- [ ] Repair kit (needle, thread, safety pins)

### Family Support
- [ ] Small games, cards, or books for kids
- [ ] Comfort items
- [ ] Family photos (to help identify missing family members)

### Documents
- [ ] Second set of document copies
- [ ] USB drive with digital copies

## Older Family Members

Focus on essentials and keep weight low.

### Critical Items
- [ ] Medications (at least 7 days) and a written list with doses and allergies
- [ ] Spare batteries for medical devices and hearing aids
- [ ] List of medical devices with model and serial numbers
- [ ] Backup glasses
- [ ] Denture supplies
- [ ] Mobility aids (folding cane, walker)

### Basic Needs
- [ ] Water they can carry and purification tablets
- [ ] Easy-open foods
- [ ] Emergency blanket
- [ ] Flashlight
- [ ] Whistle

### Documents
- [ ] ID and insurance copies (including Medicare or other health insurance)
- [ ] Medical information card
- [ ] Emergency contacts
- [ ] Doctors' and pharmacy phone numbers

### Comfort
- [ ] Warm layers

## Bag Selection Guide

### Features to Look For
- Padded shoulder straps and a waist belt (for heavier bags)
- Water-resistant material
- Durable zippers
- A few well-organized compartments
- Neutral colors, with reflective strips for walking near traffic at night

### Bag Types

| Type | Pros | Cons | Best For |
|------|------|------|----------|
| **Backpack** | Hands-free, balanced | Can get heavy | Adults, teens |
| **Duffel** | Easy to pack | Hard to carry far | Vehicle storage |
| **Rolling** | Easy on pavement | Fails on rough ground | Older adults |
| **Sling bag** | Quick access | Unbalanced | Secondary bag |

### Avoid
- Military-style bags that draw attention
- Cheap zippers and straps
- So many compartments that you can't find things

## Storage & Maintenance

### Storage Location
- Near your main exit
- Not in extreme heat or cold
- Where every adult knows to find it
- A smaller kit at work and in the car

### Maintenance Schedule

**Every 6 months:**
- Replace water you bottled yourself; check dates on store-bought water
- Check food dates and rotate
- Refresh medications and check expiration dates
- Test flashlights and radios; replace batteries
- Swap clothes for the season

**Every year:**
- Full inventory
- Replace expired items
- Update documents and contact cards
- Adjust for family changes (new baby, new pet, kids outgrowing clothes)

## Final Checklist

- [ ] Every family member has a bag sized for them
- [ ] Bags are packed and stored near an exit
- [ ] Household water (1 gallon per person per day for at least 3 days) is ready to load into the car
- [ ] Everyone knows where the bags are
- [ ] Everyone can carry their own bag
- [ ] Bags have been test-worn
- [ ] Contents have been used and tested
- [ ] Maintenance dates are on the calendar
