UNDERSTANDING SIRENS & ALERT SYSTEMS
====================================

When sirens sound or alerts appear on your phone, knowing what they
mean and how to respond can save your life.

TYPES OF ALERT SYSTEMS
----------------------

OUTDOOR WARNING SIRENS:
- Public address speakers
- Located throughout communities
- Meant to warn those outdoors
- Not meant to wake you up inside
- Different sounds may mean different things

EMERGENCY ALERT SYSTEM (EAS):
- TV and radio interruption
- Official government warnings
- Distinctive attention tone
- Specific message follows

WIRELESS EMERGENCY ALERTS (WEA):
- Cell phone alerts
- No sign-up required
- Three categories:
  - Presidential alerts (cannot disable)
  - Imminent threat alerts
  - Amber alerts

NOAA WEATHER RADIO:
- 24/7 weather broadcasts
- Can program for your county (SAME)
- Automatic alerts
- Works during power outages (battery)

COMMUNITY NOTIFICATION:
- Opt-in local systems
- Phone calls, texts, emails
- Local emergency management
- Sign up in advance

SIREN MEANINGS
--------------

IMPORTANT: Meanings vary by location!
Know YOUR community's system.

COMMON PATTERNS:

STEADY TONE (1-3 minutes):
Usually means: Take shelter/seek information
Common for: Tornado warning, severe weather

WAVERING/WARBLING TONE:
Usually means: Take action immediately
Common for: Various emergencies

ALL-CLEAR:
Many communities have no all-clear siren
Listen to radio for all-clear announcement

ATTACK WARNING (legacy):
Warbling tone up and down
Nuclear/attack warning
Rarely tested, may not exist in your area

WHAT TO DO WHEN YOU HEAR SIRENS
-------------------------------

1. GO INSIDE IMMEDIATELY
   Sirens warn those outdoors

2. SEEK INFORMATION
   - Radio (AM/FM or weather radio)
   - TV
   - Phone alerts
   - Internet (if available)

3. DON'T ASSUME WHAT IT MEANS
   Wait for official information

4. TAKE PROTECTIVE ACTION
   Based on the threat type

5. WAIT FOR ALL-CLEAR
   Don't go outside until cleared

WIRELESS EMERGENCY ALERTS (WEA)
-------------------------------

PRESIDENTIAL ALERTS:
- National-level emergencies
- Cannot be disabled
- Highest priority

EXTREME THREATS:
- Imminent danger to life
- Tornado, tsunami, etc.
- Can be disabled (not recommended)

SEVERE THREATS:
- Less immediate danger
- Flash flood, etc.
- Can be disabled

AMBER ALERTS:
- Missing children
- Can be disabled

TO ENABLE/DISABLE:
Settings > Notifications > Emergency Alerts
(Exact path varies by phone)

RECOMMENDED: Keep all alerts enabled

MONTHLY TEST ALERTS
-------------------

REGULAR TESTING:
- First Wednesday of month (many areas)
- Usually during daytime
- Tests ensure systems work
- Should be announced as tests

IF YOU HEAR TEST:
- No action needed
- System is working
- Pay attention to format for real event

BUILDING YOUR ALERT SYSTEM
--------------------------

LAYERS OF ALERTS:

1. NOAA Weather Radio
   - Battery/hand-crank
   - Automatic alerts
   - Works without power/internet

2. Wireless Emergency Alerts
   - Already on your phone
   - Keep enabled

3. Local notification system
   - Sign up with your county
   - Provides local detail

4. News apps
   - Local TV station apps
   - Weather apps
   - Set up notifications

5. Social media
   - Follow local emergency management
   - As backup, not primary

RESPONDING TO ALERTS
--------------------

TORNADO WARNING:
Shelter immediately
Interior room, lowest floor
Away from windows

FLASH FLOOD WARNING:
Move to higher ground
Don't drive through water

TSUNAMI WARNING:
Move inland or to high ground immediately
Don't wait for shaking

SEVERE THUNDERSTORM WARNING:
Go inside
Avoid windows

CHEMICAL/HAZMAT:
Shelter in place
Close windows/doors
Turn off HVAC

QUICK REFERENCE
---------------

WHEN SIRENS SOUND:
1. Go inside
2. Get information (radio, TV, phone)
3. Take action based on threat
4. Wait for all-clear

WEA ALERTS:
Presidential = can't disable
Extreme threat = severe danger
Severe threat = significant danger
Amber = missing child

IMPORTANT:
Know YOUR local siren meanings
Test alerts are normal
Keep phone alerts enabled
Have backup information sources
