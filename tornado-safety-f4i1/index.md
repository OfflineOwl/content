# Tornado & Severe Wind Safety

> **Nature's Most Violent Storms** - Tornadoes can develop rapidly and strike with little warning. Knowing where to shelter and how to respond can save your life.

## Tornado Warning Signs

### Watch vs. Warning

- **Tornado Watch:** Conditions favorable, be prepared
- **Tornado Warning:** Tornado spotted or indicated, take shelter NOW

### Natural Warning Signs

- Dark, greenish sky
- Large hail
- Loud roar (like freight train)
- Rotating, low-hanging cloud (funnel)
- Debris cloud at ground level

**If you see these, take shelter immediately without waiting for sirens or alerts.**

## Where to Shelter

### Best Locations (in order)

1. **Basement** - Best protection
2. **Storm cellar** - Purpose-built shelter
3. **Interior room** - Lowest floor, no windows
4. **Small interior room** - Bathroom, closet, under stairs

### In a House

**Go to:**
- Basement (center of basement, under sturdy furniture)
- No basement: Interior room, lowest floor
- Bathroom (plumbing adds structural support)
- Closet (small = stronger)
- Under stairs

**Stay away from:**
- Windows
- Exterior walls
- Large rooms (gymnasiums, auditoriums)

### In Apartment/High-Rise

- Go to lowest floor possible
- Interior hallway
- Stairwell (interior, away from doors)
- Interior bathroom or closet
- Stay away from windows

### In Mobile Home

**Leave mobile home immediately.**

Mobile homes are extremely dangerous in tornadoes:
- Go to designated shelter
- Go to sturdy building
- As last resort: Lie flat in ditch, cover head

### In a Vehicle

**Options (in order of preference):**

1. Drive away at right angles to storm path (if visible and distant)
2. Seek sturdy building
3. If no shelter: Get out and lie in ditch below road level
4. **Do NOT shelter under overpass** (dangerous)
5. Last resort: Stay in vehicle, buckled, head below windows

### Outdoors with No Shelter

- Get into a ditch or low-lying area
- Lie flat, face down
- Cover head and neck with arms
- Stay away from trees, cars, overpasses
- Watch for flash flooding in low areas

## During the Tornado

### Protection Position

1. Get as low as possible
2. Face down
3. Cover head and neck with arms
4. Use mattress, blankets, pillows for protection
5. Wear helmet if available
6. Hold on to something sturdy

### For Additional Protection

- Pull mattress over you
- Wear bicycle helmet
- Have sturdy shoes nearby
- Cover with thick blankets

## After the Tornado

### Immediate Safety

- Stay in shelter until threat passes
- Be aware of broken glass, debris
- Watch for downed power lines
- Don't enter damaged buildings
- Watch for gas leaks

### Check for Injuries

- Account for all family members
- Administer first aid
- Call 911 for emergencies
- Help neighbors if safe

### Document and Report

- Take photos of damage
- Contact insurance
- Report dangerous conditions

## Tornado Kit

Keep in shelter area:
- [ ] Helmet (bike or hard hat)
- [ ] Sturdy shoes
- [ ] Flashlight
- [ ] Weather radio
- [ ] Whistle
- [ ] First aid kit
- [ ] Water
- [ ] Phone charger
- [ ] Thick blankets or mattress
