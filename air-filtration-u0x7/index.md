# Air Filtration for Emergencies

> **Breathe Clean Air** - During smoke events, pandemics, or chemical releases, filtering your air can significantly reduce health risks.

## Why Air Filtration Matters

### Threats to Air Quality

- Wildfire smoke
- Industrial accidents
- Pandemic diseases
- Chemical releases
- Volcanic ash
- General pollution

### Health Effects of Poor Air

- Respiratory irritation
- Worsened asthma/COPD
- Cardiovascular stress
- Eye irritation
- Headaches
- Long-term lung damage

## Types of Air Filters

### MERV Ratings

| Rating | Filters | Use |
|--------|---------|-----|
| 1-4 | Large particles | Basic HVAC |
| 5-8 | Mold, dust | Improved residential |
| 9-12 | Smaller particles | Better residential |
| 13-16 | Bacteria, smoke | Hospital grade |
| 17-20 | Viruses, fumes | Clean rooms |

### HEPA Filters

- High Efficiency Particulate Air
- Captures 99.97% of particles ≥0.3μm
- Gold standard for particle filtration
- Doesn't filter gases/odors

### Activated Carbon

- Filters gases and odors
- Chemical absorption
- Often combined with HEPA
- Needs replacement more often

## Home Air Filtration

### Using Your HVAC

1. Upgrade to highest MERV your system handles
2. Run fan continuously during events
3. Ensure good filter fit
4. Replace more frequently during events

### Portable HEPA Filters

**Sizing:**
- Check room size rating
- Generally need 2+ air changes per hour
- May need multiple units

**Placement:**
- Away from walls
- Where airflow isn't blocked
- In rooms where you spend most time

### DIY Box Fan Filter

**Materials:**
- 20" box fan
- 20" x 20" furnace filter (MERV 13+)
- Tape

**Assembly:**
1. Tape filter to back of fan
2. Ensure complete seal
3. Run fan on low or medium

**Note:** Not as effective as HEPA but helps during emergencies

## Creating a Clean Room

### Concept

One room with cleanest possible air:
- Seal from outside air
- Filter air inside
- Where family spends most time

### Setup

1. Choose interior room
2. Close all windows
3. Seal gaps if needed
4. Run air purifier
5. Keep door closed
6. Minimize entry/exit

## Respirators and Masks

### N95 Masks

- Filters 95% of particles ≥0.3μm
- Must fit properly
- Single use
- Good for smoke, disease

### P100 Masks

- Filters 99.97% of particles
- Reusable with replaceable filters
- Better seal than N95

### When to Use

- Going outside during poor air quality
- Can't maintain clean indoor air
- During evacuation through smoke

## Monitoring Air Quality

| 51-100 | Moderate |
| 101-150 | Unhealthy for sensitive groups |
| 151-200 | Unhealthy |
| 201-300 | Very unhealthy |
| 300+ | Hazardous |
