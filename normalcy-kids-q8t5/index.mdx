# Re-Establishing Normalcy for Kids

> **Children Need Stability** - After a crisis, children may struggle to return to normal. This guide helps parents support their children's recovery.

## Understanding Children's Responses

### Common Reactions by Age

**Toddlers (1-3 years):**
- Clinginess
- Regression (thumb-sucking, bedwetting)
- Sleep disturbances
- Irritability
- Separation anxiety

**Preschool (3-6 years):**
- Fear of dark, monsters, being alone
- Regression in toilet training
- Repetitive play about the event
- Changes in appetite
- Withdrawal or hyperactivity

**School-age (6-12 years):**
- Worry about safety
- Physical complaints (headaches, stomach aches)
- Difficulty concentrating
- Sleep problems
- Behavior changes at school
- Guilt or responsibility feelings

**Teenagers (13-18 years):**
- Withdrawal from family
- Risk-taking behavior
- Changes in peer relationships
- Anger, irritability
- Physical complaints
- Academic problems

## Restoring Routine

### Why Routine Matters

- Provides predictability
- Gives sense of control
- Reduces anxiety
- Signals safety
- Promotes healing

### Key Routines to Restore

**Daily routines:**
- Regular wake/sleep times
- Meal times
- Play time
- Family time
- Bedtime rituals

**Weekly routines:**
- School attendance
- Activities
- Religious practices
- Family traditions

### Adaptations May Be Needed

- Temporary relaxation of rules
- Extra comfort at bedtime
- Modified expectations
- Patience with regression
- Gradual return to normal

## Communication Strategies

### Talking to Children

**Do:**
- Use simple, honest language
- Answer questions truthfully
- Validate their feelings
- Reassure safety
- Listen more than talk
- Let them lead conversations

**Don't:**
- Make promises you can't keep
- Dismiss their fears
- Provide too many details
- Lie about the situation
- Prevent questions

### Age-Appropriate Conversations

**Young children:**
- Simple explanations
- Focus on safety
- Repetition is okay
- Use drawings/play

**School-age:**
- More detailed explanations
- Answer questions honestly
- Address specific fears
- Involve in planning

**Teenagers:**
- Honest, direct conversation
- Acknowledge their perspective
- Include in decisions
- Respect their coping style

## Comfort Strategies

### Physical Comfort

- Extra hugs and physical closeness
- Comfort items (blankets, stuffed animals)
- Creating cozy spaces
- Physical activity
- Relaxation techniques

### Emotional Comfort

- Validation of feelings
- Active listening
- Patience with regression
- Reassurance of love
- Quality time together

### Creating Safety

- Consistent presence
- Clear expectations
- Follow-through on promises
- Explaining safety measures
- Involving in preparation

## When to Seek Help

### Warning Signs

- Symptoms lasting more than a month
- Severe regression
- Withdrawal from friends/family
- Talk of self-harm
- Dramatic behavior changes
- Unable to function at school
- Substance use (teens)

### Resources

- School counselors
- Pediatrician
- Child therapist
- Family counseling
- Support groups

## Activities That Help

### Processing Activities

- Drawing pictures about feelings
- Playing out scenarios
- Writing in journal
- Creating photo albums
- Making thank-you cards

### Normalizing Activities

- Playing favorite games
- Watching familiar movies
- Reading favorite books
- Spending time with friends
- Resuming hobbies

### Empowering Activities

- Helping with cleanup
- Donating to relief
- Writing thank-you notes
- Teaching younger siblings
- Planning for future
