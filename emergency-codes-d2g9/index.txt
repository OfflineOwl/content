EMERGENCY CODES & SIGNALS
=========================

Having pre-established codes and signals with family helps
communicate quickly and safely during emergencies.

WHY USE CODES
-------------

BENEFITS:
- Quick communication
- Privacy in public
- Works when phones don't
- Children can remember
- Confirms identity

TYPES OF CODES:
- Verbal codes (words/phrases)
- Visual signals (items/gestures)
- Audio signals (whistle patterns)
- Written codes (symbols/shorthand)

VERBAL CODES
------------

FAMILY SAFE WORDS:
Create codes for common situations:

ALL CLEAR = _______________
(Use when confirming safety)

HELP NEEDED = _______________
(Use when in distress)

EVACUATE = _______________
(Use when need to leave immediately)

DANGER = _______________
(Use to warn of threat)

MEET AT POINT A = _______________
(Primary meeting point)

MEET AT POINT B = _______________
(Backup meeting point)

PHONE CODES:
Can be inserted into normal conversation:

COME GET ME NOW = _______________
Example: "Tell mom I'll be home by seven"

I'M IN DANGER = _______________
Example: "Did you feed the dog?"

CALL 911 = _______________
Example: "How's Uncle Charlie?"

DURESS CODE:
If forced to communicate while in danger:
Word that indicates "I'm saying this under threat"
Example: Using middle name = duress

VISUAL SIGNALS
--------------

HOME SIGNALS:
Pre-arranged items or placements:

WE'RE HOME/SAFE = _______________
Example: Specific item in window

WE'VE EVACUATED = _______________
Example: Chalk mark on mailbox

NEED HELP = _______________
Example: Flag/cloth in specific location

VEHICLE SIGNALS:
Item on dashboard = _______________
Parking position = _______________
Horn pattern = _______________

MEETING POINT SIGNALS:
"I was here" = _______________
"Went to point B" = _______________
"Check for note" = _______________

3 short blasts (international)

Example: 2 short blasts

All clear = _______________

Recognition signal = _______________

WRITTEN CODES
-------------

SHORTHAND FOR NOTES:
OK = all is well
X = danger
-> = went this direction
# = number of people
@ = location/place
? = need help/information

MESSAGE SYMBOLS:

Arrow = direction of travel

HIDE MESSAGE LOCATIONS:
Primary: _______________
Backup: _______________

IDENTITY VERIFICATION
---------------------

CONFIRMING FAMILY:
Question only family knows:
Example: "What's our dog's middle name?"

Answer: _______________

VERIFYING STRANGERS:
If someone claims to be sent by family:
"What's the password?" = _______________

This confirms family actually sent them.

CHILDREN'S CODES
----------------

KEEP IT SIMPLE:
Children need codes they can remember

TEACH THEM:
- Safe word to go with trusted adult
- Danger word to indicate problem
- How to ask for help in code

PRACTICE:
- Quiz periodically
- Make it a game
- Ensure they remember

CREATING YOUR FAMILY CODES
--------------------------

STEP 1: DECIDE ON CODES
- What situations need codes?
- What words/signals will you use?
- Who needs to know each code?

STEP 2: DOCUMENT
- Write down all codes
- Give copies to family members
- Store securely

STEP 3: MEMORIZE
- Everyone must know their codes
- Practice using them
- Refresh periodically

STEP 4: PROTECT
- Don't share codes outside family
- Change if compromised
- Update as needed
