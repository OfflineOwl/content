EXTREME COLD SURVIVAL
=====================

Extreme cold can kill in hours or even minutes with wind chill.
Understanding the risks and how to protect yourself is essential.

UNDERSTANDING COLD DANGER
-------------------------

WIND CHILL:
Wind makes cold far more dangerous.
Temperature + Wind = Effective temperature on skin

Example:
- 0°F + 15mph wind = -19°F wind chill
- Frostbite can occur in 30 minutes or less

HYPOTHERMIA STAGES:
1. Mild (90-95°F body temp): Shivering, poor coordination
2. Moderate (82-90°F): Violent shivering, confusion
3. Severe (below 82°F): Shivering stops, unconsciousness
4. Death below ~78°F body temperature

FROSTBITE:
Freezing of skin and underlying tissue
- Ears, nose, fingers, toes most vulnerable
- Can occur within minutes in extreme cold
- Signs: Numbness, white/grayish skin, hard texture

STAYING WARM
------------

LAYERING SYSTEM:
1. Base layer: Moisture-wicking (NOT cotton)
2. Mid layer: Insulating (fleece, wool)
3. Outer layer: Wind/water resistant

KEY AREAS TO PROTECT:
- Head (25% heat loss)
- Hands
- Feet
- Face

SHELTER PRIORITIES:
1. Get out of wind
2. Get dry
3. Create insulation from ground
4. Trap warm air

INDOOR SURVIVAL WITHOUT HEAT
----------------------------

CREATE WARM ROOM:
- Choose smallest interior room
- Close off rest of house
- Cover windows with blankets
- Block door drafts
- Gather everyone together

BODY HEAT CONSERVATION:
- Stay together (shared body heat)
- Use sleeping bags
- Layer blankets
- Stay dry
- Stay active (but don't sweat)
- Eat regularly (body needs fuel for heat)

EMERGENCY HEAT SOURCES:
Safe indoors:
- Fireplace (with working chimney)
- Indoor-rated propane heater (ventilated)

NEVER use indoors:
- Charcoal grills
- Gas grills  
- Generators
- Outdoor heaters

OUTDOOR SURVIVAL
----------------

IF STRANDED IN VEHICLE:
1. Stay with vehicle (shelter, visible)
2. Run engine 10-15 minutes per hour
3. Clear exhaust pipe (CO danger)
4. Open window slightly when running
5. Move around periodically
6. Use floor mats, seat covers for insulation

IF CAUGHT OUTSIDE:
1. Find or create shelter (from wind)
2. Insulate from ground
3. Cover exposed skin
4. Stay dry
5. Keep moving (but don't exhaust yourself)
6. If with others, huddle together

EMERGENCY SHELTERS:
- Dig into snow (insulating)
- Use vehicle as windbreak
- Find natural windbreak
- Create windbreak from available materials

RECOGNIZING COLD EMERGENCIES
----------------------------

HYPOTHERMIA SIGNS:
- Shivering (stops in severe hypothermia)
- Confusion, poor judgment
- Slurred speech
- Drowsiness
- Loss of coordination
- Slow breathing

HYPOTHERMIA RESPONSE:
1. Get to warm environment
2. Remove wet clothing
3. Warm core first (not extremities)
4. Warm, sweet drinks if conscious
5. Skin-to-skin contact with warm person
6. Don't rub or massage
7. Seek medical care

FROSTBITE SIGNS:
- Numbness
- White, grayish, or waxy skin
- Hard or waxy texture
- Blisters (after warming)

FROSTBITE RESPONSE:
1. Get to warm area
2. DON'T rub affected area
3. Don't walk on frostbitten feet if possible
4. Immerse in warm (not hot) water
5. Don't use direct heat
6. Expect pain during rewarming
7. Seek medical care

QUICK REFERENCE
---------------

LAYER UP:
Base = moisture-wicking
Middle = insulation
Outer = wind protection

HYPOTHERMIA = EMERGENCY
Warm the person, warm drinks, get help

FROSTBITE = EMERGENCY
Warm slowly in warm water, get help

VEHICLE:
Stay with it, run engine sparingly, clear exhaust

HEAT SOURCES - NEVER INDOORS:
- Grills
- Generators
- Outdoor heaters
