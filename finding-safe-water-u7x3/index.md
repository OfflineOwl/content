# Finding Safe Water Sources

> **Water is Life** - You can survive weeks without food, but only about 3 days without water. Knowing how to find water sources is a critical survival skill.

## Daily Water Needs

| Situation | Minimum Daily Need |
|-----------|-------------------|
| Sedentary, cool weather | 2 liters (0.5 gal) |
| Normal activity | 3 liters (0.8 gal) |
| Strenuous activity | 4+ liters (1+ gal) |
| Hot weather | 4-6+ liters |
| Children | 1-2 liters |

**Plan for 1 gallon per person per day for drinking and basic hygiene.**

## Water Source Priority

Not all water sources are equal. Prioritize in this order:

### Tier 1: Best Sources

**Rainwater (freshly collected)**
- Generally cleanest natural source
- Low contamination risk
- Still recommend filtering/treating
- Collection method matters

**Springs (flowing from ground)**
- Natural filtration through rock/soil
- Look for clear, cold water
- Test flow consistency
- Still treat before drinking

**Deep wells (with proper construction)**
- Protected from surface contamination
- Consistent quality
- Requires pump/rope access
- Test periodically if possible

### Tier 2: Good Sources (with treatment)

**Fast-flowing streams and rivers**
- Moving water = less stagnation
- Clearer water better
- Collect upstream from camps/towns
- Always treat

**Large lakes**
- Large volume dilutes contaminants
- Collect away from shores
- Avoid areas near pollution sources
- Always treat

### Tier 3: Emergency Sources (require thorough treatment)

**Small ponds**
- More concentrated contamination
- Higher biological load
- Pre-filter and treat thoroughly
- Last resort

**Slow-moving water**
- Higher contamination risk
- More treatment needed
- Better than nothing

**Snow and ice**
- Must be melted (uses energy)
- Can contain pollutants
- Avoid colored snow
- Treat after melting

## Finding Water in Different Environments

### Urban Environment

**Home sources:**
- Water heater tank (30-80 gallons)
- Toilet tanks (NOT bowls)
- Pipe drainage
- Ice cubes
- Canned goods liquid
- Water bed (NOT for drinking—treat external use only)

**Community sources:**
- Fire hydrants (with proper tools/permission)
- Swimming pools (for hygiene, heavy treatment for drinking)
- Fountains
- Building storage tanks
- Rain collection from roofs

**Avoid:**
- Toilet bowls
- Radiator fluid
- Water near industrial areas
- Standing water in buildings

### Suburban/Rural Environment

**Natural sources:**
- Streams and rivers
- Ponds and lakes
- Springs
- Wells
- Rainwater

**Property sources:**
- Wells (hand pump if power out)
- Cisterns
- Rain barrels
- Livestock troughs (with treatment)
- Swimming pools

### Wilderness Environment

**Finding water signs:**
- Vegetation (green plants need water)
- Animal tracks (often lead to water)
- Insects (especially bees)
- Birds (especially in morning/evening)
- Low ground (water flows down)
- Rock crevices and caves

**Terrain indicators:**
- Valley bottoms
- Base of cliffs
- Shady areas
- Dry stream beds (dig in bends)

## Water Collection Methods

### Rainwater Collection

**Simple method:**
1. Clean collection surface (tarp, clean roof)
2. Funnel into clean container
3. Let first few minutes wash off debris
4. Store in covered containers

**Improvised collectors:**
- Tarps funneled to container
- Large leaves channeled together
- Plastic sheet suspended with weight in center
- Any clean, sloped surface

### Ground Water

**Digging for water:**

Where to dig:
- Outside bends of dry streams
- Base of hills or cliffs
- Green vegetation in dry areas
- Lowest points in area

How to dig:
1. Dig 1-3 feet initially
2. Wait for water to seep in
3. Let sediment settle
4. Skim water from top
5. Always treat before drinking

### Transpiration Bag

**Extract water from plants:**

1. Find non-poisonous leafy plant in sun
2. Place clear plastic bag over branch with leaves
3. Seal around branch
4. Place rock in bag to create low point
5. Wait several hours
6. Collect condensed water
7. Yields small amounts (1/4 to 1 cup)

### Solar Still

**Ground still:**

1. Dig hole 2-3 feet wide, 2 feet deep
2. Place container in center
3. Add green vegetation around container
4. Cover with clear plastic
5. Seal edges with soil
6. Place small rock in center (over container)
7. Water condenses and drips into container
8. Yields 1-3 cups per day

### Morning Dew

**Collection method:**
1. Early morning, before sun evaporates dew
2. Drag absorbent cloth across grass
3. Wring cloth into container
4. Repeat
5. Treat before drinking

### Tree Water

**Some trees hold water:**
- Banana/plantain stems
- Bamboo (between joints)
- Birch trees (spring tapping)
- Maple trees (tapping)
- Some vines (test carefully)

**Caution:** Many plants contain harmful substances. Only use methods you know to be safe.

## Assessing Water Quality

### Visual Assessment

| Observation | Indicates |
|-------------|-----------|
| Clear water | Fewer particles (still may have pathogens) |
| Cloudy water | Sediment, possibly contamination |
| Surface film | Possible chemical contamination |
| Foam | Possible pollution |
| Green color | Algae growth |
| Strong color | Possible chemical contamination |

**Clear water is NOT necessarily safe—pathogens are invisible.**

### Environmental Clues

**Better signs:**
- Flowing water
- Animals drinking from source
- Plants growing healthy nearby
- Distance from human activity
- Natural filtration (through rock/sand)

**Warning signs:**
- Dead animals near water
- No vegetation around water
- Near industrial or agricultural areas
- Strange odors
- Unusual colors
- Downstream from population centers

### Smell Test

Water should NOT have:
- Chemical smell
- Sewage smell
- Sulfur (rotten egg) smell
- Sweet smell
- Any strong odor

**Odor-free doesn't guarantee safety, but odors indicate problems.**

## Water to Avoid

### Never Drink

- **Saltwater** (makes dehydration worse)
- **Urine** (waste products harm kidneys)
- **Alcohol** (causes dehydration)
- **Blood** (pathogen risk, not proper hydration)
- **Water with strong chemical smell**

### High Risk (avoid if possible)

- Stagnant water with no flow
- Water downstream of obvious pollution
- Flood water
- Water in industrial areas
- Agricultural runoff areas
- Water with visible contamination

## Emergency Water Sources at Home

### Water Heater

Your water heater holds 30-80 gallons.

**To access:**
1. Turn off power to heater
2. Turn off water supply to heater
3. Turn on a hot water faucet somewhere (breaks vacuum)
4. Connect hose to drain valve at bottom
5. Open drain valve
6. Collect water in clean containers

**Note:** May have sediment—let settle or filter.

### Pipes in Home

When water supply fails:

1. Open highest faucet in house (lets in air)
2. Collect water from lowest faucets
3. Amount depends on pipe size and layout
4. May only yield a few gallons

### Toilet Tanks

**Tank only, NOT bowl:**
- Holds 1-2 gallons
- Water is clean (unless treated with tablets/cleaners)
- Check for cleaning chemicals before using
- Treat before drinking if chemicals used

## Key Takeaways

1. **Know your sources** before emergency
2. **Prioritize** cleaner sources when possible
3. **Always treat** water from natural sources
4. **Multiple options** = better resilience
5. **Store water** so you don't have to find it immediately
6. **Assess carefully** before collecting
