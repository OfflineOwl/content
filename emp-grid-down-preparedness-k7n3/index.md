# EMP & Grid-Down Preparedness

> **When the Lights Go Out for Good** - An electromagnetic pulse or major grid failure could leave millions without power for weeks or months. Understanding the real risks and preparing practical defenses puts you ahead of the curve.

## What Is an EMP?

An **electromagnetic pulse (EMP)** is a burst of electromagnetic energy that can disrupt or destroy electronic equipment. There are two primary sources:

### Solar EMP (Coronal Mass Ejection)

- Massive eruption of solar plasma directed at Earth
- Can induce powerful currents in long conductors (power lines, pipelines)
- The **Carrington Event** of 1859 is the most famous example: telegraph lines sparked and caught fire
- A Carrington-level event today could damage large power transformers that take **12-24 months to replace**
- NOAA and NASA actively monitor for solar storms

### Man-Made EMP (Nuclear or Non-Nuclear)

- **HEMP (High-Altitude EMP):** Nuclear detonation 25-250 miles above Earth creates three pulse phases (E1, E2, E3)
- **E1 pulse:** Extremely fast (nanoseconds), damages small electronics and semiconductors
- **E2 pulse:** Similar to lightning, most systems already have some protection
- **E3 pulse:** Slow pulse, similar to solar storm, damages transformers and long-line infrastructure
- **Non-nuclear EMP devices:** Limited range, primarily a concern for military and critical infrastructure

## Realistic Risk Assessment

**Be prepared, not paranoid.** Here is an honest look at the probabilities:

| Threat | Likelihood | Potential Impact |
|--------|-----------|-----------------|
| Major solar storm (Carrington-level) | Moderate (estimated 12% chance per decade) | Regional to continental grid damage |
| Nuclear HEMP attack | Very low | Continental-scale electronics damage |
| Localized non-nuclear EMP | Very low | Limited area, targeted |
| Conventional grid failure (ice storm, cyber attack, infrastructure failure) | **Moderate to high** | Regional, days to weeks |

**Key takeaway:** You are far more likely to experience a conventional extended power outage than a true EMP. The good news is that preparing for an EMP also prepares you for any grid-down scenario.

### What an EMP Will NOT Do

- It will **not** cause cars to explode or all vehicles to stop permanently (testing shows most vehicles restart after an EMP)
- It will **not** affect people directly (no health effects from the pulse itself)
- It will **not** destroy every single electronic device (shielded, unplugged, and battery-powered devices have some natural protection)

## Building a Faraday Cage

A **Faraday cage** is a conductive enclosure that blocks electromagnetic fields. You do not need expensive military-grade equipment. Two proven DIY methods work well.

### Method 1: Galvanized Trash Can

**Materials needed:**
- New galvanized steel trash can with tight-fitting lid (20 or 30 gallon)
- Aluminum foil tape (not regular duct tape)
- Heavy-duty aluminum foil
- Cardboard or foam padding

**Steps:**
1. Line the inside of the can completely with cardboard or foam (items must **not** touch the metal walls)
2. Wrap each electronic device individually in cloth or bubble wrap
3. Place wrapped devices inside the lined can
4. Ensure the lid makes good contact all the way around
5. Seal the lid seam with **aluminum foil tape** (overlap generously)
6. If the lid fit is loose, add a layer of aluminum foil between the lid and can rim
7. Store the can off the ground and away from long metal conductors

**Effectiveness:** Provides approximately **40-60 dB of shielding** when properly sealed. This is sufficient to protect against most EMP scenarios.

### Method 2: Ammo Can

**Materials needed:**
- Metal military surplus ammo can (.50 cal size works well)
- Aluminum foil tape
- Cardboard or foam for lining

**Steps:**
1. Line the interior completely with cardboard (cover all metal surfaces)
2. Place small electronics inside (this works well for radios, USB drives, small chargers)
3. Close the lid firmly
4. Seal the rubber gasket area with aluminum foil tape (the rubber gasket is a weak point)
5. For extra protection, wrap the entire sealed can in two layers of heavy-duty aluminum foil

**Effectiveness:** Excellent for small items. The tight-fitting lid provides better baseline shielding than a trash can.

### Testing Your Faraday Cage

1. Place an AM/FM radio inside, tuned to a strong local station at high volume
2. Close and seal the cage
3. If you can still hear the radio, the cage has leaks
4. Check seams and seal any gaps with aluminum foil tape
5. Also test with a cell phone inside: call it and see if it rings

## What to Protect

Prioritize items that would be most difficult to replace or most critical for survival.

### High Priority

- **Hand-crank or solar AM/FM/NOAA weather radio** (your primary information source)
- **LED flashlights and headlamps** (with extra batteries)
- **Portable solar charger and power bank**
- **Two-way radios (FRS/GMRS or ham)**
- **Critical medical devices** (glucose meters, CPAP backup batteries, hearing aids)
- **USB drives with important documents and survival references**

### Medium Priority

- **Battery charger** (for AA/AAA rechargeable batteries)
- **Small portable solar panel** (10-25 watts)
- **Spare cell phone** (even without service, it holds your maps, references, contacts)
- **Multimeter** (for testing batteries and circuits post-event)
- **Inverter** (12V to 120V, for car battery power)

### Lower Priority (But Still Useful)

- Laptop or tablet loaded with offline references
- Spare ignition module for your vehicle
- Digital watch
- Calculator

## Grid-Down Scenarios

A long-term grid failure can result from many causes beyond EMP. Understanding the cascading effects helps you prepare.

### The Cascade of Failure

**Hours 1-6:**
- Traffic lights fail, roads gridlock
- Cell towers switch to battery backup (4-8 hours typical)
- Gas stations cannot pump fuel (electric pumps)
- ATMs and credit card systems go offline

**Hours 6-24:**
- Cell service fails as tower batteries die
- Water pressure drops (pumping stations need power)
- Refrigerated food begins to spoil
- Hospitals switch to generators (limited fuel supply)

**Days 2-7:**
- Municipal water may stop flowing
- Stores are emptied or closed
- Fuel becomes unavailable
- Sanitation systems begin to fail
- Law enforcement is overwhelmed

**Weeks 2-4:**
- Food supply chain completely disrupted
- Medical supply shortages become critical
- Social order under significant stress
- Improvised community systems begin forming

## Living Without Electricity

### Water

- **Municipal water may stop** within 24-72 hours without grid power
- Store a minimum of **1 gallon per person per day** (2 weeks minimum)
- Fill bathtubs and large containers immediately when power fails
- Have water purification methods ready (filters, purification tablets, boiling)
- Know your nearest natural water source

### Food

- **Eat perishables first** (refrigerator, then freezer)
- A full freezer stays cold approximately **48 hours** if kept closed
- Transition to shelf-stable foods (canned goods, dried foods, rice, beans)
- Cook with camp stoves, grills, or open fire (outdoors only for combustion)
- Stock at least **2 weeks of shelf-stable food** per person

### Light

- LED flashlights and lanterns (most efficient)
- Candles (fire risk -- use with extreme caution)
- Oil lamps (store lamp oil safely)
- Solar-charged garden lights (bring indoors at night)
- Chemical light sticks for safe no-fire lighting

### Temperature Control

- **In winter:** Layer clothing, seal one small room, use sleeping bags rated for cold, consider a safe indoor-rated propane or kerosene heater with ventilation
- **In summer:** Open windows for cross-ventilation at night, use battery fans, stay hydrated, minimize activity during peak heat

### Sanitation

- Without water pressure, toilets stop flushing
- Store supplies for a **bucket toilet** (5-gallon bucket, heavy bags, kitty litter or lime)
- Have a plan for waste disposal away from water sources
- Stock hand sanitizer and hygiene supplies

## First 72 Hours of Total Grid Failure

### Immediate Actions (Hours 0-4)

- [ ] **Confirm the outage scope** -- check battery radio for emergency broadcasts
- [ ] **Fill all available containers with water** (bathtubs, pots, bottles)
- [ ] **Unplug sensitive electronics** to protect from surge when power returns
- [ ] **Check on neighbors**, especially elderly or medically dependent
- [ ] **Locate your emergency supplies** (flashlights, radio, first aid kit)
- [ ] **Fuel up vehicles** if gas stations are still operational
- [ ] **Withdraw cash** from ATM if still functional (small bills)
- [ ] **Charge all devices** from car battery if possible

### Short-Term Actions (Hours 4-24)

- [ ] **Assess food supply** and plan consumption order (perishables first)
- [ ] **Establish a light and communication routine** (conserve batteries)
- [ ] **Set up rain collection** or identify alternative water sources
- [ ] **Secure your home** (lock doors, close curtains for security and insulation)
- [ ] **Inventory all batteries, fuel, and critical supplies**
- [ ] **Establish contact with neighbors** for mutual aid
- [ ] **Monitor emergency radio broadcasts** on the hour

### Extended Actions (Days 1-3)

- [ ] **Establish a cooking plan** using non-electric methods
- [ ] **Set up sanitation system** if water pressure is lost
- [ ] **Create a security watch** with trusted neighbors if needed
- [ ] **Ration non-renewable supplies** (batteries, fuel, disposable items)
- [ ] **Deploy Faraday-stored electronics** if the event was EMP-related (wait 24 hours to confirm no secondary pulses)
- [ ] **Begin water purification** from alternative sources if tap water is unavailable
- [ ] **Document the situation** (notes for insurance, future reference)

## Maintaining Preparedness

### Quarterly Check

- [ ] Inspect Faraday cage seals and tape
- [ ] Test stored electronics (turn on, verify function)
- [ ] Rotate batteries in stored devices
- [ ] Review and update your emergency plan

### Annual Review

- [ ] Replace aluminum foil tape on Faraday cages
- [ ] Update USB drives with current documents
- [ ] Refresh water storage
- [ ] Restock any used or expired supplies
- [ ] Practice your grid-down plan with your household for at least 24 hours

## Key Principles

1. **Prepare for a grid-down event, and you are automatically prepared for EMP.** The practical response is nearly identical.
2. **Redundancy matters.** Have backup methods for water, light, heat, and communication.
3. **Community is your best asset.** No one survives a long-term grid failure alone. Build relationships with neighbors now.
4. **Information is power.** A working radio is one of your most valuable post-EMP assets. Protect it.
5. **Stay calm and think clearly.** Most grid outages resolve within days. Even severe events are survivable with basic preparation.
