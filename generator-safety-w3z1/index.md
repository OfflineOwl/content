# Generator Safety (If Available)

> **Power with Responsibility** - Generators provide valuable backup power, but improper use kills people every year through carbon monoxide poisoning, electrocution, and fires. This guide covers safe operation.

## Carbon Monoxide: The Invisible Killer

### The Danger

- Generators produce carbon monoxide (CO)
- CO is colorless and odorless
- Exposure can kill in minutes
- Most generator deaths are from CO poisoning

### Absolute Rules

**NEVER run a generator:**
- Indoors
- In a garage (even with door open)
- Near windows, doors, or vents
- In any enclosed or partially enclosed space

**ALWAYS:**
- Operate outside, far from openings
- 20+ feet from building
- Exhaust pointed away from structure
- Have working CO detectors in home

## Placement Guidelines

### Safe Distance

| Location | Minimum Distance |
|----------|-----------------|
| From windows | 20 feet |
| From doors | 20 feet |
| From vents | 20 feet |
| From air intakes | 20+ feet |

### Positioning

- Place on level, stable surface
- Exhaust pointing away from all buildings
- Away from areas where people congregate
- Protected from rain (but not enclosed)
- Away from combustibles

### Weather Protection

- Use canopy or tarp ABOVE generator
- Ensure adequate ventilation all sides
- Never enclose sides
- Keep dry but not enclosed
- Don't operate in flooded areas

## Electrical Safety

### Extension Cords

**Requirements:**
- Outdoor-rated heavy-duty cords
- Proper gauge for load
- No damage or exposed wires
- Proper length (not coiled when in use)

**Cord gauge guide:**

| Load | Up to 50 ft | 50-100 ft |
|------|-------------|-----------|
| Up to 7 amps | 16 gauge | 14 gauge |
| 7-10 amps | 14 gauge | 12 gauge |
| 10-15 amps | 12 gauge | 10 gauge |

### Never Back-feed

**NEVER plug generator into wall outlet (back-feeding):**
- Can electrocute utility workers
- Can damage your home's wiring
- Can damage generator
- Illegal in most areas

**Proper connection methods:**
1. Extension cords to individual appliances
2. Transfer switch (professionally installed)
3. Interlock device (professionally installed)

### Transfer Switch

**Safest method for whole-house connection:**
- Professionally installed
- Isolates home from grid
- Allows safe use of home wiring
- Required by electrical codes
- Must be sized for generator

## Fueling Safety

### Before Refueling

1. Turn off generator
2. Let cool (at least 2 minutes)
3. Open container slowly
4. Ground container if possible

### During Refueling

1. Don't overfill (expansion space needed)
2. Use funnel to avoid spills
3. Wipe up any spills immediately
4. Keep fuel away from hot surfaces

### After Refueling

1. Replace gas cap securely
2. Move fuel container away
3. Check for leaks
4. Then restart

**NEVER refuel a running or hot generator.**

---

## Operation Guidelines

### Starting

1. Ensure nothing is plugged in
2. Turn fuel valve to ON
3. Set choke (if equipped)
4. Start generator
5. Let warm up (2-3 minutes)
6. Plug in devices gradually

### Running

- Don't exceed rated capacity
- Add loads gradually
- Monitor for overloading signs
- Check fuel level regularly
- Keep area clear of debris
- Monitor exhaust direction

### Shutting Down

1. Turn off connected devices
2. Unplug all loads
3. Let run a few minutes (cools down)
4. Turn off generator
5. Turn fuel valve to OFF
6. Let cool before storage or covering

## Load Management

### Understanding Ratings

| Rating | Meaning |
|--------|---------|
| Running watts | Continuous power output |
| Surge/starting watts | Brief high output for starting motors |

### Common Appliance Loads

| Appliance | Running | Starting |
|-----------|---------|----------|
| Refrigerator | 100-400W | 1200W |
| Sump pump | 500-1000W | 1500-2500W |
| Window AC | 500-1500W | 2000-3000W |
| Space heater | 1500W | 1500W |
| Lights (LED) | 5-15W | 5-15W |
| Phone charger | 5-10W | 5-10W |

### Load Priority

1. Medical devices
2. Refrigerator/freezer (cycle on/off)
3. Sump pump (if needed)
4. Minimal lighting
5. Communication charging
6. Other needs

## Maintenance

### Before Emergency Season

- [ ] Run generator monthly (15-30 minutes)
- [ ] Check oil level (change per manual)
- [ ] Check fuel system for leaks
- [ ] Test under load
- [ ] Clean air filter
- [ ] Check spark plug
- [ ] Verify all safety features work
- [ ] Check extension cords

### Fuel Storage

- Use fuel stabilizer
- Rotate fuel stock
- Don't store fuel in generator long-term
- See Fuel Storage Safety guide

### After Extended Use

- Run until carburetor is dry (or drain)
- Change oil if heavy use
- Clean unit
- Address any issues noted
- Store properly

## Safety Checklist

### Before Each Use

- [ ] Generator is outdoors, 20+ feet from building
- [ ] Exhaust points away from all openings
- [ ] CO detectors working in home
- [ ] Oil level checked
- [ ] Fuel level adequate
- [ ] Extension cords in good condition
- [ ] Nothing plugged in before starting

### During Use

- [ ] Monitor for CO detector alarms
- [ ] Don't exceed rated capacity
- [ ] Check for overheating
- [ ] Keep area clear
- [ ] Never run indoors for any reason

## Emergency Responses

### If CO Detector Alarms

1. Get everyone outside immediately
2. Call 911
3. Don't re-enter until cleared
4. Relocate generator farther away
5. Check for symptoms (headache, dizziness)

### If Generator Overloads

1. Turn off high-draw items
2. Let generator recover
3. Reduce total load
4. Add loads gradually

### If Generator Won't Start

1. Check fuel
2. Check oil (low oil shutoff)
3. Check choke setting
4. Check spark plug
5. Consult manual
