# Generator Safety

> **Outside only, far from the house.** Generator exhaust is full of carbon monoxide (CO), a gas you can't see or smell that can kill in minutes. In the US, about 100 people die each year from CO from portable generators, and generators also cause electrocutions and fires when used wrong.

## Do this now

**If a CO alarm sounds, or anyone feels dizzy, sick, weak, confused, or gets a headache while a generator is running:**

1. **Get everyone outside into fresh air immediately.** Don't stop to open windows or look for the source.
2. **Call 911** (or your local emergency number) **from outside.**
3. **Stay outside** until emergency responders say it's safe to go back in.
4. **Get medical care** for anyone with symptoms. CO poisoning can be serious even after the person feels better.

**Before you start a generator:**

- **Outdoors only, at least 20 feet from the house**, with the exhaust pointed away from windows, doors, and vents.
- **Never** in a home, garage, basement, crawlspace, shed, or carport, **even with doors and windows open**.
- **Working CO alarms** inside, on every level and outside sleeping areas.
- **Never plug the generator into a wall outlet.**

## Carbon Monoxide: The Main Danger

### Why It Kills

- CO comes from burning fuel. A generator's exhaust contains very high levels of it.
- You can't see or smell it. If you can smell exhaust, you're breathing it, and CO with it.
- Opening doors and windows or running fans **does not** make it safe to run a generator indoors or in an attached garage. CO can build up to deadly levels in minutes and linger for hours, even after the generator shuts off.
- **People who are asleep, drunk, or otherwise impaired can die before they ever notice symptoms.**

### Symptoms of CO Poisoning

The most common symptoms are:

- Headache
- Dizziness
- Weakness
- Upset stomach or vomiting
- Chest pain
- Confusion

They're often described as "flu-like." Clues that it's CO, not the flu: more than one person in the same place feels sick, symptoms ease when you leave the building, and a fuel-burning device was running. High levels cause loss of consciousness and death.

**At higher risk:** infants, older adults, and people with chronic heart disease, anemia, or breathing problems. But CO can kill anyone.

### CO Alarms

- Install battery-operated or battery-backup CO alarms **outside each sleeping area and on every level of your home.** Interconnected alarms (where one sounds them all) are best.
- Choose alarms listed to a recognized standard (in the US, UL 2034 or CSA 6.19).
- Test them monthly, and replace batteries as the manufacturer says.
- Replace the alarm when the manufacturer says to or when its end-of-life signal sounds. Check the date on the back.
- A CO alarm is not a substitute for running the generator in the right place.

## Where to Put the Generator

- **Outdoors, at least 20 feet from your home** and other buildings, and from any window, door, or vent, yours or a neighbor's. Use an extension cord long enough to reach.
- **Point the exhaust away** from windows, doors, vents, and any building people could be in.
- **Keep windows and other openings closed** on the side of the house facing the generator.
- **Never** run it inside a home, garage, basement, crawlspace, or shed, or on a porch or in a carport, even with doors open and fans running.
- Leave 3 to 4 feet of clear space on all sides and above it, and keep it away from anything that can burn.
- Don't stand downwind of the exhaust, and keep it away from where people sleep, play, or gather.

### Rain and Snow

- Water and generators can electrocute you. **Dry your hands before touching the generator.**
- In rain or snow, run it on a dry surface under an open, canopy-like structure (or a non-combustible tent made for generators) where water can't reach it or puddle underneath. Otherwise, wait until the weather passes.
- Don't drape a tarp over it, and never move it into a garage, porch, or carport to keep it dry.
- Don't run a generator in standing water or on a flooded surface.

## Electrical Safety

### Never Backfeed

**Don't plug a generator into a wall outlet** (with a "double male" or "suicide" cord). This is called backfeeding, and it:

- Sends power back out through the utility lines and transformer, where it can **electrocute utility workers** and neighbors served by the same transformer
- Bypasses some of your home's built-in circuit protection, which can lead to fires

### Safe Ways to Connect

1. **Plug appliances directly into the generator** using heavy-duty, outdoor-rated extension cords.
2. **To power home circuits,** have a qualified, licensed electrician install a properly rated **transfer switch**. It disconnects your house from the grid so power can't flow back into the lines.

### Extension Cords

- Use heavy-duty cords made for outdoor use, with all three prongs intact.
- **Each cord's wattage rating must be more than the total load** of everything plugged into it. Undersized cords overheat and start fires.
- Check cords for cuts and damaged plugs before every use.
- Protect cords where they pass through a window or door so they aren't pinched, and don't run them under rugs.
- Use ground-fault circuit interrupter (GFCI) protection where you can, and keep connections dry.

## Fuel and Fire Safety

- **Turn off the generator and let it cool down before refueling.** Gasoline spilled on hot engine parts can ignite, and engines stay hot for a long time after they stop.
- **Never store or bring gasoline into your home** or basement. Store it outside living areas, such as in a detached shed, in labeled safety containers (not glass).
- Keep stored fuel away from fuel-burning appliances, such as a water heater in a garage.
- Fill portable gas cans on the ground, not in a vehicle or truck bed.
- Wipe up spills before restarting, and keep a fire extinguisher nearby.

## Running It

### Starting

1. Place it outdoors in a safe spot (see above).
2. Make sure nothing is plugged in.
3. Check oil and fuel.
4. Start it following the manual, and let it run for a few minutes.
5. Plug in appliances one at a time.

### Load Management

Generators are rated in **running watts** (continuous) and **starting or surge watts** (the short burst motors need to start). Don't exceed either. Check each appliance's label or manual for its wattage; motors such as refrigerators, sump pumps, well pumps, and air conditioners need much more power to start than to run.

Prioritize:

1. Medical devices
2. Refrigerator and freezer (they can be cycled on and off)
3. Sump pump, if needed
4. A few lights
5. Phone and radio charging

If the generator labors or trips its breaker, unplug things until it recovers, then add loads back gradually.

### Shutting Down

1. Turn off and unplug appliances.
2. Turn off the generator.
3. Let it cool before covering, refueling, or storing it.

## Choosing a Generator

- Look for a model with an **automatic CO shutoff**, certified to the PGMA G300-2023 or UL 2201 safety standard. These turn the generator off if CO builds up, and UL 2201 models also give off less CO. These features reduce the risk but don't make it safe to run a generator indoors or near openings.
- Read and follow the manufacturer's instructions and warning labels.
- Battery power stations (no engine) give off no exhaust and can be used indoors, but they hold limited power.

## Maintenance

- [ ] Run the generator periodically (follow the manual) so it starts when you need it
- [ ] Change the oil and clean the air filter on schedule
- [ ] Check for fuel leaks and damaged cords
- [ ] Test your CO alarms
- [ ] Use fuel stabilizer, or follow the manual on draining fuel for storage
- [ ] Rotate stored fuel

## Safety Checklist

### Before Each Use

- [ ] Outdoors, 20+ feet from the house
- [ ] Exhaust pointed away from all windows, doors, and vents
- [ ] Not in a garage, basement, carport, shed, or on a porch
- [ ] CO alarms inside are working
- [ ] Cords are outdoor-rated, undamaged, and rated above the load
- [ ] Not connected to a wall outlet
- [ ] Generator is dry, and so are your hands

### During Use

- [ ] Listen for CO alarms
- [ ] Watch for headaches, dizziness, or nausea in anyone
- [ ] Don't exceed the rated load
- [ ] Shut down and let cool before refueling
