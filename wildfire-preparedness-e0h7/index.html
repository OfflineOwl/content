Wildfire Preparedness

When Fire Threatens - Wildfires can move faster than you can run. Preparation and early evacuation save lives.

Understanding Wildfire Threat

How Fires Spread

  • Direct flame contact - Fire reaches structure
  • Radiant heat - Intense heat ignites materials
  • Embers - Flying embers land on combustibles

Ember attack is the most common cause of home ignition during wildfires.

Risk Factors

Higher risk if:

  • Near wildland areas
  • Dense vegetation near home
  • Dry climate/drought conditions
  • Limited evacuation routes
  • Wooden construction

Defensible Space

Zone 0: 0-5 feet from home

Hardscape zone:

  • No combustibles
  • No mulch
  • No plants except well-watered lawn
  • Clean gutters
  • No stored combustibles
Zone 1: 5-30 feet from home

Lean, clean, green zone:

  • Minimal vegetation
  • No ladder fuels
  • Trees spaced 10+ feet apart
  • Trim branches 6+ feet from ground
  • Remove dead vegetation
Zone 2: 30-100 feet

Reduced fuel zone:

  • Reduced vegetation density
  • Space trees appropriately
  • Remove dead material
  • Create fuel breaks

Home Hardening

Priority Upgrades

Area Improvement
Roof Class A fire-rated materials
Vents 1/8" or smaller mesh (ember-resistant)
Windows Double-pane, tempered glass
Decking Non-combustible or ignition-resistant
Fencing Non-combustible near house
Eaves Enclosed or fire-resistant

When Fire Threatens

Warning Levels

  • Fire Weather Watch - Conditions favorable for fire
  • Red Flag Warning - Extreme fire conditions expected
  • Evacuation Warning - Be ready to leave
  • Evacuation Order - Leave NOW

Evacuation Preparation

Go-bag ready with:

  • Important documents
  • Medications
  • Phone chargers
  • Clothing
  • Water
  • Pet supplies
  • N95 masks

Ready-Set-Go

READY

Prepare home and family

SET

Be alert, ready to leave

GO

Evacuate immediately

If Ordered to Evacuate

  1. Leave immediately
  2. Follow designated routes
  3. Don't block roadways
  4. Check in with out-of-area contact
  5. Don't return until cleared

If Trapped by Fire

This is last resort - always evacuate early.

If trapped at home:

  1. Call 911 with location
  2. Stay inside
  3. Close all windows and doors
  4. Close interior doors
  5. Move away from exterior walls/windows
  6. Get in room with fewest windows
  7. Stay low if smoke enters
  8. Fill sinks/tubs with water

Air Quality During Wildfires

Smoke Protection

  • Stay indoors
  • Keep windows/doors closed
  • Run AC on recirculate
  • Use HEPA air purifiers
  • Wear N95 mask outdoors
  • Avoid physical exertion

Health Effects

  • Irritated eyes/throat
  • Coughing
  • Difficulty breathing
  • Headaches
  • May worsen existing conditions
