VOLCANIC ASH SAFETY
===================

Volcanic ash poses serious health and safety risks even far from
the volcano. This guide covers protection from ashfall.

UNDERSTANDING VOLCANIC ASH
--------------------------

WHAT IT IS:
- Pulverized rock, glass, minerals
- NOT like wood ash
- Abrasive and potentially toxic
- Can travel hundreds of miles
- Very fine particles

HEALTH HAZARDS:
- Respiratory irritation
- Eye damage
- Skin irritation
- Aggravates existing conditions
- Contains silica (lung damage)

OTHER HAZARDS:
- Visibility near zero
- Slippery surfaces
- Electrical shorts
- Roof collapse (heavy when wet)
- Water contamination
- Engine damage

BEFORE ASHFALL
--------------

PREPARE YOUR HOME:
- Close all windows and doors
- Turn off HVAC
- Cover vents with damp cloth/filters
- Bring animals inside
- Move vehicles to garage
- Cover outdoor equipment

PREPARE SUPPLIES:
- Dust masks (N95 or better)
- Goggles (wrap-around, no vents)
- Long sleeves and pants
- Water supply
- Plastic sheeting
- Wet cleaning supplies

DURING ASHFALL
--------------

STAY INSIDE:
- Don't go outside unless necessary
- Keep doors and windows closed
- Use damp towels under doors
- Run air purifier if available

IF YOU MUST GO OUTSIDE:
Wear:
- N95 mask (or damp cloth)
- Goggles (sealed type)
- Long sleeves and pants
- Hat
- Covered shoes

Keep exposure brief
Decontaminate when returning

DRIVING:
- Avoid if possible
- Greatly reduced visibility
- Use headlights
- Drive very slowly
- Don't use AC (clogs filter)
- Engine damage risk
- Brake carefully (slippery)

WATER:
- Cover all water sources
- Don't use contaminated water
- Wait for official guidance
- Municipal supply may be affected

PROTECTING YOUR HEALTH
----------------------

RESPIRATORY:
- N95 mask minimum
- P100 preferred
- Wet cloth as last resort
- Stay indoors as much as possible
- Those with breathing conditions stay inside

EYES:
- Wear goggles
- Don't wear contacts (traps ash)
- Flush eyes with clean water if irritated
- Don't rub eyes

SKIN:
- Cover all exposed skin
- Wash off ash promptly
- Don't let ash dry on skin

AFTER ASHFALL
-------------

CLEANING UP:
Wear PPE:
- Mask (N95+)
- Goggles
- Gloves
- Long sleeves

WET methods preferred:
- Lightly wet ash before sweeping
- Prevents re-suspension
- Avoid dry sweeping/blowing

ROOF:
- Ash is heavy, especially when wet
- May need to clear to prevent collapse
- Use extreme caution on roof
- Work in pairs

DON'T:
- Use leaf blowers
- Dry sweep
- Wash ash into drains (clogs)
- Let ash accumulate too long

PROPER DISPOSAL:
- Bag ash in heavy plastic
- Follow local disposal guidance
- Keep away from storm drains
- May require special disposal

VEHICLES:
- Don't run engine until cleaned
- Ash damages engines
- Don't use wipers dry (scratches)
- Wet down before washing
- Replace air filter before running

WATER SAFETY:
- Wait for official all-clear
- Rainwater may be contaminated
- Well water may be affected
- Don't use for drinking until tested

SPECIAL CONCERNS
----------------

CHILDREN:
- Keep inside
- Schools may close
- Extra respiratory protection

PETS:
- Keep inside
- Wipe paws if they go out
- Don't let them drink outside water
- Watch for respiratory distress

AGRICULTURE:
- Protect water sources
- Cover or shelter animals
- Don't let livestock eat ash-covered grass
- Wash produce thoroughly

ELECTRONICS:
- Ash is conductive when wet
- Cover equipment
- Don't use until cleaned
- Computers especially vulnerable

QUICK REFERENCE
---------------

DURING ASHFALL:
- Stay inside
- Close all openings
- Turn off HVAC
- Wet cloths under doors
- Mask if must go out

OUTSIDE PROTECTION:
- N95 mask (minimum)
- Sealed goggles
- Cover all skin
- Keep exposure brief

CLEANUP:
- Wet methods only
- Don't dry sweep
- Wear mask and goggles
- Don't wash into drains
- Clear roof if heavy

DON'T:
- Use contacts
- Dry sweep or blow
- Run vehicles until cleaned
- Drink water until cleared
