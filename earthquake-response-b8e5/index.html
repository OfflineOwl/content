Earthquake Response & Aftermath

When the Ground Shakes - Earthquakes strike without warning. Knowing what to do during and after can save your life.

During an Earthquake

If Indoors

DROP, COVER, and HOLD ON

  1. DROP - Get on hands and knees
  2. COVER - Get under sturdy table/desk, or against interior wall
  3. HOLD ON - Protect head and neck, hold on to shelter

Stay away from:

  • Windows
  • Exterior walls
  • Heavy furniture that can fall
  • Hanging objects
Do NOT:
  • Run outside (falling debris)
  • Stand in doorways (myth - not safer)
  • Use elevators

If Outdoors

  • Move away from buildings, power lines, trees
  • Drop and cover in open area
  • Stay away from overpasses and bridges
  • Stay there until shaking stops

If Driving

  • Pull over safely away from overpasses/bridges
  • Stay in vehicle with seatbelt on
  • Vehicle provides protection from falling debris
  • When shaking stops, proceed carefully

If Near Coast

After shaking stops:

  • If strong shaking lasts 20+ seconds, move to high ground
  • Don't wait for official warning
  • Tsunami may follow earthquake
  • Go inland or uphill immediately

After the Earthquake

Immediate Actions

  1. Check yourself for injuries
  2. Check others around you
  3. Expect aftershocks (drop, cover, hold on again)
  4. Exit carefully if building is damaged
  5. Don't use elevators

Safety Assessment

Check for:

  • Fire and fire hazards
  • Gas leaks (smell, hissing)
  • Water damage
  • Structural damage
  • Downed power lines
  • Chemical spills

If You Smell Gas

  1. Don't turn on/off any switches
  2. Don't use flames or create sparks
  3. Open windows if possible
  4. Leave building immediately
  5. Shut off gas at meter if you know how
  6. Call gas company from outside

Structural Damage Signs

Leave building if:
  • Large cracks in walls/ceiling
  • Shifted foundation
  • Partial collapse
  • Gas smell or broken pipes
  • Electrical problems

Aftershocks

What to Expect

  • Can occur minutes to months after main quake
  • Often nearly as strong as main quake
  • Frequency decreases over time
  • Response: Same as main quake (drop, cover, hold)

Psychological Impact

  • Heightened anxiety is normal
  • Startle response common
  • Sleep disruption expected
  • Children may need extra reassurance

Recovery Steps

First Hours

  • Account for all family members
  • Check for injuries
  • Assess damage
  • Check utilities
  • Contact out-of-area contact
  • Document damage (photos)

First Days

  • Secure your home if safe to do so
  • Assess supplies
  • Connect with neighbors
  • Monitor emergency communications
  • File insurance claims
  • Be cautious entering damaged buildings

Water and Food Safety

  • Use stored water until supply is verified safe
  • Don't use questionable tap water
  • Check food for contamination
  • Discard food from damaged containers

Earthquake Preparedness

Home Safety

  • Secure heavy furniture to walls
  • Secure water heater
  • Use latches on cabinets
  • Don't hang heavy items over beds
  • Know how to shut off gas, water, electricity
  • Store supplies (2+ weeks recommended in earthquake zones)

Go Bag Ready

Earthquakes can require immediate evacuation:

  • Go bag near exit
  • Sturdy shoes near bed
  • Flashlight near bed
