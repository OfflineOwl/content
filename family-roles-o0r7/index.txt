FAMILY ROLES & RESPONSIBILITIES
===============================

Assigning specific roles ensures everyone knows what to do during
an emergency. This guide helps you organize your family for response.

WHY ASSIGN ROLES
----------------

BENEFITS:
- Eliminates confusion during stress
- Ensures critical tasks get done
- Empowers each family member
- Reduces panic through clear expectations
- Creates accountability
- Speeds response time

CORE FAMILY ROLES
-----------------

COMMUNICATIONS COORDINATOR
Primary: (Usually adult 1)
Backup: (Usually adult 2 or teen)

Responsibilities:
- Contact out-of-area contact
- Monitor emergency broadcasts
- Communicate family status
- Coordinate if family separated
- Track check-in times

Backup: _______________

Backup: _______________

ROLES BY AGE
------------

ADULTS:
- Take primary roles
- Make critical decisions
- Handle dangerous tasks
- Coordinate family response
- Model calm behavior

TEENAGERS (13-17):
- Can take backup roles
- Assist with younger children
- Help with physical tasks
- Carry own go-bag
- Follow directions while showing initiative

CHILDREN (8-12):
- Simple assigned tasks
- Carry personal go-bag
- Help younger siblings
- Follow instructions
- Know emergency information

YOUNG CHILDREN (4-7):
- Stay with assigned adult
- Know full name and address
- Know emergency meeting point
- Carry comfort item
- Practice staying calm

TODDLERS/INFANTS:
- Always assigned to specific adult
- Needs met by designated person
- Supplies prepared by adult

ROLE ASSIGNMENT PROCESS
-----------------------

STEP 1: IDENTIFY TASKS
List all tasks needed during emergencies:
- Who will grab supplies?
- Who will manage children?
- Who will contact family?
- Who will handle utilities?
- Who will manage pets?
- Who will handle medical?

STEP 2: MATCH SKILLS
Assign based on:
- Ability to perform
- Location during typical day
- Physical capability
- Knowledge and training
- Reliability

STEP 3: DESIGNATE BACKUPS
Every critical role needs backup:
- Primary may not be present
- Primary may be incapacitated
- Cross-training essential

STEP 4: DOCUMENT
Write down assignments:
- Who does what
- Where supplies are
- How tasks are done
- When to perform tasks

STEP 5: PRACTICE
Drills reveal problems:
- Does everyone know their role?
- Can backups perform duties?
- Are there gaps?
- What needs improvement?

SPECIAL CONSIDERATIONS
----------------------

SINGLE PARENT FAMILIES:
- Identify trusted neighbors as backup
- Older children take more responsibility
- Pre-arrange mutual aid
- Have outside contact for support

MULTI-GENERATIONAL FAMILIES:
- Assign roles based on capability
- Ensure elderly are supported
- Use experience of elders
- Clear communication chain

FAMILIES WITH SPECIAL NEEDS:
- Assign specific caregiver
- Backup caregiver designated
- Equipment responsibilities assigned
- Medical information accessible

BLENDED FAMILIES:
- Clear roles regardless of custody
- Both households have plans
- Children know both plans
- Adults coordinate

UPDATING ROLES
--------------

REVIEW REGULARLY:
- After practice drills
- When family composition changes
- When capabilities change
- At least annually

ADJUST FOR:
- New family members
- Children growing up
- Physical changes
- New skills learned
- Lessons from events
