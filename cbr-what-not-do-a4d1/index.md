# What NOT to Do During a CBR Event

> In chemical, biological, and radiological (CBR) emergencies, the most dangerous mistakes are usually reasonable-sounding instincts: going to the basement, going outside to look, driving to get the kids. This guide lists the ones to avoid and what to do instead.

## The Five Most Dangerous Mistakes

1. **Using the wrong shelter room.**
   - **Chemical release:** do NOT go to the basement. Many toxic gases are heavier than air and sink. Use a sealed interior room **above ground**.
   - **Radiation:** do NOT go upstairs or stay by windows. Go to the **basement or center** of the building. You don't need to seal it.
2. **Going outside to look, or to collect family.** Fallout and many chemical clouds are invisible. Schools, daycares, and nursing homes have their own shelter plans. Stay where you are and text.
3. **Sheltering in a car.** Cars are not airtight and give little protection from radiation. Get into a building.
4. **Leaving shelter before officials give the all-clear.** Quiet doesn't mean safe.
5. **Taking potassium iodide (KI) without being told to.** It protects only the thyroid, only from radioactive iodine, and too much can cause severe illness or death.

**Do call 911** (or your local emergency number) for trouble breathing, seizures, collapse, burns, or serious injury. **Poison Control: 1-800-222-1222** (US) for chemical exposure questions.

## General Mistakes to Avoid

### Don't Panic or Freeze

- Act on your plan: get inside, pick the right room, close up, tune in
- Help others stay calm, especially children
- Move to the protective spot first, then think about the next step

### Don't Ignore or Second-Guess Warnings

- Take every official warning seriously
- Don't wait to confirm it yourself; seconds matter in a chemical release
- Some toxic chemicals have no smell, so "I don't smell anything" doesn't mean safe
- A false alarm costs little; a missed warning can cost your life

### Don't Tie Up Emergency Lines

- Don't call 911 for general information; use radio, TV, and official alerts
- Text family instead of calling; texts get through more easily and leave lines free
- Do call 911 for real emergencies, even if networks are busy
- Go to a hospital if you have symptoms or are hurt. Officials may set up separate screening sites for people who are worried but not sick; follow their directions

## Chemical Event Mistakes

### Don't Shelter in the Basement

- Chlorine, sulfur mustard, and many other toxic gases are heavier than air and collect in low areas
- Use an interior room above ground, as high as practical, and seal it

### Don't Go Outside to Look

- Chemical clouds are often invisible
- "Just checking" exposes you
- Stay inside until officials give the all-clear

### Don't Walk or Drive Through the Affected Area

- Cars are not airtight
- If you're in a car, close windows and vents and turn off the air conditioner and heater, then get inside a building
- Move upwind of the source, not downwind

### Don't Use Fans to "Clear the Air"

- Fans and HVAC pull contaminated air inside
- Turn off heating, air conditioning, and all fans
- Seal, don't ventilate, until the all-clear. Then air the building out.

### Don't Make Yourself Vomit

- If a chemical was swallowed, don't make yourself throw up and don't drink anything else
- Call Poison Control or 911

### Don't Pull Contaminated Clothing Over Your Head

- Cut it off instead, so the chemical doesn't touch your eyes, nose, or mouth
- Double-bag it and don't put it in the regular trash

### Don't Mix Cleaning Chemicals

- Never mix bleach with ammonia or acids; it creates toxic gases
- Follow product directions

## Biological Event Mistakes

### Don't Touch or Clean Up a Suspicious Substance

- Get away from it
- Cover your mouth and nose with layers of fabric or a mask
- Report it to 911 or local authorities

### Don't Get Close to Others If Officials Say an Illness Is Spreading

- Avoid crowds; keep your distance from people who are sick
- Wash hands often with soap and water for at least 20 seconds

### Don't Share Food, Drinks, or Utensils

- Keep personal items personal
- Don't share masks or respirators

### Don't Ignore Quarantine or Isolation Orders

- They limit spread to your family and community
- Legal consequences may apply

### Don't Self-Medicate with Stockpiled Antibiotics

- They may not work against the actual agent
- Public health officials will say who needs medicine or vaccines and where to get them
- Follow medical guidance

## Radiological Event Mistakes

### Don't Go Upstairs or Stay by Windows

- Fallout settles on the roof and ground
- The basement or center of the building gives the most protection
- In a tall building, use the center of a middle floor, not the top floor

### Don't Waste Time Taping Windows

- Buildings don't need to be airtight to protect you from fallout; mass and distance do
- Close windows, doors, and vents and get to the best spot

### Don't Go Outside to Watch

- Fallout is invisible and most dangerous in the first hours
- Stay inside at least 24 hours unless officials say otherwise

### Don't Take Potassium Iodide Without Direction

- Protects only the thyroid, only from radioactive iodine
- Doesn't protect against fallout on the ground or other radioactive materials
- Too much can cause severe illness or death
- Table salt and iodine-rich foods are not substitutes
- Take it only if public health or emergency officials, or your doctor, tell you to

### Don't Use Conditioner After Exposure

- Conditioner makes radioactive material stick to hair
- Use shampoo or soap and rinse well

### Don't Scrub, Scald, or Scratch Your Skin

- Broken skin lets contamination in
- Use warm water and plenty of soap, and wash gently

### Don't Count on Hand Sanitizer or Cleaning Wipes

- Hand sanitizer doesn't remove radioactive material
- Don't use household cleaning wipes on skin
- Take off your outer clothing and wash with soap and water instead

### Don't Drink Alcohol "for Radiation"

- This is a myth
- It doesn't protect you
- It impairs judgment

## Information Mistakes

### Don't Spread Unverified Information

- Rumors cause panic and bad decisions
- Share only official information
- Check the source before forwarding

### Don't Switch Off Your Phone or Leave It in Airplane Mode

- Wireless Emergency Alerts don't reach phones that are off or in airplane mode
- Save battery by dimming the screen and closing apps instead
- Keep it charged

### Don't Rely on a Single Warning Source

- Sirens are meant for people outdoors and may not be heard inside
- Power or internet may be down
- Have a battery or hand-crank radio as well as phone alerts

---

## Protection Mistakes

### Don't Use Plastic Bags as Masks

- Suffocation risk
- Not effective
- Use a mask or layers of fabric over your nose and mouth

### Don't Stay Sealed Longer Than Officials Advise

- Sealed rooms are for short chemical releases, usually a few hours
- Outside air slowly leaks in, so protection drops over time
- About 10 square feet of floor space per person gives enough air for up to about 5 hours at rest
- Unseal and ventilate when officials say to, or evacuate if told; call 911 if anyone has trouble breathing

### Don't Use Candles, Grills, or Generators Inside

- Fire risk near plastic sheeting
- Generators, grills, and camp stoves give off carbon monoxide; use them only outdoors, at least 20 feet from the house

### Don't Leave Before the All-Clear

- Exposure is higher outside
- Leave early only for an immediate danger like fire or collapse, or if officials tell you to evacuate

## Quick Reference

```
DON'T DO THIS:

GENERAL:
- Panic or freeze
- Ignore warnings ("I don't smell anything")
- Call 911 just for information
- Spread rumors

CHEMICAL:
- Shelter in the BASEMENT (gases sink)
- Go outside to look
- Drive through the area
- Run fans or HVAC
- Pull dirty clothes over your head

BIOLOGICAL:
- Touch or clean up the substance
- Share items
- Break quarantine
- Self-medicate with antibiotics

RADIOLOGICAL:
- Go UPSTAIRS or stay by windows
- Shelter in a car
- Waste time taping windows
- Go out to collect family
- Take KI without being told
- Use conditioner or scrub skin

ALWAYS:
- Right room: chemical = UP and sealed
              radiation = DOWN or center
- Follow official guidance
- Wait for the all-clear
```
