# Evacuation Routes & Rally Points

> **Pre-Planning Saves Lives** - When disaster strikes, you won't have time to figure out where to go. This guide helps you establish evacuation routes and rally points before you need them.

## Why Plan Evacuation Routes?

During an emergency:
- Main roads become gridlocked
- GPS may not work
- Cell service may be down
- Signs may be damaged or missing
- Panic impairs navigation ability

**A pre-planned route that you've actually driven gives you a critical advantage.**

---

## Step 1: Identify Your Rally Points

You need three levels of rally points:

### Rally Point A: Near Home (Walking Distance)

**Purpose:** Immediate evacuation from your home
**When to use:** House fire, gas leak, domestic emergency

**Criteria:**
- Within 5-minute walk
- Easily identifiable location
- Safe from your home's immediate threats
- Known to all family members

**Good examples:**
- Neighbor's driveway (with their permission)
- Street corner with distinctive feature
- Community mailbox area
- Church or school parking lot

### Rally Point B: Neighborhood (Short Drive)

**Purpose:** Evacuation from immediate neighborhood
**When to use:** Neighborhood-wide emergency, localized flooding, civil unrest

**Criteria:**
- 1-5 miles from home
- Multiple routes to reach it
- Has basic facilities (shelter possible)
- Outside likely affected area

**Good examples:**
- Library or community center
- Shopping center parking lot
- Friend's house in adjacent neighborhood
- Fire station (not during their emergency)

### Rally Point C: Out of Area (Regional)

**Purpose:** Full evacuation from region
**When to use:** Major disaster, mandatory evacuation, regional threat

**Criteria:**
- 50+ miles from home (different direction than likely threats)
- Confirmed with contact person
- Multiple route options
- Sustainable for several days

**Good examples:**
- Family member's home in another city
- Pre-arranged hotel/motel
- Campground (with supplies)
- Community evacuation shelter

## Step 2: Map Your Routes

### Primary Route
Your fastest route under normal conditions. This is your default.

### Alternate Route 1
Different direction, avoids major highways. Use when primary is blocked.

### Alternate Route 2
Back roads option. Slower but avoids population centers.

### Route Documentation

For each route, document:

## Step 3: Route Planning Considerations

### Avoid These During Evacuation

- **Bridges:** May be damaged, closed, or gridlocked
- **Tunnels:** Can become death traps in some emergencies
- **Low-lying areas:** Flooding risk
- **Near industrial facilities:** Secondary hazards
- **Major highways:** Maximum traffic
- **Downtown areas:** Congestion and crowds
- **Single-access roads:** No alternatives if blocked

### Prefer These

- **Multiple lane roads:** More capacity
- **Routes with elevation:** Above flood levels
- **Roads with alternatives:** Can turn off if needed
- **Routes away from coast:** (if coastal threat)
- **Routes with services:** Gas, food, water available
- **Less populated routes:** Less competition

### Special Considerations by Vehicle

**Standard vehicle:**
- Stick to paved roads
- Monitor fuel carefully
- Consider trailer/cargo capacity

**4x4 / Off-road capable:**
- Can use unpaved alternatives
- Carry recovery gear
- Know your vehicle's limits

**Motorcycle:**
- Can navigate traffic
- Vulnerable to weather/debris
- Limited cargo capacity

**On foot:**
- Shortest route may not be safest
- Consider terrain and physical ability
- Plan rest stops and water sources

## Step 4: Create Route Maps

### Paper Maps Are Essential

Digital navigation fails when:
- Cell towers are down
- GPS satellites are unavailable
- Phones run out of battery
- Internet is disrupted

**Every family member should have:**
1. Paper map of local area
2. Routes marked with highlighter
3. Rally points marked
4. Key landmarks noted
5. Alternative turns marked

### Map Marking System

Use consistent marking:
- **Green highlighter:** Primary route
- **Yellow highlighter:** Alternate route 1
- **Orange highlighter:** Alternate route 2
- **Red circle:** Rally points
- **Blue star:** Gas stations
- **Black X:** Known problem areas

## Step 5: Practice Your Routes

### Drive Each Route

Before you need it:
1. Drive each route during normal conditions
2. Note actual drive time
3. Identify landmarks for navigation
4. Find alternates for potential blocks
5. Locate gas, food, and rest stops

### Practice Variations

- Drive at different times of day
- Try during bad weather (safely)
- Note how long to reach each point
- Identify areas that might flood/block

### Family Practice

- Make sure all drivers know all routes
- Quiz family on rally point locations
- Practice meeting at Rally Point A
- Verify everyone can read the maps

## Step 6: Communication Protocol

### Before Separating

- Confirm which rally point to use
- Set check-in times
- Confirm route each person will take
- Share final destination

### During Evacuation

| If... | Then... |
|-------|---------|
| Route is blocked | Take predetermined alternate |
| Separated from family | Go to agreed rally point |
| Cannot reach rally point | Contact out-of-area contact |
| Plans must change | Leave message at rally point |

### Rally Point Procedures

When arriving at a rally point:

1. **Wait at designated spot** (be specific—"the flagpole," not "the park")
2. **Wait predetermined time** (30 minutes minimum recommended)
3. **Leave a note if leaving** (hidden but findable by family)
4. **Move to next rally point** if no contact
5. **Contact OAC** with your status and location

## Evacuation Timing

### When to Leave Early

| Warning Level | Action |
|---------------|--------|
| Watch issued | Review plans, prepare to leave |
| Warning issued | Final preparations, consider leaving |
| Voluntary evacuation | Leave if conditions allow |
| Mandatory evacuation | Leave immediately |

### Go Early Advantages

- Less traffic
- Better route options
- Gas stations still have fuel
- Hotels still have rooms
- Less stress, better decisions

### Signs You've Waited Too Long

- Gridlock traffic on main routes
- Gas stations closed or empty
- Hotels fully booked
- Emergency services overwhelmed
- Weather/conditions deteriorating

## Special Situations

### Evacuation with Pets

- Have carriers ready
- Know pet-friendly hotels on route
- Bring vaccination records
- Consider boarding facilities en route
- Never leave pets behind

### Evacuation with Special Needs

- Allow extra time
- Ensure medical equipment is portable
- Have backup power for devices
- Know accessible routes and facilities
- Register with local emergency services

### Work/School Evacuation

- Know workplace evacuation procedures
- Know children's school procedures
- Pre-authorize emergency pickup people
- Have backup transportation plans
