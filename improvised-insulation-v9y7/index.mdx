# Improvised Insulation Techniques

> **Trap the Heat You Have** - When heating is limited or unavailable, insulation becomes critical. These techniques help you retain body heat and maintain livable temperatures with minimal resources.

## Understanding Heat Loss

### How Heat Escapes

| Method | Description | Solution |
|--------|-------------|----------|
| **Conduction** | Heat transfers through materials | Insulation layers |
| **Convection** | Heat carried by air movement | Reduce drafts |
| **Radiation** | Heat radiates from warm surfaces | Reflective barriers |

### Where Heat Escapes Fastest

1. **Windows** - Largest heat loss area
2. **Doors** - Gaps and thin materials
3. **Walls** - If poorly insulated
4. **Floors** - Especially over crawl spaces
5. **Ceilings** - Heat rises and escapes

## Window Insulation

### Blanket/Quilt Method

1. Hang thick blankets over windows
2. Seal edges with tape or pins
3. Create air gap between blanket and glass
4. Multiple layers work better

### Bubble Wrap Insulation

1. Cut bubble wrap to window size
2. Spray water on glass
3. Press bubble side against glass
4. Stays by surface tension
5. Adds R-1 to R-2 insulation
6. Still allows some light

### Cardboard Insulation

1. Cut cardboard to fit window frame
2. Multiple layers if possible
3. Tape edges to seal
4. Blocks all light but adds insulation

### Plastic Sheeting

1. Cut plastic larger than window
2. Tape edges to frame
3. Create sealed air pocket
4. Use hairdryer to shrink tight (if power available)

## Door Insulation

### Draft Blockers

**Bottom of door:**
- Rolled towel
- Pool noodle
- Rolled blanket
- Draft snake (tube filled with sand/rice)

**Around frame:**
- Weatherstripping
- Foam tape
- Rope caulk
- Paper strips wadded into gaps

### Hanging Insulation

- Hang blanket over door frame
- Overlap with wall to seal gaps
- Use curtain rod or nails
- Creates air barrier

---

## Floor Insulation

### Layering on Floors

- Rugs and carpets
- Blankets
- Cardboard
- Newspaper (multiple layers)
- Yoga mats
- Foam camping pads

### Raised Sleeping

Cold floors steal body heat:
- Sleep on raised surface
- Use thick padding
- Air mattress
- Cot or bed frame
- Multiple blanket layers beneath

## Creating a Warm Room

### Choosing the Room

**Ideal characteristics:**
- Small size (easier to heat)
- Fewest windows
- Interior location (fewer outside walls)
- Access to bathroom nearby
- Can be sealed

### Sealing the Room

1. Close all doors to rest of house
2. Seal door gaps with towels
3. Cover windows with blankets
4. Hang blankets over unused doors
5. Block any vents or chimneys
6. Close fireplace damper (if no fire)

### Heat Sources in Warm Room

- Body heat (multiple people)
- Safe heater (if available)
- Candles (minimal heat, fire risk)
- Hot water bottles
- Terracotta pot heaters (limited)

## Personal Insulation

### Layering Principles

| Layer | Purpose | Materials |
|-------|---------|-----------|
| **Base** | Moisture management | Synthetic, wool (not cotton) |
| **Mid** | Insulation | Fleece, wool, down |
| **Outer** | Wind/water protection | Windbreaker, shell |

### Body Heat Priority Areas

Focus on keeping warm:
1. **Core** (torso)
2. **Head** (25% heat loss)
3. **Neck**
4. **Hands**
5. **Feet**

### Improvised Clothing Insulation

**Newspaper:**
- Crumple and stuff into layers
- Between shirt and jacket
- Into shoes for feet
- Historical hobo technique

**Plastic bags:**
- Vapor barrier against wet
- Over socks, under outer layer
- Creates warmth (but can cause sweat)

**Cardboard:**
- Inside shoes for insulation
- Under body when sitting/sleeping

## Sleeping Warm

### Bed Insulation

**Under you:**
- Foam pad
- Multiple blankets
- Yoga mats
- Cardboard
- Newspapers

**Over you:**
- Multiple blankets (trap air)
- Sleeping bag
- Emergency blankets (over regular blankets)
- Wear hat and socks

### Emergency Sleeping

**Sleeping bag warmth tricks:**
- Wear layers inside
- Hat and socks
- Hot water bottle at feet
- Don't sleep in wet clothes
- Two people share bag

**No sleeping bag:**
- Multiple blanket layers
- Create cocoon
- Emergency blanket as outer layer
- Cardboard under, newspaper stuffing

## Improvised Insulation Materials

### Good Insulators (by R-value/inch)

| Material | Effectiveness | Notes |
|----------|--------------|-------|
| Down/feathers | Excellent | If kept dry |
| Wool | Very good | Works when wet |
| Fleece | Good | Common, accessible |
| Newspaper | Fair | Free, abundant |
| Cardboard | Fair | Easy to obtain |
| Straw/hay | Good | If available |
| Leaves (dry) | Fair | Natural, free |
| Bubble wrap | Fair | Good for windows |
| Foam mats | Good | Easy to use |

### Poor Insulators (Avoid)

- Wet materials
- Cotton (loses insulation when wet)
- Compressed materials (need air pockets)
- Single thin layers

---

## Emergency Shelter Insulation

### Tent Insulation

- Ground pad (critical)
- Interior tent walls (blankets)
- Snow piled outside (insulates)
- Avoid cotton materials

### Vehicle Overnight

- Park in sheltered location
- Crack windows slightly (moisture)
- Insulate windows with blankets
- Use sleeping bag or many blankets
- Pad floor
- Run engine periodically (with ventilation) - last resort
