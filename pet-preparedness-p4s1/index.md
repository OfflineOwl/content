# Pet Preparedness & Care

> **They Depend on You** - Your pets rely on you completely during emergencies. Proper planning ensures they survive and stay safe alongside your family.

## Pet Emergency Kit

### Essential Supplies

**Food and Water:**
- [ ] 7-day supply of pet food (minimum)
- [ ] Collapsible bowls
- [ ] Water (1 gallon per pet per day)
- [ ] Manual can opener (if canned food)

**Medical:**
- [ ] Medications (2-week supply)
- [ ] Vaccination records (copies)
- [ ] First aid supplies
- [ ] Flea/tick prevention

**Identification:**
- [ ] Current collar with ID tags
- [ ] Microchip (registered with current info)
- [ ] Recent photos (for identification)
- [ ] Proof of ownership documents

**Containment:**
- [ ] Leash and harness
- [ ] Carrier/crate (familiar to pet)
- [ ] Extra collar

**Comfort:**
- [ ] Familiar toy or blanket
- [ ] Treats
- [ ] Waste bags/litter

## Evacuation Planning

### Before Emergency

- Know which shelters accept pets
- Identify pet-friendly hotels on evacuation routes
- Have boarding facility contacts
- Arrange with friends/family as backup

### During Evacuation

**Never leave pets behind:**
- If it's not safe for you, it's not safe for them
- Evacuate early with pets
- Use carriers for transport safety
- Keep cats/small animals contained

### If You Cannot Take Pets

**Last resort only:**
- Notify rescue organizations
- Leave them in secure, interior room
- Leave plenty of food and water
- Post pet information visibly for rescuers
- Contact organizations for rescue coordination

## Shelter-in-Place with Pets

### Safety Considerations

- Bring pets inside immediately
- Identify safe room for pets
- Keep pets contained and calm
- Maintain familiar routines when possible

### Stress Reduction

Pets sense stress. Help them by:
- Maintaining regular feeding times
- Providing comfort items
- Speaking calmly
- Physical comfort (if they seek it)
- Keeping them contained safely

## Pet First Aid Basics

### Emergency Signs

**Seek veterinary care for:**
- Difficulty breathing
- Severe bleeding
- Obvious broken bones
- Unconsciousness
- Seizures
- Suspected poisoning
- Severe burns

### Basic First Aid

**Bleeding:**
- Apply pressure with clean cloth
- Keep pet calm
- Seek veterinary care

**Heat stroke:**
- Move to shade/cool area
- Apply cool (not cold) water
- Offer small amounts of water
- Veterinary care needed

### Pet First Aid Kit

- Gauze and bandages
- Medical tape
- Hydrogen peroxide (induce vomiting ONLY if directed by poison control)
- Saline solution
- Tweezers
- Muzzle (injured pets may bite)
- Pet thermometer

## Special Considerations

### Dogs

- Practice crate training before emergency
- Have secure leash and collar
- Consider stress-relief aids
- Know their triggers

### Cats

- Cat carrier essential (familiar)
- Portable litter box
- Calming pheromone spray
- Keep contained (cats bolt when stressed)

### Small Animals

- Appropriate enclosure
- Bedding
- Food supply
- Keep away from drafts/temperature extremes

### Birds

- Secure cage
- Cage cover (reduces stress)
- Familiar perches
- Protect from fumes/smoke
