Off-Grid Lighting Options

Light Drives Away Fear - When the power goes out, darkness becomes an immediate concern. Having reliable light sources improves safety, morale, and functionality.

Lighting Priorities

  1. Safety lighting - Prevent injuries, navigate safely
  2. Task lighting - Reading, cooking, first aid
  3. Area lighting - General illumination
  4. Signaling - Emergency communication

Battery-Powered Options

Flashlights

Type Pros Cons Best For
LED handheld Efficient, durable, bright Directional only General use, every person
Headlamp Hands-free Limited area Tasks requiring both hands
Lantern-style 360° light Bulkier Area lighting
Tactical Very bright, rugged Battery drain, expensive High-output needs

Recommended: At least one flashlight AND one headlamp per person.

Lanterns

Battery lanterns:

  • LED camping lanterns
  • 360° illumination
  • Can last 100+ hours on low setting
  • Some have USB charging ports

Features to look for:

  • Multiple brightness settings
  • Long battery life
  • Water resistance
  • Hanging hook
  • USB charging output

Rechargeable Options

Solar-Powered Lights

Type Charge Time Runtime Best For
Garden solar lights All day sun 8-12 hours Pathway marking
Solar lanterns 6-8 hours 6-12 hours Area lighting
Solar flashlights 6-8 hours 2-4 hours Backup
Pros: Free energy, indefinitely renewable
Cons: Weather dependent, slower charging

Hand-Crank Lights

  • No batteries or sun needed
  • 1 minute cranking = 10-30 minutes light
  • Good backup option
  • Often include radio, USB charging

Rechargeable Batteries

Eneloop/NiMH rechargeable batteries:

  • Can recharge with solar panel
  • Last hundreds of cycles
  • Standard AA/AAA sizes
  • Good charge retention

Fuel-Based Options

Candles

Pros:

  • Inexpensive, widely available
  • Long-lasting
  • Provides some warmth
  • Psychological comfort

Cons:

  • Fire hazard
  • Limited brightness
  • Soot production
  • Not windproof

Safe candle use:

  • Stable, heat-resistant holder
  • Never leave unattended
  • Keep away from flammables
  • Keep away from children/pets
  • Don't move while lit

Emergency candles:

  • 100-hour emergency candles
  • Beeswax (cleaner burning)
  • Tea lights (many, varied use)

Oil Lamps

Kerosene/lamp oil:

  • Brighter than candles
  • Adjustable flame
  • Economical fuel use
  • Traditional, reliable

Safety:

  • Use only approved fuels
  • Adequate ventilation
  • Stable placement
  • Keep fuel stored safely

Propane/Butane Lanterns

Pros:

  • Very bright
  • Adjustable output
  • Common fuel

Cons:

  • CO risk (ventilation required)
  • Fire hazard
  • Fuel storage needed
  • Not truly indoor-safe

Chemical Light Sources

Glow Sticks/Cyalume

Pros:

  • No heat, no fire risk
  • Waterproof
  • Instant activation
  • Safe around children

Cons:

  • Single use
  • Limited brightness
  • 4-24 hour duration
  • Can't turn off

Best uses:

  • Marking paths/rooms
  • Keeping track of children
  • Night safety
  • Signaling

Improvised Lighting

Improvised Candles

Crisco/shortening candle:

  1. Fill jar with shortening
  2. Insert string or cotton wick
  3. Light wick
  4. Burns for many hours

Butter lamp:

  1. Butter in small container
  2. Cotton or string wick
  3. Less bright, shorter duration

Improvised Oil Lamp

  1. Any oil (vegetable, olive)
  2. Small container
  3. Cotton wick or shoelace
  4. Float wick or suspend

Strategic Lighting Placement

Key Areas to Light

  • Entry/exit points - Safety navigation
  • Stairs - Prevent falls
  • Kitchen - Food preparation
  • Bathroom - Sanitation
  • Central gathering area - Family hub

Light Conservation

  • Use lowest effective brightness
  • Group activities in one lit area
  • Use task lighting (headlamps) vs. area lighting
  • Natural light during day
  • Reflective surfaces increase effectiveness

Battery Management

Have Multiple Types

Standardize on common sizes:

  • AA (most common)
  • AAA (many devices)
  • D cells (lanterns)
  • 18650 (rechargeable, many modern lights)

Storage

  • Cool, dry location
  • Original packaging
  • Rotate stock
  • Check expiration dates
  • Remove from devices for long storage

Lighting Kit Checklist

Essential

  • LED flashlight (per person)
  • LED headlamp (per person)
  • LED lantern (1-2 per household)
  • Extra batteries (100+)
  • Candles (24+ hours worth)
  • Matches/lighters (multiple)

Enhanced

  • Solar-charged lantern
  • Hand-crank flashlight
  • Oil lamp with fuel
  • Rechargeable batteries + charger
  • Solar panel for charging
  • Glow sticks (for kids/marking)

Quick Reference

LIGHTING PRIORITIES

1. Every person: Flashlight + headlamp
2. Every room: Candles or glow sticks
3. Main area: Lantern
4. Backup: Hand-crank, solar

BATTERY RULE:
Have 10x more batteries than you think you need

CANDLE SAFETY:
- Stable holder
- Never unattended
- Away from flammables
- Away from kids/pets
