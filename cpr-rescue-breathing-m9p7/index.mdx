# CPR & Rescue Breathing (No Equipment)

> **The Most Important Skill** — When someone's heart stops, brain damage begins within 4–6 minutes. CPR keeps blood flowing until the heart can restart. You can do this with nothing but your hands.

## When CPR is Needed

Start CPR when someone is:
- **Unresponsive** — doesn't respond to voice or touch
- **Not breathing** — or only gasping (agonal breathing is NOT normal breathing)
- **Has no pulse** — if you're trained to check; don't spend more than 10 seconds

> **When in doubt, start CPR.** It won't harm someone who doesn't need it, but delay can kill someone who does.

---

## Adult CPR (Age 12+)

### The Sequence: C–A–B

**Compressions first, then Airway, then Breathing.** The heart needs compressions immediately — don't waste time checking the airway first.

### Step 1 — Scene Safety & Response Check

| Action | Detail |
|--------|--------|
| Scene safe? | Check for hazards before approaching |
| Tap shoulders | Firmly, both sides — "Are you okay?" |
| No response? | Shout for help or send someone to call |
| Check breathing | Look at chest, listen, feel — **no more than 10 seconds** |

### Step 2 — Position the Patient

1. Place on their **back** on a **firm, flat surface** (floor, ground — not a bed)
2. Kneel beside them at **chest level**
3. Remove or open clothing over the chest

### Step 3 — Chest Compressions

**Hand position:**

| Step | Placement |
|------|-----------|
| 1 | Heel of one hand on **center of chest** (lower half of breastbone) |
| 2 | Heel of other hand **on top** |
| 3 | **Interlock fingers** — keep them off the ribs |
| 4 | Shoulders **directly over** hands, arms straight, elbows locked |

**Compression technique:**

| Parameter | Target |
|-----------|--------|
| Depth | At least **2 inches** (5 cm), no more than 2.4 inches |
| Rate | **100–120 per minute** |
| Recoil | Allow **full chest recoil** between every compression |
| Interruptions | **Minimize** — no more than 10 seconds for any pause |

> **Rhythm guide:** Push to the beat of "Stayin' Alive" by the Bee Gees (100 BPM) or "Another One Bites the Dust" by Queen (110 BPM). Count out loud: "1 and 2 and 3 and 4..."

### Step 4 — Open the Airway

**Head-tilt, chin-lift:**
1. One hand on the **forehead** — push down gently
2. Two fingers under the **chin bone** (not the soft tissue under the jaw)
3. **Tilt head back, lift chin up** — this pulls the tongue off the airway

> **Suspected spinal injury?** Use the **jaw-thrust maneuver** instead — push the jaw forward from behind the angles without tilting the neck. If you can't open the airway this way, use head-tilt chin-lift anyway — an open airway is more critical than a potential spinal injury.

### Step 5 — Rescue Breaths

| Step | Action |
|------|--------|
| 1 | Maintain head-tilt, chin-lift |
| 2 | **Pinch the nose** shut |
| 3 | Create a complete **seal over the mouth** |
| 4 | Blow for **1 second** — normal breath, not a huge one |
| 5 | Watch for **chest rise** — that's your confirmation |
| 6 | Let air exit, then give **second breath** |

**If the chest doesn't rise:**
- Re-tilt the head (more extension)
- Check for visible obstructions in the mouth
- Try again — but **don't delay compressions** for more than 2 failed attempts

### Step 6 — Continue CPR

**Ratio: 30 compressions : 2 breaths**

1. Give **30 compressions** (~18 seconds)
2. Give **2 rescue breaths** (~4 seconds)
3. Immediately resume compressions — **don't pause**
4. Continue until:
   - Person shows signs of life (breathing, coughing, movement)
   - An AED arrives and is ready to analyze
   - Professional help takes over
   - You're physically unable to continue
   - The scene becomes unsafe

> **Fatigue warning:** Compression quality drops significantly after 2 minutes, even if you don't feel tired. Switch rescuers every 2 minutes if possible. Switch quickly — less than 10 seconds.

## Hands-Only CPR

**When to use:** If you're untrained, unwilling to give breaths, or there's no barrier device available.

| Step | Action |
|------|--------|
| 1 | **Call for help** |
| 2 | **Push hard and fast** in the center of the chest |
| 3 | **Don't stop** until help arrives or someone takes over |

> **Hands-only CPR is remarkably effective** for adults with sudden cardiac arrest. The blood still has oxygen in it immediately after the heart stops. Compressions alone keep that oxygenated blood moving to the brain. **Any CPR is far better than no CPR.**

## Child CPR (Ages 1–12)

### How It Differs from Adult

| Factor | Adult | Child |
|--------|-------|-------|
| Compression depth | At least 2 inches | **2 inches** (about 1/3 of chest depth) |
| Hands | Always two | **One or two** (use what works for the child's size) |
| Rate | 100–120/min | 100–120/min (same) |
| Ratio (1 rescuer) | 30:2 | 30:2 (same) |
| Ratio (2 rescuers) | 30:2 | **15:2** |
| If alone | Call first, then CPR | **2 minutes of CPR first**, then call |

### Child CPR Sequence

1. **Check responsiveness** — tap and shout, check breathing (10 seconds max)
2. **If alone: do 2 minutes of CPR first**, then call for help (children more often arrest from breathing problems — early CPR is critical)
3. **Open airway** — head-tilt, chin-lift with **less extension** than for adults
4. **Compressions** — center of chest, one or two hands depending on child's size
5. **Rescue breaths** — gentler than adult, just enough to see chest rise
6. **Continue 30:2** — switch rescuers every 2 minutes if available

## Infant CPR (Under Age 1)

### Key Differences

| Factor | Infant technique |
|--------|-----------------|
| Check responsiveness | **Flick the sole of the foot**, call their name |
| Compression location | **Just below the nipple line** on the breastbone |
| Compression method | **Two fingers** (1 rescuer) or **two thumbs** (2 rescuers) |
| Compression depth | **1.5 inches** (about 1/3 of chest depth) |
| Breath size | **Small puffs from your cheeks** — not a full breath |
| Head position | **Neutral** — slight tilt only, don't extend the neck |
| Mouth seal | Cover **both mouth AND nose** |
| Ratio | 30:2 (1 rescuer), 15:2 (2 rescuers) |

### Infant Compressions

**Two-finger technique (one rescuer):**
1. Place **two fingers** on the breastbone, just below the nipple line
2. Push down about **1.5 inches**
3. Allow **full recoil** between each compression
4. Rate: **100–120/min**

**Two-thumb technique (two rescuers — more effective):**
1. Wrap both hands around the infant's chest
2. Place **thumbs side-by-side** on the lower breastbone
3. **Squeeze** the chest with your thumbs
4. This generates better pressure and blood flow

### Infant Rescue Breaths

1. Keep head in **neutral position** (very slight tilt only)
2. Cover **both mouth AND nose** with your mouth
3. Give a **gentle puff from your cheeks** — not a deep breath from your lungs
4. Watch for **chest rise** — that confirms air is going in
5. If the chest doesn't rise: **reposition** the head slightly and try again

> **Infants are not small adults.** Their airways are tiny and easily obstructed by over-extension of the neck. Keep the head neutral. Use puffs, not breaths. Be gentle but deliberate.

## Choking & CPR

### Adult/Child: Unresponsive Choking

If a choking person loses consciousness:

1. **Lower them to the ground** carefully, supporting the head
2. **Begin CPR** — start with chest compressions (compressions may expel the object)
3. **Before each set of breaths**, open the mouth and **look** for the object
4. **Remove only what you can see** — never blindly sweep the mouth
5. Attempt breaths, then continue compressions
6. Repeat until the object clears or help arrives

### Infant Choking

**Conscious infant who can't cry, cough, or breathe:**

| Step | Action |
|------|--------|
| 1 | Support the head, place **face-down on your forearm** (head lower than chest) |
| 2 | Give **5 firm back blows** between the shoulder blades with the heel of your hand |
| 3 | **Turn over** — support the head and back |
| 4 | Give **5 chest thrusts** (same location and depth as CPR compressions) |
| 5 | **Check the mouth** — remove visible object |
| 6 | Repeat back blows and chest thrusts until the object clears |

**If the infant becomes unresponsive:** Begin infant CPR immediately. Check the mouth before each set of breaths.

## Special Situations

| Situation | Key modifications |
|-----------|------------------|
| **Drowning** | Prioritize rescue breaths — give 5 initial breaths before starting compressions. Expect vomiting: turn to the side, clear the mouth, continue. |
| **Hypothermia** | Handle gently (the cold heart is electrically unstable). Check pulse for up to 60 seconds. Continue CPR until rewarming is possible. **"Not dead until warm and dead."** |
| **Pregnancy** | Place a wedge (rolled towel, bag) under the right hip for a left tilt. Standard CPR otherwise. Hand position may be slightly higher on the sternum. Two lives at stake — don't delay. |
| **Trauma** | Use jaw thrust if spinal injury is suspected. Control severe bleeding first (it may be the cause of arrest). Survival rates are lower but still attempt CPR. |
| **Electric shock** | Ensure the power source is off or the person is clear before touching them. Standard CPR once safe. |
| **Opioid overdose** | If naloxone (Narcan) is available, administer it. Give rescue breaths — breathing failure is the primary problem. Begin CPR if no pulse. |

## Rescuer Care

### Switching Rescuers

- CPR quality **drops significantly after 2 minutes** — even if you don't feel tired
- Switch during the transition from compressions to breaths
- The incoming rescuer should be positioned and ready before the switch
- **Complete the switch in under 10 seconds**

### Protecting Yourself

| Barrier | Use |
|---------|-----|
| CPR face shield | Thin plastic sheet with one-way valve — place over the face |
| Pocket mask | Rigid mask with valve — best protection and seal |
| Plastic wrap | Emergency substitute — poke a hole, place over mouth |
| Nothing available | **Hands-only CPR is acceptable** — the risk of disease transmission from rescue breathing is very low |

---

## When to Stop CPR

**Stop when:**
- Person shows signs of life (breathing, coughing, movement, eye opening)
- An AED advises you to stop
- Professional rescuers take over
- You are physically unable to continue and no one can switch in
- The scene becomes unsafe

> **Do NOT stop because** you're tired (find a switch), it's been a long time (continue until help arrives), or you think it's hopeless (let professionals make that call). People have survived after 30+ minutes of bystander CPR.

## CPR Quality Checklist

- [ ] Patient on a **firm, flat surface** (not a bed or couch)
- [ ] Correct **hand position** — center of chest
- [ ] Compression **depth** adequate (2+ inches adult, 2 inches child, 1.5 inches infant)
- [ ] Compression **rate** correct (100–120/min)
- [ ] **Full chest recoil** between every compression
- [ ] **Minimal interruptions** — less than 10 seconds for any pause
- [ ] Visible **chest rise** with each rescue breath
- [ ] **Switching** rescuers every 2 minutes
- [ ] **Counting out loud** — helps maintain rhythm and track cycles

## Quick Reference

|---|---|---|---|
| **Check** | Tap shoulders, shout | Tap shoulders, shout | Flick sole of foot |
| **Compression spot** | Center of chest | Center of chest | Just below nipple line |
| **Hands** | 2 hands | 1 or 2 hands | 2 fingers (or 2 thumbs) |
| **Depth** | 2+ inches | 2 inches | 1.5 inches |
| **Rate** | 100–120/min | 100–120/min | 100–120/min |
| **Ratio** | 30:2 | 30:2 (or 15:2) | 30:2 (or 15:2) |
| **Breaths** | Pinch nose, mouth seal | Pinch nose, mouth seal | Cover mouth + nose |

## Training

> **This guide is not a substitute for hands-on training.** Reading instructions is a start, but muscle memory from practice is what makes CPR effective under stress. Take a course, practice on mannequins, and refresh annually.

**Where to train:**
- American Heart Association (heartsaver courses)
- American Red Cross
- Community CPR classes (often free)
- Employer-offered training
- Online + in-person hybrid courses
