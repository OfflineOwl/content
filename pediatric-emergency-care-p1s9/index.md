# Pediatric Emergency Care

> **Children Are Not Small Adults** - Children have unique physiological needs and respond differently to illness and injury. This guide covers essential pediatric emergency care for when professional help is unavailable.

## Key Differences in Children

### Why Children Are Different

| Factor | Children | Adults |
|--------|----------|--------|
| Airway | Smaller, more easily blocked | Larger, more stable |
| Breathing | Higher rate normal | Lower rate |
| Heart rate | Higher normal rate | Lower rate |
| Body surface area | Larger relative to weight | Smaller relative to weight |
| Dehydration | Happens faster | More reserve |
| Heat loss | Faster | Slower |
| Communication | Limited | Can describe symptoms |

### Normal Vital Signs by Age

| Age | Heart Rate | Respiratory Rate | Blood Pressure |
|-----|-----------|-----------------|----------------|
| Infant (0-1) | 100-160 | 30-60 | 70-90 / 50-65 |
| Toddler (1-3) | 90-150 | 24-40 | 80-100 / 55-70 |
| Preschool (3-6) | 80-140 | 22-34 | 85-110 / 55-75 |
| School (6-12) | 70-120 | 18-30 | 95-115 / 60-80 |
| Adolescent (12+) | 60-100 | 12-20 | 100-130 / 65-85 |

## Assessment of a Sick or Injured Child

### Pediatric Assessment Triangle (PAT)

Observe from a distance before touching:

**1. Appearance**
- Alert, interacting? Or listless, unresponsive?
- Recognizing parents?
- Crying normally or weak/abnormal cry?
- Moving normally?

**2. Work of Breathing**
- Breathing fast or slow?
- Using extra muscles (ribs showing, belly moving)?
- Making unusual sounds (grunting, wheezing)?
- Nose flaring?

**3. Circulation**
- Skin color (pink, pale, blue, mottled)?
- Capillary refill (press finger, color return < 2 seconds?)

**If any component looks bad → child is seriously ill**

### Approaching an Ill Child

- Get down to their level
- Speak calmly and quietly
- Let them stay with parent/caregiver
- Examine least threatening areas first
- Explain what you're doing
- Allow them to hold comfort items

## Fever in Children

### When Fever is Concerning

| Age | Temperature | Action |
|-----|------------|--------|
| Under 3 months | 100.4°F (38°C) | Seek medical care |
| 3-6 months | 102°F (38.9°C)+ | Seek medical care |
| 6+ months | 104°F (40°C)+ | Seek medical care |
| Any age | Fever + lethargy, rash, stiff neck | Emergency |

### Managing Fever

**Treatment:**
- Acetaminophen (Tylenol) - follow dosing chart
- Ibuprofen (Motrin) - 6 months and older
- Light clothing
- Fluids, fluids, fluids
- Lukewarm bath (not cold)

**Do NOT:**
- Give aspirin to children (Reye's syndrome risk)
- Bundle up fevering child
- Use cold water or alcohol baths
- Alternate medications without medical guidance

### Dosing Guidance (Acetaminophen)

| Weight | Age | Dose |
|--------|-----|------|
| 6-11 lbs | 0-3 mo | 40 mg |
| 12-17 lbs | 4-11 mo | 80 mg |
| 18-23 lbs | 12-23 mo | 120 mg |
| 24-35 lbs | 2-3 yr | 160 mg |
| 36-47 lbs | 4-5 yr | 240 mg |
| 48-59 lbs | 6-8 yr | 320 mg |
| 60-71 lbs | 9-10 yr | 400 mg |
| 72-95 lbs | 11 yr | 480 mg |

*Every 4-6 hours, max 5 doses in 24 hours*

## Dehydration

### Signs of Dehydration in Children

| Mild | Moderate | Severe |
|------|----------|--------|
| Slightly dry mouth | Very dry mouth | Extremely dry mouth |
| Slightly decreased urine | Significantly decreased urine | No urine 6-8 hours |
| Normal activity | Irritable or tired | Lethargic, difficult to wake |
| Normal eyes | Slightly sunken eyes | Very sunken eyes |
| Normal skin | Skin slow to return when pinched | Skin stays tented |
| Tears present | Few or no tears | No tears |

### Treating Dehydration

**Oral Rehydration:**

Best options:
- Pedialyte or similar oral rehydration solution
- Homemade solution: 1 liter water + 6 tsp sugar + 1/2 tsp salt
- Diluted juice (half water, half juice)
- Broth

**Rehydration schedule:**
- Small, frequent sips (1-2 tbsp every 5 minutes)
- Don't give large amounts at once (causes vomiting)
- Gradually increase as tolerated
- Popsicles work well for toddlers

**Seek medical care if:**
- Cannot keep any fluids down
- Signs of moderate-severe dehydration
- Bloody diarrhea
- High fever with dehydration
- Decreasing responsiveness

## Respiratory Emergencies

### Choking (Infant Under 1 Year)

**Conscious infant:**

1. Hold infant face down on forearm, head lower than body
2. Support head and jaw with hand
3. Give 5 back blows between shoulder blades
4. Turn infant face up, support head
5. Give 5 chest thrusts (two fingers on breastbone)
6. Repeat until object expelled or infant loses consciousness

**Unconscious infant:**
1. Place on firm surface
2. Open mouth, look for visible object (remove only if clearly visible)
3. Begin infant CPR
4. Check mouth before each breath

### Choking (Child 1+ Years)

**Conscious child:**
- Stand or kneel behind child
- Wrap arms around waist
- Make fist, place above navel
- Grasp with other hand
- Quick upward thrusts
- Repeat until object expelled

**Unconscious child:**
- Begin CPR
- Check mouth for object before breaths

### Croup

**Symptoms:**
- Barking cough (like a seal)
- Hoarse voice
- Stridor (high-pitched breathing sound)
- Usually worse at night
- Often follows cold symptoms

**Home treatment:**
1. Stay calm (anxiety worsens symptoms)
2. Cool mist humidifier
3. Steam from hot shower
4. Cool night air (bundle up and go outside)
5. Fluids
6. Keep head elevated

**Seek emergency care if:**
- Blue lips or fingernails
- Severe difficulty breathing
- Drooling or can't swallow
- Very high fever
- Child unresponsive
- Stridor at rest that doesn't improve

### Asthma Attack

**Symptoms:**
- Wheezing
- Coughing
- Shortness of breath
- Chest tightness

**Treatment:**
1. Stay calm
2. Sit child upright
3. Give rescue inhaler (2-4 puffs with spacer)
4. Wait 1 minute between puffs
5. Repeat in 20 minutes if needed

**Emergency signs:**
- Rescue inhaler not helping
- Can't speak in sentences
- Ribs showing with each breath
- Lips turning blue
- Getting worse despite treatment

## Common Pediatric Injuries

### Head Injuries

**When to worry:**
- Loss of consciousness (any duration)
- Vomiting more than twice
- Increasingly confused
- Severe headache
- Clear fluid from nose/ears
- Seizure
- Unequal pupils
- Increasing sleepiness
- Weakness on one side

**After minor head bump:**
- Ice pack for swelling (15 minutes)
- Monitor closely for 24 hours
- Wake every 2-3 hours at night to check alertness
- No sports or rough play for 24-48 hours

### Burns (See Burns guide for details)

**Special considerations for children:**
- Burns are more severe at same temperature (thinner skin)
- Smaller body = bigger percentage of body affected
- Higher risk of dehydration from burns
- Burns on face, hands, feet, genitals always need care

### Poisoning

**If you suspect poisoning:**
1. Remove child from source
2. Call Poison Control: 1-800-222-1222
3. Have container/label ready for information
4. Do NOT induce vomiting unless told to
5. Do NOT give milk or water unless instructed

**Information needed:**
- Child's age and weight
- What was ingested
- How much (estimate)
- When it happened
- Current symptoms

## Pediatric CPR

### Infant CPR (Under 1 Year)

**Compressions:**
- Two fingers on breastbone, just below nipple line
- Or two thumbs (two-rescuer technique)
- Depth: 1.5 inches (1/3 chest depth)
- Rate: 100-120 per minute

**Breaths:**
- Cover mouth AND nose
- Small puffs (cheek breath, not lung breath)
- Just enough to see chest rise

**Ratio:** 30:2 (one rescuer) or 15:2 (two rescuers)

### Child CPR (1-12 Years)

**Compressions:**
- One or two hands on lower half of breastbone
- Depth: 2 inches (1/3 chest depth)
- Rate: 100-120 per minute

**Breaths:**
- Pinch nose, seal mouth
- Give over 1 second
- Watch for chest rise

**Ratio:** 30:2 (one rescuer) or 15:2 (two rescuers)

## Comfort and Communication

### Talking to Scared Children

**Do:**
- Get down to their eye level
- Use simple, honest words
- Let them keep comfort items
- Explain what you're doing
- Give them small choices when possible
- Praise their bravery
- Stay calm yourself

**Avoid:**
- "This won't hurt" (if it will)
- Medical jargon
- Showing your fear
- Separating from parents unnecessarily
- Forcing cooperation

### Distraction Techniques

- Counting
- Singing
- Story telling
- Looking at pictures
- Bubbles
- Deep breathing games
- Flashlight on ceiling
- Music

## When to Seek Emergency Care

**Always seek emergency care for:**
- Difficulty breathing
- Blue lips or fingernails
- Unresponsive or hard to wake
- Seizure (first time, or lasting >5 minutes)
- Severe allergic reaction
- Severe dehydration
- Fever in infant under 3 months
- Signs of meningitis (fever, stiff neck, sensitivity to light)
- Significant head injury
- Severe bleeding
- Suspected fracture
- Ingestion of poison or unknown substance
- Burn on face, hands, feet, genitals, or large area

**Trust your instincts:**
If something seems seriously wrong, seek help.
Parents often know when their child is sicker than usual.
