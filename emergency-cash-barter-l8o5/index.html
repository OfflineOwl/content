Emergency Cash & Barter Basics

When Cards Don't Work - During emergencies, electronic payment systems may fail. Cash and barter become essential for obtaining goods and services.

Why Cash Matters in Emergencies

Systems That Can Fail

  • Credit/debit card networks
  • ATMs (no power, no network)
  • Bank systems
  • Internet-based payments
  • Point-of-sale systems

When Cash is Critical

  • Power outages
  • Natural disasters
  • Bank holidays/closures
  • Network outages
  • Civil unrest
  • Economic disruptions

Emergency Cash Guidelines

How Much to Keep

DurationRecommended
72-hour kit$200-300
2-week supply$500-1000
Extended prep$1000+

Denomination Strategy

Keep variety of bills:

  • $100s (high-value, compact)
  • $50s (moderate purchases)
  • $20s (most useful)
  • $10s (smaller transactions)
  • $5s (making change)
  • $1s (small purchases)
  • Quarters (vending, laundry)

Recommended mix:

  • 40% in $20s
  • 30% in smaller bills ($10, $5, $1)
  • 30% in larger bills ($50, $100)
  • $10-20 in quarters

Storage

Requirements:

  • Waterproof container
  • Fireproof if possible
  • Accessible but secure
  • Not all in one location
  • Some in go-bag

Locations:

  • Home safe
  • Hidden at home
  • Go-bag
  • Vehicle (secured)
  • With trusted family member

Barter Tips

When bartering:

  • Know item's value
  • Don't reveal full inventory
  • Start high in negotiations
  • Have multiple items to trade
  • Build relationships first
  • Fair dealing builds trust

Safety:

  • Meet in neutral locations
  • Bring backup if possible
  • Don't reveal where you live
  • Don't show all you have
  • Trust your instincts

Protecting Your Resources

Operational Security

  • Don't advertise what you have
  • Vary where you get supplies
  • Don't make yourself a target
  • Share wisely
  • Maintain discretion

Documentation

  • Know what you have
  • Keep records hidden
  • Have inventory for insurance
