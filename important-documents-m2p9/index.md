# Important Documents Offline Kit

> **Recovery runs on paperwork.** After a disaster you will be asked to prove who you are, where you lived, what you owned and how you were insured, often before the power or the internet is back. A small kit of copies, built on a quiet afternoon, answers all of it.

## Start here

1. **Photograph your wallet tonight.** Lay out your driver's license, insurance cards and bank cards and photograph the front and back of each. That is 10 minutes, and it already covers the most common losses.
2. **Pick one home for the papers.** A fire-resistant, waterproof box or safe is the standard advice from FEMA and the FDIC. Until you have one, a zip-top bag inside a folder beats a drawer.
3. **Gather the "prove it" five:** photo ID, Social Security number, proof of address (lease, deed or a utility bill), insurance policies with the claims phone numbers, and a list of your bank and card accounts.
4. **Walk the house with your phone on video.** Open closets and drawers and say what things are. This is your household inventory, and it is the hardest thing to recreate afterwards.
5. **Put copies in two more places:** one set in your go-bag, and one away from home, either in an encrypted digital copy or with a person you trust.

Then work through the checklists below at your own pace. FEMA's free Emergency Financial First Aid Kit (EFFAK) has fill-in forms for all of it.

## Why this matters

When you apply for FEMA disaster assistance, by phone or online, you are asked for a Social Security number, your address before the disaster and now, a phone number, your insurance information, your total household income, a description of what you lost, and bank routing and account numbers if you want money deposited directly. FEMA may also ask for proof that you lived in or owned the damaged home. Your insurer will want policy numbers and a list of what was lost. Your bank will want to know you are you.

None of that is hard at your kitchen table. All of it is hard from a shelter cot with a dead phone and a wallet that got wet.

## What goes in the kit

The EFFAK sorts everything into four groups. You will not need every line: skip what does not apply.

### 1. Household identification

- [ ] Driver's license or other photo ID for each adult (copy front and back)
- [ ] Birth certificates, adoption papers, child custody documents
- [ ] Marriage license, divorce decree
- [ ] Social Security cards
- [ ] Passport (copy the first two pages) or Green Card (front and back)
- [ ] Naturalization documents
- [ ] Military ID and discharge record (DD 214)
- [ ] For each child: a recent photo and any child ID card
- [ ] For each pet: ID tag and microchip numbers, and a current photo of you with the pet

### 2. Financial and legal

- [ ] Lease or rental agreement, or mortgage and deed
- [ ] A recent utility bill (it proves where you live if the lease is gone)
- [ ] Vehicle registration, title and loan papers
- [ ] Insurance policies: home or renters, flood, auto, life, pet. Write the claims phone number on each.
- [ ] For each bank, card, loan and retirement account: the institution, type of account, last four digits, and its phone number. For cards, the number to call to cancel.
- [ ] One or two recent pay stubs, and benefit award letters
- [ ] Last year's tax return
- [ ] Will or trust, and powers of attorney
- [ ] A list of bills that are paid automatically, with amounts and due dates

### 3. Medical

- [ ] Health insurance, Medicare, Medicaid and pharmacy cards (copies)
- [ ] List of medicines you take regularly, with doses, and copies of current prescriptions, glasses included
- [ ] Allergies and immunization records
- [ ] Doctors, dentist and specialists, with phone numbers
- [ ] Models, serial numbers and suppliers of medical equipment (wheelchair, oxygen, feeding pump, hearing aids)
- [ ] Living will and medical power of attorney
- [ ] Veterinarian contact, pet vaccination records and pet prescriptions

### 4. Contacts

- [ ] Family, and one out-of-town contact
- [ ] Landlord or mortgage company
- [ ] Insurance agent
- [ ] Employers and schools
- [ ] Lawyer, financial advisor, caseworker or benefits office
- [ ] Medical equipment and home care providers

Write the contacts on paper. A phone's address book is no help when the phone is dead or lost.

## Where to keep it

No single place survives everything, so the answer is three places.

| Place | What goes there | Watch out for |
|-------|-----------------|---------------|
| **Fire-resistant, waterproof box or safe at home** | Originals you may need quickly: passports, birth certificates, Social Security cards, titles, will. A little emergency cash. | It is only useful if you can reach it. Know where the key is. |
| **Go-bag** | Copies of the "prove it" five, medicine list, contacts, in a waterproof pouch | Copies only. A lost bag should not mean a lost birth certificate. |
| **Bank safe deposit box** | Hard-to-replace originals you rarely need | Banks close in disasters, and the FDIC notes that most boxes are not waterproof, so seal papers in a zip-top bag. Ask the bank who can open the box if you die or are ill. |
| **A trusted person outside your area** | A paper set in a sealed envelope, with instructions about when to open it | Pick someone a different storm would not also hit. |
| **Encrypted digital copy** | Scans or photos of everything, plus the home inventory video | Password-protect or encrypt it. See below. |

**Tell the right people where things are.** File living wills and advance directives with your doctor and hospital, not just in a box. Make sure the person who holds your power of attorney knows it and can find the document.

## Digital copies, done safely

A scan is only helpful if you can open it and a thief cannot.

- **Scan or photograph every page,** front and back, and check that each image is readable.
- **Keep one copy on a password-protected or encrypted flash drive** in the home box, and consider a second in the go-bag. FEMA and the FDIC both recommend password-protected removable storage.
- **Keep one copy off-site,** in a secure cloud storage service with a strong, unique password and two-step sign-in.
- **Do not store a list of your passwords and PINs with the kit.** FEMA says so plainly. If you must write something down, write a hint only you would understand.
- **Save a few things to the phone itself** (ID, insurance cards, medicine list) so they open with no signal.

The Cyber Preparedness & Data Backup guide walks through encrypted drives, offline password managers and the 3-2-1 backup rule step by step.

## The household inventory

Insurance pays for what you can show you owned. An inventory turns "everything in the living room" into a claim.

1. **Video each room,** including the garage, attic, closets and inside drawers. Narrate: what it is, about when you bought it.
2. **Photograph serial and model numbers** on electronics, appliances and tools.
3. **Photograph receipts or appraisals** for anything valuable: jewelry, instruments, art, bicycles.
4. **Store it with the digital copies,** not only on the phone that lives in the house.
5. **Redo it after big purchases** and at your yearly review.

## Review it, or it rots

A kit with an expired policy and an old address is a trap. FEMA suggests tying the review to a date you already notice: tax time, a clock change, your birthday or the new year.

Update it right away when you:

- [ ] Move, buy a home or sign a new lease
- [ ] Change insurance company or policy
- [ ] Open or close a bank or card account
- [ ] Marry, divorce, or have a child
- [ ] Have a death in the household
- [ ] Get a new prescription, diagnosis or piece of medical equipment

## If documents are lost or stolen

**Stolen kit or wallet:** call your banks and card issuers first, then your insurers. Under US law, if you report a lost debit or ATM card before anyone uses it, you owe nothing for later charges, and reporting within two business days limits your loss. Check your statements for the next few months.

**Replacing documents (US):**

| Document | Who replaces it |
|----------|-----------------|
| Birth, marriage, death certificates | The vital records office of the state where the event happened |
| Social Security card | Social Security Administration: 1-800-772-1213, or online |
| Driver's license or state ID | Your state motor vehicle agency |
| Passport | Report it lost to the State Department, then apply in person. Passport information: 1-877-487-2778 |
| Military discharge (DD 214) | National Archives: 1-866-272-6272 |
| Medicare card | Your online Medicare account, or 1-800-MEDICARE |
| Insurance policies, bank statements | The company. This is why the phone numbers go in the kit. |

Having a copy makes each of these faster. Outside the US, the offices differ but the idea is the same: civil registry, tax or social insurance office, licensing agency and your embassy or consulate for passports.

## People who need extra thought

- **Children:** custody or guardianship papers, a recent photo, and the school's contact and pickup rules.
- **Older adults and people with disabilities:** benefit letters, equipment serial numbers and suppliers, caregiver agency contracts, and the name of whoever holds power of attorney.
- **Immigrants and visitors:** copies of every immigration document, front and back. For citizens born outside the US, naturalization papers are key proof of citizenship, so keep copies in every location.
- **Veterans:** the DD 214 opens the door to most benefits. Keep several copies.
- **Pets:** shelters and boarding kennels may ask for vaccination records, and a photo of you with your pet helps prove it is yours.

## Practice

**10-minute drill:** Set a timer. Without warning the household, ask: "We have to leave in 10 minutes and may not come back for a month. Bring me what proves who we are and what we own." See what turns up and how long it takes. Whatever nobody could find, or found in three different rooms, is your to-do list.

**What would you do?**

1. *You keep every original document in a bank safe deposit box across town. A hurricane is two days out and you are packing to evacuate.*

   **Answer:** The box protects the originals, but it cannot help you on the road, and the bank may be closed for weeks. Before you leave, make sure copies of ID, insurance and account details are in your go-bag and on an encrypted drive or your phone. If there is still time and the bank is open, retrieve anything you may need in the next month, such as passports. Then fix the gap for next time: copies should never live in only one place.

2. *To make things easy, your partner suggests taping a sheet with all your bank logins and PINs inside the lid of the document box.*

   **Answer:** No. Anyone who gets the box would get every account. FEMA's advice is not to keep password lists with the kit at all. Record the institution, the last four digits and the phone number instead, which is enough to call and prove who you are. Keep passwords in a password manager or as hints only you understand.

3. *A week after a flood, a man in a FEMA jacket knocks and offers to speed up your application if you give him your Social Security number and a $50 processing fee.*

   **Answer:** Close the door. FEMA does not charge for applications or inspections, and a jacket is not identification. Ask for a government photo ID badge, and if in doubt call FEMA yourself at 1-800-621-3362. Report the visit to local police and to the FTC at ReportFraud.ftc.gov. See the Legal & Insurance Readiness guide for more on disaster scams.
