NUCLEAR FALLOUT PROTECTION
==========================

GET INSIDE. STAY INSIDE. STAY TUNED.

After a nuclear explosion you will usually have 10 minutes or more
before fallout arrives. Use that time to get into the best shelter
you can reach. Do not stop to gather belongings.

============================================================
DO THIS NOW
============================================================

1. GET INSIDE the nearest sturdy building. Brick or concrete is
   best. Any building is better than staying outside. A car is
   NOT a shelter.
   - If you are outside when the blast happens: take cover behind
     anything solid and lie face down to protect exposed skin.
     When the blast wave has passed, get inside right away.
   - If you can't get inside right away, cover your nose and
     mouth with a mask, cloth, or towel.

2. GO TO THE BASEMENT OR THE CENTER OF THE BUILDING.
   Put as many walls and floors as you can between you and the
   outside. Stay away from windows, doors, outside walls, and the
   roof. In a tall building, go to the center of a middle floor.

3. IF YOU WERE OUTSIDE, TAKE OFF YOUR OUTER LAYER OF CLOTHING
   before you join others. This can remove up to 90% of the
   radioactive material. Bag it. Then wash (see Section 4).

4. CLOSE UP THE BUILDING.
   - Close and lock windows and doors.
   - Close fireplace dampers.
   - Turn off fans, air conditioners, and forced-air heating that
     pull in outside air.
   You do NOT need to tape or seal the room. Heavy materials and
   distance protect you from fallout; airtightness doesn't. Never
   delay getting to the basement or center to tape windows.

5. STAY INSIDE FOR AT LEAST 24 HOURS unless officials tell you
   otherwise, or there is an immediate danger such as fire, a gas
   leak, or building collapse.

6. STAY TUNED. Use a battery or hand-crank radio, phone alerts,
   or TV. Follow official instructions. If told to evacuate, do
   it, and don't come back until officials say it's safe.

7. BRING PETS INSIDE. Do NOT go outside to collect family.
   Schools, daycares, hospitals, and nursing homes have their own
   shelter plans. Going out exposes you and them.

CALL 911 (or your local emergency number) for serious injuries,
burns, fire, or someone not breathing. Networks may be jammed:
keep trying for emergencies, and text family instead of calling.

============================================================
SECTION 1: WHERE TO SHELTER
============================================================

Fallout settles on the ground and on roofs. Its radiation passes
through walls, so the more mass (concrete, brick, earth) and the
more distance between you and the outside, the safer you are.

BEST:
+ Basement (the deeper and more central, the better)
+ Center of a large brick or concrete building
+ Center of the middle floors of a tall building
+ Underground parking garage, subway, or tunnel

POOR:
- Vehicles
- Single-story wood-frame house without a basement
- Rooms next to windows or outside walls
- The top floor (fallout lands on the roof) and the ground floor
  of tall buildings

PROTECTION FACTOR (PF): how much a spot reduces your dose. PF 10
means you get 1/10 of the dose you'd get outside. FEMA counts PF
10 or more as adequate shelter.

Where you are                                      | About PF
---------------------------------------------------|---------
Outside                                            | 1
Car                                                | Very low
1-story wood-frame house, main floor               | 2-3
1-story wood-frame house, basement                 | 10
2-story brick-veneer house, main floor             | 3
2-story brick-veneer house, basement               | 20
3-story brick/concrete building, middle floor      | 7
3-story brick/concrete building, basement          | 50
5-story brick/concrete building, middle floor      | 20
5-story brick/concrete building, basement          | 100-200
Large office/apartment building, center of the     | 50-100
  middle floors                                    |
Large office/apartment building, top or ground     | about 10
  floor                                            |
Large office/apartment building, underground       | 200
(Source: FEMA, Planning Guidance for Response to a Nuclear
Detonation, 3rd ed., Figure 24)

IF YOUR SHELTER IS POOR (a car, or a wood house with no basement)
and a much better building is within about 15 minutes, FEMA's
planning guidance says to move there early, within roughly the
first 30 minutes after the explosion, then stay put. If no better
shelter is close, stay where you are, in the most central spot.

============================================================
SECTION 2: HOW FALLOUT DECAYS (THE 7-10 RULE)
============================================================

Fallout is most dangerous right after it arrives. It gives off
over half of its energy in the first hour, then drops fast.

Rule of thumb: every time the time since the explosion multiplies
by 7, the radiation level drops to about one-tenth.

Time after explosion   | Radiation level (vs. 1 hour after)
-----------------------|-----------------------------------
1 hour                 | 100%
7 hours                | about 10%
2 days (49 hours)      | about 1%
2 weeks                | about 0.1%

This is an estimate. Real levels depend on the weapon, the
weather, and where fallout lands. Only officials with radiation
meters can tell you an area is safe. Lower levels later on may
make it safer to leave, not necessarily safe to stay outside.

============================================================
SECTION 3: IF YOU MUST MOVE OR GO OUTSIDE
============================================================

Leave shelter early only for an immediate danger (fire, gas leak,
collapse, a medical emergency) or when officials tell you to.

1. Spend as little time outside as possible.
2. Cover your skin and hair. Cover your nose and mouth with a
   mask or cloth.
3. Don't touch or pick up things outside.
4. Before going back in, take off your outer layer of clothing
   and shoes, bag them, and wash (Section 4).
5. Wipe sealed containers you bring in with a damp cloth.

============================================================
SECTION 4: DECONTAMINATION (CLEANING UP)
============================================================

Do this as soon as you get inside, before joining others. Tap
water is fine for washing even if officials say not to drink it.

1. TAKE OFF YOUR OUTER LAYER of clothing (and shoes) carefully,
   without shaking it, so dust doesn't get into the air. This
   can remove up to 90% of the radioactive material.
2. BAG IT. Seal clothing in a plastic bag or container. Keep the
   bag away from people and pets.
3. SHOWER if you can. Use warm water and lots of soap.
   - Do NOT scald, scrub, or scratch your skin. Broken skin lets
     contamination in.
   - Wash your hair with shampoo or soap.
   - Do NOT use conditioner. It makes radioactive material stick
     to your hair.
   - Keep cuts and scrapes covered while you wash.
4. NO SHOWER? Wash your hands, face, and any skin that was
   uncovered at a sink. No sink? Use a moist wipe, clean wet
   cloth, or damp paper towel.
5. Gently BLOW YOUR NOSE, then wipe your eyelids, eyelashes, and
   ears with a moist wipe or wet cloth.
6. PUT ON CLEAN CLOTHES (clothes that were stored inside are
   fine). If you have none, shake or brush off what you have, put
   it back on, and rewash your hands, face, and exposed skin.
7. Bag used wipes and towels and keep them away from people and
   pets.

DON'T:
- Use household cleaning wipes on skin.
- Rely on hand sanitizer. It does not remove radioactive material.
- Touch your eyes, nose, or mouth until you've washed.

HELPING OTHERS: wear waterproof gloves and a dust mask if you
have them, keep their cuts covered, then rewash your own hands,
face, and exposed skin.

============================================================
SECTION 5: POTASSIUM IODIDE (KI)
============================================================

- Take KI ONLY if public health or emergency officials (or your
  doctor) tell you to.
- KI protects ONLY the thyroid, and ONLY from radioactive iodine.
  It does not protect the rest of your body or from other
  radioactive materials. Getting inside protects you far more.
- Infants, children, and pregnant or breastfeeding women benefit
  most. Adults over 40 should take it only when officials
  recommend it.
- Never take more than directed. Too much can cause severe
  illness or death.
- Table salt and iodine-rich foods are NOT substitutes.

============================================================
SECTION 6: FOOD AND WATER
============================================================

+ Food and drinks that were inside the building are safe.
+ Sealed containers that were outside are safe once you wipe them
  with a damp cloth. Bag the cloth.
+ Bottled water and sealed drinks are safe.
~ Tap water: safe for washing. For drinking, follow official
  instructions.
- Do NOT eat garden produce, or food or water that sat uncovered
  outside, until officials say it is safe.
- Never ration drinking water unless officials tell you to.

============================================================
SECTION 7: PETS
============================================================

- Bring pets and service animals inside.
- If a pet was outside after fallout arrived: wear gloves if you
  can, gently brush its coat, then wash it with shampoo or soap
  and water and rinse well. Wash your own hands and face after.
- Sealed pet food is safe. Wipe containers before opening.

============================================================
SECTION 8: STAYING INFORMED
============================================================

- AM/FM radio is the most reliable source. Keep a battery or
  hand-crank radio and spare batteries.
- Wireless Emergency Alerts on phones, NOAA Weather Radio, TV,
  and the internet if they work.
- Officials will tell you where to go for contamination screening
  and medical care.
- Do NOT leave shelter to look for information.

============================================================
SECTION 9: SPECIAL SITUATIONS
============================================================

CHILDREN: Keep them away from windows and in the most central
spot. Decontaminate them the same way; help them so they don't
rub their eyes or mouth. Keep them busy and reassured.

OLDER ADULTS, PEOPLE WITH DISABILITIES OR MEDICAL NEEDS: Move them
to the best spot first. Keep medications, glasses, hearing aids,
and spare batteries for medical devices with you. Plan ahead for
power-dependent equipment.

PEOPLE WHO ARE SICK OR HURT: Call 911 for life-threatening
emergencies. Otherwise follow official instructions on where to
get care, and tell shelter staff.

============================================================
SECTION 10: SIGNS OF RADIATION SICKNESS
============================================================

Only a large dose in a short time causes radiation sickness.
Early signs can start within minutes to days:
- Nausea and vomiting
- Headache
- Diarrhea
Skin redness or burns may appear within hours or be delayed for
days. People often feel better for a while before getting sick
again (tiredness, fever, loss of appetite).

Seek medical care as soon as officials say it is safe to travel.
Call 911 for emergencies.

============================================================
PREPARE NOW (BEFORE ANYTHING HAPPENS)
============================================================

[ ] Pick the best shelter spot at home, work, and school
    (basement or the center of a large building).
[ ] Learn the shelter plans of your children's schools and care
    facilities, and your workplace.
[ ] Kit: water (1 gallon per person per day for several days),
    sealed food, medications, battery or hand-crank radio, spare
    batteries, flashlight, first aid kit, a spare set of clothes
    for each person, and trash bags for contaminated clothing.
[ ] Make a family communication plan with an out-of-town contact
    and a meeting place.
[ ] Sign up for local alerts and make sure your phone receives
    Wireless Emergency Alerts.
[ ] If you live near a nuclear power plant, ask your local
    emergency management office about its plan and KI.

============================================================
QUICK ACTION CHECKLIST
============================================================

[ ] GET INSIDE a sturdy building (not a car)
[ ] Go to the BASEMENT or CENTER, away from windows and roof
[ ] Outer clothing OFF and bagged if you were outside
[ ] WASH: warm water, soap, shampoo, NO conditioner
[ ] Close windows, doors, dampers; ventilation OFF
[ ] Pets inside; don't go out for family
[ ] STAY INSIDE at least 24 hours
[ ] LISTEN to the radio for official instructions
[ ] KI only if officials say so

Remember:
TIME      - Less time near fallout = less dose
DISTANCE  - Farther from fallout on the ground and roof = less dose
SHIELDING - More concrete, brick, or earth between you = less dose
