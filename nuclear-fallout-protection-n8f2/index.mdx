## Do This Now

<TimelineBadge time="First 10+ min" label="Get inside before fallout arrives" />

### 1. Get inside now

Get into the nearest sturdy building. **Brick or concrete is best.** Any building is better than staying outside. **A car is not a shelter.**

- **Outside when the blast happens?** Take cover behind anything solid and lie face down to protect exposed skin. When the blast wave has passed, get inside right away.
- **Can't get inside right away?** Cover your nose and mouth with a mask, cloth, or towel.

### 2. Go to the basement or the center of the building

Put as many walls and floors as you can between you and the outside. Stay away from windows, doors, outside walls, and the roof. In a tall building, go to the center of a middle floor.

### 3. If you were outside, take off your outer clothing

Do it **before** you join others. Taking off your outer layer can remove **up to 90%** of the radioactive material. Bag it, then wash. See Decontamination below.

### 4. Close up the building

- Close and lock windows and doors
- Close fireplace dampers
- Turn off fans, air conditioners, and forced-air heating that pull in outside air

**You do not need to tape or seal the room.** Heavy materials and distance protect you from fallout; airtightness doesn't. Never delay getting to the basement or center to tape windows. (Sealing a room with plastic is for **chemical** releases, and that uses an above-ground room. See the Sealing a Room guide.)

### 5. Stay inside at least 24 hours

<CountdownTimer hours={24} />

### 6. Stay tuned

Use a battery or hand-crank radio, phone alerts, or TV, and follow official instructions. If you're told to evacuate, do it, and don't come back until officials say it's safe.

<CriticalAlert title="DON'T GO OUT FOR FAMILY">
Bring pets inside, but **do not go outside to collect family members.** Schools, daycares, hospitals, and nursing homes have their own shelter plans. Going out exposes you and them.
</CriticalAlert>

**Call 911** (or your local emergency number) for serious injuries, burns, fire, or someone not breathing. Networks may be jammed: keep trying for emergencies, and text family instead of calling.

## Where to Shelter: Protection Factors

Fallout settles on the ground and on roofs, and its radiation passes through walls. The more concrete, brick, or earth and the more distance between you and the outside, the safer you are.

The **Protection Factor (PF)** shows how much a spot cuts your dose. PF 10 means you get one-tenth of the dose you would get outside. FEMA counts **PF 10 or more** as adequate shelter. Values below are from FEMA's nuclear detonation planning guidance.

A **car** gives very little protection and is not an adequate shelter.

**If your shelter is poor** (a car, or a wood house with no basement) and a much better building is within about 15 minutes, FEMA's planning guidance says to move there early, within roughly the first 30 minutes after the explosion, then stay put. If no better shelter is close, stay where you are, in the most central spot.

## How Fallout Decays: The 7-10 Rule

Fallout is most dangerous right after it arrives. It gives off over half of its energy in the first hour, then drops fast. **Rule of thumb:** every time the time since the explosion multiplies by 7, the radiation level drops to about one-tenth.

| Time after explosion | Radiation level (vs. 1 hour after) |
|-----------------|-----------------|
| 1 hour | 100% (most dangerous) |
| 7 hours | about 10% |
| 2 days (49 hours) | about 1% |
| 2 weeks | about 0.1% |

This is an estimate. Real levels depend on the weapon, the weather, and where fallout lands. Only officials with radiation meters can tell you an area is safe.

## If You Must Move or Go Outside

Leave shelter early only for an immediate danger (fire, gas leak, collapse, a medical emergency) or when officials tell you to.

1. Spend as little time outside as possible
2. Cover your skin and hair; cover your nose and mouth with a mask or cloth
3. Don't touch or pick up things outside
4. Before going back in, take off your outer layer of clothing and shoes, bag them, and wash
5. Wipe sealed containers you bring in with a damp cloth

## Decontamination: Cleaning Up

Do this as soon as you get inside, before joining others. **Tap water is fine for washing** even if officials say not to drink it.

<DecontaminationSteps />

1. **Take off your outer layer** of clothing and shoes carefully, without shaking it. This can remove up to 90% of the radioactive material.
2. **Bag it.** Seal clothing in a plastic bag or container, away from people and pets.
3. **Shower** with warm water and lots of soap. **Do not scald, scrub, or scratch your skin**; broken skin lets contamination in. Keep cuts and scrapes covered.
4. **Wash your hair** with shampoo or soap. **Do not use conditioner**; it makes radioactive material stick to hair.
5. **No shower?** Wash hands, face, and uncovered skin at a sink. No sink? Use a moist wipe, clean wet cloth, or damp paper towel.
6. **Gently blow your nose**, then wipe your eyelids, eyelashes, and ears.
7. **Put on clean clothes** that were stored inside. If you have none, shake or brush off your clothes, put them back on, and rewash hands, face, and exposed skin.

**Don't** use household cleaning wipes on skin, and **don't** rely on hand sanitizer: it does not remove radioactive material.

**Helping others:** wear waterproof gloves and a dust mask if you have them, keep their cuts covered, then rewash your own hands, face, and exposed skin.

## Potassium Iodide (KI)

- Take KI **only** if public health or emergency officials (or your doctor) tell you to.
- KI protects **only the thyroid**, and only from radioactive iodine. It does not protect the rest of your body or from other radioactive materials. Getting inside protects you far more.
- Infants, children, and pregnant or breastfeeding women benefit most. Adults over 40 should take it only when officials recommend it.
- **Never take more than directed.** Too much can cause severe illness or death.
- Table salt and iodine-rich foods are **not** substitutes.

## Pets

- Bring pets and service animals inside.
- If a pet was outside after fallout arrived: wear gloves if you can, gently brush its coat, then wash it with shampoo or soap and water and rinse well. Wash your own hands and face afterward.
- Sealed pet food is safe. Wipe containers before opening.

## Staying Informed

- **AM/FM radio** is the most reliable source. Keep a battery or hand-crank radio and spare batteries.
- **Wireless Emergency Alerts** on phones, **NOAA Weather Radio**, TV, and the internet if they work.
- Officials will tell you where to go for contamination screening and medical care.

## Special Situations

- **Children:** keep them in the most central spot. Help them wash so they don't rub their eyes or mouth. Keep them busy and reassured.
- **Older adults, people with disabilities or medical needs:** move them to the best spot first. Keep medications, glasses, hearing aids, and spare batteries for medical devices with you.
- **Sick or hurt:** call 911 for life-threatening emergencies. Otherwise follow official instructions on where to get care.

---

## Signs of Radiation Sickness

Only a large dose in a short time causes radiation sickness. Early signs can start within minutes to days: **nausea, vomiting, headache, and diarrhea.** Skin redness or burns may appear within hours or be delayed for days. People often feel better for a while before getting sick again.

Seek medical care as soon as officials say it is safe to travel. Call 911 for emergencies.

## Prepare Now

- Pick the best shelter spot at home, work, and school (basement or the center of a large building)
- Learn the shelter plans of your children's schools, care facilities, and your workplace
- Kit: water (1 gallon per person per day for several days), sealed food, medications, a battery or hand-crank radio, spare batteries, flashlight, first aid kit, a spare set of clothes for each person, and trash bags for contaminated clothing
- Make a family communication plan with an out-of-town contact and a meeting place
- Make sure your phone receives Wireless Emergency Alerts, and sign up for local alerts
- Near a nuclear power plant? Ask your local emergency management office about its plan and KI

## Quick Action Checklist

```
□ GET INSIDE a sturdy building (not a car)
□ Go to the BASEMENT or CENTER, away from windows and roof
□ Outer clothing OFF and bagged if you were outside
□ WASH: warm water, soap, shampoo, NO conditioner
□ Close windows, doors, dampers; ventilation OFF
□ Pets inside; don't go out for family
□ STAY INSIDE at least 24 hours
□ LISTEN to the radio for official instructions
□ KI only if officials say so
```
