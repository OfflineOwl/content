# Fire Safety During Blackouts

> **When the Lights Go Out, Fire Risk Goes Up** - Power outages lead to increased use of candles, alternative heating, and open flames. This dramatically increases fire risk. This guide covers prevention and response.

## Why Fire Risk Increases

During blackouts, people use:
- Candles for lighting
- Portable heaters
- Fireplaces (sometimes unused for years)
- Camp stoves for cooking
- Generators
- Open flames of all kinds

**Fire departments see significant increases in fire calls during extended outages.**

---

## Candle Safety

### Before Using Candles

- Clear area of flammables (paper, curtains, bedding)
- Use sturdy, non-tip holders
- Place on heat-resistant surface
- Keep away from edges (can be knocked over)
- Keep away from drafts (can blow flames toward flammables)

### While Using Candles

- Never leave unattended
- Never leave while sleeping
- Keep away from children and pets
- Don't move lit candles
- Keep at least 12 inches from anything flammable
- Use battery lights instead when possible

### Safer Candle Alternatives

- Battery-powered LED candles
- Battery-powered lanterns
- Flashlights and headlamps
- Solar-charged lights
- Glow sticks

---

## Heating Safety

### Safe Indoor Options

- Fireplace (with working chimney)
- Wood stove (properly installed)
- Indoor-rated propane heater (ventilated)

### Unsafe Indoor Options (Never Use)

- Charcoal or gas grills
- Outdoor propane heaters
- Camp stoves (for heating)
- Generators
- Oven (for heating)

### Space Heater Safety

If using portable heaters:
- Keep 3+ feet from flammables
- Place on stable, level surface
- Plug directly into outlet (not extension cord unless rated)
- Turn off when sleeping or leaving
- Ensure automatic shutoff features
- Never cover or block vents

---

## Cooking Safety

### Safe Methods

- Outdoor grilling (away from structures)
- Camp stoves (outdoors or well-ventilated)
- Fireplace cooking (if equipped)
- Chafing dishes with Sterno (brief, ventilated)

### Kitchen Fire Prevention

- Stay in kitchen while cooking
- Keep flammables away from heat
- Have fire extinguisher accessible
- Know how to extinguish grease fires (smother, don't use water)
- Turn off heat before leaving

---

## Generator Safety

Fire-related risks:
- Fuel storage and handling
- Hot exhaust
- Electrical overload

### Prevention

- Store fuel properly (away from heat sources)
- Let generator cool before refueling
- Don't overload circuits
- Use properly rated extension cords
- Keep generator away from flammables

---

## Smoke Detectors

### During Power Outage

- Battery backup should be working
- Test detectors at start of outage
- Replace batteries if needed
- Don't remove batteries for other uses

### Best Practice

- Smoke detector in every bedroom
- Smoke detector on every level
- CO detector near sleeping areas
- Test monthly
- Replace batteries annually
- Replace detectors every 10 years

## Fire Extinguisher Use

### Types

| Type | Use For |
|------|---------|
| A | Ordinary combustibles (paper, wood) |
| B | Flammable liquids (gas, oil, grease) |
| C | Electrical fires |
| ABC | All of the above (recommended for home) |

### PASS Technique

**P**ull the pin
**A**im at base of fire
**S**queeze the handle
**S**weep side to side

### When to Fight Fire

**Only if:**
- Fire is small (contained to origin)
- Room is not filled with smoke
- You have clear escape route
- You're confident using extinguisher
- Everyone else is evacuating

**If in doubt, get out.**

---

## Emergency Escape Planning

### Know Your Exits

- Two ways out of every room
- Practice in darkness
- Know where to meet outside
- Don't assume familiar routes

### If Fire Occurs

1. Alert everyone ("FIRE!")
2. Get out immediately
3. Stay low if smoke present
4. Feel doors before opening
5. Close doors behind you (slows fire)
6. Meet at designated spot
7. Call 911 from outside
8. NEVER re-enter

### If Trapped

1. Close door, seal cracks
2. Signal from window
3. Call 911 with location
4. Don't jump (unless immediate danger)
5. Wait for rescue

## Fire Prevention Checklist

### Daily During Outage

- [ ] Clear flammables from candles/heaters
- [ ] Never leave flames unattended
- [ ] Extinguish all flames before sleeping
- [ ] Check heating equipment before use
- [ ] Keep fire extinguisher accessible

### Before Outage

- [ ] Test smoke detectors
- [ ] Check fire extinguisher charge
- [ ] Review escape routes with family
- [ ] Identify meeting point outside
- [ ] Stock battery-powered lights
