FUEL STORAGE SAFETY
===================

Storing fuel for emergencies requires understanding the risks.
Improper storage can cause fires, explosions, and toxic exposure.

FUEL TYPES AND USES
-------------------

GASOLINE:
Uses: Vehicles, generators, some equipment
Storage life: 3-6 months (up to 1 year with stabilizer)
Containers: Approved gas cans only (red)

DIESEL:
Uses: Diesel vehicles, some generators
Storage life: 6-12 months (longer with treatment)
Containers: Approved diesel cans (yellow)

PROPANE:
Uses: Grills, heaters, some generators
Storage life: Indefinite (check tank condition)
Containers: Approved propane tanks only

KEROSENE:
Uses: Heaters, lamps
Storage life: 2-5 years if stored properly
Containers: Approved kerosene cans (blue)

WHITE GAS/CAMP FUEL:
Uses: Camp stoves, some lanterns
Storage life: 5-7 years sealed
Containers: Original container or approved

WOOD:
Uses: Fireplace, wood stove
Storage life: Indefinite if kept dry
Storage: Covered, off ground, away from house

GENERAL STORAGE RULES
---------------------

FOR ALL LIQUID FUELS:

1. CONTAINER REQUIREMENTS:
   - Use only approved containers
   - Containers must be in good condition
   - Keep caps/lids tight
   - Don't overfill (leave expansion space)
   - Label clearly if not color-coded

2. STORAGE LOCATION:
   - Outdoors preferred
   - If indoors: detached structure (shed, garage)
   - Away from living spaces
   - Away from any ignition sources
   - Cool, well-ventilated area
   - On stable, level surface
   - Protected from sunlight

3. QUANTITY LIMITS:
   - Check local fire codes
   - Typical home limit: 25 gallons gasoline
   - Keep minimum practical amount
   - More fuel = more risk

4. SAFETY MEASURES:
   - Fire extinguisher nearby
   - No smoking near storage
   - No open flames
   - Ground containers when filling
   - Clean up spills immediately

GASOLINE STORAGE
----------------

RISKS:
- Highly flammable
- Vapors are explosive
- Toxic if inhaled
- Degrades over time

PROPER STORAGE:
- Use only approved red gas cans
- Maximum 5 gallons per container typical
- Store outdoors if possible
- In detached garage/shed if needed
- NEVER store in basement or living area
- Keep away from water heaters, furnaces
- Store below 80°F if possible

EXTENDING SHELF LIFE:
- Add fuel stabilizer (Sta-Bil, PRI-G)
- Fill containers full (reduces moisture)
- Rotate stock (use oldest first)
- With stabilizer: up to 1-2 years
- Without stabilizer: 3-6 months

ROTATION SCHEDULE:
- Use in vehicles every 3-6 months
- Refill with fresh fuel
- Treat with stabilizer upon storage

PROPANE STORAGE
---------------

TANKS:
- 20 lb (grill size): Most common
- 100 lb tanks: Larger capacity
- Fixed tanks: Requires professional install

SAFETY:
- Store upright only
- Store outdoors
- Keep valve closed when not in use
- Protect from physical damage
- Check for leaks with soapy water
- Transport upright and secured
- Never store in basement/enclosed space

ADVANTAGES:
- Indefinite shelf life
- Clean burning
- Safe when handled properly

INSPECTION:
- Check tank date (must be recertified after 12 years)
- Check for rust, damage
- Check valve condition
- Smell for leaks (propane has added odorant)

KEROSENE STORAGE
----------------

REQUIREMENTS:
- Use only K-1 (clear) kerosene for heaters
- Blue containers designated for kerosene
- Store in cool, dark location
- Ensure cap is tight

SHELF LIFE:
- Can last 2-5 years properly stored
- Older kerosene may produce more soot
- Don't use if water-contaminated

SAFETY:
- Keep away from heat sources
- Ensure good ventilation
- Never mix with other fuels

WOOD STORAGE
------------

SEASONING:
- Fresh-cut wood: Too wet to burn efficiently
- Seasoning time: 6-12 months
- Properly seasoned: 20% moisture or less

STORAGE BEST PRACTICES:
- Off the ground (pallets, rack)
- Covered on top
- Open sides for airflow
- Away from house (pest/fire risk)
- Split wood seasons faster

QUANTITY:
- 1 cord = 128 cubic feet (4x4x8 ft)
- Usage varies widely by climate and use
- 2-4 cords typical for heating season

HANDLING FUEL SAFELY
--------------------

WHEN FILLING EQUIPMENT:

1. Turn off equipment before fueling
2. Let hot equipment cool
3. Fuel outdoors when possible
4. Ground container to prevent static
5. Don't overfill
6. Wipe up spills
7. Move away before starting

WHEN TRANSPORTING:

1. Use approved containers
2. Secure containers (won't tip)
3. Ensure caps are tight
4. Ventilate vehicle
5. Don't transport in passenger area
6. Don't leave in hot vehicle

SPILL RESPONSE:
1. Eliminate ignition sources
2. Ventilate area
3. Contain spill (absorbent material)
4. Don't wash into drains
5. Dispose properly
6. Large spills: Call fire department

FIRE SAFETY
-----------

FIRE EXTINGUISHER:
- Have Class B extinguisher (flammable liquids)
- Near but not in fuel storage area
- Know how to use it
- Check pressure regularly

PREVENTION:
- No smoking near fuels
- No open flames
- Ground containers
- Eliminate static
- Good ventilation

IF FIRE OCCURS:
- Small fire: Use appropriate extinguisher
- Large fire: Evacuate, call 911
- Don't fight large fuel fires
- Propane tank in fire: Evacuate far distance

LOCAL REGULATIONS
-----------------

Check local fire codes for:
- Maximum quantities allowed
- Required container types
- Storage location requirements
- Permit requirements
- Inspection requirements

Typical residential limits:
- Gasoline: 25 gallons
- Propane: Varies (often 2x 20lb tanks)
- Kerosene: Often same as gasoline
