# Emergency Heating Without Power

> **Cold Can Kill** - Hypothermia is a serious risk during winter power outages. This guide covers safe ways to stay warm when your normal heating system doesn't work.

## Understanding Cold Risk

### Hypothermia Danger

Body temperature drops below 95°F (35°C):
- **Mild (90-95°F):** Shivering, confusion
- **Moderate (82-90°F):** Drowsiness, slurred speech
- **Severe (below 82°F):** Unconsciousness, life-threatening

**Vulnerable populations:**
- Infants and young children
- Elderly
- Those with health conditions
- Those without adequate clothing/shelter

## Safe Indoor Heating Options

### Fireplace (Wood-Burning)

**If you have a working fireplace:**
- Most effective emergency heat
- Ensure chimney is clean (fire risk)
- Open damper before lighting
- Use dry, seasoned wood
- Keep fire manageable
- Never leave unattended
- Install glass doors if possible
- CO detector essential

### Wood Stove

**Most efficient wood heating:**
- High heat output
- Can cook on top
- Requires installation and chimney
- Keep area around stove clear
- CO detector essential

### Propane/Kerosene Heaters (With Caution)

**Only if designed for indoor use:**

| Type | Safety | Notes |
|------|--------|-------|
| Vented propane | Safer | Requires installation |
| Ventless propane | Caution | Needs fresh air, CO detector |
| Indoor kerosene | Caution | Needs ventilation, proper fuel |

**Critical safety rules:**
- Use ONLY indoor-rated heaters
- Never use outdoor heaters inside
- Ensure adequate ventilation
- Keep away from flammables
- Never refuel while hot
- CO detector is mandatory
- Never leave unattended while sleeping

### Electric Alternatives (With Generator)

If you have generator power:
- Electric space heaters
- Electric blankets
- Heat pump (if enough power)

---

## NEVER Use Indoors

These produce deadly carbon monoxide:
- ❌ Charcoal grills
- ❌ Gas grills
- ❌ Outdoor propane heaters
- ❌ Camp stoves (extended use)
- ❌ Generators
- ❌ Car engines (in garage)
- ❌ Oven for heating (gas)

**Carbon monoxide is colorless and odorless. You won't know it's killing you.**

## Passive Heating Strategies

### Isolate One Room

**Create a warm room:**
1. Choose smallest room with fewest windows
2. Close doors to rest of house
3. Seal door gaps with towels
4. Cover windows with blankets
5. Gather family in this room
6. Body heat adds warmth

### Reduce Heat Loss

**Windows:**
- Close curtains/blinds
- Hang blankets over windows
- Use bubble wrap on glass
- Cover with plastic sheeting

**Doors:**
- Block drafts with towels
- Close off unused rooms
- Hang blankets over doorways

**Floors:**
- Lay rugs or blankets
- Stay off cold floors
- Especially in basements

### Use Solar Gain

During daylight:
- Open south-facing curtains
- Let sunlight in
- Close curtains at dusk to trap heat

## Personal Warmth Strategies

### Layering

**Layer system:**
1. Base layer (moisture-wicking)
2. Mid layer (insulating—fleece, wool)
3. Outer layer (wind/water resistant)

**Key areas to cover:**
- Head (significant heat loss)
- Hands
- Feet
- Core

### Body Heat Conservation

- Stay dry (wet = cold)
- Stay active (but don't sweat)
- Eat regularly (calories = heat)
- Stay hydrated
- Huddle together (shared body heat)
- Use sleeping bags
- Layer blankets

### Sleeping Warm

- Sleeping bags rated for temperature
- Layer multiple blankets
- Wear hat and socks to bed
- Hot water bottle (safely wrapped)
- Share body heat
- Sleep in warm room

## Emergency Heat Sources

### Hot Water Bottles

1. Heat water (carefully)
2. Fill bottle 2/3 full
3. Remove air, seal tightly
4. Wrap in cloth
5. Place at feet or core
6. Retains heat for hours

### Heated Rocks/Bricks

1. Heat rocks near fire (not in fire—can explode)
2. Wrap in towel
3. Place in bed or seating area
4. Monitor temperature

### Terracotta Pot Heater

**Candle-powered (minimal heat):**
1. Place candles on fireproof surface
2. Invert terracotta pot over candles
3. Pot absorbs and radiates heat
4. Very limited effectiveness
5. Fire hazard—use caution

## Fuel Storage

### Wood

- Store dry, seasoned wood
- Keep covered but ventilated
- 1 cord = 128 cubic feet
- Estimate 2-3 cords per winter (varies greatly)

### Propane

- Store tanks outdoors
- Check for leaks
- Know tank capacity
- 20lb tank = ~400,000 BTU

### Kerosene

- Use only K-1 grade (clear)
- Store in approved containers
- Keep away from heat sources
- Check heater compatibility

---

## Carbon Monoxide Safety

### CO Detector

- Essential with any fuel-burning heat
- Battery backup required
- Test regularly
- Replace per manufacturer guidelines

### CO Poisoning Symptoms

**Early:**
- Headache
- Dizziness
- Nausea
- Confusion

**Severe:**
- Loss of consciousness
- Death

**If suspected:**
1. Get to fresh air immediately
2. Call for help
3. Don't re-enter until cleared

---

## When to Leave

Consider evacuation if:
- Indoor temp drops below 50°F
- Vulnerable persons present
- No safe heating options
- Extended cold expected
- Pipes at risk of freezing

**Options:**
- Warming center
- Family/friends with heat
- Hotel
- Emergency shelter
