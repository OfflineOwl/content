# Emergency Gardening & Food Growing

> **Grow Your Own Food** - When supply chains are disrupted, a garden can be the difference between going hungry and feeding your family. This guide covers how to start growing food quickly, even with no prior experience.

## Why Emergency Gardening Matters

In a prolonged emergency, stored food will eventually run out. Growing your own food provides:
- A renewable source of nutrition
- Fresh vegetables with essential vitamins
- Improved morale and sense of purpose
- Reduced dependence on outside supply chains
- Food you can barter or share with neighbors

## Best Crops for Beginners

Focus on crops that are **fast-growing, high-yield, and nutritious**. These require minimal experience and tolerate imperfect conditions.

### Tier 1: Start Here

| Crop | Days to Harvest | Why It's Great |
|------|----------------|----------------|
| Lettuce/greens | 30-45 days | Fast, cut-and-come-again |
| Radishes | 25-30 days | Fastest root vegetable |
| Bush beans | 50-60 days | High protein, fixes nitrogen |
| Zucchini/squash | 50-65 days | Extremely productive |
| Tomatoes | 60-85 days | Versatile, preservable |

### Tier 2: High Calorie Staples

| Crop | Days to Harvest | Why It's Great |
|------|----------------|----------------|
| Potatoes | 70-120 days | Calorie-dense, stores well |
| Sweet potatoes | 90-120 days | Nutritious, drought-tolerant |
| Winter squash | 85-110 days | Stores for months |
| Dry beans | 80-100 days | Protein, stores indefinitely |
| Corn | 60-100 days | Calorie staple |

### Tier 3: Nutrient Boosters

- **Kale** - Cold-hardy, packed with vitamins A, C, K
- **Swiss chard** - Heat-tolerant, continuous harvest
- **Carrots** - Store well in ground, high in vitamin A
- **Beets** - Eat roots and greens, fast growing
- **Herbs (basil, cilantro, parsley)** - Flavor and medicinal uses

## Starting a Survival Garden

### Step 1: Choose Your Location

**Ideal conditions:**
- 6-8 hours of direct sunlight daily
- Access to water
- Relatively flat ground
- Protected from strong winds
- Away from large tree roots

**If space is limited:**
- Use any south-facing area
- Balconies and patios work for containers
- Window boxes for herbs and greens
- Rooftops if structurally sound

### Step 2: Prepare the Soil

**Without commercial products:**
1. Clear the area of grass, weeds, and debris
2. Loosen soil to 12 inches deep with a shovel or fork
3. Mix in available organic matter:
   - Decomposed leaves
   - Kitchen scraps (buried 6 inches deep)
   - Grass clippings (thin layers)
   - Aged animal manure if available
   - Wood ash (small amounts for potassium)
4. Break up large clumps
5. Rake smooth before planting

**Quick start method (lasagna gardening):**
1. Lay cardboard directly on grass/weeds
2. Add 4-6 inches of compostable material on top
3. Add 2-3 inches of soil or finished compost
4. Plant directly into the top layer
5. The layers decompose and feed the soil over time

### Step 3: Plant Strategically

**Succession planting:**
- Plant small amounts every 2-3 weeks
- Ensures continuous harvest rather than all at once
- Reduces waste and extends food supply

**Companion planting basics:**
- **Beans + corn + squash** (Three Sisters method) - beans fix nitrogen, corn provides support, squash shades soil
- **Tomatoes + basil** - basil repels pests
- **Carrots + onions** - confuse each other's pests
- **Avoid:** tomatoes near potatoes, beans near onions

## Raised Bed Gardening

Raised beds are excellent for emergency gardening because they offer better soil control and drainage.

### Building a Simple Raised Bed

**Materials:**
- Untreated lumber, cinder blocks, stones, or even stacked logs
- Avoid pressure-treated wood (chemicals leach into soil)
- Minimum 6 inches deep, 12 inches is ideal

**Dimensions:**
- 4 feet wide maximum (reach the center from either side)
- Any length that fits your space
- Orient north-south for best sun exposure

**Filling the bed:**
- Bottom third: sticks, branches, leaves (hugelkultur method)
- Middle third: partially decomposed organic matter
- Top third: best soil/compost you have available

### Advantages of Raised Beds

- Better drainage
- Soil warms faster in spring
- Easier on your back and knees
- Keep out some pests
- Define garden areas clearly
- Can place on concrete, gravel, or poor soil

## Container Gardening

When ground space is unavailable, almost anything can become a planter.

### Suitable Containers

- 5-gallon buckets (drill drainage holes)
- Large cans or drums
- Storage totes
- Old tires stacked for potatoes
- Reusable grocery bags
- Cut-open soil or feed bags

### Container Size Guide

| Crop | Minimum Container Size |
|------|----------------------|
| Lettuce, herbs | 1-2 gallons |
| Beans, peppers | 3-5 gallons |
| Tomatoes | 5+ gallons |
| Potatoes | 10+ gallons |
| Squash | 10+ gallons |

### Container Tips

- **Drainage is critical** - always drill holes in the bottom
- Containers dry out faster than ground beds - water daily
- Use saucers to catch and reuse water
- Dark containers absorb heat - wrap with light fabric in summer
- Group containers to create shade and reduce water loss
- Feed monthly with compost tea or diluted liquid from composting

## Composting Basics

Compost turns waste into valuable garden soil. In an emergency, it is your best fertilizer source.

### What to Compost

**Greens (nitrogen-rich):**
- Kitchen scraps (fruit, vegetables)
- Fresh grass clippings
- Coffee grounds and tea leaves
- Fresh plant trimmings

**Browns (carbon-rich):**
- Dried leaves
- Cardboard and newspaper (shredded)
- Straw or hay
- Wood chips or sawdust (small amounts)

**Never compost:**
- Meat, bones, or dairy (attracts pests)
- Diseased plants
- Pet waste
- Treated or painted wood

### Simple Composting Method

1. Choose a spot near the garden
2. Alternate layers of greens and browns (aim for 3:1 brown to green ratio)
3. Keep moist like a wrung-out sponge
4. Turn the pile every 1-2 weeks for faster decomposition
5. Compost is ready when it's dark, crumbly, and smells earthy (4-8 weeks in warm weather)

### Quick Compost Tea

For a fast liquid fertilizer:
1. Fill a bucket 1/3 with finished compost
2. Add water to fill
3. Stir daily for 3-5 days
4. Strain and dilute until it looks like weak tea
5. Water plants with the liquid

## Seed Saving

Saving seeds from your harvest means you never need to buy seeds again. **Use heirloom (open-pollinated) varieties**, not hybrids.

### Easy Seeds to Save

- **Beans and peas** - Let pods dry on the plant, shell and store
- **Tomatoes** - Scoop seeds, ferment in water 3 days, rinse, dry
- **Peppers** - Remove seeds from mature fruit, dry thoroughly
- **Lettuce** - Let bolt to flower, collect seed heads when dry
- **Squash** - Scoop seeds from mature fruit, wash and dry

### Seed Storage

- Dry seeds completely (1-2 weeks in a cool, dry spot)
- Store in paper envelopes inside a sealed jar
- Add a small silica packet or dry rice to absorb moisture
- Label with variety name and date
- Store in a cool, dark place
- Most seeds remain viable 2-5 years when stored properly

### Avoiding Cross-Pollination

- Grow only one variety of each species if saving seed
- Separate varieties by 50-100 feet minimum
- Use hand-pollination for squash family (cover flowers, transfer pollen manually)
- Beans and tomatoes self-pollinate, so cross-pollination is less of a concern

## Extending the Growing Season

### Cold Weather Techniques

- **Row covers** - Drape fabric (old sheets, curtains) over plants on cold nights
- **Cold frames** - Old windows over a raised bed create a mini greenhouse
- **Mulch heavily** - 4-6 inches of straw or leaves insulates soil
- **Wall of Water** - Circle tomato transplants with water-filled bottles (water absorbs heat during day, releases at night)
- **Plant cold-hardy crops** - Kale, spinach, carrots, and garlic can survive frost

### Hot Weather Techniques

- **Shade cloth** - Use old sheets or window screens to reduce direct sun
- **Mulch** - Keeps roots cool and retains moisture
- **Water in early morning** - Reduces evaporation
- **Choose heat-tolerant varieties** - Sweet potatoes, okra, peppers, beans

## Pest Control Without Chemicals

### Physical Methods

- **Hand-pick** large insects (caterpillars, beetles) daily
- **Row covers** prevent flying insects from reaching plants
- **Copper tape** around raised beds deters slugs
- **Diatomaceous earth** sprinkled around plants kills soft-bodied insects
- **Sticky traps** (yellow cards with petroleum jelly) catch whiteflies and aphids

### Natural Sprays

**Garlic spray:**
1. Blend 2 garlic bulbs with 1 quart water
2. Let sit overnight
3. Strain and add a few drops of dish soap
4. Spray directly on affected plants

**Neem oil spray (if available):**
- Mix 2 tablespoons neem oil per gallon of water
- Add a few drops of soap as emulsifier
- Spray weekly as prevention

### Companion Planting for Pest Control

- **Marigolds** - Repel aphids, whiteflies, and nematodes
- **Nasturtiums** - Trap crop for aphids (they attack nasturtiums instead)
- **Garlic/onions** - Deter many insects when planted among crops
- **Dill/fennel** - Attract beneficial predatory insects

### Beneficial Insects

Encourage these natural pest controllers:
- **Ladybugs** eat aphids
- **Praying mantis** eat many pest species
- **Ground beetles** eat slugs and caterpillars
- **Attract them** by planting flowers among vegetables

## Watering Strategies

### Conserving Water

- **Mulch everything** - Reduces water needs by 50%
- **Water at the base** of plants, not overhead
- **Water deeply but less often** - Encourages deep root growth
- **Collect rainwater** - Set up barrels or any large container under downspouts
- **Reuse gray water** - Dishwater (without bleach) can water non-edible plants and fruit trees

### Signs of Water Stress

- Wilting in the morning (evening wilting is often normal)
- Yellowing lower leaves
- Slow growth
- Blossom drop on tomatoes and peppers
- Bitter-tasting lettuce or cucumbers

### Emergency Irrigation

- **Drip method** - Poke a small hole in a water bottle cap, bury bottle next to plant
- **Olla method** - Bury unglazed clay pot in soil, fill with water, it seeps out slowly
- **Wicking bed** - Line a container with plastic, add a water reservoir below the soil

## Emergency Garden Checklist

- [ ] Identify sunniest area available
- [ ] Gather containers or build raised beds
- [ ] Source seeds (heirloom/open-pollinated preferred)
- [ ] Prepare soil or growing medium
- [ ] Set up water collection or access
- [ ] Plant fast-growing crops first (greens, radishes)
- [ ] Start compost pile with kitchen scraps
- [ ] Plan succession plantings every 2-3 weeks
- [ ] Identify local plants that are edible (dandelion, purslane, clover)
- [ ] Save seeds from every harvest
- [ ] Set up pest monitoring routine
- [ ] Mulch all planted areas
