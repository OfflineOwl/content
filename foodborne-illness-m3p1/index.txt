RECOGNIZING FOODBORNE ILLNESS
=============================

Food poisoning usually passes in a few days, but it can turn
dangerous, especially when clean water and medical care are hard to
reach. This guide covers what to do, the warning signs that need
help, and how to prevent it.

DO THIS NOW
-----------

1. CALL 911 (or your local emergency number) for:
   - Double or blurred vision, drooping eyelids, slurred speech,
     trouble swallowing, or weakness that spreads down the body
     (possible botulism)
   - Trouble breathing
   - Confusion, fainting, a seizure, or unresponsiveness
   - Signs of severe dehydration (no urine, very dizzy, rapid
     heartbeat)
2. Stop eating the food you suspect. Warn anyone else who ate it.
3. Drink fluids. Take small, frequent sips of water, broth, or an
   oral rehydration solution. This is the most important treatment.
4. Get medical care soon (see WHEN TO GET MEDICAL CARE) for bloody
   diarrhea, fever over 102°F (38.9°C), vomiting that stops you
   keeping fluids down, diarrhea lasting more than 3 days, or signs
   of dehydration.
5. Infants: keep breastfeeding or formula feeding as usual. Ask a
   doctor before giving oral rehydration solution to an infant.
6. Prevent spread: wash hands with soap and water after using the
   toilet and before handling food. Don't prepare food for others
   while sick.

WHEN TO GET MEDICAL CARE
------------------------

SEE A DOCTOR OR CLINIC FOR (CDC):
- Bloody diarrhea
- Diarrhea lasting more than 3 days
- Fever over 102°F (38.9°C)
- Vomiting so often you can't keep liquids down
- Signs of dehydration: peeing very little, dry mouth and throat,
  feeling dizzy when standing up

GET HELP SOONER FOR HIGH-RISK PEOPLE:
- Infants and young children
- Adults over 65
- Pregnant women (see a doctor for fever and flu-like symptoms;
  Listeria can cause miscarriage, stillbirth, or a life-threatening
  infection in the newborn)
- People with weakened immune systems (for example, cancer
  treatment, transplant medicines, HIV, or diabetes)

BOTULISM IS A MEDICAL EMERGENCY:
- Symptoms: double or blurred vision, drooping eyelids, slurred
  speech, trouble swallowing, trouble moving the eyes, muscle
  weakness that starts in the head and moves down, trouble
  breathing. Nausea, vomiting, stomach pain, or diarrhea may come
  first.
- Usually starts 18-36 hours after eating the food, but timing
  varies.
- Infants: constipation, poor feeding, weak or unusual cry,
  drooping eyelids, reduced facial expression.
- Go to an emergency room or call 911 immediately. Botulism can
  paralyze the breathing muscles and needs hospital treatment.
- Common sources: improperly home-canned, preserved, or fermented
  foods, and bulging or leaking cans.

WHAT CAUSES FOODBORNE ILLNESS
-----------------------------

BACTERIA:
- Salmonella (poultry, eggs, produce)
- E. coli (undercooked ground beef, raw produce, contaminated water)
- Listeria (deli meats, soft cheeses, unpasteurized milk)
- Campylobacter (raw or undercooked poultry, unpasteurized milk)
- Clostridium perfringens (large batches of meat or gravy kept warm
  or cooled slowly)
- Staphylococcus (food handled by people, then left at room
  temperature; its toxin isn't destroyed by reheating)
- Clostridium botulinum (improperly canned or preserved food)

VIRUSES:
- Norovirus (food handled by a sick person, contaminated water;
  spreads easily person to person)
- Hepatitis A (contaminated food or water)

PARASITES:
- Giardia, Cryptosporidium (contaminated water)
- Cyclospora (contaminated produce)

TOXINS:
- From certain mushrooms and wild plants
- From contaminated shellfish and some fish

WHEN SYMPTOMS START (CDC)
-------------------------

Symptoms can start within 30 minutes or not for weeks, depending on
the germ:

Staph                     30 minutes - 8 hours
Clostridium perfringens   6 - 24 hours
Salmonella                6 hours - 6 days
Norovirus                 12 - 48 hours
Botulism                  usually 18 - 36 hours
Campylobacter             2 - 5 days
E. coli (toxin-producing) 3 - 4 days
Cryptosporidium           2 - 10 days (average 7)
Cyclospora                about 1 week
Giardia                   1 - 2 weeks
Listeria (invasive)       about 2 weeks

The last thing you ate is not always the cause.

COMMON SYMPTOMS
---------------

- Diarrhea (watery or bloody)
- Stomach pain or cramps
- Nausea and vomiting
- Fever
- Headache, body aches

Most cases last 1 to 3 days. Some parasites cause diarrhea, greasy
stools, gas, and bloating that can last weeks. E. coli infections
that produce bloody diarrhea can lead to kidney failure (hemolytic
uremic syndrome) in some people, especially young children; watch
for very little urine, unusual tiredness, or pale skin, and get
care right away.

TREATMENT AT HOME
-----------------

1. REPLACE FLUIDS (most important)

Adults:
- Water, broth, diluted fruit juice, or sports drinks
- Saltine crackers help replace salt
- Small, frequent sips if vomiting; increase as tolerated

Older adults, people with weak immune systems, and anyone with
severe diarrhea or dehydration:
- Use an oral rehydration solution (ORS), store-bought if possible

Children:
- Give an oral rehydration solution as directed on the package
- Infants: keep breastfeeding or formula feeding as usual; ask a
  doctor before giving ORS

HOMEMADE ORAL REHYDRATION SOLUTION (WHO):
- 1 liter (about 1 quart) of safe drinking water
- 6 level teaspoons of sugar
- 1/2 level teaspoon of salt
Stir until dissolved. Measure carefully: too much salt is harmful.
Use treated or boiled-and-cooled water. Store-bought ORS packets
are better if you have them.

2. EAT WHEN YOU'RE READY
- While vomiting, stick to small sips of clear liquids.
- When your appetite returns, you can usually go back to your
  normal diet, even if you still have diarrhea.
- Bland foods (rice, toast, crackers, bananas, soup) may be easier
  at first.
- Avoid alcohol, and go easy on fatty or spicy foods until you feel
  better.

3. MEDICINES
- Adults may use loperamide (Imodium) or bismuth subsalicylate
  (Pepto-Bismol, Kaopectate) for diarrhea.
- DON'T use these if there is blood in the stool or a fever. See a
  doctor instead.
- DON'T give them to children unless a doctor says to.
- For fever or aches: acetaminophen or ibuprofen, following the
  label dose. Don't give aspirin to children or teenagers.
- Antibiotics are only for some infections and need a doctor's
  prescription.

4. REST
- Rest and stay cool. Keep drinking.

PREVENT SPREAD TO OTHERS
------------------------

- Wash hands with soap and water after using the toilet, changing
  diapers, or cleaning up vomit or diarrhea. Hand sanitizer does
  not work well against norovirus.
- Clean up vomit and diarrhea right away. Wear gloves. Disinfect
  the area with a bleach solution of 5 to 25 tablespoons of
  household bleach per gallon of water, leave it at least 5 minutes,
  then clean with soap and hot water.
- Wash soiled laundry in hot water on the longest cycle. Don't
  shake it out.
- Don't prepare food or care for others while sick, and for at
  least 2 days after symptoms stop.

PREVENTION: CLEAN, SEPARATE, COOK, CHILL
----------------------------------------

CLEAN:
- Wash hands before and after handling food and after using the
  toilet
- Clean surfaces and utensils
- Rinse produce with safe water

SEPARATE:
- Keep raw meat, poultry, seafood, and eggs away from other foods
- Use separate cutting boards and plates for raw meat
- Don't reuse marinade from raw meat

COOK (use a thermometer, USDA temperatures):
- Poultry: 165°F (74°C)
- Ground meat: 160°F (71°C)
- Steaks, chops, roasts: 145°F (63°C), then rest 3 minutes
- Fish: 145°F (63°C)
- Egg dishes: 160°F (71°C)
- Leftovers and casseroles: 165°F (74°C)

CHILL:
- Keep cold food at 40°F (4°C) or below
- Don't leave perishable food out more than 2 hours (1 hour above
  90°F / 32°C)
- Cool leftovers quickly in shallow containers

HIGH-RISK FOODS
---------------

RAW OR UNDERCOOKED:
- Meat (especially ground), poultry, eggs, seafood, shellfish
- Unpasteurized milk and juice
- Sprouts

HIGH-RISK IN AN EMERGENCY:
- Perishable food after a power outage (see the Food Safety Without
  Refrigeration guide)
- Food that touched floodwater
- Home-canned food, especially low-acid foods such as vegetables
  and meat
- Bulging, leaking, or deeply dented cans
- Food prepared with untreated water
- Wild mushrooms and plants you can't identify with certainty

EMERGENCY FOOD SAFETY
---------------------

WITHOUT REFRIGERATION:
- Eat perishables first, then frozen food, then shelf-stable food
- Never taste food to test it
- When in doubt, throw it out

WITHOUT CLEAN WATER:
- Treat all water used for drinking, cooking, washing produce, and
  washing hands (see the Water Purification guide)
- Use hand sanitizer between handwashes, but wash with soap and
  safe water when you can

IF SOMEONE GETS SICK:
- Stop serving the suspected food
- Tell others who ate it
- Write down what was eaten and when (see the log below)

FOOD POISONING LOG
------------------

If someone gets sick, write down:
- What they ate in the last 3 days, and when
- When symptoms started
- Symptoms and how they changed
- Fluids and medicines given, with times
- Who else ate the same food

This helps doctors and health departments find the cause.

QUICK REFERENCE
---------------

CALL 911:
Vision problems, drooping eyelids, slurred speech, trouble
swallowing or breathing, confusion, fainting, severe dehydration

SEE A DOCTOR:
Bloody diarrhea, fever over 102°F, can't keep fluids down,
diarrhea over 3 days, dehydration, pregnant with fever

AT HOME:
1. Stop eating the suspect food
2. Sip fluids often (ORS if available)
3. Rest
4. Eat normally once appetite returns

1 liter safe water + 6 level tsp sugar + 1/2 level tsp salt
