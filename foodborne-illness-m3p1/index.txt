RECOGNIZING FOODBORNE ILLNESS
=============================

When medical care is limited, preventing and recognizing 
foodborne illness becomes critical. This guide covers 
symptoms, treatment, and prevention.

WHAT CAUSES FOODBORNE ILLNESS
-----------------------------

BACTERIA:
- Salmonella (poultry, eggs, produce)
- E. coli (undercooked beef, contaminated water)
- Listeria (deli meats, soft cheese)
- Campylobacter (raw poultry, unpasteurized milk)
- Clostridium botulinum (improperly canned foods)
- Staphylococcus (foods left at room temperature)

VIRUSES:
- Norovirus (contaminated food/water)
- Hepatitis A (contaminated food/water)

PARASITES:
- Giardia (contaminated water)
- Cryptosporidium (contaminated water)

TOXINS:
- From bacteria in spoiled food
- From certain mushrooms and plants
- From contaminated shellfish

COMMON SYMPTOMS
---------------

TYPICAL FOOD POISONING:
Onset: 2-6 hours after eating

Symptoms:
- Nausea
- Vomiting
- Diarrhea
- Stomach cramps
- Fever (sometimes)
- Headache

Duration: Usually 1-3 days

BACTERIAL INFECTIONS:
Onset: 12-72 hours after eating

Symptoms:
- Watery or bloody diarrhea
- Severe abdominal cramps
- Fever
- Vomiting
- May last longer (up to a week)

VIRAL INFECTIONS:
Onset: 12-48 hours

Symptoms:
- Sudden onset of vomiting
- Watery diarrhea
- Stomach cramps
- Low-grade fever
- Highly contagious

PARASITIC INFECTIONS:
Onset: 1-2 weeks after exposure

Symptoms:
- Prolonged diarrhea
- Greasy, foul-smelling stools
- Stomach cramps
- Bloating and gas
- Weight loss
- Fatigue

WARNING SIGNS - SEEK MEDICAL HELP
---------------------------------

EMERGENCY SYMPTOMS:
- Blood in vomit or stool
- High fever (over 101.5°F)
- Signs of severe dehydration
- Symptoms lasting more than 3 days
- Inability to keep any fluids down
- Severe abdominal pain
- Neurological symptoms (blurred vision, weakness)
- Symptoms of botulism (see below)

BOTULISM WARNING SIGNS (Medical Emergency):
- Double or blurred vision
- Drooping eyelids
- Slurred speech
- Difficulty swallowing
- Difficulty breathing
- Muscle weakness spreading down body
- Onset: 12-36 hours after eating contaminated food

Botulism requires immediate medical attention.
It can be fatal without treatment.

HIGH-RISK GROUPS:
- Young children
- Elderly
- Pregnant women
- Immunocompromised individuals
Seek help sooner for these groups.

TREATMENT WITHOUT MEDICAL CARE
------------------------------

HYDRATION (Most Important):

Goal: Replace fluids and electrolytes

Options:
1. Oral rehydration solution (best)
   - Commercial: Pedialyte, Drip Drop
   - Homemade: 1L water + 6 tsp sugar + 1/2 tsp salt
   
2. Clear fluids
   - Water
   - Broth
   - Diluted juice (not citrus)
   - Weak tea

How to rehydrate:
- Small, frequent sips (not large amounts)
- 1-2 tablespoons every 5 minutes
- Increase as tolerated
- Don't wait until thirsty

DIET PROGRESSION:

First 24-48 hours:
- Clear liquids only
- No solid food
- No dairy
- No fatty foods

When vomiting stops:
- BRAT diet: Bananas, Rice, Applesauce, Toast
- Plain crackers
- Boiled potatoes
- Plain pasta

Gradually add:
- Lean proteins (chicken, fish)
- Cooked vegetables
- Eggs

Avoid for 24-48 hours after recovery:
- Dairy products
- Fatty foods
- Spicy foods
- Alcohol
- Caffeine

SYMPTOM MANAGEMENT:

For nausea:
- Ginger (tea, chews, candies)
- Peppermint tea
- Small sips of fluids
- Avoid strong odors

For vomiting:
- Small, frequent sips of fluids
- Don't eat until vomiting stops
- Rest
- Avoid activity

For diarrhea:
- Stay hydrated
- Avoid anti-diarrheal medications initially
  (body is trying to expel toxin)
- After 24 hours, may use as needed
- Bismuth subsalicylate (Pepto-Bismol) can help

For fever:
- Acetaminophen or ibuprofen
- Rest
- Stay hydrated
- Cool compresses

PREVENTION PRINCIPLES
---------------------

CLEAN:
- Wash hands before food prep
- Wash hands after bathroom
- Clean surfaces and utensils
- Wash produce

SEPARATE:
- Keep raw meat separate from other foods
- Use separate cutting boards
- Don't reuse marinade from raw meat

COOK:
- Cook to proper temperatures
- Use food thermometer
- Don't eat raw or undercooked meat/eggs

CHILL:
- Keep cold foods cold (below 40°F)
- Don't leave food out more than 2 hours
- Cool leftovers quickly

HIGH-RISK FOODS
---------------

MOST LIKELY TO CAUSE ILLNESS:

Raw/undercooked:
- Meat (especially ground)
- Poultry
- Eggs
- Seafood
- Unpasteurized dairy

Prepared foods:
- Deli meats and salads
- Sprouts
- Pre-cut fruits/vegetables
- Foods left at room temperature

In emergencies:
- Food without refrigeration
- Water from unknown sources
- Food of unknown age/handling

EMERGENCY FOOD SAFETY
---------------------

WITHOUT REFRIGERATION:
- Eat perishables first
- When in doubt, throw it out
- Don't rely on smell/taste alone
- Use shelf-stable alternatives

WITHOUT CLEAN WATER:
- Treat all water before use
- Use treated water for food prep
- Avoid raw produce you can't peel

CONTAMINATION SUSPECTED:
- Don't eat potentially contaminated food
- Inform others who may have eaten it
- Note what was eaten and when

FOOD POISONING LOG
------------------

If someone gets sick, document:
- What was eaten (last 72 hours)
- When eaten
- When symptoms started
- Symptoms experienced
- Severity and progression
- Treatment given

This helps identify cause and aids medical care.

QUICK REFERENCE
---------------

FOOD POISONING RESPONSE:
1. Stop eating suspected food
2. Hydrate (small, frequent sips)
3. Rest
4. BRAT diet when ready
5. Seek help if severe symptoms

DEHYDRATION SOLUTION (homemade):
1 liter clean water
6 teaspoons sugar
1/2 teaspoon salt
Mix well, sip frequently

WARNING SIGNS (seek help):
- Blood in stool/vomit
- High fever
- Can't keep fluids down
- Symptoms over 3 days
- Neurological symptoms
