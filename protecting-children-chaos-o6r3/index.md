# Protecting Children During Chaos

> **Their Safety is Your Priority** - Children are vulnerable during emergencies. Special planning ensures their physical and emotional protection.

## Physical Safety

### Keep Children Close

- Assign each child to an adult
- Use buddy system
- Physical contact when moving
- Don't let children wander
- Carry small children when needed

### Identification

If separated:
- Write name/contact on arm (permanent marker)
- ID bracelet or necklace
- Recent photo in wallet
- Know what they're wearing
- Teach them your phone number

### Meeting Points

Age-appropriate plans:
- Young children: Stay with assigned adult
- Older children: Know meeting points
- Teens: Communication plan
- Practice the plan

## Age-Appropriate Preparation

### Toddlers (1-3)

**Needs:**
- Diapers and wipes
- Formula/food
- Comfort items
- Carrier/stroller
- Extra clothes

**Preparation:**
- Always with designated adult
- Familiar routines matter
- Comfort items essential

### Preschool (4-5)

**Needs:**
- Simple instructions
- Comfort items
- Activities/distractions
- Familiar foods

**Can Learn:**
- Full name and parents' names
- Address (simple)
- Stay with adult
- Basic "what to do"

### Elementary (6-10)

**Can Learn:**
- Emergency procedures
- Basic first aid
- Phone numbers
- Meeting points
- Simple tasks

**Assign Simple Roles:**
- Carry their own go-bag
- Help younger siblings
- Know the plan

### Pre-Teens (11-13)

**Can Learn:**
- More complex procedures
- Helping roles
- Communication plans
- Decision-making basics
- Resource management

**Can Help With:**
- Younger children
- Simple tasks
- Communication
- Maintaining morale

### Teenagers (14-17)

**Treat as:**
- Junior adults
- Include in planning
- Assign real responsibilities
- Trust with important tasks

**Can Handle:**
- Complex tasks
- Caring for others
- Independent action if needed
- Communication coordination

## Emotional Protection

### During Emergency

- Stay calm (children mirror adults)
- Simple, honest explanations
- Physical comfort
- Maintain routines when possible
- Validate their feelings

### What to Say

**Good:**
- "We're together and that's what matters"
- "Our job is to stay safe"
- "I'll tell you what you need to know"
- "It's okay to be scared"

**Avoid:**
- Detailed scary information
- Your own panic
- Minimizing their fears
- Lying about the situation

### Signs of Distress

Watch for:
- Regression (thumb-sucking, bed-wetting)
- Clinginess
- Sleep problems
- Appetite changes
- Behavioral changes
- Withdrawal

## Supplies for Children

### Go-Bag Additions

- [ ] Diapers/pull-ups if needed
- [ ] Formula/special foods
- [ ] Comfort item (blanket, toy)
- [ ] Activities (small toys, coloring)
- [ ] Change of clothes
- [ ] Medications
- [ ] Snacks they'll eat

### Comfort Essentials

Even in emergencies:
- Familiar items
- Favorite snacks
- Something to do
- Photos of family
- Comfort items from home

## Special Needs Children

### Additional Planning

- Medical needs documented
- Supplies for specific conditions
- Communication aids if needed
- Sensory considerations
- Routine accommodation

### School Plans

- Know school emergency procedures
- Reunification plans
- Authorization for pickup
- School contact information
