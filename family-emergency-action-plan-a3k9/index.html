Family Emergency Action Plan (All Hazards)

Foundation Document - Your family emergency plan is the single most important preparedness document you'll create. This guide walks you through building a comprehensive plan that works for any emergency.

Why You Need a Written Plan

During emergencies, stress impairs decision-making. A written plan:

  • Eliminates guesswork when seconds count
  • Ensures all family members know their roles
  • Provides clarity when communication is limited
  • Reduces panic through predetermined actions

Step 1: Identify Your Risks

Before planning responses, understand what you're preparing for.

Assess Your Specific Risks

  1. Research local history - What disasters have occurred in your area?
  2. Check FEMA maps - Are you in a flood zone, earthquake zone, etc.?
  3. Consider proximity - Near industrial facilities? Major highways? Fault lines?
  4. Evaluate infrastructure - How reliable are local utilities?

Step 2: Establish Communication Protocols

Primary Contact System

Designate an out-of-area contact (OAC) - someone 100+ miles away who can relay messages.

Family Communication Chain:
1. Try to reach family directly (call, text)
2. If unsuccessful, contact OAC
3. Leave status update with OAC
4. Check in with OAC at predetermined times

Communication Methods Priority

  1. Text messages - Often work when calls don't
  2. Phone calls - Direct communication
  3. Social media check-in - Facebook Safety Check, etc.
  4. Ham radio - If licensed and equipped
  5. Physical meeting point - Last resort

Step 3: Define Meeting Points

Meeting Point A: Near Home

  • Walking distance from home
  • Easily identifiable (park, school, church)
  • Known to all family members
  • Use for: House fires, gas leaks, immediate evacuations

Meeting Point B: Outside Neighborhood

  • 1-5 miles from home
  • Accessible by multiple routes
  • Has basic facilities (shelter, water)
  • Use for: Neighborhood-wide evacuations

Meeting Point C: Out of Area

  • 50+ miles away
  • Friend/family home or predetermined location
  • Use for: Regional disasters requiring evacuation

Step 5: Create Scenario Plans

🔥 Scenario: Fire

IMMEDIATE ACTIONS:
1. Alert family - yell "FIRE" loudly
2. Everyone exits using nearest safe route
3. Meet at Meeting Point A
4. Call 911 from safe location
5. Do NOT re-enter for any reason

IMPORTANT:
- Feel doors before opening
- Stay low if smoke present
- Use window exits if doors blocked

🏠 Scenario: Shelter-in-Place

IMMEDIATE ACTIONS:
1. Get all family members indoors
2. Lock all doors and windows
3. If chemical threat: seal room, turn off HVAC
4. Monitor emergency radio
5. Do NOT leave until all-clear given

🚗 Scenario: Evacuation Order

IMMEDIATE ACTIONS:
1. Grab go-bags (assigned family member)
2. Grab documents kit (assigned family member)
3. Secure pets (assigned family member)
4. Shut off utilities if time permits
5. Follow predetermined evacuation route
6. Contact OAC with status and destination
7. Meet at appropriate meeting point

👥 Scenario: Family Separated

IF SEPARATED:
1. Go to nearest safe location
2. Contact OAC with your location and status
3. Check in with OAC every 2 hours
4. Proceed to Meeting Point B if local
5. Proceed to Meeting Point C if evacuation needed
6. Do NOT search for family members in dangerous areas

Step 6: Document Everything

Emergency Plan Binder Contents

  1. Contact list (laminated)
  2. Meeting point addresses with maps
  3. Evacuation routes (multiple options)
  4. Utility shutoff instructions with diagrams
  5. Medical information for each family member
  6. Insurance documents (copies)
  7. Pet information (vet contacts, medications)
  8. Scenario action plans

Digital Backup

  • Store encrypted copies in cloud storage
  • Email copies to OAC
  • Save to USB drive in go-bag
  • Take photos of critical documents
